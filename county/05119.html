📊 Local Market Analysis

Pulaski County’s cost of living sits well below the national benchmark, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices are only modestly lower than average (goods index 93.1), while the biggest deviation appears in services—healthcare, utilities and dining register at 56.7, roughly a 43 % discount to the national level. Rental costs, measured by the housing component of the RPP, are 81.9, indicating that a typical apartment costs about 18 % less than the countrywide average. The expense profile is dominated by goods, which, at 93.1, are the highest‑priced segment in the county. Services are markedly cheap, pulling the overall index down and making day‑to‑day living relatively affordable. Median monthly rent of $1,036 translates to 20.6 % of the median household income of $60,385, a rent‑burden ratio that sits comfortably under the 30 % threshold often cited as a stress point. After rent, the median household retains roughly $49,300 annually, or $4,108 per month, to cover food, transportation, health, and discretionary spending. The Purchasing Power Index of 69,560 underscores that, despite a lower income than the national median ($65,047), residents enjoy greater real buying capacity because of the pronounced service‑price discount. Demographically, the county’s labor force is relatively young (median age 37.5) and modestly educated, with 22.1 % holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. Unemployment at 4.9 % hovers near the national rate, suggesting a stable job market but limited high‑skill wage premiums. The combination of a younger, less‑credentialed workforce and lower service costs keeps household budgets tight but manageable, especially for families that can leverage the cheap utility and healthcare environment. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median income rose 30.9 % from $46,140 to $60,385. Rent climbed 33 % in the same period, outpacing income growth by a narrow margin. The modest deflation in overall price levels has largely offset the rent increase, leaving disposable income roughly on par with the decade‑ago baseline. Wage growth has kept pace with most expense categories, though renters should anticipate a slightly tighter margin for housing than for other necessities. Prospective residents should budget rent as the primary fixed cost, allocating about one‑fifth of gross earnings. With services priced well below national averages, food, utilities and healthcare will consume a smaller share of the budget than in most regions. A realistic monthly plan would earmark $1,036 for rent, $400–$500 for utilities, $300–$400 for groceries, and $200–$300 for transportation, leaving roughly $1,600 for discretionary spending and savings. Understanding this allocation helps newcomers gauge the affordability of everyday life in Pulaski County without relying on speculative market forecasts.

What this means for residents of Pulaski County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69560.0, a median household income of $60,385 in Pulaski County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $42,003,806 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
