📊 Local Market Analysis

Pawnee County residents operate within a cost structure roughly 10 percent below the national baseline, reflected in a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 89.9, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—sit at 96.5, nearly matching the national average and representing the highest cost component locally, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 68.6, the lowest segment and the primary driver of the overall affordability advantage. Rental costs, indexed at 90.8, track closer to goods than services, meaning daily essentials and shelter consume a disproportionate share of disposable income relative to the broader service economy. That purchasing power dynamic translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 70,766.8, effectively boosting the median household income of $63,625 well above its nominal value and surpassing the national median of $65,047 in real terms. Housing absorbs only 15.2 percent of gross income at a median rent of $806, a stark contrast to the national median of $928 and a threshold that leaves significant latitude for savings or discretionary spending. The labor market reinforces this stability; an unemployment rate of 1.2 percent signals extreme labor scarcity, likely supporting wage floors despite an educational attainment rate where only 12.8 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. An older median age of 44.9 years suggests a settled workforce with established consumption patterns, less prone to the volatility of transient rental markets. Over the past decade, the affordability calculus has shifted measurably. The RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, indicating that local consumer prices fell relative to the national basket. Nominal median income surged 47.1 percent from $43,250, yet median rent accelerated 86.6 percent from $432, outpacing wage growth by nearly a factor of two. While the overall price level eased, the shelter component exerted significant upward pressure on the household budget, eroding a portion of the real income gains captured by the purchasing power index. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on the divergence between cheap services and stubbornly average goods and rent. Grocery bills and fuel costs will feel familiar to anyone relocating from a metro area, offering little discount, while healthcare, utilities, and restaurant meals deliver meaningful savings. The low rent burden provides a buffer, but the rapid historical escalation of housing costs—nearly doubling since 2010—warrants scrutiny of lease terms and local supply constraints. With a tight labor market supporting income stability, the primary variable for household solvency remains the trajectory of rental and goods inflation relative to wage adjustments.

What this means for residents of Pawnee County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70766.8, a median household income of $63,625 in Pawnee County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $45,025,376 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
