📊 Local Market Analysis

Smith County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 89.9, meaning consumer prices are roughly 10 percent below the national average of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel, and fuel—registers at 96.5, only marginally cheaper than the nation, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly inexpensive at 68.6. Rental costs, measured by the BEA RPP housing sub‑index, are 90.8, indicating rents are about 9 percent lower than the national norm. The structure of local expenses is dominated by goods, which sit just under parity with national prices, whereas services and housing provide the bulk of the affordability. Daily life in Smith County is therefore inexpensive in the categories that consume the most discretionary spending—healthcare, utilities, and restaurant meals—yet residents do not benefit from dramatically lower grocery or fuel bills. The median monthly rent of $601 reinforces the modest housing cost, representing only 12.6 percent of the median household income of $57,024. By contrast, the national median rent of $928 consumes a larger share of income, highlighting the relative rent‑burden relief in the county. Purchasing power, quantified by the index of 63,424.8, reveals that a typical household retains roughly $63,425 of its annual earnings after adjusting for local price levels. When expressed as a percentage of the median income, this translates to about 111 percent of earnings, indicating that residents can afford slightly more than their nominal income would suggest in a neutral‑price environment. The low rent burden—12.6 percent versus the often‑cited 30 percent affordability threshold—means that a substantial portion of disposable income remains for non‑housing expenditures, reinforcing the county’s purchasing advantage. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight: unemployment stands at 1.6 percent, well below the national rate, and the median age of 50 years suggests a mature workforce, likely with established earnings and lower consumption of child‑related goods. However, only 18.6 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a labor pool concentrated in sectors that may not command premium wages. The combination of low unemployment and modest educational attainment yields stable but modest income growth, which aligns with the observed affordability in services and housing. Over the past decade, the regional price parity index fell from 92.99 to 89.91, a 3.3 percent decline, signaling that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Simultaneously, median household income rose from $43,848 to $57,024, a 30 percent increase, outpacing the modest drop in price levels. Rent followed a similar trajectory, climbing from $459 to $601, a 30.9 percent rise that mirrors income growth and keeps the rent‑to‑income ratio stable. Wages have therefore kept pace with, and exceeded, the pace of inflation, enhancing real purchasing power. Prospective residents should budget around $601 for monthly rent, allocate a larger share of their budget to groceries and fuel given the near‑national pricing, and expect substantially lower expenditures on healthcare, utilities, and dining out. With a median household income of $57,024, after rent and typical service costs, a family can comfortably allocate the remainder to savings or discretionary spending. The low rent burden and favorable purchasing power index suggest that, despite modest wage levels, Smith County offers a household budget environment where essential expenses consume a smaller slice of income than in many parts of the country.

What this means for residents of Smith County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63424.8, a median household income of $57,024 in Smith County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $36,167,358 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
