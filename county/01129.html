📊 Local Market Analysis

Washington County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—track at 94.6, modestly above the county’s overall index but still 5.4 % cheaper than the countrywide average. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are the most affordable component at 61.6, indicating that everyday expenses such as medical visits and restaurant meals cost roughly 38 % less than elsewhere. Rental costs, measured by the housing sub‑index, are 89.9, aligning closely with the overall index and confirming that monthly rent remains 11 % lower than the national median of $928. The consumption profile is driven primarily by the low cost of services, which offsets the relatively higher goods price. With median monthly rent at $628, a household earning the county’s median income of $60,503 spends only 12.5 % of its earnings on housing—well under the 30 % affordability threshold commonly cited. After accounting for rent, the median family retains roughly $4,900 per month before taxes and other mandatory deductions. The Purchasing Power Index of 67,907 translates to a real‑income advantage of about 8 % over the national average, meaning residents can afford a larger basket of goods and services with their earnings despite the modest wage level. Demographically, the county’s labor market reflects limited high‑skill employment: only 8.5 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the unemployment rate stands at 6.2 %, above the national rate. The median age of 44.1 suggests a mature population, often associated with stable consumption patterns and lower discretionary spending on education or child‑related services. The combination of modest wages and a high proportion of older, possibly retired, residents reinforces the reliance on low‑cost services and the importance of affordable housing in maintaining disposable income. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened slightly relative to the nation. In contrast, median household income surged 41.3 % from $42,811 to $60,503, while median rent rose only 10.4 % from $569 to $628. The disparity between income growth and price stability suggests that wages have outpaced living‑cost increases, enhancing real purchasing power for most households. Prospective residents should budget for a monthly rent of $628, allocate a modest share to utilities and healthcare given the 61.6 services index, and anticipate grocery and fuel expenses that hover near the national average. With a rent burden well under 15 % of income, the county offers a relatively generous margin for discretionary spending, provided that employment aligns with the prevailing wage structure.

What this means for residents of Washington County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 67906.9, a median household income of $60,503 in Washington County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $41,085,712 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
