📊 Local Market Analysis

Cook County residents navigate a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity registering 98.8 for 2023 against a national index of 100. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: the goods basket—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—runs 1.6 percent above the national average at 101.6, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in 7.6 percent cheaper at 92.4. The most pronounced deviation sits in rental housing, where costs register a striking 80.4, nearly twenty points below the national norm, establishing shelter as the primary deflationary force in the local consumer budget. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life in the county is structurally inexpensive relative to peer metropolitan areas, driven almost entirely by the rental discount. While households pay a slight premium at the grocery store and the pump, the 19.6 percent rent savings on a median monthly outlay of $1,381—compared to the $928 national median—generates substantial slack in the monthly ledger. This dynamic pushes the median rent burden down to 20.3 percent of the $81,797 median household income, a level that suggests housing costs are not crowding out other consumption categories to the degree observed in coastal metros where burdens frequently exceed 30 percent. Purchasing power metrics confirm the advantage. The Purchasing Power Index of 82,830.7 indicates that the median dollar stretches considerably further here than the nominal income gap against the national median of $65,047 would suggest. Local demographics reinforce this profile: a median age of 37.8 and a bachelor’s attainment rate of 24.4 percent signal a workforce concentrated in prime earning years with moderate credentialing, while a 6.9 percent unemployment rate points to labor market slack that may suppress wage growth but simultaneously dampens demand-side price pressures on services. Over the 2010 to 2023 window, the RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, even as nominal median income surged 48.0 percent against a 40.9 percent rise in median rent. Real income growth has therefore been robust, with wages outpacing the primary household expense category by a meaningful margin. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: allocate slightly above-average funds for the weekly grocery trip and vehicle fuel, but expect significant relief on lease renewals and utility bills. The services discount further reduces the friction of routine expenditures like medical visits and restaurant meals, creating a consumption environment where discretionary capacity remains elevated despite the unemployment headwind.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Cook County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 82830.7, a median household income of $81,797 in Cook County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $67,753,028 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
