📊 Local Market Analysis

Gooding County’s cost of living sits well below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 92.2 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register an index of 95.9, only five percent under the national level, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 86.9, a 13.1‑point discount. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component, with an index of 68.1, indicating that rents are roughly 32 % lower than the national benchmark. The median monthly rent of $859 further underscores this advantage, falling $69 short of the national median of $928 and representing just 16.5 % of the county’s median household income of $62,395. The structure of local expenses is driven primarily by the low housing cost component. While goods remain close to national levels, the steep discount on rentals pulls the overall cost index down. Daily life in Gooding County can be characterized as inexpensive for housing but only modestly cheaper for everyday purchases. A typical household spending $859 on rent retains $1,416 of its monthly income (after rent) before accounting for other necessities, whereas a comparable household in a higher‑cost area would see a larger share of earnings consumed by housing. The service sector’s 86.9 index suggests that utilities and healthcare are also more affordable, further stretching disposable income. Purchasing power in the county is reflected in the Purchasing Power Index of 67,647.1, a figure that translates to roughly $67,600 of real buying capacity per household when national price levels are used as a baseline. After deducting the median rent, the median earner still has about $61,536 left for other expenses, equating to a rent‑burden ratio of 16.5 %—well under the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a stress point. This residual income supports a relatively comfortable standard of living, especially given the county’s lower service costs. However, the modest educational attainment—only 11.1 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—limits access to higher‑paying occupations, which tempers the overall purchasing power despite the favorable price environment. Local demographics shape the cost experience in several ways. The median age of 38.4 years suggests a mature workforce, yet the unemployment rate of 5.0 % exceeds the national average, indicating some friction in the labor market. The limited share of college‑educated residents points to a concentration of jobs in sectors that typically offer lower wages, reinforcing the importance of the county’s cheap housing in maintaining affordability. The combination of a relatively youthful median age and modest educational levels creates a labor pool that benefits from low living costs but may face constrained upward mobility. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.2 in 2010 to 92.2 in 2023, a 1.0 % decline, signaling a slight easing of price pressures. Median household income, however, surged 56.3 % from $39,930 to $62,395, outpacing the 14.5 % rise in median rent (from $750 to $859). This divergence indicates that wages have grown substantially faster than housing costs, enhancing real disposable income and reinforcing the county’s affordability advantage. The modest increase in goods prices, as reflected by the 95.9 index, has not eroded the overall purchasing power gains. Prospective residents should budget with housing as the primary lever for savings. Allocating roughly 16‑17 % of income to rent leaves ample room for groceries, utilities, and transportation, which are only marginally higher than the national average for goods but lower for services. A realistic monthly budget might allocate $850 to rent, $400 to groceries and fuel, $250 to utilities and healthcare, and $300 to discretionary spending, leaving a cushion for savings or debt repayment. Understanding that the low rent component offsets higher‑than‑average goods prices will help newcomers plan a balanced household budget that leverages Gooding County’s overall cost advantage.

What this means for residents of Gooding County, Idaho

With a Purchasing Power Index of 67647.1, a median household income of $62,395 in Gooding County, Idaho has the equivalent buying power of $42,208,408 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 68.1 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
