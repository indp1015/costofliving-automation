📊 Local Market Analysis

Menard County residents experience a cost of living modestly below the national benchmark, registering a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 98.8 against the U.S. average of 100. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—run 1.6 percent above the national index at 101.6, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining sit 7.6 percent cheaper at 92.4. The dominant discount driver is rental housing, which clocks in at 80.4, nearly twenty percentage points below the national norm and substantially lower than the national median rent of $928; the local median monthly rent of $780 consumes only 11.5 percent of the median household income of $81,334, a burden roughly half the standard affordability threshold. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is structurally inexpensive primarily because shelter costs are deeply discounted, offsetting the slight premium paid for tangible goods. A household allocating a typical budget share to rent captures immediate surplus relative to peers in higher-cost metros, yet the 101.6 goods index implies that weekly trips to the grocery store or fuel pump extract a marginally higher toll than the national average. Services, often the stickiest component of household expenditure, provide meaningful relief at 92.4, suggesting lower outlays for medical visits, insurance premiums, and utility bills. Consequently, the overall basket is cheap not because consumption is uniformly discounted, but because the largest fixed expense—housing—carries a profound local discount. Purchasing power metrics confirm the advantage: the Purchasing Power Index of 82,361.9 indicates that the median earner commands real income roughly 26.6 percent above the national median income of $65,047 after adjusting for local prices. This surplus stems from a 34.5 percent nominal income surge since 2010—rising from $60,486 to $81,334—while median rent increased only 20.6 percent over the same period, moving from $647 to $780. The RPP itself declined 3.0 percent across the decade, falling from 101.778 to 98.752, meaning local inflation has effectively run cooler than the national rate, amplifying real wage growth. Local demographics frame this affordability within a specific labor context. An unemployment rate of 3.9 percent signals a tight labor market, yet educational attainment remains modest with only 16.0 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and a median age of 42.8 years suggests a settled, experienced workforce rather than a transient student population. This profile implies that the wage gains supporting high purchasing power are likely tied to established industries or public-sector employment rather than high-skill tech inflows. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is clear: housing costs will be remarkably low, freeing cash flow for savings or discretionary spending, but grocery and fuel budgets should be calibrated to national or slightly above-national levels, while healthcare and utility line items offer consistent discounts.

What this means for residents of Menard County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 82361.9, a median household income of $81,334 in Menard County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $66,988,228 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
