📊 Local Market Analysis

Hopkins County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — represent the highest cost component at 94.5, merely 5.5 points shy of the national baseline, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 62.9, a staggering 37.1 points below average. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 80.9, suggesting that while shelter remains relatively affordable, the daily expense of keeping a household stocked and mobile erodes the broad affordability narrative. This inversion, where tangible goods command near-parity pricing while labor-intensive services are deeply discounted, defines the local consumption reality: a trip to the grocery store or gas station feels nationally priced, but a visit to the doctor or a utility bill reflects the region’s lower wage structure. The median household income of $57,610 trails the national median of $65,047 by 11.4 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 64,089.4 indicates that the lower cost environment effectively boosts real income by roughly $6,500 above the nominal figure. Rent burden stands at a manageable 17.5 percent of gross income, with the median monthly rent of $841 comparing favorably against the $928 national average, leaving considerable discretionary capacity for a two-earner household. However, the 6.4 percent unemployment rate and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 9.1 percent — less than half the national rate — signal a labor market concentrated in lower-wage sectors where the high relative cost of goods consumes a disproportionate share of take-home pay. The median age of 41.3 years suggests a stable, established population, but the educational profile limits upward mobility into the service-sector roles that benefit most from the area’s 37 percent service discount. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP barely budged, slipping a marginal 0.6 percent from 90.4 to 89.9, indicating remarkable price stability relative to the nation. Nominal income growth of 36 percent, rising from $42,346 to $57,610, comfortably outpaced the 42.1 percent surge in median rent from $592 to $841, a dynamic that has improved the rent-to-income ratio despite the faster pace of shelter inflation. Because the overall price level remained flat while wages climbed, the purchasing power gap has widened in the resident’s favor over the decade, though the acceleration in rental costs since 2020 warrants monitoring for low-income renters who lack the equity buffer of homeowners. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror metropolitan prices, leveraging the 94.5 goods index as a planning baseline rather than assuming a blanket rural discount. The 62.9 services index translates to tangible savings on electricity, water, and outpatient care, effectively subsidizing fixed monthly overhead. Conversely, the tight labor market and low educational attainment imply limited high-wage opportunities, meaning the purchasing power advantage relies heavily on maintaining employment in a county where joblessness exceeds the national rate by nearly two percentage points. Household budgeting here hinges on managing the "goods gap" — the near-national cost of consumables — while capturing the full value of the deep service discount to build savings or absorb the rent trajectory that has historically outpaced income growth.

What this means for residents of Hopkins County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64089.4, a median household income of $57,610 in Hopkins County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $36,921,903 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
