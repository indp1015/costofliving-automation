📊 Local Market Analysis

Clinton County’s cost of living sits well below the national benchmark, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 88.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 96.6, still under the national level, while services are markedly cheaper at 66.0. Housing costs, measured by the rental‑cost index, register at 87.3, indicating that renters pay roughly 13 % less than the average American household. The median monthly rent of $825 confirms this advantage, falling $103 short of the national median rent of $928 and representing only 15.2 % of the county’s median household income of $65,177. The consumption profile reveals that goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—drive the bulk of local expenses, accounting for the highest index score among the three categories. Yet even this “high” segment remains under national price pressure, suggesting that day‑to‑day purchases are modestly affordable. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are the cheapest pillar, at two‑thirds of the national level, which cushions household budgets. Rental costs, while not the cheapest, are still comfortably lower than the national average, reinforcing a picture of overall affordability. The combined effect is a low rent‑burden ratio of 15.2 % of income, far beneath the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a stress point, allowing residents to allocate a larger share of earnings to discretionary spending or savings. Purchasing power in Clinton County is quantified by an index of 73,423.2, translating to roughly $73,400 of real purchasing ability for the median household after adjusting for local price levels. Subtracting the average rent of $825 leaves about $4,800 annually, or $400 monthly, for other housing‑related outlays such as utilities and insurance, which are themselves cheap relative to national norms. After accounting for the 66.0 services index, a median earner retains a sizable discretionary margin, reinforcing the county’s reputation for a “stretchable” paycheck despite a median income ($65,177) that mirrors the national median ($65,047) almost exactly. Demographically, the county’s labor market features a 3.8 % unemployment rate, modestly lower than the national average, indicating stable employment prospects. However, only 15.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, possibly manufacturing or service‑oriented occupations. The median age of 42.5 years points to a mature population, which may temper consumption intensity, especially in categories like apparel and entertainment. This demographic mix contributes to the low services index, as demand for premium healthcare and high‑end dining is limited, while the stable job market helps keep rent‑burden low. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 91.139 in 2010 to 88.769 in 2023, a 2.6 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. In contrast, median household income rose from $50,498 to $65,177, a 29.1 % increase, outpacing the 33.1 % rise in median rent ($620 to $825). The faster growth in income relative to rent and the modest decline in overall price levels suggest that wages have more than kept pace with expenses, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. Prospective residents should budget around $825 for monthly rent, allocate roughly $200–$250 for utilities and basic services given the low services index, and expect grocery and fuel costs to be near national averages but still modest. With a rent‑burden of only 15.2 % of income, a typical household can comfortably cover essential expenses and retain a meaningful portion of earnings for savings, transportation, or leisure. Planning for a modest discretionary budget of $400–$500 per month after rent and utilities will align with the county’s cost structure and preserve the financial flexibility that the data demonstrate.

What this means for residents of Clinton County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 73423.2, a median household income of $65,177 in Clinton County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $47,855,039 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
