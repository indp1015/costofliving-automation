📊 Local Market Analysis

Monona County presents a cost of living profile that sits 11.2 percentage points below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 for 2023, though this aggregate masks significant divergence across consumption categories. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 96.6, nearly converging with national pricing, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 66.0, a staggering 34-point discount to the U.S. average. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 87.3, reinforcing a structural dynamic where daily necessities remain relatively expensive compared to the deep discounts available in locally delivered services. This consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is fundamentally shaped by the goods-services split; residents face near-national prices at the pump and grocery aisle but capture substantial savings on recurring service obligations. The median monthly rent of $795 consumes only 15.2 percent of the $62,944 median household income, a rent burden significantly lighter than the national comparison where median rent of $928 weighs heavier against a national median income of $65,047. Consequently, the Purchasing Power Index of 70,907.6 indicates the median household commands roughly $8,000 more in real purchasing capacity than their nominal income suggests, effectively stretching every dollar 12.7 percent further than the national baseline allows. Local demographics sharpen this advantage. An unemployment rate of 2.1 percent signals a tight labor market that supports wage stability, while a median age of 44.7 years and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 12.7 percent suggest an economy anchored in experienced, non-degreed labor rather than high-wage knowledge sectors. This composition likely suppresses nominal wage growth relative to metropolitan areas but simultaneously dampens local service inflation, creating a self-reinforcing loop where lower service costs sustain real incomes despite modest educational premiums. Over the past decade, the RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8, even as median income surged 56.2 percent from $40,302 to $62,944, outpacing a 44.5 percent rise in median rent from $550 to $795. Wages have not merely kept pace with expenses; they have expanded the household buffer considerably. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is clear: allocate near-national budgets for groceries and transportation, but expect materially lower outlays for healthcare, utilities, and housing services, resulting in a discretionary surplus that exceeds the nominal income gap with the national median.

What this means for residents of Monona County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70907.6, a median household income of $62,944 in Monona County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $44,632,080 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
