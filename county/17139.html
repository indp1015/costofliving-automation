📊 Local Market Analysis

Moultrie County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet the aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the primary inflationary pressure at 101.6, exceeding the national average by 1.6 percent and functioning as the highest cost component in the local basket. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining operate at a substantial 7.6 percent discount to the national norm (92.4), while rental housing costs sit 19.6 percent below average at 80.4, marking the lowest cost driver. This structure creates a distinctive consumer environment where daily variable expenses for tangible goods erode savings more aggressively than fixed shelter or service outlays, a dynamic that inverts the typical urban pattern where housing dominates budgetary stress. The median household income of $71,676 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 10.2 percent, and when adjusted for the local price level, the Purchasing Power Index rises to $72,581.8, indicating that the average earner commands roughly $7,500 more in real goods and services than the national median income would suggest. Rent burden remains exceptionally low at 12.9 percent of gross income, with the median monthly rent of $773 comparing favorably against the national $928 benchmark. This surplus capacity — driven by the 80.4 rental index — effectively subsidizes the 101.6 goods index, allowing households to absorb above-average grocery and fuel prices without compromising discretionary spending or savings rates. The labor market reinforces this stability; a 2.6 percent unemployment rate signals tight demand for labor, supporting wage resilience despite a relatively low educational attainment threshold where only 13.0 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the county experienced a rare favorable divergence: the RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, meaning the area became relatively cheaper over the decade, while nominal median income surged 44.3 percent from $49,681 to $71,676. Median rent increased only 24.3 percent over the same horizon, from $622 to $773, lagging income growth by a full 20 percentage points. This trajectory suggests that wage gains have not merely kept pace with expenses but have substantially outstripped the primary cost-of-living drivers, particularly shelter. The median age of 40.4 years indicates a workforce in prime earning years, likely contributing to the income trajectory, though the low college attainment rate implies reliance on sectors — potentially manufacturing, agriculture, or logistics — where productivity gains have translated into compensation without requiring credential inflation. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. Housing affordability is a structural advantage, locking in a low baseline expenditure that insulates against interest-rate volatility affecting mortgage markets elsewhere. However, the 101.6 goods index demands vigilance on grocery procurement and transportation logistics; fuel and food budgets will track national inflation more closely than local wages might offset in a downturn. Healthcare and utility costs, indexed at 92.4, provide a moderate buffer, but the overall consumer proposition hinges on maintaining employment within the tight local labor market. A household relocating from a high-cost metro area would experience immediate shelter relief but should not extrapolate that savings rate across the entire consumption basket, as the goods penalty narrows the real arbitrage to roughly the 3.5 percent gap between the purchasing power index and nominal income.

What this means for residents of Moultrie County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 72581.8, a median household income of $71,676 in Moultrie County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $52,023,731 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
