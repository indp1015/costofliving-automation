📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Aleutians East Borough runs modestly above the national baseline, with an overall price‑level index of 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices sit at 103.7, indicating that everyday consumables are roughly 4 % pricier than the country average, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are 3.3 % cheaper (index 96.7). Housing is the outlier: rental costs are indexed at 113.3, a full 13 % premium over the national norm. Housing dominates the local cost structure. Median monthly rent of $1,040 exceeds the national median of $928 by 12 %, and rent consumes 17.2 % of median household income. By contrast, the lower‑priced services sector offsets part of the higher goods cost, making day‑to‑day expenses for food and transportation only slightly above average. The net effect is a mixed affordability picture: essential non‑housing items are affordable, but the elevated rent pushes the overall cost profile upward. Disposable income calculations reveal that the median household, earning $72,692, retains roughly $61,652 after rent, leaving about $5,040 per month for all other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index of 70,371.8—well below the national reference of 100—reflects that, despite a median income 11.6 % higher than the national figure, the higher rent erodes real buying power. In practical terms, a resident can afford a comparable basket of goods and services as a national median household only after allocating a larger share of earnings to shelter. Demographic factors reinforce the cost experience. With a median age of 41.8, the population is older than the national median, suggesting a larger proportion of established households rather than students or young families. Only 12.9 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a labor force concentrated in sectors that do not command premium wages, yet the unemployment rate of 3.8 % is comfortably low, implying stable employment for those in the local economy. The modest educational attainment and older age profile likely limit upward pressure on wages, while the tight labor market sustains the current income level. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity rose from 103.099 to 103.297, a negligible 0.2 % increase, whereas median household income climbed 18.2 % from $61,518 to $72,692. Rent rose 11.8 % in the same period, outpacing the modest inflation in the overall price index but lagging behind income growth. Consequently, real purchasing power has improved, though the housing premium continues to temper that gain. Prospective residents should budget with housing as the primary cost driver. Allocating roughly one‑sixth of gross earnings to rent leaves about $5,000 per month for groceries, fuel, healthcare, utilities and discretionary spending. Because services are cheaper than the national average, budgeting for dining out or utility bills can be modest, but the higher goods index means food and apparel will cost slightly more. A realistic monthly budget therefore hinges on securing affordable rental arrangements and leveraging the lower service costs to balance the overall household expenditure.

What this means for residents of Aleutians East Borough, Alaska
