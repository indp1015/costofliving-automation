📊 Local Market Analysis

Stark County residents operate within a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that index reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—represent the primary inflationary pressure at 101.6, exceeding the national average by 1.6 percentage points and functioning as the highest cost component in the local consumption basket. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in at a substantial discount of 92.4, while rental housing costs sit 19.6 points below the national norm at 80.4, creating a bifurcated reality where shelter is remarkably accessible but the recurring expense of daily necessities exerts outsized pressure on disposable income. This structural dynamic translates into a purchasing power index of 63,071.1, effectively boosting the nominal median household income of $62,284 above its face value relative to national purchasing capacity. The rent burden metric underscores this advantage: at a median monthly rent of $765, housing consumes only 14.7 percent of gross income, a figure that sits well below the standard 30 percent affordability threshold and dwarfs the national median rent of $928. However, the local labor market context tempers this optimism. With a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 14.9 percent and a median age of 44.6 years, the workforce skews older and less credentialed than national averages, likely capping wage growth potential despite a low 3.1 percent unemployment rate that signals a tight but potentially low-wage labor market. Over the past decade, the trajectory of affordability has deteriorated despite a declining overall RPP, which fell from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023. Nominal median income rose 24.4 percent over the period, climbing from $50,085 to $62,284, yet median rent accelerated faster at 34.0 percent, jumping from $571 to $765. This divergence indicates that while the broader price level has softened relative to the nation, the specific cost of shelter—a non-discretionary fixed cost—has claimed a growing share of local earnings, eroding the real wage gains that the headline income growth suggests. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on a trade-off between exceptionally cheap housing and above-average prices for physical goods. A household relocating from a metro area will likely realize immediate shelter savings of roughly $163 per month versus the national median rent, but the grocery and fuel bill will run hotter than the national baseline. The low service cost index provides a cushion for healthcare and utilities, yet the demographic profile suggests limited high-income mobility. Residents should model their budgets assuming housing consumes roughly 15 percent of gross income while allocating a heavier-than-average share to the goods basket, recognizing that the purchasing power advantage exists primarily because shelter is cheap, not because wages are high.

What this means for residents of Stark County, Illinois

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
