📊 Local Market Analysis

McCreary County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits 10.1 percent below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a highly asymmetric consumer environment. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — run only 5.5 percent cheaper than the U.S. average at an index of 94.5, representing the highest cost component in the local basket. By contrast, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 62.9, a staggering 37.1 percent below national levels, while rental costs settle at 80.9. This structure means daily essentials like food and gasoline offer modest relief, but the deep discount on services — often non-discretionary items like medical care and electricity — drives the aggregate index down, creating a scenario where the baseline cost of survival is low, but the marginal cost of consumption goods remains sticky near national norms. The median household income of $37,355 sits 42.6 percent below the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 41,556.3 indicates that local wages stretch roughly 11.2 percent further than their nominal value suggests once the 89.9 RPP is applied. Housing affordability is a distinct bright spot: median monthly rent of $588 consumes only 18.9 percent of gross income, a burden significantly lighter than the national standard where $928 rent against higher incomes still pressures budgets. However, the low educational attainment — only 5.3 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher — paired with a median age of 38.7 and an exceptionally tight 2.3 percent unemployment rate, signals a labor market dominated by low-wage, likely service-sector or extraction employment. The scarcity of high-skill jobs caps earning potential, meaning the low cost of living functions less as a lifestyle arbitrage and more as a necessary equilibrium for the prevailing wage floor. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP barely budged, slipping a marginal 0.6 percent from 90.4 to 89.9, indicating remarkable price stability in a region often prone to volatility. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 93.3 percent from $19,328 to $37,355, vastly outpacing the 5.8 percent climb in median rent from $556 to $588. This divergence has dramatically improved the real income trajectory for incumbent households, effectively doubling purchasing power relative to shelter costs over thirteen years. The data suggests wages have not merely kept pace but have aggressively outstripped the primary household expense, a rare dynamic in contemporary regional economics that has likely reduced financial fragility for renters despite the persistently low absolute income ceiling. A prospective resident should budget for a daily experience where the grocery bill and fuel stops feel nearly identical to metropolitan areas, but the monthly nut for rent, utilities, and healthcare visits carries a steep discount. The 94.5 goods index implies limited savings on consumables, so household budgets should allocate roughly national-average shares for food and transportation. Conversely, the 62.9 services index suggests significant latitude for healthcare out-of-pocket costs and utility deposits, though access to specialized care may require travel. With rent claiming under 19 percent of median income, the residual cash flow is unusually high for a low-income county, but the lack of degree density and the tight labor market imply limited upward mobility; budgeting must account for income stagnation risk rather than cost inflation.

What this means for residents of McCreary County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 41556.3, a median household income of $37,355 in McCreary County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $15,523,356 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
