📊 Local Market Analysis

Knox County’s cost of living remains below the national benchmark, with an overall index of 92.1 in 2023, indicating that consumer prices are roughly 8 percent cheaper than the average American household faces. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 95.6, slightly above the county’s overall index and near the national parity, while Services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly inexpensive at 71.3, well under the national baseline. Housing costs, measured by rental rates, sit at 84.7, also cheaper than the national average, though the median monthly rent of $782 consumes 15.9 percent of the median household income of $58,863. The disparity among categories shows that daily life is driven primarily by the relatively higher expense of Goods, whereas Services provide a substantial discount that offsets overall affordability. The median worker’s disposable income reflects the interplay of earnings and local price levels. At $58,863, the county’s median household earns about $6,200 less than the national median of $65,047, yet the lower cost structure preserves purchasing power. The Purchasing Power Index of 63,914.1, while not directly comparable to a national figure, suggests that the median income retains more buying capacity after accounting for the modest cost of Living. After allocating rent at $782, a household has roughly $4,800 remaining from monthly income, leaving room for other expenditures, especially given that Services are inexpensive. Low unemployment of 2.5 percent signals a stable labor market, but the education profile—only 11.3 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher—may constrain higher‑wage opportunities, reinforcing the modest income level observed. Over the past decade, consumer costs have trended slightly downward, with the Regional Price Parity (RPP) falling from 93.022 in 2010 to 92.097 in 2023, a modest 1 percent decline. In contrast, median household income has risen sharply, up 37.8 percent from $42,725 to $58,863, while median rent has increased by 25.5 percent, from $623 to $782. Wage growth has outpaced rent inflation, and the overall cost of living has edged lower, indicating that earnings are gradually catching up to and surpassing housing expenses. The modest decline in the RPP, coupled with rising incomes, suggests that the county’s affordability has improved slightly, though the higher Goods index remains a point of concern for household budgets. Prospective residents should budget for a modest rent burden of 15.9 percent, leaving ample room for other expenses, but should anticipate that Goods constitute the largest share of out‑of‑pocket costs. What this means for residents of Knox County, Indiana
