📊 Local Market Analysis

Vermillion County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 92.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register an index of 95.6, still under the national average but the highest component locally. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheap at 71.3, while rental costs score 84.7, indicating that housing is roughly 15 % less expensive than the countrywide benchmark. The dominant driver of the county’s price structure is the services sector, whose index is 28.7 points lower than the national level. This compression translates into lower utility bills, medical expenses, and restaurant prices, making day‑to‑day living comparatively affordable. Goods remain the only category where prices approach parity with the broader market, so residents still feel national‑level pressure on food and transportation budgets. Rental costs, though higher than the services component, are still modest; the median monthly rent of $785 is 15 % below the national median of $928, reinforcing the view that housing does not dominate household outlays. Disposable income analysis shows a mixed picture. The median household income of $59,363 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 9 %, yet the Purchasing Power Index (PPI) of 64,457 places Vermillion County residents at 64 % of the national purchasing power baseline. After accounting for the average rent burden of 15.9 % of income, a typical household retains about $49,000 of annual earnings for all other expenses. When juxtaposed with the low services index, this retained income stretches further on health, utility, and dining costs, while the near‑national goods index limits savings potential on essential consumables. Demographic factors shape this cost environment. Only 12.6 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor force concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations, which aligns with the 6.0 % unemployment rate—slightly above the national average. The median age of 42.5 years points to a mature population, likely with established household spending patterns and limited reliance on student‑related expenditures. The combination of modest educational attainment and higher unemployment constrains wage growth, reinforcing the importance of the county’s inexpensive services and housing in preserving household solvency. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.0 to 92.1, a 1 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Median household income, however, surged 36.2 % from $43,600 to $59,363, outpacing the 41.4 % rise in median rent. While wages have grown faster than housing costs, they have not kept pace with the near‑national level of goods prices, leaving a narrower margin for discretionary spending. The modest decline in the overall price index suggests that the county’s affordability advantage has been preserved despite rising incomes. Prospective residents should budget with the services advantage in mind: utilities, medical care, and dining out will consume a smaller share of income than in most U.S. locales. However, grocery and fuel bills will resemble national averages, so allocating a proportionate share of the budget to these items remains prudent. With rent representing roughly 16 % of median earnings, housing costs are manageable, but the limited local wage premium means that discretionary spending—entertainment, travel, and savings—must be calibrated against the higher goods price component. A realistic monthly budget would prioritize low‑cost services, monitor grocery expenditures, and leverage the affordable rental market to maintain a balanced household cash flow.

What this means for residents of Vermillion County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64457.0, a median household income of $59,363 in Vermillion County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $38,263,609 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
