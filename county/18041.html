📊 Local Market Analysis

Fayette County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 7.9 percent below the national average, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 92.1 for 2023. The composite figure masks sharp divergence across categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run at 95.6, nearly parity with the U.S. baseline, while services — healthcare, utilities, and dining — collapse to 71.3, the lowest component and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs settle at 84.7, meaning a median monthly rent of $779 claims just 16.5 percent of the $56,659 median household income, a burden significantly lighter than the national median rent of $928 against a $65,047 national income. That rent-to-income ratio translates into a purchasing power index of 61,521, effectively granting the median household roughly $4,862 in additional real purchasing capacity compared with nominal earnings. The local labor market reinforces this dynamic: a 4.1 percent unemployment rate signals tightness, yet educational attainment remains modest at 12.4 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and a median age of 43.9 years suggests an established, aging workforce. These demographics correlate with the low services index; an older population with limited high-wage professional density suppresses demand for premium healthcare, dining, and personal services, keeping those prices depressed relative to goods, which are priced on broader national supply chains. Over the 2010–2023 window, the RPP drifted downward from 93.0 to 92.1, a marginal 1.0 percent decline, while nominal median income surged 43.9 percent from $39,379 to $56,659. Median rent rose only 20.2 percent, from $648 to $779, meaning real income growth substantially outpaced shelter cost inflation. The gap widened because goods inflation tracked close to national levels — the goods RPP barely budged from the mid-90s — while the services component remained structurally cheap, amplifying real wage gains for households whose consumption baskets weight heavily toward housing and services. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly identical to metropolitan areas, with the goods index at 95.6 offering little discount at the checkout counter. The savings materialize in monthly service obligations: utility contracts, insurance premiums, and routine medical visits carry price tags roughly 29 percent below the national norm. Rent consumes a modest slice of cash flow, but the low services environment also signals fewer high-end amenities and specialist providers. Households relocating from higher-cost regions will experience immediate relief on recurring service expenses, yet should not expect comparable discounts on tangible goods or vehicle-related costs.

What this means for residents of Fayette County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 61521.0, a median household income of $56,659 in Fayette County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $34,857,183 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
