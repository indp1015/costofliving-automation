📊 Local Market Analysis

Mason County’s cost of living sits just below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 98.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are modestly higher than the countrywide norm at 101.6, while services are noticeably cheaper at 92.4. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component, registering an index of 80.4—nearly 20 % below the national benchmark. The composite picture suggests that everyday expenses are generally lighter on the wallet than in many other parts of the country, driven primarily by low rent and inexpensive services such as healthcare and utilities. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the housing sector, which pulls the overall index down despite the slight premium on goods. With a median monthly rent of $760, residents pay 18 % less than the national median rent of $928, and rent consumes only 14.7 % of median household income. By contrast, the goods index indicates that groceries, apparel, and fuel cost about 1.6 % more than the national average, a modest surcharge that is more than offset by the housing advantage. Consequently, daily life in Mason County is relatively inexpensive; the low rent burden leaves a larger share of income for discretionary spending, even though residents pay a small premium for tangible consumer goods. Disposable income calculations reinforce the affordability narrative. The median household earns $62,127, which is 4.6 % below the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index (PPI) stands at 62,912.1, reflecting that the lower cost of housing and services translates into real purchasing power comparable to, and in some respects exceeding, national levels. After subtracting the median rent of $760 (9,120 annually) from the median income, roughly $53,000 remains for all other expenses, taxes, and savings—a figure that aligns with the PPI’s implication that residents retain a substantial portion of earnings for non‑housing consumption. Demographic and labor‑market characteristics shape this affordability. Only 12.4 % of the population holds a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age of 45.5 years points to a mature workforce, potentially limiting high‑skill wage growth. The unemployment rate of 6.1 % exceeds the national average, suggesting that while jobs exist, they may be concentrated in lower‑wage sectors. This labor profile explains why incomes have risen sharply—median household income jumped 49.5 % since 2010—yet remain modest relative to higher‑education counties. The combination of an older, less‑educated labor pool and a modest unemployment rate sustains a cost environment where essential services stay cheap, reinforcing the low‑rent advantage. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. During the same period, median income surged 49.5 %, outpacing the 25 % rise in median rent. The divergence between income growth and price pressure confirms that wages have more than kept pace with expenses, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. However, the modest increase in goods prices (still above national levels) signals that not all categories have benefited equally from income gains. Prospective residents should budget around $760 per month for rent, a figure that consumes less than 15 % of a median household’s earnings. Allocating roughly 10 % of income to utilities and healthcare aligns with the services index, while groceries and fuel may require a slightly higher share—approximately 12 % of income—due to the 101.6 goods index. With the remaining 63 % of earnings available for transportation, discretionary spending, and savings, households can maintain a comfortable standard of living without the rent burden typical in many regions. Careful tracking of goods prices, which sit above the national average, will be essential to preserve the budgetary cushion afforded by the county’s low housing costs.

What this means for residents of Mason County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 62912.1, a median household income of $62,127 in Mason County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $39,085,400 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
