📊 Local Market Analysis

Decatur County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percent below the national average, reflected in an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8, yet the composition of that discount reveals a sharply bifurcated consumer environment. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — track near parity at 96.6, meaning households pay nearly the same shelf prices as metropolitan counterparts for tangible necessities. The dominant deflationary force is the services category, which registers a striking 66.0, or 34 percent below the U.S. baseline, driven by deeply discounted healthcare, utilities, and dining-out expenses. Rental costs, indexed at 87.3, provide meaningful but less dramatic relief at 12.7 percent below average. Consequently, daily life is inexpensive for service-intensive consumption patterns — a household allocating a larger share of its budget to medical care, electricity, or restaurant meals captures outsized savings — while the weekly grocery run and fuel stops deliver minimal arbitrage. Purchasing power metrics confirm that the median household retains substantial real income after housing obligations. With median annual earnings of $57,146 against a national benchmark of $65,047, nominal income lags by 12.1 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 64,376.1 exceeds nominal wages by 12.7 percent, indicating that local price levels more than offset the earnings gap. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 13.9 percent of gross income — the median monthly outlay of $663 contrasts sharply with the national median of $928 — freeing discretionary capacity for savings, transportation, or the near-national-cost goods basket. The labor market reinforces this stability: a 3.2 percent unemployment rate signals tight conditions, while a median age of 36.2 and a 14.2 percent bachelor's attainment rate suggest a workforce anchored in established, if not highly credentialed, occupations that benefit from the low service-cost environment. Over the 2010–2023 period, the county's cost advantage has widened modestly, with the RPP declining 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8 even as median income surged 48.2 percent from $38,560 to $57,146 and median rent climbed 43.5 percent from $462 to $663. Wage growth has comfortably outpaced both inflation and shelter costs, expanding real purchasing power across the decade. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the goods–services split: expect to pay metropolitan prices for food, clothing, and gasoline, but anticipate materially lower outlays for healthcare visits, utility bills, and prepared meals. Housing consumes a minimal share of cash flow, yet the goods-heavy components of a typical budget — groceries, vehicle fuel, household supplies — will not reflect the county's overall discount. Households relocating from higher-cost regions should model their specific consumption mix rather than rely on the headline index, as the 34 percent services discount only translates to total savings if spending patterns align with that category.

What this means for residents of Decatur County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64376.1, a median household income of $57,146 in Decatur County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $36,788,366 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
