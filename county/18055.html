📊 Local Market Analysis

Greene County residents operate in a cost environment running 7.9 percent below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 92.1 against a U.S. average of 100. The composite figure masks sharp divergence across categories: goods — groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — sit at 95.6, only marginally discounted, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 71.3, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs settle at 84.7, meaning a median monthly rent of $788 consumes just 15.8 percent of the $59,753 median household income, well under the 30-percent burden threshold and substantially lighter than the national median rent of $928. Goods pricing drives the local cost structure because everyday consumables track closer to national wholesale and logistics networks, limiting the discount available in a rural Midwestern market. Services, by contrast, reflect lower provider density, reduced overhead, and wage structures tied to the county's 10.0 percent bachelor's-degree attainment rate and 6.4 percent unemployment rate, both of which suppress labor-intensive service fees. The median age of 43.1 years suggests a workforce skewed toward established earners rather than early-career renters, reinforcing a consumption basket weighted toward healthcare and utilities where the 28.7-percent service discount delivers outsized relief. Purchasing power translates to an index value of 64,880.5, indicating the median household commands roughly $5,100 more real purchasing capacity than its nominal income suggests after adjusting for local prices. Over the 2010–2023 window, the RPP slipped only 1.0 percent from 93.022 to 92.097, while nominal median income rose 31.6 percent from $45,397 and median rent climbed 35.4 percent from $582. Rent growth has modestly outpaced income growth, narrowing the affordability cushion, yet the absolute burden remains low because the starting base was inexpensive. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly national in magnitude, while anticipating significantly lower outlays for medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals. The 15.8-percent rent-to-income ratio leaves roughly 84 cents of every dollar for non-housing essentials, savings, or discretionary spend — a margin that accommodates the county's higher unemployment risk and lower educational wage premium. Households relocating from higher-cost metros will experience immediate service-sector savings, but should not assume goods inflation will remain decoupled from national trends.

What this means for residents of Greene County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64880.5, a median household income of $59,753 in Greene County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $38,768,045 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
