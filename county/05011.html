📊 Local Market Analysis

Bradley County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 93.1, merely 6.9 percent below the U.S. average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 56.7, a staggering 43.3 percent discount. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9. This configuration means daily essentials like food and transportation consume a disproportionate share of disposable income relative to the national basket, even as service-intensive expenditures such as medical care or restaurant meals offer significant relief. The median household income of $39,565 trails the national median of $65,047 by nearly 40 percent, but the Purchasing Power Index of 45,576.5 demonstrates that the local price level partially bridges that nominal gap, effectively boosting real income by roughly 15 percent compared to raw dollar figures. Housing affordability remains a relative bright spot: median monthly rent of $638 claims only 19.4 percent of gross income, well under the national median rent of $928 and the standard 30 percent burden threshold. However, with goods pricing near parity, a household allocating a standard 12 to 15 percent of income to groceries and fuel faces steeper effective rates than counterparts in higher-wage metros, compressing the margin for savings or discretionary spending on the very services—healthcare, childcare, dining—where local prices are cheapest. Demographic and labor market dynamics reinforce this squeeze. An unemployment rate of 4.4 percent suggests a tight labor market, yet educational attainment remains low at 10.4 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, capping wage growth potential in a region where median income rose just 17.4 percent between 2010 and 2023, from $33,701. Over that same span, the RPP actually declined from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while median rent increased 12.9 percent from $565. Wages have outpaced rent growth nominally, but the persistent near-national pricing of goods limits the translation of those gains into broader consumption capacity, particularly for the 38.5-year-old median resident likely balancing prime earning years with family formation costs. A prospective resident should budget for a "barbell" expense profile: housing consumes a modest, manageable slice of the paycheck, and services like utilities or medical visits are genuinely inexpensive, but the weekly grocery run and fuel fill-up will feel nearly as expensive as in Little Rock or Dallas on a per-unit basis. The 17.4 percent income growth over thirteen years has not fundamentally altered the structural reality that goods—the highest frequency, least substitutable purchases—anchor the cost of living at 93.1. Households moving from higher-cost areas will find immediate shelter savings, yet must recalibrate expectations for the variable costs of daily maintenance, which remain stubbornly tethered to national supply chains and transportation networks regardless of local wage floors.

What this means for residents of Bradley County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 45576.5, a median household income of $39,565 in Bradley County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $18,032,342 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
