📊 Local Market Analysis

Calhoun County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 88.8 against a benchmark of 100. Goods prices are the closest to the national level at 96.6, while services are markedly cheaper at 66.0, and rental costs lag modestly behind the country at 87.3. The disparity between the county’s median household income of $66,875 and the national median of $65,047 further underscores the relative affordability; residents earn roughly 2.9 % more than the average American while paying only 74 % of the typical national rent price ($690 versus $928). The composition of expenses reveals that goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—are the primary driver of the local price structure, accounting for the highest index value among the three categories. However, even the goods index remains under the national average, indicating that everyday purchases are only marginally more expensive than elsewhere. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are the cheapest component, at two‑thirds of the national cost, which translates into lower monthly bills for electricity, water, and medical visits. Rental costs, while below the national figure, are higher than the county’s historical rent level (up 33.5 % since 2010), yet the rent‑burden metric shows that only 12.4 % of household income is devoted to rent—far less than the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a strain. Consequently, daily life in Calhoun County can be characterized as inexpensive, driven primarily by low service costs and modest housing expenses. Disposable income calculations illustrate the purchasing power advantage. After subtracting the median rent of $690 from the median monthly income (approximately $5,573 before taxes), roughly $4,883 remains for all other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index of 75,336 reinforces this surplus, indicating that residents can buy a basket of goods and services worth about 25 % more than the national average consumer could with the same income. This surplus is amplified by the low service index, allowing households to allocate a larger share of their budget to discretionary spending or savings. Demographic factors shape this affordability landscape. With a unemployment rate of 2.8 %, the labor market is tight, supporting steady wages that have risen 48.9 % over the past decade. Yet only 15 % of the population holds a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor force concentrated in sectors that may not command premium salaries but benefit from low cost inputs. The median age of 42.7 years points to a mature, possibly dual‑income household base that can leverage the low cost environment to maintain a comfortable standard of living. Historical trends reveal a modest decline in the RPP from 91.1 in 2010 to 88.8 in 2023, a 2.6 % reduction in consumer prices, while median income surged nearly 49 % in the same period. Rent increased 33.5 %, outpacing inflation but remaining proportionally lower than income growth, confirming that wages have outpaced expenses. The net effect is an expanding real purchasing power for residents. Prospective residents should budget with the county’s low service and housing costs in mind. Assuming the median rent of $690, a realistic monthly allocation might include $300 for groceries (reflecting the near‑national goods price), $150 for utilities and healthcare, $100 for transportation fuel, and $150 for discretionary dining or entertainment. This leaves roughly $2,500 for other obligations such as debt service, insurance, or savings. The modest rent burden and strong purchasing power suggest that a household can comfortably cover essential expenses while retaining a sizable discretionary margin, making Calhoun County an economically favorable environment for those prioritizing cost efficiency over urban amenities.

What this means for residents of Calhoun County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 75336.0, a median household income of $66,875 in Calhoun County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $50,380,950 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
