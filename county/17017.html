📊 Local Market Analysis

Cass County’s cost‑of‑living index sits just below the national benchmark, with an overall RPP of 98.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are modestly higher than average at 101.6, while services are 7.6 points cheaper (92.4) and rental housing is markedly lower at 80.4. The disparity between the three components creates a mixed affordability picture: everyday necessities such as groceries, apparel and fuel cost slightly more than the nation, but utilities, healthcare and dining out are substantially cheaper, and rent is 22 % below the national median of $928, at $723 per month. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the goods sector, which pushes the overall index upward despite the pronounced housing discount. Because services are inexpensive and rent consumes only 13.4 % of median household income, daily life feels relatively affordable for residents whose spending patterns lean toward services rather than discretionary goods. Households that prioritize home‑cooked meals, public transportation and modest apparel will find their budgets stretched less than those whose consumption is goods‑heavy. The low rent burden—well under the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag housing stress—means that a larger share of disposable income remains for non‑housing categories. Median household income in Cass County is $64,907, essentially on par with the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median monthly rent of $723 (annualized $8,676), the median family retains roughly $56,231 of pre‑tax earnings for other expenses, equating to a purchasing‑power index of 65,727.3. This figure indicates that, relative to a national baseline of 100, residents enjoy about 65 % of the buying capacity of an average American household when housing costs are removed. The modest rent premium, combined with cheaper services, translates into a net disposable income that exceeds the national average by a small margin, especially for households that can avoid the higher‑priced goods segment. Demographically, the county’s labor market is modestly constrained: an unemployment rate of 4.9 % sits slightly above the national rate, while only 15 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, well below the national average. The median age of 38.1 suggests a mature workforce, but limited higher‑education attainment may suppress wage growth in high‑skill sectors, reinforcing reliance on lower‑cost service jobs. Consequently, the affordability advantage derived from cheap housing and services is partially offset by a narrower earnings ceiling for workers without advanced credentials. Over the past decade, the regional RPP fell from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline that signals a modest deflationary trend relative to the nation. Median household income, however, surged 36.8 % from $47,434 to $64,907, outpacing the 21.7 % rise in median rent (from $594 to $723). The income trajectory has therefore outstripped housing cost growth, enhancing real purchasing power and confirming that wages have kept pace with, and exceeded, the pace of consumer price changes. Prospective residents should anchor their monthly budget around the $723 rent figure and allocate roughly 7 % of income to utilities, healthcare and dining, given the 92.4 services index. A realistic grocery and apparel budget will need to reflect the 101.6 goods index, implying a 1‑2 % premium over national averages. With a median disposable income near $56,000 after housing, a household can comfortably cover essential expenses while retaining modest capacity for savings or discretionary spending, provided it avoids high‑priced consumer goods. Careful tracking of goods‑related outlays will be the key to preserving the county’s overall affordability advantage.

What this means for residents of Cass County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 65727.3, a median household income of $64,907 in Cass County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $42,661,619 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
