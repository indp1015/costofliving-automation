📊 Local Market Analysis

Butte County’s cost of living sits well above the national norm, with the regional price parity (RPP) index at 112.2 versus the United States benchmark of 100. Goods prices are only modestly higher than average (106.8), while services—healthcare, utilities, and dining—are markedly expensive at 157.8, more than one‑and‑a‑half times the national level. Housing, measured by rental costs, registers at 147.3, indicating that renters pay roughly 47 % more than the countrywide average. The median household income of $68,574 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 5.4 %, yet the income advantage is largely eroded by the steep service and rental premiums. The expense structure is driven principally by services and housing. Rental costs alone consume $1,369 per month, 24 % of median household income, a burden that aligns with the national rent‑burden threshold but remains higher in absolute dollars because of the elevated rent level ($1,369 vs. $928 nationally). When services are added, the combined non‑goods component pushes total monthly outlays well beyond the 30 % of income typically considered affordable. Goods remain the cheapest segment, at just 6.8 % above the national average, suggesting that everyday purchases such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are relatively affordable. However, the high cost of utilities, medical care, and dining out inflates the overall cost picture, making daily life in Butte County more expensive than in most parts of the country. Purchasing power, measured by the index of $61,120.4, reveals that a median resident retains roughly 89 % of the national purchasing power benchmark. After accounting for the $1,369 monthly rent, the remaining disposable income is approximately $5,300 per month. Subtracting an estimated $1,200 for utilities, healthcare, and food—reflecting the 157.8 services index—leaves around $4,100 for discretionary spending, transportation, and savings. This residual amount is modest compared with the national context, where lower service costs free up a larger share of income for non‑essential expenditures. Demographically, the county’s labor market exerts pressure on household budgets. Unemployment stands at 7.1 %, well above the national rate, limiting earnings potential for a sizable segment of the population. Only 20.7 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, a figure that correlates with lower-wage occupations and constrains upward mobility. The median age of 36.3 years suggests a relatively young workforce, yet the combination of modest educational attainment and elevated unemployment dampens the ability to absorb high service and housing costs. Consequently, many households experience a tighter budget squeeze despite earnings that modestly outpace the national median. Over the past decade, RPP rose from 109.7 to 112.2, a 2.3 % increase, while median household income surged 57.8 % from $43,444 to $68,574. Median rent climbed 51.3 % from $905 to $1,369. Income growth outpaced the overall price index, but rent and service inflation have narrowed the gap, leaving disposable income gains modest. The slower rise in goods prices has helped preserve purchasing power for essential items, yet the faster escalation of services and housing costs has offset much of the wage advantage. Prospective residents should plan budgets around a $1,369 monthly rent commitment, allocate roughly $1,200 for utilities, healthcare, and food, and recognize that goods will be comparatively inexpensive. With a disposable pool of about $4,100 after these core expenses, households must prioritize essential spending and limit discretionary outlays. Understanding that services dominate the cost structure will guide realistic expectations for dining, medical bills, and utility bills, ensuring that household finances remain sustainable in a county where higher-than‑average service and housing costs dominate the consumer landscape.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Butte County, California

With a Purchasing Power Index of 61120.4, a median household income of $68,574 in Butte County, California has the equivalent buying power of $41,912,703 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 147.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
