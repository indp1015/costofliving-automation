📊 Local Market Analysis

Kossuth County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percent below the national average, reflected in an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 88.8, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct asymmetry in daily spending. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—command an index of 96.6, nearly matching the national baseline, while Services plummet to 66.0, the lowest component by a wide margin and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs, indexed at 87.3, reinforce the discount but sit closer to the goods category than the deep service deflation. This profile indicates that while housing and services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are substantially cheaper than the U.S. norm, the unavoidable weekly basket of tangible goods offers residents little relative savings, anchoring the practical cost of daily life closer to parity than the headline RPP suggests. The median household income of $65,656 virtually matches the national median of $65,047, but the local purchasing power index of 73,963 illustrates the tangible benefit of the lower price level: the typical household commands roughly $8,900 more in real goods and services than their nominal earnings imply. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 15.7 percent of income, with median monthly rent of $859 comparing favorably against the $928 national figure, leaving significant disposable income for other categories. However, the labor market context complicates the picture; an unemployment rate of 2.1 percent signals extreme labor tightness, yet educational attainment lags significantly with only 16.3 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and a median age of 45.0 years suggests an established, potentially aging workforce. This dynamic implies that while current wage levels support comfortable budgets, the local economy may lack the high-wage knowledge sectors that typically drive income growth above inflation over time. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8, meaning the county became marginally cheaper relative to the nation even as nominal costs rose. Median income grew 27.5 percent, rising from $51,496 to $65,656, yet median rent accelerated 48.4 percent, jumping from $579 to $859. This divergence indicates that housing costs have consumed a disproportionate share of wage gains, eroding the shelter affordability advantage that once existed. While the overall cost environment remains favorable, the trajectory suggests the gap between local earnings and rental outlays is narrowing faster than the broader inflation differential would predict, a trend that warrants monitoring for households allocating budget shares to housing. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on a trade-off: exceptionally low service costs—particularly healthcare and utilities—provide a structural tailwind for disposable income, but the near-national pricing on goods means grocery and fuel bills will feel familiar rather than discounted. The low rent burden offers immediate cash flow relief compared to metropolitan areas, yet the rapid historical appreciation of rents signals that lease renewals may not preserve the current 15.7 percent burden ratio indefinitely. Households should model budgets assuming goods consumption tracks national averages while treating the service discount as the primary source of financial flexibility, recognizing that the tight labor market may support wage negotiation but offers limited industrial diversity for career mobility.

What this means for residents of Kossuth County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 73962.8, a median household income of $65,656 in Kossuth County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $48,561,016 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
