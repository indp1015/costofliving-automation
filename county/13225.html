📊 Local Market Analysis

Peach County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 96.5 versus 100 nationwide. Goods prices register at 97.7, just under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 88.3. Housing costs, measured by rental price parity, are also lower than the countrywide figure, with an index of 92.3. These indices collectively signal that everyday expenses in the county are generally less burdensome than in most U.S. metros, though the gap varies across categories. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the goods segment, which, at 97.7, exceeds both services and rental costs. Consumers therefore encounter relatively higher prices for groceries, apparel, and fuel than for healthcare, utilities, or dining out. Nonetheless, the service index of 88.3 translates into tangible savings on routine expenditures such as medical visits and restaurant meals, offsetting the modest premium on goods. Rental costs reinforce the affordability narrative: median monthly rent stands at $901, 3.0% below the national median of $928, and rent burden consumes only 15.8% of median household income. The combination of lower rent and cheaper services suggests that daily life in Peach County leans toward the inexpensive side, especially for households that can manage the slightly elevated cost of goods. Disposable income calculations reinforce this view. Median household income in the county is $68,365, surpassing the national median of $65,047 by 5.0%. After allocating the median rent of $901, a typical household retains roughly $57,464 annually for other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 70,849—derived from the ratio of income to cost of living—places Peach County well above the national baseline of 100, indicating that residents can purchase more with each dollar earned than the average American. In practical terms, the median earner’s budget stretches farther on groceries and fuel, while the lower service costs further amplify the net purchasing power. Demographic factors shape this affordability profile. With only 12.2% of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market is skewed toward lower‑skill occupations, which typically command modest wages. Yet the median income remains robust, suggesting that a sizable share of the workforce occupies stable, middle‑income positions, perhaps in manufacturing, logistics, or public sector roles prevalent in the region. The unemployment rate of 6.7% exceeds the national average, hinting at some labor market slack that could temper wage growth. A median age of 37.8 years points to a predominantly working‑age population, likely balancing household formation and consumption needs without the heightened healthcare costs associated with an older demographic. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest 2.0% increase, indicating that price pressures have been relatively tame. In contrast, median household income surged from $41,588 to $68,365, a 64.4% gain, while median rent climbed 31.1% from $687 to $901. Income growth has outpaced both overall price inflation and housing cost escalation, expanding real purchasing power and reinforcing the county’s affordability advantage. The divergence between wage gains and modest price increases suggests that residents have experienced a genuine improvement in living standards over the last ten years. Prospective residents should anchor their budgeting around three pillars: rent, goods, and services. With rent accounting for roughly 16% of median income, housing remains the least strain on the budget. Allocating about 30% to groceries, fuel, and apparel aligns with the slightly elevated goods index, while a 15% allocation to healthcare, utilities, and dining reflects the lower service costs. The remaining 39% can cover transportation, savings, and discretionary spending. By respecting these proportional guidelines, households can comfortably navigate Peach County’s cost environment, leveraging the county’s lower service and housing expenses to offset the modestly higher price of everyday goods.

What this means for residents of Peach County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70849.0, a median household income of $68,365 in Peach County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $48,435,919 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
