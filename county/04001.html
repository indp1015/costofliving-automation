📊 Local Market Analysis

Apache County residents face a cost of living marginally above the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 100.7 for 2023. This aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in household budgets: services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—run 8.6 percent above the national average at 108.6, while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel come in 2.1 percent cheaper at 97.9. The dominant cost pressure stems squarely from the services category, making daily life more expensive than the headline index suggests, particularly for households with high medical or utility consumption. Housing provides the primary offset. Rental costs sit at 83.3 on the RPP scale, roughly 17 percent below the national level, translating to a median monthly rent of $647 against a national median of $928. This discount caps the median rent burden at 19.2 percent of household income, a manageable threshold that preserves cash flow for other obligations. Consequently, the Purchasing Power Index lands at $40,041, essentially matching the nominal median household income of $40,338. In real terms, the median dollar holds its ground locally, but only because depressed housing costs neutralize the premium paid for services. The labor market context complicates this equilibrium. An unemployment rate of 10.0 percent signals significant slack, while educational attainment remains low—just 8.9 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. These factors suppress wage growth and concentrate employment in sectors unlikely to generate income gains that outpace service-sector inflation. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted up a negligible 0.2 percent, while median income rose 27.0 percent and median rent increased 23.7 percent. Wages have outpaced shelter costs historically, yet the persistent services premium erodes discretionary spending for a workforce already constrained by limited high-skill opportunities. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is distinct: expect below-average shelter outlays but allocate a heavier share of the budget to utilities, healthcare, and transportation services. The low rent burden offers a buffer, yet the high unemployment rate and low educational attainment suggest income volatility is a greater risk than cost escalation. Households moving from higher-wage metros will find their dollars stretch further on rent but encounter surprisingly stiff prices for everyday services, requiring a recalibration of spending priorities rather than a simple assumption of affordability.

What this means for residents of Apache County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 40040.9, a median household income of $40,338 in Apache County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $16,151,698 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
