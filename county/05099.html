📊 Local Market Analysis

Nevada County sits 13.2 percent below the national price level with an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, but that aggregate masks a sharp bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods — groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 93.1, only a modest discount to the U.S. average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9, meaning shelter is cheaper than the national benchmark but significantly more expensive than the local service economy would suggest. The median household income of $41,761 trails the national median of $65,047 by 35.8 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 48,106.2 indicates that lower local prices recover roughly $6,300 in real value for the typical earner. Rent consumes 24.2 percent of gross income at a median of $841, well below the national median rent of $928 and a threshold that leaves meaningful discretionary capacity. However, that capacity is tested at the checkout counter; because goods prices sit near parity with the nation, the savings generated by cheap services and housing are partially offset every time a resident fills a tank or stocks a pantry. Demographic and labor conditions reinforce the frugality of the local cost structure. An 8.3 percent unemployment rate and a bachelor’s attainment rate of just 9.1 percent signal a labor market anchored in lower-wage sectors, while a median age of 44.2 suggests a population less likely to absorb price shocks through career mobility. Over the last decade, the RPP has drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0 percent decline in relative prices, while nominal median income surged 36.1 percent against a 32.4 percent rise in median rent. Wages have outpaced shelter costs in real terms, a rare dynamic that has incrementally improved the household balance sheet. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is specific: expect near-national grocery and fuel bills, deeply discounted healthcare and utility outlays, and rent that demands roughly one-quarter of gross pay. The primary friction point remains the goods basket; a household moving from a high-cost metro will find shelter relief immediate but the weekly supermarket receipt stubbornly familiar.

What this means for residents of Nevada County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 48106.2, a median household income of $41,761 in Nevada County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $20,089,630 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
