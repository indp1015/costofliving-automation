📊 Local Market Analysis

Nevada County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 93.1, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 56.7. Housing, measured by rental costs, registers at 81.9, indicating that renters pay roughly 18 % less than the average American household. The consumption profile is shaped primarily by the modest rental market and the pronounced discount on services. Although goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are only 7 % cheaper than the national average, the low service index drives the overall affordability. Daily life therefore feels inexpensive: a resident spending $300 on utilities, healthcare, and dining would pay roughly $170 compared with a typical U.S. household. Rent further reinforces this advantage; the median monthly rent of $841 is $87 below the national median of $928, translating to a rent‑burden of 24.2 % of the median household income of $41,761. By contrast, the national rent‑burden averages around 30 % of median income, underscoring the relative ease of covering housing costs in Nevada County. Disposable income calculations reveal a purchasing power index of 48,106.2, roughly 48 % of the national reference point. After allocating 24.2 % of earnings to rent, the median household retains approximately $31,600 annually for other expenses. Subtracting an estimated $5,000 for services (based on the 56.7 service index) and $4,500 for goods (derived from the 93.1 goods index), the median family still enjoys a modest surplus for discretionary spending, albeit far below the national average disposable income. The index underscores that while costs are low, earnings are also constrained, limiting overall purchasing strength. Demographically, the county’s labor market tempers the affordability narrative. An unemployment rate of 8.3 % exceeds the national rate, signaling fewer job opportunities and heightened income insecurity. Only 9.1 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑pay occupations. The median age of 44.2 years points to a mature population, potentially reflecting a higher share of retirees whose fixed incomes are more sensitive to service costs. Together, these factors mean that the low price environment benefits those with stable earnings but may strain households reliant on precarious or part‑time work. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 88.56 in 2010 to 86.81 in 2023, a modest 2 % decline, indicating that relative prices have softened. Median household income, however, surged 36.1 % from $30,691 to $41,761, outpacing the 32.4 % rise in median rent (from $635 to $841). Real wages therefore improved faster than housing costs, enhancing affordability for many residents, though the gains are partially offset by the higher unemployment rate. Prospective residents should budget with the following concrete figures: allocate roughly 24 % of gross income to rent, 12 % to goods, and 7 % to services, leaving about 57 % for transportation, healthcare premiums, savings, and discretionary items. The low service cost offers savings on utilities and medical care, but the limited local wage base and elevated unemployment risk require a cautious approach to cash‑flow planning, especially for households without supplemental income streams.

What this means for residents of Nevada County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 48106.2, a median household income of $41,761 in Nevada County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $20,089,630 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
