📊 Local Market Analysis

Los Angeles County’s cost of living index of 112.2 underscores a consumer environment that is markedly more expensive than the national baseline of 100, with services leading the expense profile at an index of 157.8, followed closely by housing at 147.3 and goods at 106.8. The disparity between categories reveals that daily life is driven primarily by service costs—healthcare, utilities, and dining—rather than by the purchase of tangible items. While goods remain only 6.8 percent above the national average, the combined weight of services and rental costs pushes the overall index well above parity, making routine expenditures noticeably pricier than in most other regions. Median household income of $87,760 provides a buffer against these elevated costs, yet the rent burden of 25.9 percent—$1,893 monthly rent—consumes a substantial share of earnings, leaving limited discretionary margin. The Purchasing Power Index of 78,221.0 reflects the relative ability of local consumers to meet expenses after essential outlays, indicating that despite higher wages, the premium price structure compresses real buying power. The local labor market, with an unemployment rate of 7.0 percent, suggests a modestly tight environment where higher educational attainment—22.8 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—correlates with elevated service demand and wage expectations, further reinforcing cost pressures. Over the past decade, consumer costs have risen modestly, with the Regional Price Parity moving from 109.703 in 2010 to 112.195 in 2023, a 2.3 percent increase, while median income has surged 56.2 percent to $87,760 and median rent has climbed 53.8 percent to $1,893. This divergence shows wages outpacing rent growth, though the overall price index has not kept pace with income gains, suggesting a gradual improvement in affordability. The median age of 37.9 years points to a mature workforce that typically commands higher wages but also faces entrenched service cost structures. Prospective residents should budget for rent to occupy roughly one‑quarter of monthly earnings, allocate a significant portion of discretionary funds to utilities and healthcare, and anticipate that dining and other services will exceed national norms by roughly 58 percent. Goods purchases remain relatively affordable, but the cumulative effect of services and housing means that household planning must prioritize predictable, recurring expenses over discretionary spending to maintain financial stability.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Los Angeles County, California

With a Purchasing Power Index of 78221.0, a median household income of $87,760 in Los Angeles County, California has the equivalent buying power of $68,646,750 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 147.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
