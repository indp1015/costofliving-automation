📊 Local Market Analysis

Crawford County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a distinct bifurcation in consumer expenditure patterns. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—run 1.6 percent above the U.S. average at 101.6, establishing them as the primary friction point for daily household budgets. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a significant 7.6 percent discount at 92.4, while rental costs offer the deepest relief at 80.4, nearly twenty points below the national benchmark. This structure creates an environment where the marginal dollar spent on recurring necessities like food and fuel stretches less far than in the average American county, even as the largest fixed expense—shelter—demands a substantially smaller share of income. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life affordability hinges on the tension between elevated goods pricing and depressed housing costs. With median monthly rent at $761 against a national median of $928, housing consumes only 13.5 percent of the local median household income of $67,614, a ratio that signals exceptional shelter affordability relative to the broader economy. However, the 101.6 goods index implies that a household allocating a standard share of its budget to groceries and transportation faces a persistent, albeit modest, headwind. The resulting Purchasing Power Index of 68,468.5—derived by adjusting the median income for the local price level—indicates that the typical Crawford County household commands real purchasing power roughly 5.3 percent above its nominal income, a direct dividend of the low rental and services environment that more than offsets the goods premium. Local demographic and labor dynamics reinforce this economic portrait. An unemployment rate of 4.0 percent suggests a reasonably tight labor market supporting the 42.4 percent nominal income growth observed since 2010, which outpaced the 35.7 percent rise in median rent over the same period. The median age of 42.4 years and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of 14.3 percent—well below the national average—point toward a workforce concentrated in trades, manufacturing, or service sectors where wage growth has historically tracked closer to goods inflation than asset appreciation. This composition helps explain why the Regional Price Parity has drifted downward from 101.8 in 2010 to 98.8 in 2023, a 3.0 percent decline in relative price levels, even as nominal incomes surged; the local cost structure has become progressively cheaper relative to the nation, largely driven by housing and services disinflation. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the trade-off between low fixed overhead and variable consumption costs. The shelter discount effectively functions as a permanent income boost of roughly $2,000 annually compared to the national median rent burden, freeing cash flow for the above-average grocery and fuel bills implied by the 101.6 goods index. Households relocating from higher-cost metros should anticipate that while lease negotiations and utility setup will yield immediate savings, the weekly trip to the supermarket and the daily commute will not offer commensurate bargains. The data suggests that a household earning the local median income retains significant discretionary capacity after housing, but that capacity is eroded more quickly by physical consumption than the headline RPP figure might suggest.

What this means for residents of Crawford County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 68468.5, a median household income of $67,614 in Crawford County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $46,294,292 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
