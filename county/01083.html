📊 Local Market Analysis

Limestone County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—are 5.4 % lower than average (94.6), while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 61.6, a 38.4 % discount to the national level. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $908, are only slightly under the national median rent of $928, translating to a rental index of 89.9. The combined effect is a consumer price environment that is roughly 11 % cheaper than the country as a whole. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index of 61.6 pulls the overall cost picture down far more than the modestly lower goods index. Daily expenses for essentials—food, clothing, transportation—are modestly below national levels, but the real bargain lies in healthcare, utility bills, and restaurant meals, which cost less than two‑thirds of what they do elsewhere. Rent, while close to the national median, remains affordable relative to local earnings; at $908 per month it consumes only 13 % of the median household income of $83,534, well under the 30 % rent‑burden threshold commonly cited as a stress point. Purchasing power in Limestone County is robust. The Purchasing Power Index of 93,756.2 indicates that a median household can buy nearly $28,000 more in goods and services than the average American household earning the national median income of $65,047. After deducting the average rent payment, the median family retains roughly $78,000 of annual income for discretionary spending, savings, and other expenses—a disposable income share that outpaces many higher‑cost metros. Demographically, the county’s labor market reinforces this affordability. An unemployment rate of 3.7 % signals a tight labor market, while only 19.1 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, higher‑wage sectors such as manufacturing and logistics that dominate the local economy. The median age of 40.5 years points to a mature, established population with stable earning potential, further cushioning households against price volatility. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a modest 0.8 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have barely moved relative to the nation. In contrast, median household income surged 68.5 % from $49,570 to $83,534, outpacing the 47.4 % rise in median rent. Wage growth has therefore outstripped housing cost inflation, expanding real purchasing power for residents. Prospective movers should budget for a rent bill near $908, allocate a modest portion of income to utilities and healthcare—both significantly cheaper than the national average—and expect a sizable surplus for discretionary spending, given the county’s strong income trajectory and low service‑sector price base.
