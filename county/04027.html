📊 Local Market Analysis

Yuma County’s cost of living sits just above the national baseline, with an overall Relative Price Parity (RPP) of 100.7 versus the U.S. average of 100. Goods are priced at 97.9, indicating grocery, apparel and fuel costs are 2.1 % lower than the countrywide norm. Services—healthcare, utilities and dining—register 108.6, a 8.6 % premium, while rental housing is markedly cheaper at 83.3, or 16.7 % below national levels. The mixed picture shows that everyday necessities such as food and fuel are modest, but the expense of essential services pushes the aggregate index above parity. The dominant driver of Yuma’s cost structure is the services sector. At 108.6 it outweighs both the sub‑national goods index and the low‑cost housing component, meaning that a typical household feels the pinch most in medical bills, utility bills and restaurant prices. Daily life is therefore a blend of affordability and pressure: a resident can stretch a grocery dollar further than the average American, yet must allocate a larger share of income to health‑related and utility expenditures. The housing advantage—median rent of $983, 5.9 % higher than the national median of $928 but 15 % lower than the national RPP‑adjusted housing cost—keeps the rent‑burden at 19.5 % of household income, well under the 30 % threshold that defines affordability. Disposable income calculations underscore the modest purchasing power. Median household earnings stand at $60,417, 7.2 % below the national median of $65,047. After subtracting median rent ($983 × 12 = $11,796) the median family retains roughly $48,621 for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 59,972 quantifies this gap: residents can purchase about 60 % of the goods and services that a median American household can afford with the same nominal income. The high service cost erodes the advantage of cheaper housing, leaving a narrower margin for discretionary spending. Local demographics shape this dynamic. Only 10.6 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, limiting access to higher‑wage occupations that typically offset elevated service costs. The median age of 35.8 suggests a relatively young labor force, yet the unemployment rate of 8.1 %—well above the national average—signals underutilized human capital. A less‑educated, younger workforce often commands lower wages, reinforcing the modest median income and constraining the ability to absorb higher service prices. Over the past decade, RPP rose from 100.558 to 100.742, a negligible 0.2 % increase, while median income surged 48.3 % from $40,743 to $60,417. Rent climbed 17.2 % from $839 to $983. Income growth far outpaced both overall price parity and housing costs, indicating that wages have generally kept pace with, and even exceeded, inflationary pressures. However, service price inflation—reflected in the 108.6 index—has not been fully captured by the modest RPP shift, suggesting a sector‑specific squeeze. Prospective residents should budget with a clear hierarchy: allocate a larger portion of income to healthcare, utilities and dining out, while leveraging the low housing cost to keep rent‑burden under 20 %. A realistic monthly plan might earmark $300–$350 for utilities and medical expenses, $200–$250 for groceries, and $150–$200 for dining, leaving discretionary funds modest. Understanding that the primary cost pressure stems from services, not shelter, enables households to prioritize insurance coverage, energy‑efficiency measures and price‑shopping for medical services to preserve purchasing power in Yuma County.

What this means for residents of Yuma County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 59972.0, a median household income of $60,417 in Yuma County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $36,233,283 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
