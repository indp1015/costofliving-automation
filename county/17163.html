📊 Local Market Analysis

St. Clair County presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally below the national baseline, with a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 98.8, yet the aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the primary inflationary pressure, registering at 101.6, or 1.6 percent above the U.S. average, making daily discretionary spending noticeably steeper than in comparable Midwestern metros. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in at 92.4, providing a 7.6 percent discount that partially offsets goods inflation. The most pronounced deviation occurs in rental housing, where the index reads 80.4, signaling shelter costs nearly 20 percent below the national norm; this single category anchors the county’s overall affordability advantage, keeping the composite index sub-100 despite the premium paid at the pump and the checkout aisle. Purchasing power metrics confirm that the median household translates this cost structure into tangible financial breathing room. With a median income of $70,178—roughly 7.9 percent above the national median of $65,047—and a median gross rent of $1,032, the rent burden sits at a low 17.6 percent of gross income, well under the 30 percent stress threshold. The resulting Purchasing Power Index of 71,064.9 indicates that after adjusting for local price levels, the typical household commands real resources exceeding the nominal national median. However, the labor market context introduces friction: an unemployment rate of 5.7 percent exceeds the national rate, and educational attainment remains modest, with only 17.6 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. A median age of 39.5 suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the elevated jobless rate implies that income distribution may be skewed, leaving a segment of renters more vulnerable to the above-average goods inflation than the median figures suggest. Over the past decade, the trajectory has favored wage earners. The RPP declined from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 40.7 percent from $49,895 to $70,178. Median rent increased a more modest 29.6 percent over the same span, rising from $796 to $1,032. This divergence means real income growth has significantly outpaced both general inflation and shelter costs, expanding discretionary capacity for incumbent residents. The decline in the overall RPP was likely driven by the persistent discount in housing and services relative to national trends, even as goods prices maintained their premium. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on a distinct trade-off: housing consumes a smaller slice of the paycheck than almost anywhere else in the region, but the weekly grocery bill and fuel costs will run hotter than the national average. A household relocating from a higher-cost metro should anticipate immediate shelter savings—roughly $200 to $300 monthly versus the national median rent—but must allocate a larger share of the remaining budget to goods consumption. Utility and healthcare outlays offer modest relief at roughly 8 percent below average, yet the 5.7 percent unemployment rate warrants maintaining a larger emergency reserve than the low rent burden alone would justify. The data suggests a viable environment for savers who can secure stable employment, provided they absorb the persistent goods premium without lifestyle inflation.

What this means for residents of St. Clair County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71064.9, a median household income of $70,178 in St. Clair County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $49,871,926 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
