📊 Local Market Analysis

Payette County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 92.2 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 95.9, still under the national benchmark but the highest component within the county. Services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are the cheapest segment at 86.9, while rental housing is markedly low at 68.1, indicating that landlords charge roughly 32 % less than the average American market. The structure of local expenses is dominated by goods, which lift the overall index by a few points above the service and housing levels. Daily life therefore feels inexpensive in most respects: utilities and medical costs are nearly 14 % cheaper than elsewhere, and the median rent of $874 is $54 below the national median. However, the modestly elevated goods index means that food and fuel costs are only slightly under national levels, tempering the otherwise low‑cost impression. Residents who spend a larger share of their budget on groceries or commuting will feel the pinch more than those whose consumption leans toward services. Disposable income calculations reveal a relatively comfortable margin. The median household earns $65,723, essentially matching the national median of $65,047. After paying the median rent of $874—equating to 16 % of income, well under the 30 % rent‑burden threshold—the median family retains roughly $54,849 annually for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 71,255.3 quantifies this advantage: a dollar in Payette County purchases about 12 % more than a dollar in the average U.S. locality, reflecting the combined effect of lower housing costs and modestly priced services. Demographic factors reinforce the affordability narrative. With only 13.8 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market is skewed toward occupations that typically command lower wages but also benefit from the county’s low price environment. The unemployment rate of 3.3 % is well beneath the national rate, suggesting a stable job base that supports steady household incomes. A median age of 38.8 years points to a mature, working‑age population likely to have established spending patterns focused on essential goods and services rather than discretionary luxury. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.2 to 92.2, a 1 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Meanwhile, median household income surged 48.5 % from $44,257 to $65,723, outpacing the 30.8 % rise in median rent. The divergence between income growth and price pressure shows that wages have more than kept pace with living costs, expanding real purchasing power for most families. Prospective residents should anchor their budgets around three pillars: housing, goods, and services. With rent consuming only 16 % of income, allocating roughly 30 % to groceries, fuel, and personal items aligns with the county’s goods index, while another 20 % to utilities, healthcare, and dining reflects the low service costs. The remaining 34 % can cover transportation, savings, and discretionary spending. Because goods prices hover near national levels, households that rely heavily on vehicle travel or frequent grocery trips should budget slightly higher in those categories. Overall, Payette County offers a cost structure that preserves a sizable portion of earnings for non‑housing expenses, making it one of the more budget‑friendly locales for households with median incomes.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Payette County, Idaho

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71255.3, a median household income of $65,723 in Payette County, Idaho has the equivalent buying power of $46,831,121 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 68.1 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
