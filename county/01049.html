📊 Local Market Analysis

DeKalb County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—are 5.4 % lower than the national average (94.6), while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are dramatically cheaper at 61.6, reflecting a 38.4 % discount. Rental costs are modestly under the national level, with a housing index of 89.9 and a median monthly rent of $683, compared with the national median of $928. The combined effect is a household budget that feels markedly inexpensive, especially for everyday expenditures that dominate most consumers’ outlays. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index of 61.6 pulls the overall cost picture down. Even though goods are only slightly cheaper than the countrywide average, the deep discount on services—particularly healthcare and utilities—means that routine expenses such as doctor visits, electricity, and restaurant meals cost less than two‑thirds of what they would elsewhere. Consequently, daily life in DeKalb County is inexpensive for most residents, with the low rent burden of 16 % of income underscoring the affordability of housing relative to earnings. Median household income in the county stands at $51,149, 21.5 % below the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median rent, the median family retains roughly $45,000 in annual discretionary income. The Purchasing Power Index of 57,408 further quantifies this gap: a resident’s dollar buys about 57 % of the goods and services a typical American consumer can purchase. Nevertheless, the low service costs offset the income shortfall, allowing households to allocate a larger share of their earnings to non‑housing consumption than the raw income figure suggests. Local demographics shape this affordability landscape. With only 8.5 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market is skewed toward lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations, which aligns with the modest median income. The unemployment rate of 4.2 % mirrors the national average, indicating a stable job environment, while a median age of 39.6 suggests a mature, working‑age population that is likely to have established consumption patterns. The limited educational attainment constrains wage growth, but the inexpensive service sector cushions household budgets. Over the past decade, RPP slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8 % decline, while median income rose 33.9 % from $38,192 to $51,149. Rent increased 26.5 % in the same period, outpacing the modest rise in the overall price index. Wage growth has outstripped the inflation of goods and services, preserving purchasing power despite higher rents. Prospective residents should budget $683 for rent, allocate roughly 30 % of income to groceries and fuel, and expect service expenses to consume less than one‑quarter of their budget. With a rent‑to‑income ratio comfortably below the 30 % threshold, the county offers a viable environment for households seeking low‑cost daily living while accepting modest earnings and limited educational opportunities.

What this means for residents of DeKalb County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57408.2, a median household income of $51,149 in DeKalb County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $29,363,720 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
