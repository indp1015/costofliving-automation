📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Kendall County, Illinois, registers at 98.8 on the Beacon's Relative Price Index, placing it slightly below the national benchmark of 100 while remaining above the baseline of 101.778 recorded in 2010. The goods category—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—stands at 101.6, indicating that local consumer prices are 1.6 percent higher than the national average, whereas services including healthcare, utilities, and dining trade at 92.4, reflecting 7.6 percent below the national level. Housing costs, measured as rental expenses, score lowest at 80.4, suggesting that residential accommodation in Kendall County carries a relative discount compared to the broader national market. This distribution reveals a cost structure heavily weighted toward consumer goods and transportation while housing remains comparatively affordable, though the overall index suggests that everyday household expenses in Kendall County are marginally elevated relative to the national average. The consumption breakdown demonstrates that goods dominate the local cost profile, accounting for the largest share of spending at 101.6, which compresses daily budgeting toward food, clothing, and fuel expenditures. Services, at 92.4, represent the second-largest category but remain below national norms, indicating that healthcare, utilities, and dining costs are somewhat more affordable for residents. Rental costs at 80.4 constitute the lowest portion of the index, yet the median monthly rent of $1,763—up 35.1 percent from $1,305 a decade ago—highlights that while housing is relatively inexpensive by national standards, the absolute dollar amount remains substantial for many households. The combination of elevated goods prices and rising rents creates a household budget environment where discretionary spending must compete with essential living expenses, particularly for families and individuals with limited savings. Purchasing power analysis reveals a median household income of $110,474, which nominally supports a robust standard of living. However, the median rent of $1,763 consumes 19.2 percent of income, placing the rent burden within a range that remains moderate by national standards but significant relative to the county's income level. The purchasing power index of 111,870.1 suggests that, after accounting for local price levels, the median household retains more disposable income than the national average, though the high goods index of 101.6 indicates that the cost of essential consumer goods continues to pressure budgets. The median age of 36.0 years and a bachelor's degree attainment rate of 23.4 percent reflect a workforce that is moderately educated and employed, with an unemployment rate of 3.6 percent that suggests a stable labor market. These demographic factors contribute to a cost of living experience characterized by solid employment conditions but persistent pressure from elevated consumer prices, particularly in food and fuel categories. Historical trends show a modest decline in the overall cost of living index from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent reduction that partially offsets the 30.9 percent increase in median household income from $84,385 to $110,474 over the same period. Median rent has risen more sharply at 35.1 percent, outpacing income growth, which suggests that housing costs have become the primary driver of cost-of-living pressure in the county.
