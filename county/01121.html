📊 Local Market Analysis

Talladega County’s cost of living sits well below the national benchmark, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 94.6, still under the national average but the highest component locally. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheap at 61.6, while rental costs are close to the overall index at 89.9. The median household income of $56,249 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 14 %, yet the county’s median rent of $772 is 17 % lower than the national median of $928, yielding a rent‑burden ratio of 16.5 % of income—well under the 30 % threshold that defines affordability. The expense structure is driven primarily by goods, which lift the overall index toward the national level, while the services sector pulls the cost base down sharply. Daily life for a typical household is therefore inexpensive in categories that dominate discretionary spending, such as dining out and utility bills, but modestly higher for essential consumables. With a Purchasing Power Index of 63,132.3, residents retain roughly 63 % of the purchasing power of an average American household after accounting for local price levels. Subtracting the median monthly rent from income leaves about $4,200 of disposable monthly earnings, enough to cover the low‑cost service environment and still allow for modest savings or non‑essential purchases. Demographic factors reinforce the affordability picture. Only 9.2 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age of 42.2 suggests a mature workforce, both of which correlate with lower wage growth and limited demand for premium services. The unemployment rate of 8.1 % exceeds the national average, indicating a labor market with excess supply that dampens wage pressure. Consequently, while incomes have risen dramatically—up 60 % since 2010—from $35,155 to $56,249, they have outpaced rent growth, which climbed 32.2 % in the same period, and modestly outstripped the 0.8 % decline in the RPP index. Wage gains have thus more than kept pace with the slight easing of consumer prices, preserving real purchasing power. Prospective residents should budget around $772 for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 for utilities and healthcare, and expect grocery and fuel expenses to be near national averages. With services costing only about two‑thirds of the national level, discretionary spending on dining and personal services can be stretched further than in most regions. A realistic monthly budget would therefore center on housing and essential goods, leaving a comfortable margin for savings or leisure, provided the household remains within the median income bracket and does not exceed the modest rent‑burden threshold.

What this means for residents of Talladega County, Alabama
