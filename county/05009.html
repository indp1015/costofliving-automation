📊 Local Market Analysis

Boone County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity registering 86.8 for 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental housing costs land at 81.9, meaning a median monthly rent of $715 consumes just 15.8 percent of the $54,195 median household income, a burden substantially lighter than the national median rent of $928 against a $65,047 national income. Daily life is inexpensive primarily because service-sector pricing — haircuts, restaurant meals, medical visits, and utility bills — reflects a low-wage local labor market rather than any structural efficiency; goods prices, by contrast, remain tethered to national supply chains and offer limited relief. The purchasing power index of 62,429.4 translates the nominal median income into a real income figure that exceeds the nominal dollar amount by roughly 15 percent, illustrating how far a paycheck stretches when housing and services are deeply discounted. Yet the local economic context tempers this advantage: only 10.8 percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher, unemployment sits at 3.9 percent, and the median age of 41.3 years signals a workforce tilted toward established, lower-skill employment. These demographics anchor wages in sectors where productivity gains are modest, capping income growth even as the low cost of living preserves disposable income. The labor market suggests stability without dynamism — jobs exist, but they cluster in occupations that do not command premium compensation, reinforcing the very service-price depression that makes the county affordable. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined 2.0 percent from 88.561 to 86.81, indicating that local consumer prices fell slightly relative to the national basket. Meanwhile, median household income surged 38.1 percent from $39,251 to $54,195, outpacing a 20.0 percent rise in median rent from $596 to $715. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have widened the gap between earnings and shelter costs, improving the rent-to-income ratio materially. The divergence reflects nominal wage growth in a region where housing supply has not tightened dramatically and service-sector inflation has remained subdued, a combination that has steadily enhanced real purchasing power for incumbent households. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that track near-national levels — expect to pay roughly 93 cents on the dollar compared to a typical U.S. city — while capturing outsized savings on rent, healthcare copays, utility deposits, and restaurant meals. The $715 median rent implies a two-bedroom unit in many neighborhoods, but the 15.8 percent rent burden assumes full-time employment at the median wage; part-time or entry-level workers will face a steeper climb. Utility costs benefit from the 56.7 services index, yet winter heating and summer cooling in the Ozark climate can still generate seasonal spikes. Childcare, a service-category expense, may be scarce rather than cheap, and the low bachelor's attainment rate correlates with limited high-wage remote-work opportunities. The budget calculus favors households with portable income or established local employment; those reliant on job-seeking after relocation should verify that their target sector exists at a wage that clears the goods-cost floor.

What this means for residents of Boone County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 62429.4, a median household income of $54,195 in Boone County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $33,833,613 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
