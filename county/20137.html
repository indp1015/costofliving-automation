📊 Local Market Analysis

Norton County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 89.9 compared with the United States benchmark of 100. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 96.5, still modestly under the national level, while services register a pronounced discount at 68.6. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $642, translate to a housing index of 90.8, again trailing the national median rent of $928. The composite picture is one of a community where everyday expenses are consistently cheaper than the countrywide average, driven primarily by low service costs and modest rental rates. The cost structure is anchored by the services sector, which depresses the overall index more than any other category. At 68.6, service costs—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are roughly 31 % lower than the national average, offsetting the relatively higher goods index. Rental costs, while below national levels, are only slightly cheaper (90.8) and contribute less to the affordability equation than services. Consequently, daily life in Norton County is inexpensive for most households, especially those whose consumption patterns lean heavily on services such as medical care and utility bills. Grocery and apparel expenditures remain near‑national levels, suggesting that price advantages are concentrated in non‑goods categories. Disposable income calculations reveal a modest purchasing cushion. The median household earns $50,305, which is 22.6 % lower than the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the median rent of $642 (15.3 % of income), the remaining income is $44,663 annually. The Purchasing Power Index of 55,951.6—derived from the ratio of local income to the cost‑of‑living index—confirms that residents retain roughly 56 % of the purchasing power of an average American household. In practical terms, a family earning the median income can afford the same basket of goods and services that a national median‑income household could purchase with about $28,000 less in nominal dollars, thanks to the lower price environment. Demographic factors reinforce the affordability narrative. Unemployment is exceptionally low at 0.6 %, indicating a tight labor market that likely sustains wage stability despite limited high‑skill employment; only 14.5 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, reflecting a workforce concentrated in sectors that do not command premium wages. The median age of 40.2 years suggests a mature population, often associated with stable household formation and predictable consumption patterns. Together, these characteristics produce a cost of living experience where essential expenses are subdued, but limited high‑earning opportunities temper overall income growth. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, a 3.3 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the national average. Median household income rose 5.1 % in the same period, from $47,852 to $50,305, while median rent fell 0.8 % from $647 to $642. The modest income gain outpaced the slight reduction in rent, and both trends outstrip the downward pressure on the overall price index, suggesting that wages have kept pace with, if not exceeded, the pace of expense decline. Prospective residents should budget around $642 per month for rent, allocate roughly 30 % of income to services such as utilities and healthcare, and expect grocery and apparel costs near the national average. With rent consuming only 15.3 % of median earnings, a typical household can comfortably cover essential expenses while retaining discretionary funds for savings or non‑essential purchases. The key budgeting lever is the low cost of services; allocating a larger share of the budget to these categories yields the greatest savings advantage in Norton County.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Norton County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55951.6, a median household income of $50,305 in Norton County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $28,146,452 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
