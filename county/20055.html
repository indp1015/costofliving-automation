📊 Local Market Analysis

Finney County residents navigate a cost environment sitting 10.1 percentage points below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 89.9 against a U.S. average of 100. This aggregate discount masks significant internal divergence: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run only 3.5 percent cheaper than the national norm at 96.5, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 68.6, a staggering 31.4 percent discount. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 90.8. Consequently, the local cost structure is defined not by cheap housing or groceries, but by an exceptionally low service price level that drags the overall index down, making daily operational expenses for healthcare and utilities the primary source of budget relief for households. The median household income of $72,437 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 11.4 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 80,567.9 reveals that local wages, when adjusted for the 89.9 RPP, yield a real income equivalent significantly higher than the nominal figure suggests. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 16.4 percent of gross income, with median monthly rent at $991 compared to the national $928; the higher local rent is more than offset by the 11.4 percent income premium. A worker earning the median wage retains substantial disposable income after shelter costs, a dynamic reinforced by a youthful median age of 32.1 years and an unemployment rate of 5.4 percent that, while above the national low, signals a labor market with slack relative to tighter metros. However, the low bachelor’s degree attainment of 13.1 percent suggests the wage premium is likely driven by industry-specific demand—potentially agriculture processing or logistics—rather than knowledge-economy density, capping long-term earning trajectory for a segment of the workforce. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 to 89.908, indicating the county became relatively cheaper versus the nation even as nominal costs rose. Median income surged 47.1 percent from $49,227 to $72,437, outpacing the 43.8 percent climb in median rent from $689 to $991. This 3.3 percentage point spread between income and rent growth confirms that wage acceleration has preserved, and slightly improved, housing affordability metrics over the decade. The decoupling of local service costs from national inflation trends—evidenced by the plunging services RPP—suggests structural factors, possibly provider consolidation or lower labor costs in the care economy, are insulating residents from the healthcare and utility inflation squeezing budgets elsewhere. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on a high-value trade-off: grocery and fuel bills will feel nearly identical to a coastal city, but the monthly outlay for health insurance premiums, electricity, and dining out will be roughly two-thirds the national average. The 16.4 percent rent-to-income ratio leaves roughly 83.6 percent of gross income for taxes, savings, and the 96.5-indexed goods basket. However, the 5.4 percent unemployment rate and low educational attainment metric imply income volatility risk; a household losing a primary earner in the dominant local sectors may find replacement wages difficult to secure without relocation. The data supports a profile where a dual-income household or a remote worker importing a national salary captures maximum arbitrage, while a single earner reliant on the local labor market faces a ceiling defined by the 13.1 percent college attainment rate.

What this means for residents of Finney County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 80567.9, a median household income of $72,437 in Finney County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $58,360,970 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
