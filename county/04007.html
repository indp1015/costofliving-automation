📊 Local Market Analysis

Gila County’s cost of living sits marginally above the national baseline, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 100.7 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced at 97.9, indicating a slight discount relative to the countrywide average, while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining out—run at 108.6, a full 8.6 % premium. Rental costs are the most pronounced outlier, recorded at 83.3, meaning that rents are 16.7 % lower than the national norm. This mixed profile creates a nuanced affordability picture: everyday essentials are modestly cheaper, but the higher service price tag erodes part of the savings. The dominant driver of Gila County’s cost structure is the services sector. At 108.6, it outweighs both the goods index (97.9) and the housing index (83.3). Consequently, daily life feels inexpensive in terms of tangible purchases—groceries and fuel cost less than the national average—but more costly when households require medical care, utility bills, or restaurant meals. The low rental index tempers the overall cost pressure; median monthly rent of $1,001 is 7.8 % above the national median of $928, yet the RPP‑derived housing index signals that the underlying price level remains below national levels because the index reflects broader housing cost components beyond the headline rent figure. Disposable income for the median household can be approximated by subtracting rent burden from median earnings. With a median household income of $59,089 and a rent burden of 20.3 %, annual rent expenses total roughly $12,072, leaving $46,997 for other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index (PPI) of 58,653.8 quantifies this residual purchasing ability, placing Gila County at roughly 90 % of the national purchasing power benchmark (which would be 65,047 × 100/100 ≈ 65,047). In practical terms, a resident retains about $7,400 less buying power than a median American household after covering housing costs, largely due to the elevated service prices. Demographically, the county skews older, with a median age of 51.4 years, and only 12.4 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The unemployment rate of 7.4 % exceeds the national average, suggesting a labor market with limited high‑skill, high‑wage opportunities. An older, less‑educated workforce typically commands lower wages, which aligns with the county’s median income trailing the national figure by $5,958. The combination of higher service costs and a labor pool that earns less than the national median compresses discretionary spending, especially for younger families or newcomers without retirement income. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 100.558 in 2010 to 100.742 in 2023, a modest 0.2 % increase, indicating price stability relative to the nation. Median household income, however, surged 48.6 % from $39,751 to $59,089, outpacing the slight RPP rise. Rent climbed 34.9 % in the same period, from $742 to $1,001, reflecting a steeper upward trajectory than the overall price index but still below national rental growth rates. Wages have therefore kept pace with, and exceeded, inflationary pressures, though the benefit is partially offset by the higher service cost component. Prospective residents should budget with a clear hierarchy: allocate roughly 20 % of gross income to rent, anticipate service expenses that exceed national averages by 8–9 %, and plan for modest grocery and fuel outlays. A household earning the county median would need to reserve about $1,000 per month for rent, $300–$350 for utilities and healthcare, and $400–$500 for food and transportation combined. The remaining $2,000–$2,500 should cover discretionary spending, savings, and any unexpected costs. Understanding this expense distribution is essential for maintaining a balanced budget in Gila County’s mixed‑price environment.

What this means for residents of Gila County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58653.8, a median household income of $59,089 in Gila County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $34,657,944 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
