📊 Local Market Analysis

Washington County’s cost of living sits well below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most expensive component locally, registering at 96.5, only a few points shy of the national level, while services are markedly cheaper at 68.6. Housing, measured by rental costs, is modestly lower than the national benchmark at 90.8, reflecting a median monthly rent of $547 compared with the national median of $928. The combined effect of these indices means that everyday purchases—groceries, apparel, fuel—cost roughly 3.5 % less than the countrywide average, whereas health care, utilities, and dining out are about 31 % cheaper. The cost structure is driven primarily by the goods category, which accounts for the highest relative price level in the county. Even though goods are near‑national, the pronounced discount on services pulls the overall cost of living down. Residents therefore experience a relatively inexpensive daily life, especially in discretionary spending on health and leisure. The low rent burden—10.5 % of median household income—underscores the affordability of housing; at $547 per month, rent consumes a fraction of the median annual income of $62,452, leaving ample room for other expenditures. Disposable income calculations reveal a purchasing power index of 69,462.1, indicating that the median household can purchase roughly 69 % of the goods and services that a typical national household can buy with its income. After allocating the median rent ($547 × 12 = $6,564) and assuming average service costs at the local index level, the median earner retains roughly $55,000 of pre‑tax income for discretionary spending, savings, and taxes. This residual amount translates into a real‑terms advantage over many parts of the country where higher rents and service costs erode take‑home pay. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of 1.5 %, well below the national rate. The low unemployment combined with a modest educational attainment—15.9 % holding a bachelor’s degree or higher—suggests a labor force concentrated in sectors that typically offer lower wages but also lower cost structures, such as agriculture and local services. The median age of 43.9 years points to a mature population, often associated with stable consumption patterns and less price sensitivity for essential goods, reinforcing the observed low service cost index. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 92.991 to 89.908, a 3.3 % decline, while median household income rose from $45,159 to $62,452, a 38.3 % increase. Rental rates grew from $460 to $547, an 18.9 % rise. Income growth outpaced both the modest deflation in overall prices and the faster‑than‑inflation increase in rent, indicating that wages have more than kept pace with household expenses. The widening gap between income and cost of living enhances the county’s purchasing power relative to the national average. Prospective residents should budget around $547 for rent, allocate roughly 30 % of income to services (healthcare, utilities, dining) given the 68.6 index, and plan for goods expenditures near the national norm. With a rent burden of just over one‑tenth of income, a typical household can comfortably cover essential costs and retain a sizable discretionary margin. Emphasizing low service costs and affordable housing, Washington County offers a budget environment where a median earner can maintain a standard of living that feels financially spacious compared with many higher‑cost regions.

What this means for residents of Washington County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69462.1, a median household income of $62,452 in Washington County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $43,380,471 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
