📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Prowers County sits just above the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 101.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced marginally lower than the country average at 99.2, while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are markedly higher at 130.5. Rental costs are the most affordable component, recorded at 86.8, indicating that housing expenses are 13.2 % below the national level. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector. A 30.5 % premium on services outweighs the modest 0.8 % discount on goods and the 13.2 % discount on rents, meaning that everyday expenditures on medical care, electricity, water, and restaurant meals inflate household budgets more than any other category. For a typical resident, daily life feels relatively inexpensive when it comes to purchasing food or clothing, but the high price of essential services erodes that advantage. The low rent index translates to a median monthly rent of $741, which is 20 % cheaper than the national median of $928 and represents only 15.4 % of the median household income of $57,601. Disposable income after housing costs remains modest. Subtracting the median rent from the median household income leaves $56,860 annually, or $4,738 monthly, before other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 56,551.4—well below the national benchmark of 100—reflects that, after accounting for the elevated services prices, the median household retains roughly 56 % of the purchasing power of an average American household. In practical terms, a family must allocate a larger share of its budget to healthcare and utilities, limiting discretionary spending despite the lower rent burden. Demographic factors reinforce this profile. Only 10 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor force concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 38.5 aligns with a mature, working‑age population, while the unemployment rate of 4.8 % is slightly above the national rate, indicating modest labor market slack. These characteristics help explain why wages have risen faster than rents but lag behind the surge in service costs; limited educational attainment constrains upward mobility and keeps income growth tied to sectors that do not command premium service pricing. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 100.59 in 2010 to 101.856 in 2023, a modest 1.3 % increase, whereas median household income surged 43.4 % from $40,179 to $57,601. Rental prices climbed 27.3 % from $582 to $741. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rent increases, yet the steep rise in service costs has narrowed the net benefit for residents. The wage‑to‑price gap remains positive but is tempered by the high service index, meaning that real purchasing power gains are modest. Prospective residents should plan budgets around the disproportionate cost of services. With rent consuming only 15 % of income, the remaining 85 % must cover utilities, health insurance, medical visits, and everyday dining, all of which are priced well above national averages. Allocating roughly 30 % of after‑rent income to utilities and healthcare, another 15 % to transportation and groceries, and preserving a buffer for discretionary spending will be essential to maintain a balanced household budget in Prowers County.

What this means for residents of Prowers County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 56551.4, a median household income of $57,601 in Prowers County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $32,574,172 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
