📊 Local Market Analysis

Walton County’s cost of living sits just below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 96.5 versus 100 nationwide. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—register a 97.7 index, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 88.3. Housing, measured by rental costs, trails the national median rent of $928, with a local median of $1,231 but a rent‑burden ratio of only 17.9 % of median household income, reflecting a comparatively affordable housing component within the county’s price structure. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the goods segment, which sits just under the national benchmark yet remains the highest of the three categories. Services are the most inexpensive element, suggesting that routine expenditures on medical care, utilities, and restaurants cost roughly 12 % less than the national norm. Rental costs, while higher in absolute dollars than the national median, impose a modest share of income because median household earnings in Walton County reach $82,381—substantially above the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the median rent of $1,231, a typical household retains roughly $81,150 in annual income, translating to a disposable income pool that comfortably exceeds the national average. The Purchasing Power Index of 85,374.2 quantifies this advantage: residents can purchase about 15 % more goods and services than the average American household with the same nominal income. Demographic factors reinforce the purchasing environment. The unemployment rate of 5.4 % aligns closely with the national rate, indicating a stable labor market that supports steady earnings. However, only 15.9 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, a figure well below the national average, suggesting that higher‑paid professional occupations are less prevalent. The median age of 39.7 years points to a mature workforce, likely anchored in established industries such as manufacturing, logistics, and local services, which tend to offer wages sufficient to cover the modest cost premium. This demographic mix yields a cost of living experience where essential expenses are manageable, but limited educational attainment may constrain upward mobility for those seeking higher‑earning roles. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a 2.0 % increase that outpaces inflation but remains modest. Median household income surged 51.3 % in the same period, from $54,453 to $82,381, while median rent climbed 45.7 % from $845 to $1,231. The faster growth in earnings relative to both the price index and rental costs indicates that wages have generally kept pace with, and even outstripped, consumer expenses. Consequently, the net effect is an expanding purchasing cushion for the median household. Prospective residents should budget with the goods sector as the primary expense driver, allocating a larger share of discretionary spending to groceries, fuel, and apparel than to services or housing. With rent consuming less than one‑fifth of income, the remaining 80 % of earnings can be apportioned to transportation, healthcare, and leisure, all of which benefit from the county’s sub‑national service cost index. A realistic monthly budget would therefore include roughly $1,200 for rent, $600–$700 for groceries and fuel, $300 for utilities and healthcare, and an additional $200–$300 for dining and entertainment, leaving a comfortable margin for savings or non‑essential purchases. This structure underscores that, despite higher absolute rents, Walton County delivers a comparatively affordable daily living environment for households earning near the median income.

What this means for residents of Walton County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 85374.2, a median household income of $82,381 in Walton County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $70,332,120 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
