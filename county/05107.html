📊 Local Market Analysis

Phillips County registers an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, placing the cost of living roughly 13 percent below the national benchmark, yet the aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in consumer expenses. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — run at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. average, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to an index of 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs sit at 81.9, offering meaningful shelter savings relative to the national median of $928, with local median rent settling at $756. The service sector depression drives the county’s affordability profile, effectively subsidizing daily life for residents despite near-parity pricing on tangible goods. A median household income of $38,874 translates to a purchasing power index of 44,781, implying that local wages stretch approximately 15 percent further than their nominal value suggests. Rent consumes 23.3 percent of gross income, a manageable burden compared to national standards, leaving a larger residual for the goods basket where prices remain sticky. However, the unemployment rate of 15.8 percent and a bachelor’s attainment rate of just 9.7 percent signal a labor market heavily weighted toward lower-wage service and agricultural employment, constraining the absolute dollar volume of disposable income available to most households. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2 percent decline in relative price levels, while median income surged 44.8 percent against a 28.4 percent increase in median rent. This divergence indicates that local earnings have outpaced shelter inflation significantly, improving the rent-to-income ratio over the decade. The median age of 39.9 suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the educational attainment gap relative to the national median income of $65,047 underscores a structural ceiling on wage growth that limits the translation of purchasing power gains into wealth accumulation. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the friction between cheap services and stubborn goods prices. Grocery and fuel budgets will resemble those in far more expensive metros, demanding careful allocation of the roughly $2,485 in monthly post-rent income implied by the median earner. Healthcare and utility savings are real but partially offset by limited local provider networks and potential travel costs for specialized care. The high unemployment rate introduces income volatility risk that a static budget cannot absorb, making an emergency reserve critical in an economy where the next paycheck is less certain than the price of milk.

What this means for residents of Phillips County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 44780.6, a median household income of $38,874 in Phillips County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $17,408,010 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
