📊 Local Market Analysis

Allen County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — represent the highest cost component at 96.5, nearly converging with national pricing, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 68.6, the lowest segment and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs sit at 90.8, moderately below the national benchmark but significantly higher than the services index, creating a bifurcated environment where discretionary service consumption is cheap but essential goods and shelter command near-national premiums. The median household income of $57,618 trails the national median of $65,047 by 11.4 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 64,085.5 indicates that local wages stretch further than nominal figures suggest once the 89.9 RPP is applied. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 14.3 percent of gross income — a figure derived from a median monthly rent of $685 against the national $928 — leaving substantial theoretical discretionary capacity for the median earner. However, an unemployment rate of 6.9 percent, notably above typical national lows, combined with a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 14.3 percent, suggests a labor market concentrated in lower-wage sectors where the high goods index (96.5) erodes real income faster than the cheap services index (68.6) can offset it, particularly for households facing food and transportation inflation. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 to 89.908, signaling a relative cheapening of the local consumption basket against the national average. Nominal median income surged 48.9 percent from $38,698 to $57,618, comfortably outpacing the 29.0 percent rise in median rent from $531 to $685. This divergence implies that, on aggregate, wage growth has absorbed housing cost escalation and expanded real purchasing power, though the persistence of a near-parity goods index means grocery and energy bills likely consumed a disproportionate share of those gains during recent inflationary cycles. A prospective resident should budget for a daily expense profile where the checkout lane and the gas pump feel nationally priced while the doctor’s office and utility bills offer deep discounts. The 96.5 goods index demands careful allocation for food and fuel — categories with low substitution elasticity — whereas the 68.6 services index provides a buffer for healthcare utilization and dining flexibility. With a median age of 41.3 years, the demographic skew toward prime working years supports the current income structure, but the low educational attainment ceiling caps upward mobility, meaning the current purchasing power equilibrium relies heavily on the RPP remaining depressed relative to national inflation trends.

What this means for residents of Allen County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64085.5, a median household income of $57,618 in Allen County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $36,924,783 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
