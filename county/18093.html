📊 Local Market Analysis

Lawrence County’s cost of living remains notably below the national benchmark, with an overall RPP of 92.1 in 2023, indicating that consumer prices are roughly 8 percent cheaper than the national average. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel and fuel—register at 95.6, just slightly above the national index, while Services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 71.3. Housing costs, measured by rental rates, sit at 84.7, also below the national baseline. The divergence among components shows that daily expenses are driven primarily by the relatively higher price of goods rather than housing or services, making routine consumption items the most expensive segment of the local cost structure. Median household income of $65,551 positions Lawrence County households above the national median of $65,047, providing a modest income advantage. After allocating $800 to rent, the rent burden of 14.6 percent of income leaves a substantial portion of earnings for other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index of 71,176.0 reflects the county’s ability to afford goods and services relative to the nation, suggesting that the typical resident retains enough disposable income to cover the elevated goods component while benefiting from low service costs. The local labor market, with an unemployment rate of 3.4 percent, supports stable wage income, though the low share of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher (12.0 percent) may constrain higher‑earning opportunities and influence the overall consumption pattern. Over the past decade, the RPP has edged down by 1.0 percent, moving from 93.022 in 2010 to 92.097 in 2023, while median household income has risen sharply by 42.9 percent to $65,551. Median rent has increased by 25.6 percent to $800, a pace that lags well behind income growth. This divergence indicates that wages are outpacing housing cost inflation, contributing to the slight decline in the overall cost‑of‑living index. The median age of 43.5 years suggests a population that may prioritize stable, essential spending over discretionary consumption, reinforcing the observed lower service costs. Prospective residents should budget for rent at $800, which consumes less than one‑sixth of median income, leaving ample room for other expenses. The goods index of 95.6 means that groceries, clothing and fuel will be marginally more expensive than the national average, so households should factor a modest premium into their food and personal‑care budgets. In contrast, service expenses—including utilities, healthcare and dining—are roughly 30 percent below the national level, offering potential savings. Overall, the combination of below‑average living costs, solid income growth and a low rent burden makes Lawrence County an affordable environment for household budgeting, provided that the higher cost of goods is incorporated into monthly planning.

What this means for residents of Lawrence County, Indiana
