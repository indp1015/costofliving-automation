📊 Local Market Analysis

Canyon County’s cost of living in 2023 sits at 92.2 on the BEA RPP index, indicating a modest 7.8 % discount relative to the national baseline of 100. The overall index is pulled down by housing, which registers at 68.1, while goods (95.9) and services (86.9) sit closer to or above the national average. This spread shows that consumer prices for groceries, apparel, and fuel are slightly higher than the country as a whole, whereas healthcare, utilities, and dining are cheaper, and rental costs are markedly inexpensive. The consumption breakdown reveals that goods dominate the local cost structure, with an index of 95.9—nearly 6 % above the national level. Services, at 86.9, are about 13 % below the benchmark, and housing, at 68.1, is the most affordable component. Because goods represent the largest share of household budgets, daily life in Canyon County is modestly expensive despite the low rental burden, and residents must allocate a higher proportion of income to food, clothing, and fuel than they would in most other regions. Median household income of $72,355 provides a solid base for discretionary spending. Rent of $1,259 consumes 20.9 % of that income, leaving roughly $57,200 for other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 78,445.5 reflects the relative ability of the median worker to purchase goods and services after local costs, suggesting that while housing is affordable, the above‑average goods index erodes some of that advantage. The index indicates that the typical household retains a purchasing capacity that is about 21 % lower than the national median, primarily due to the higher cost of everyday items. Local demographics shape the cost environment in subtle ways. An unemployment rate of 3.4 % signals a tight labor market, supporting wage stability, while only 16.2 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, limiting high‑skill, high‑wage opportunities. The median age of 34.8 years points to a relatively young workforce, which can keep housing demand moderate and rent growth contained. Together, these factors contribute to a labor market that sustains moderate income growth without the pressure that often drives up living costs in more educated, higher‑wage regions. Trend analysis shows that the RPP has edged down from 93.2 in 2010 to 92.236 in 2023, a 1.0 % decline, while median income has risen 68.7 % and median rent has increased 68.8 % over the same period. Wages have risen roughly in step with rental costs, preserving affordability in the housing segment, but the goods index has remained above the national average, preventing a full alignment of income and expenses. The modest decline in the overall cost‑of‑living index suggests that income growth has outpaced the rise in consumer prices, albeit unevenly across categories.
