📊 Local Market Analysis

Carroll County residents navigate a cost environment where overall consumer prices sit 13.2% below the national benchmark, registering a Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest relative cost component at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. average, while Services plummet to 56.7, the lowest segment by a wide margin. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9. This structure indicates that daily essentials purchased frequently at the register offer limited relief compared to the national baseline, whereas the deep discount on services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining out drives the aggregate index down, creating a bifurcated experience where discretionary service consumption is remarkably affordable but routine grocery and fuel runs remain near parity with metropolitan areas. The median household income of $55,187 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 15.2%, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 63,572.2 suggests that local wages stretch further than the nominal gap implies once the 86.8 RPP is applied. Housing absorbs a modest 17.8% of gross income at the median rent of $818, well below the national median of $928 and significantly under the standard 30% burden threshold, freeing cash flow for other categories. However, with Goods priced at 93.1, the savings on rent are partially offset every time a household fills a tank or stocks a pantry. The unemployment rate of 3.9% signals a tight labor market, but an educational attainment rate of just 14.4% for bachelor’s degrees or higher, paired with a median age of 44.7, points toward an economy reliant on sectors where wage growth may lag behind goods inflation, limiting the ability of many workers to capitalize on the service-side discount. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined modestly from 88.561 to 86.81, a 2.0% drop indicating that local consumer prices fell slightly relative to the national trend. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 49.6% from $36,897 to $55,187, vastly outpacing the 31.5% increase in median rent from $622 to $818. This divergence implies a genuine improvement in real terms for renters over the decade, as income growth exceeded both the national inflation trajectory and local shelter cost acceleration. The data suggests that while the county has become marginally cheaper relative to the country, the primary driver of improved living standards has been robust nominal wage growth rather than collapsing prices. A prospective resident should budget for a daily expense profile where the grocery bill and fuel costs feel nearly identical to higher-income regions, while the monthly rent check and utility statements provide tangible relief. The 17.8% rent-to-income ratio offers a buffer, but the 93.1 Goods index means that a household moving from a major metro area will not experience proportional savings at the checkout counter. Budgeting must account for the reality that the "cheap" label attached to the 86.8 overall index applies heavily to services consumed intermittently — healthcare visits, restaurant meals, salon appointments — rather than the weekly non-discretionary spending that dictates cash flow stability.

What this means for residents of Carroll County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63572.2, a median household income of $55,187 in Carroll County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $35,083,590 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
