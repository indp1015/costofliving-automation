📊 Local Market Analysis

Chicot County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percentage points below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — represent the highest local cost component at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the national average, while Services plummet to 56.7, the lowest segment by a wide margin. Rental costs sit at 81.9, offering meaningful shelter savings relative to the $928 national median rent, with the local median of $661 consuming just 20.0% of the $39,683 median household income, a rent burden significantly lighter than the national typical. The consumption breakdown illustrates a daily life where essential recurring purchases — food at the supermarket, gasoline for the commute, clothing for a growing family — extract nearly full national pricing power, while the steep discount in services reflects lower local price levels for healthcare, utilities, and dining out. This dynamic creates a bifurcated experience: a household can secure housing affordability that yields a Purchasing Power Index of $45,712.5, effectively stretching the nominal median income by roughly $6,000 in real terms, but the 93.1 Goods index ensures that the weekly trip to the store offers little relief. The 8.5% unemployment rate and a workforce where only 12.1% hold a bachelor’s degree suggest a labor market anchored in lower-wage sectors, limiting the ability of many households to leverage the cheap services category beyond basic necessity. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP drifted downward from 88.561 to 86.81, a modest 2.0% decline in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 37.2% from $28,913 to $39,683, vastly outpacing the 15.0% rise in median rent from $575 to $661. This divergence indicates that wage growth has substantially outstripped shelter inflation, improving the real income trajectory for renters. However, the median age of 42.9 years signals an aging population potentially facing fixed incomes, where the persistence of near-national goods pricing erodes the purchasing power gains delivered by cheap housing and services. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on the inelasticity of the goods basket. While the $661 median rent and low service costs — particularly utilities and healthcare co-pays — provide a stable floor, the 93.1 goods index means grocery and transportation budgets will resemble those in far more expensive metros. A household relocating from a high-cost area captures immediate shelter arbitrage, but the weekly cash outflow for fuel and food remains stubbornly high relative to local wages. The data suggests the primary financial risk lies not in rent escalation, which has been historically tame, but in the vulnerability of goods pricing to national supply shocks that the local wage base cannot easily absorb.

What this means for residents of Chicot County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 45712.5, a median household income of $39,683 in Chicot County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $18,140,091 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
