📊 Local Market Analysis

Chicot County presents a cost of living profile that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, a modest improvement from 88.6 in 2010. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel carry an index of 93.1, approaching national parity, while services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—plummet to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs settle at 81.9, offering meaningful shelter discounts relative to the benchmark. Consequently, the local cost structure is defined not by cheap housing alone, but by a service economy priced drastically below national norms, a dynamic that suppresses the overall index despite near-average goods expenditures. The median household income of $39,683 trails the national median of $65,047 by nearly 39 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of $45,712.5 reveals that the low price level recovers substantial real value for residents. A median monthly rent of $661 consumes only 20.0 percent of gross income, a burden significantly lighter than the national standard where $928 median rent weighs heavier against lower relative earnings. Historical trends reinforce this advantage: nominal incomes surged 37.2 percent between 2010 and 2023, rising from $28,913, while median rent increased just 15.0 percent from $575. The RPP’s 2.0 percent decline over the same period indicates that local inflation has effectively been negative in real terms, allowing wage growth to translate almost entirely into expanded discretionary capacity rather than offsetting price pressure. Demographic and labor market characteristics frame the sustainability of this dynamic. An unemployment rate of 8.5 percent signals considerable slack in the labor market, likely suppressing wage growth potential despite the recent decade’s gains. The workforce reflects limited educational attainment, with only 12.1 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, correlating with the income ceiling observed. A median age of 42.9 years suggests an established, potentially aging population with fixed-income sensitivities, particularly regarding the heavily discounted services category where healthcare costs—often a primary budget driver for this cohort—register at nearly half the national price level. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between goods and services. While housing consumes a manageable fifth of income, the 93.1 index for goods means weekly grocery trips, fuel, and clothing purchases will feel nearly as expensive as in metropolitan areas, demanding careful allocation of the roughly $3,300 monthly gross income. Conversely, the 56.7 services index provides a structural buffer; utility bills, medical co-pays, and local dining represent genuine arbitrage opportunities. However, the elevated unemployment rate and low educational attainment metrics imply limited upward mobility, meaning the current purchasing power advantage relies heavily on the persistence of a weak local service economy rather than rising prosperity.

What this means for residents of Chicot County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 45712.5, a median household income of $39,683 in Chicot County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $18,140,091 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
