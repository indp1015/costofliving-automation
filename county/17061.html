📊 Local Market Analysis

Greene County residents navigate a cost structure that sits marginally below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) registering 98.8 against the 100 national average in 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run 1.6 percent above the national index at 101.6, making daily necessities the most expensive relative component in the local basket. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a substantial 7.6 percent discount at 92.4, while rental housing costs represent the deepest value proposition at 80.4, nearly twenty percent below the national norm. This configuration creates a distinctive consumer environment where the routine act of stocking a pantry or filling a tank exerts more upward pressure on the household budget than shelter or medical services, a reversal of the dynamic observed in most metropolitan counties. The median household income of $60,976 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 6.3 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 61,746.6 indicates that local wages stretch further than the nominal gap suggests once the favorable RPP is applied. Housing affordability is the primary engine of this surplus; median monthly rent of $800 consumes only 15.7 percent of gross income, a stark contrast to the national median rent of $928 and the 30-percent burden threshold commonly used to define cost stress. This low shelter overhead effectively subsidizes the premium paid for goods, leaving discretionary income relatively insulated. The labor market context reinforces this stability: a 4.2 percent unemployment rate signals a tight but not overheated market, while an older median age of 43.1 years and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 11.2 percent suggest an economy anchored in established, non-degree-intensive sectors rather than high-wage knowledge industries. Over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023, the county has experienced a rare and favorable decoupling of income growth from price appreciation. The RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, meaning the local cost of living actually fell in real terms relative to the nation. Simultaneously, median household income surged 43.3 percent from $42,565 to $60,976, vastly outpacing the 31.8 percent increase in median rent from $607 to $800. This trajectory implies that the median worker has captured significant real wage gains, with housing costs absorbing a shrinking share of the paycheck despite nominal rent hikes. The decline in the overall RPP was likely driven by the services and housing components, offsetting the persistent premium on goods. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the trade-off between cheap shelter and expensive consumables. A household relocating from a higher-cost region will find immediate relief in lease payments and utility bills, potentially freeing several hundred dollars monthly relative to national averages. However, that surplus faces erosion at the grocery store and gas pump, where prices exceed the national benchmark. Transportation costs warrant specific scrutiny given the county’s rural character; longer commutes amplify the impact of the 101.6 goods index on fuel and vehicle maintenance. Ultimately, the financial viability of a move hinges on whether the household’s consumption basket weighs heavily toward housing and services—where Greene County offers deep value—or toward goods, where the discount disappears.

What this means for residents of Greene County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 61746.6, a median household income of $60,976 in Greene County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $37,650,607 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
