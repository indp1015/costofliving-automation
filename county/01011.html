📊 Local Market Analysis

Bullock County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Prices for goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 94.6, still under the national average but the highest component within the county. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheap at 61.6, while rental costs track closely with the overall index at 89.9. The median household earns $36,723, roughly 44 % of the national median income of $65,047, and the median monthly rent of $635 is 31 % lower than the national median of $928. Rent burden—20.7 % of income—remains modest, indicating that housing costs do not dominate the budget as they do in many higher‑priced metros. The expense profile is driven primarily by goods, which, despite being sub‑national, lift the overall cost structure relative to services. Daily necessities such as food and fuel consume a larger share of household outlays, while the low service index translates into inexpensive medical visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals. Consequently, everyday life feels affordable in terms of housing and discretionary services, but grocery bills and transportation costs erode a larger portion of disposable income than in regions where services are pricier. Disposable income after rent is thin. Subtracting the median rent of $635 from the median monthly income (approximately $3,060) leaves $2,425 for all other expenses. When adjusted for the county’s purchasing‑power index of 41,216.9—roughly 41 % of the national benchmark—residents retain less real buying power than the average American. The combination of lower wages and higher relative goods prices means that even modest households must allocate a sizable share of their remaining income to food, fuel, and clothing, leaving limited margin for savings or non‑essential spending. Demographically, the county’s labor market reflects limited high‑skill employment: only 6.7 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the unemployment rate sits at 5.9 %, modestly above the national average. The median age of 40.4 suggests a mature workforce, yet the low educational attainment constrains access to higher‑paying jobs, reinforcing the modest income profile. This labor composition sustains the low service price environment—providers operate with lower overheads and wages—while keeping goods prices anchored to regional supply chains rather than national price pressures. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 89.8 to 89.1, a 0.8 % decline, indicating marginally cheaper overall prices. Median household income rose 15 % from $31,938 to $36,723, outpacing the 11.4 % increase in median rent ($570 to $635). Although wages have grown faster than housing costs, they have not kept pace with the 5 % rise in goods prices implied by the goods index, leaving real purchasing power relatively stagnant. Prospective residents should budget tightly around food and fuel, anticipate a rent burden of roughly one‑fifth of earnings, and plan for limited discretionary spending given the modest disposable income after essential costs.

What this means for residents of Bullock County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 41216.9, a median household income of $36,723 in Bullock County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $15,136,082 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
