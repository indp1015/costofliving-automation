📊 Local Market Analysis

Prairie County residents navigate a cost structure 13.2 percent below the national average, anchored by a Regional Price Parity of 86.8 that masks sharp divergence across spending categories. Goods — groceries, apparel, and fuel — run only 6.9 percent cheaper than the U.S. baseline at 93.1, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, a staggering 43.3 percent discount. Rental costs sit at 81.9, roughly 18 percent below national levels, positioning housing as a moderate rather than primary driver of affordability. The consumption profile reveals daily life is inexpensive largely because labor-intensive services are deeply discounted, not because food or energy are bargains. That service-sector discount translates directly into purchasing power. The median household income of $51,094 falls 21.5 percent below the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 58,857 indicates the median earner effectively commands 15.2 percent more real consumption capacity than the nominal wage suggests. Rent burden sits at a remarkably low 15.0 percent of income — $637 monthly against a national median of $928 — freeing substantial cash flow for the higher-index goods basket. However, the 11.2 percent bachelor’s attainment rate and 4.1 percent unemployment rate signal a labor market tilted toward lower-wage service and manual employment, capping income growth potential for the median worker. Over the past decade, the affordability dynamic has improved measurably. The RPP declined from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 42.6 percent from $35,833 to $51,094. Median rent rose only 35.2 percent over the same span, from $471 to $637, meaning wage growth outpaced the largest recurring household expense by a meaningful margin. The median age of 45.8 suggests a settled, older population likely benefiting from fixed housing costs established before recent inflationary pressures, though younger entrants face the full weight of current goods pricing. A prospective resident should budget for a goods bill that tracks near national averages — grocery trips and fill-ups will feel familiar — while capturing outsized savings on insurance, medical visits, and utility bills. The low rent burden creates a buffer against goods-price volatility, but the narrow industrial base implies limited upside for wage negotiation. Household budgeting here hinges less on shelter costs and more on managing transportation and food expenses in an environment where service-sector deflation subsidizes the bottom line.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Prairie County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58857.3, a median household income of $51,094 in Prairie County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $30,072,549 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
