📊 Local Market Analysis

Prairie County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest local cost component at 93.1, merely 6.9 percent below the national baseline, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, a staggering 43.3 percent discount that single-handedly drags the aggregate index down. Rental costs, measured at 81.9, occupy the middle ground, running 18.1 percent cheaper than the U.S. average. This profile indicates that while keeping a roof overhead and putting food on the table carries a modest discount, the true affordability lever lies in the service economy, where a median monthly rent of $637 consumes only 15.0 percent of the $51,094 median household income, a burden significantly lighter than the national median rent of $928 against a $65,047 income. The purchasing power calculus translates that nominal income into a real index value of 58,857.3, effectively granting the median household roughly $7,800 in additional buying capacity relative to their nominal earnings when benchmarked against national price levels. However, the labor market context tempers this advantage: an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent sits alongside an educational attainment figure of just 11.2 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors where wage growth may lack the elasticity found in knowledge-dense metros. The median age of 45.8 years further implies a population cohort likely past peak earning years but not yet drawing full retirement benefits, positioning household budgets in a maintenance phase where the low service costs — particularly healthcare and utilities — function as a critical stabilizer for fixed or slowly rising incomes. Over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023, the RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a marginal 2.0 percent decline in the price level that contrasts sharply with a 42.6 percent surge in median household income from $35,833 to $51,094. Median rent increased 35.2 percent over the same period, climbing from $471 to $637, yet income growth outpaced shelter costs by a comfortable 7.4 percentage points. This divergence signals a decade of genuine real wage improvement for the area, a rarity in many rural corridors where stagnant pay often meets rising rents. The data suggests the local economy has managed to deliver compensation gains that exceed both the national inflation trajectory and the specific trajectory of housing costs, expanding the margin for discretionary spending or savings accumulation. For a prospective resident constructing a household ledger, the budgeting reality hinges on the asymmetry between goods and services. The grocery bill and fuel tank will feel familiar to anyone relocating from a metropolitan area, demanding nearly 93 cents on the national dollar, while the monthly outlay for electricity, water, medical visits, and restaurant meals will likely register a profound discount. A household earning the local median can expect to allocate roughly $7,644 annually to rent, leaving approximately $43,450 for all other expenditures — a residual that stretches considerably further than the nominal figure implies because the service-intensive portion of the consumption basket costs barely half the national rate. The primary fiscal vulnerability remains exposure to goods inflation; should food or energy prices spike nationally, Prairie County households absorb nearly the full brunt without the offsetting service-side cushion that currently defines their affordability advantage.

What this means for residents of Prairie County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58857.3, a median household income of $51,094 in Prairie County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $30,072,549 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
