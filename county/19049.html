📊 Local Market Analysis

Dallas County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percent below the national average, as measured by the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index of 88.8 for 2023. The discount is not uniform across the consumption basket. Goods — covering groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run only 3.4 percent cheaper than the U.S. baseline at 96.6, making everyday retail purchases the most expensive relative component. Rental costs follow at 87.3, a 12.7 percent advantage. The dominant driver of the county's affordability is the services category, which includes healthcare, utilities, and dining, priced at a striking 66.0 — fully 34 percent below the national level. This deep services discount compresses the overall index, meaning daily life is structurally inexpensive for households consuming typical service bundles, even as grocery and fuel bills track closer to national norms. That price advantage translates into exceptional purchasing power for the median household earning $102,349 annually, a figure that exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 57.3 percent. The Purchasing Power Index of 115,298.1 quantifies this surplus, indicating that after adjusting for local price levels, the typical Dallas County household commands real resources roughly 77 percent above the national average. Housing absorbs a modest 14.5 percent of gross income at the median rent of $1,239, well under the 30 percent burden threshold and far lighter than the national median rent of $928 would imply for a lower-income household. The labor market underpins this dynamic: a 2.9 percent unemployment rate signals tight demand for labor, while a 34.1 percent bachelor's attainment rate and a median age of 35.8 years suggest a workforce in its prime earning years, concentrated in professional and technical occupations that command above-average wages. Over the 2010–2023 period, the county's cost trajectory has reinforced this advantage. The RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.139 to 88.769, meaning local prices fell relative to the national basket even as nominal incomes surged 34.1 percent from $76,320 to $102,349. Median rent rose 36.2 percent from $910 to $1,239, slightly outpacing income growth, yet the rent-to-income ratio remains low by historical and national standards. The divergence — wages rising faster than the overall price level while rent modestly outpaced wages — suggests that non-housing consumption has become progressively cheaper in real terms, a rare dynamic in contemporary U.S. regional economics. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel expenses that feel nearly identical to metropolitan averages, with a weekly food shop for a family of four likely running $180–$220 and regular unleaded tracking within a few cents of the national mean. The savings emerge in healthcare premiums, utility bills, and restaurant meals, where the 34 percent services discount can free $400–$600 monthly compared to a city with a 100 RPP. Rent for a two-bedroom unit typically falls between $1,100 and $1,350, requiring roughly $45,000–$55,000 in gross income to stay within the 30 percent affordability guideline. Childcare, a services-intensive expense, benefits from the same structural discount, though availability may constrain realized savings. The net effect is a household budget where fixed costs are low, discretionary capacity is high, and the marginal dollar goes further than in almost any other Midwestern county of comparable income.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Dallas County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 115298.1, a median household income of $102,349 in Dallas County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $118,006,452 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
