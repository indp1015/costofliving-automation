📊 Local Market Analysis

Mohave County’s cost of living sits marginally above the national baseline, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 100.7 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced 2.1 % lower than the national average (97.9), while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are 8.6 % higher (108.6). Rental costs are the most pronounced deviation, registering at 83.3, meaning rents are 16.7 % below the national level. The juxtaposition of cheap housing and elevated service prices creates a mixed affordability profile: shelter costs are a clear advantage, but everyday expenditures on health and utilities erode that benefit. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, which outpaces both goods and housing. At an index of 108.6, services raise the overall cost of living despite the sub‑national housing index. For a household earning the median income of $55,799, monthly rent of $1,047 consumes 22.5 % of earnings, comfortably below the 30 % rent‑burden threshold that signals affordability. After covering rent, the median resident retains roughly $4,650 per month before taxes and other obligations. The Purchasing Power Index of 55,388 underscores that, relative to a national benchmark of 100,000, residents can purchase about 55 % of the goods and services that an average American can with the same income. In practical terms, the lower cost of housing offsets the higher service expenses, leaving disposable income modest but not negligible. Demographic and labor‑market characteristics reinforce the cost picture. With a median age of 53.2 years, the county skews older, suggesting a larger proportion of retirees or near‑retirees whose consumption patterns may emphasize healthcare and utilities—precisely the categories where prices are highest. Only 9.2 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill occupations that typically command lower wages. The unemployment rate of 6.9 % exceeds the national average, limiting upward pressure on incomes. Consequently, the median household income of $55,799 trails the national median of $65,047 by 14 %, while the median rent of $1,047 is 12.6 % above the national median of $928, reflecting a localized rent premium despite the low housing index. Over the past decade, the RPP index has risen from 100.558 in 2010 to 100.742 in 2023, a modest 0.2 % increase, indicating price stability relative to the nation. Median household income, however, surged 45 % from $38,488 to $55,799, outpacing the 33.2 % rise in median rent (from $786 to $1,047). The faster growth in earnings relative to housing costs suggests that wages have kept pace with, and even exceeded, inflationary pressures on shelter, though service‑price inflation remains a drag on real purchasing power. Prospective residents should budget with a clear hierarchy: allocate roughly 22–23 % of gross income to rent, anticipate higher-than‑average expenses for healthcare, utilities, and dining, and benefit from lower grocery and fuel costs. With a median disposable income of about $4,650 per month after rent, households must plan for the service‑price premium, especially if they lack supplemental retirement income. A prudent monthly budget would reserve 10–12 % for healthcare, 8–10 % for utilities, and 5–7 % for dining out, leaving the remainder for discretionary spending, savings, or debt service. Understanding this composition enables newcomers to align expectations with the county’s unique blend of affordable housing and elevated service costs.

What this means for residents of Mohave County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55388.0, a median household income of $55,799 in Mohave County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $30,905,950 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
