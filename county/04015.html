📊 Local Market Analysis

Mohave County’s cost of living sits marginally above the national baseline with a Regional Price Parity of 100.7, but the aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in household budgets: services run 8.6 percent above the U.S. average at 108.6, while rental housing costs 16.7 percent less at 83.3 and goods come in 2.1 percent cheaper at 97.9. The services component — encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining — acts as the primary inflationary driver for daily life, offsetting the substantial discount on shelter and making routine consumption more expensive than the headline index suggests. Median household income of $55,799 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 14 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 55,388 indicates that local prices erase only a fraction of that nominal gap. Rent burden stands at a manageable 22.5 percent of income against a median monthly rent of $1,047, which itself exceeds the national median of $928 despite the low rental RPP — a reflection of the area’s older housing stock and limited inventory. The labor market context complicates the picture: a 6.9 percent unemployment rate, a median age of 53.2, and just 9.2 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree point to an economy tilted toward lower-wage service and retirement-support roles, limiting upward pressure on wages even as service-sector prices climb. Over the past decade, the RPP has been essentially flat, rising just 0.2 percent from 100.558 to 100.742, while median income surged 45 percent from $38,488 and median rent increased 33.2 percent from $786. Wage growth has comfortably outpaced both shelter costs and the broader cost environment, improving real purchasing power for incumbent households. However, the income gains start from a low base, and the persistent services premium means that healthcare and utility bills consume a disproportionate share of any incremental earnings. A prospective resident should budget for service categories that run nearly nine percent above national norms — particularly health insurance premiums, electricity, and restaurant meals — while capturing significant savings on lease payments relative to most metropolitan areas. The low rent burden preserves cash flow for other obligations, but the below-average median income constrains absolute discretionary spending. Households relocating from higher-wage regions will find their dollars stretch further on housing but lose ground on recurring service expenses, a trade-off that favors those with fixed retirement income or remote earnings over those dependent on local labor markets.

What this means for residents of Mohave County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55388.0, a median household income of $55,799 in Mohave County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $30,905,950 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
