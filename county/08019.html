📊 Local Market Analysis

Clear Creek County’s cost of living sits just above the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 101.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel and fuel are priced marginally lower than the countrywide average (99.2), while services—including healthcare, utilities and dining out—are markedly higher at 130.5, more than thirty percent above the national benchmark. Rental costs are the most affordable component of the local price basket, recorded at 86.8, indicating that landlords charge roughly thirteen percent less than the average American market. The service sector drives the county’s price structure. A resident spending $200 a month on utilities, $150 on healthcare premiums and $250 on restaurant meals would encounter a combined service bill of $600, which, when adjusted for the 130.5 service index, translates to an effective cost of $782 compared with a $600 national equivalent. By contrast, a typical grocery basket of $400 would cost $398 locally, reflecting the sub‑national goods index. Consequently, everyday life feels expensive in the service realm while remaining modest in the goods and housing arenas. The low rental index further cushions household budgets: median rent of $1,230 is only 15.3 % of the median household income of $96,667, well below the often‑cited 30 % rent‑burden threshold. Disposable income calculations underscore a relatively strong purchasing position. After allocating the median rent, a household retains $95,437 annually, or $7,953 per month, for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 94,905.6—derived from the ratio of median income to the overall RPP—signals that residents can purchase roughly 5 % fewer goods and services than the average American, despite earning $31,620 more than the national median income of $65,047. In real terms, the higher income offsets the inflated service costs, leaving a modest net advantage for consumers who can manage the service expense spike. Demographic factors shape this dynamic. With 30 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor force is relatively skilled, supporting wages that have risen 42.8 % since 2010. The median age of 49.2 years suggests a mature population, likely with established earnings and lower consumption of child‑related goods, which can dampen overall household outlays. Unemployment at 4.2 % remains below the national average, reinforcing labor‑market stability and sustaining the county’s income growth trajectory. Over the past decade, the RPP index edged up from 100.59 to 101.856, a modest 1.3 % increase, while median household income surged 42.8 % and median rent climbed 51.3 %. Wage growth outpaced the slight rise in overall price levels, but rent escalated faster than the general cost index, eroding the housing affordability advantage. Nonetheless, because rent remains the lowest‑priced component, the net effect is a modest improvement in real purchasing power for most households. Prospective residents should budget with service expenses at the forefront. Assuming the median service spend of $600 per month, a realistic monthly budget would allocate roughly $1,230 for rent, $600 for services, $400 for groceries, $150 for transportation fuel, and $150 for apparel, leaving about $1,470 for discretionary spending, savings or debt repayment. The low rent‑burden ratio offers flexibility, but the elevated service costs demand careful planning, especially for households without employer‑provided health benefits. Understanding that the county’s higher income levels largely compensate for service inflation helps newcomers gauge whether their personal consumption patterns will fit comfortably within the local cost structure.

What this means for residents of Clear Creek County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 94905.6, a median household income of $96,667 in Clear Creek County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $91,742,396 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
