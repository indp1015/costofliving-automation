📊 Local Market Analysis

Garland County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 86.8 for 2023 against a U.S. average of 100. This discount is not uniform across the consumption basket; goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — represent the highest relative expense at 93.1, merely 6.9 percent cheaper than the national norm, while rental costs track at 81.9. The dominant deflationary force is the services sector, which clocks in at a striking 56.7, or 43.3 percent below the national average, a figure that likely reflects suppressed pricing for healthcare, utilities, and dining-out relative to metropolitan centers. The interaction between this price profile and local earnings defines the household budget reality. Median household income stands at $55,409, roughly 14.8 percent below the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 63,827.9 indicates that the lower cost environment more than compensates for the nominal income gap, granting the median earner approximately $8,779 in additional real purchasing capacity compared to the national average. Housing absorption remains manageable; median monthly rent of $942 — slightly above the national $928 — consumes 20.4 percent of gross income, a rent-burden threshold that leaves meaningful discretionary room for the goods-heavy consumption patterns where local prices run closest to parity. Demographic and labor market characteristics sharpen the context for these figures. An unemployment rate of 5.9 percent exceeds typical full-employment benchmarks, suggesting slack in the labor market that may suppress wage growth for the 83.4 percent of adults without a bachelor’s degree. The median age of 45.3 years skews older than the national profile, implying a population potentially drawing on fixed incomes or retirement savings where the deeply discounted services index — particularly healthcare and utilities — delivers outsized budgetary relief compared to younger, rent-dependent cohorts in higher-cost regions. Over the 2010 to 2023 window, the RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative living costs, while nominal median income surged 43.1 percent from $38,733 to $55,409. Rent appreciation of 32.3 percent over the same period, moving from $712 to $942, lagged income growth by a significant margin, improving the rent-to-income ratio structurally. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the divergence between categories: weekly grocery trips and fuel fills will feel near-national in price, while the monthly outlay for insurance, medical visits, and utility bills will likely register as a distinct discount, a dynamic that favors households with higher service consumption relative to goods intensity.

What this means for residents of Garland County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63827.9, a median household income of $55,409 in Garland County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $35,366,401 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
