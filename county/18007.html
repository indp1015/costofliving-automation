📊 Local Market Analysis

Benton County’s cost of living sits comfortably below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 92.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 95.6, still under the national average but the highest component locally. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheaper at 71.3, while rental costs score 84.7, indicating that housing expenses are roughly 15 % lower than the countrywide benchmark. The combined effect is a modestly affordable environment for everyday consumption, especially in service‑oriented spending. The dominant driver of Benton County’s price structure is the goods segment, which, at 95.6, outweighs both services and housing. Daily life therefore feels inexpensive in categories such as medical care, electricity, and restaurant meals, where costs are nearly 30 % below national levels. However, the relative proximity of goods prices to the national average tempers the overall cheapness; residents still pay near‑average rates for food and fuel, which constitute a sizable share of household outlays. The rental market, at an index of 84.7, translates to a median monthly rent of $834—$94 less than the national median of $928—further easing the budgetary pressure on housing. Disposable income analysis reveals that the median household earns $61,811, 5.5 % below the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the local rent burden of 16.2 % of income, the median family retains roughly $51,600 in annual discretionary funds. The Purchasing Power Index of 67,115.1 quantifies this advantage: residents can purchase about 6.3 % more goods and services than the average American with comparable earnings. This surplus stems largely from the low service‑price index, which stretches the median income further than raw wage figures alone would suggest. Demographically, Benton County’s labor market is modestly educated, with only 15.5 % holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age sits at 40.3 years. The unemployment rate of 3.6 % is comfortably below the national average, indicating a stable job environment despite the limited share of highly educated workers. The combination of stable employment and a relatively older population—often associated with higher homeownership and lower discretionary spending—helps keep demand for high‑priced services in check, reinforcing the low service index observed. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.0 to 92.1, a 1 % decline, while median household income surged 31.4 % from $47,046 to $61,811. Rental costs rose 22.6 % from $680 to $834. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and housing cost increases, expanding real purchasing power for residents. The modest dip in the RPP suggests that price pressures have eased even as wages climbed, reinforcing the county’s affordability trajectory. Prospective residents should budget around $834 for rent, representing roughly one‑sixth of median earnings, and allocate a larger share to groceries and fuel, which remain near‑national levels. With services costing about 30 % less than elsewhere, utilities and healthcare will consume a smaller slice of the budget, allowing discretionary spending on transportation, entertainment, or savings. A realistic monthly household budget might therefore allocate 30 % to housing, 25 % to goods, and the remaining 45 % to services, savings, and variable expenses, reflecting the county’s distinctive price composition and the purchasing power advantage it affords.

What this means for residents of Benton County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 67115.1, a median household income of $61,811 in Benton County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $41,484,514 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
