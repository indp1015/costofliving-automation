📊 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County’s cost of living remains modestly below the national benchmark, with an overall index of 98.8, indicating that consumer prices are slightly lower than the average American household. Goods, at 101.6, outpace the national baseline, while services (92.4) and housing (80.4) are markedly cheaper. The disparity shows that daily expenses are driven primarily by the goods component, which includes groceries, apparel, and fuel, making routine consumption the most expensive segment of the local budget. Housing, despite being the lowest‑indexed category, still represents a significant share of household outlays, but its relative affordability is offset by the higher cost of everyday items. The median household income of $45,572 yields a rent burden of 19.6 %, well within the conventional threshold of 30 % and suggesting that rental costs are manageable for the typical resident. The purchasing power index of 46,147.9 reflects the adjusted income after local price adjustments, indicating that a household can purchase roughly 46 % of a national basket of goods and services with its earnings. Education levels—only 18.2 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—and an unemployment rate of 7.2 % point to a labor market that may limit high‑earning opportunities, reinforcing the reliance on lower‑cost housing and services to maintain budgetary balance. Over the past decade, the regional price parity (RPP) has slipped from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a decline of 3.0 % that signals modest deflation in the overall cost environment. Median income has risen sharply, climbing 34.5 % from $33,871 to $45,572, while median rent has increased only 11.8 % from $667 to $746. This divergence suggests that wage growth has outpaced housing cost inflation, improving real disposable income. However, the goods index remains above parity, meaning that while housing and services have become relatively more affordable, the expense of everyday consumption has not followed the same trend. Prospective residents should budget for a goods‑heavy expense profile, allocating a larger share of income to groceries, clothing, and fuel than they would in a national context. Rent, at $746 per month, consumes roughly one‑fifth of the median household income, leaving room for savings or discretionary spending. The local unemployment rate and lower educational attainment may constrain higher‑earning prospects, making the relatively inexpensive housing and services critical components of household financial planning. Understanding these dynamics enables realistic budgeting that accounts for the county’s unique cost structure and income landscape.

What this means for residents of Jackson County, Illinois
