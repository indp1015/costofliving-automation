📊 Local Market Analysis

Telfair County presents a cost of living profile that sits modestly below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 96.5 against the U.S. average of 100, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct asymmetry in household budgeting pressures. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—constitute the highest local cost component at 97.7, nearly converging with national pricing and leaving residents minimal relief on daily consumables. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a substantial 11.7 percent discount at 88.3, while rental costs sit 7.7 percent below the national mean at 92.3. This structure creates a bifurcated experience: fixed shelter obligations are manageable, with median rent of $614 consuming only 16.4 percent of the $44,948 median household income, but the variable expense of goods exerts outsized pressure on disposable cash flow. The purchasing power calculus underscores the income constraint more than the price level. The Purchasing Power Index of 46,581.1 indicates that after adjusting for local prices, the median household commands real resources roughly 28 percent below the nominal national median income of $65,047. While the rent burden is low by national standards—where median rent of $928 typically claims a far larger share of earnings—the gap between local earnings and national income norms means residents possess significantly less absolute purchasing capacity for tradable goods priced at near-national levels. A dollar earned in Telfair buys slightly more housing and services locally, but it buys the same amount of gasoline, groceries, and clothing as a dollar spent in a median-cost metro, effectively penalizing households for the income differential. Demographic and labor market fundamentals reinforce this dynamic. An unemployment rate of 5.0 percent exceeds the national average, signaling slack in the labor market that likely suppresses wage growth, while an educational attainment rate of just 5.6 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher limits access to higher-wage service sectors that might otherwise benefit from the area’s low service-cost structure. The median age of 39.4 suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the occupational mix implied by the education data points toward employment in sectors where wage growth has historically lagged productivity gains. This labor market composition helps explain why the cost advantage in services—a category often driven by local labor costs—has not translated into proportionally higher real incomes for the median household. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP drifted upward from 94.6 to 96.5, a cumulative increase of roughly 2.0 percent, while nominal median income surged 69.9 percent from $26,449 to $44,948. Median rent, by contrast, rose only 7.5 percent to $614. On the surface, wages have dramatically outpaced both inflation and shelter costs, yet the persistent gap between local purchasing power and national income levels suggests the starting base was exceptionally low. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is clear: housing consumes a small, stable slice of the paycheck, but the weekly grocery bill and fuel costs will feel nearly identical to those in far more expensive regions, while the local wage ceiling caps the ability to absorb those near-national prices. The low service cost structure provides a buffer for healthcare and utilities, but it does not offset the fundamental constraint of an income base that remains significantly below the national median.

What this means for residents of Telfair County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 46581.1, a median household income of $44,948 in Telfair County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $20,937,273 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
