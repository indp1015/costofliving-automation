📊 Local Market Analysis

Northeastern Connecticut Planning Region sits 4.2 percent above the national price level with an overall Regional Price Parity of 104.2, but that aggregate masks a severe distortion in the consumption basket. Rental costs drive the premium at 153.2, more than fifty-three points above the national baseline, while goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—actually come in below average at 98.6. Services occupy an uncomfortable middle at 116.6, pushed up by healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses that outpace the broader economy. For a household managing day-to-day cash flow, this means the weekly trip to the supermarket and the gas station feels slightly cheaper than the national norm, but the monthly lease payment and the utility bill absorb a disproportionate share of take-home pay. The median household income of $87,564 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 34.6 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 84,033.7 reveals that local price levels erode roughly 4.1 percent of that nominal advantage. Rent burden stands at 15.8 percent of income against a median monthly rent of $1,153, a figure that appears manageable on paper but obscures the reality that the rental index of 153.2 reflects a market where available units command significant premiums. The labor market context complicates the picture further: a 6.2 percent unemployment rate sits above healthy thresholds, while only 14.9 percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors where wage growth may not track service-sector inflation. The median age of 43.9 years indicates a population past peak earning years for many, with fixed-income retirees particularly exposed to the 116.6 services index. Over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023, the RPP declined from 106.999 to 104.201, a 2.6 percent improvement in relative affordability. Median income rose 5.3 percent from $83,119 to $87,564, outpacing the 3.0 percent increase in median rent from $1,119 to $1,153. This divergence suggests wages have modestly outrun shelter costs in nominal terms, though the persistent elevation of the rental index implies that new entrants to the market face steeper price points than long-term tenants. The goods index remaining below 100 throughout the period has provided a consistent offset, but the services component at 116.6 continues to exert upward pressure on the overall basket, particularly for healthcare and utilities where consumption is less discretionary. A prospective resident should budget for a monthly nut where housing consumes the largest single line item at a level fifty-three percent above the national average, while allocating slightly less than the typical American household for groceries and transportation. The services premium of 16.6 percent above baseline means health insurance premiums, electricity, and water bills will run hotter than in most comparable regions. With unemployment elevated and educational attainment modest, dual-income households gain meaningful insulation against the rental index, while single earners should stress-test their budgets at the 153.2 rental multiple before committing. The data suggests the region rewards households that can lock in housing costs and minimize exposure to the services tier where inflation persists.

What this means for residents of Northeastern Connecticut Planning Region, Connecticut
