📊 Local Market Analysis

Barton County residents navigate a cost environment where overall consumer prices sit 10.1 percent below the national baseline, registering a Regional Price Parity of 89.9 for 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest cost component at 96.5, merely 3.5 percent shy of the U.S. average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plunge to 68.6, a staggering 31.4 percent discount that drags the aggregate index down. Rental costs occupy a middle ground at 90.8, suggesting that while shelter is affordable relative to coastal metros, it consumes a larger share of local purchasing power than the deeply discounted service sector would imply. The consumption breakdown indicates daily life is structurally inexpensive for service-intensive households but offers limited relief for unavoidable goods expenditures. A median household income of $56,733 translates to a Purchasing Power Index of 63,101.2, effectively granting residents a 11.3 percent real income premium over their nominal earnings once local prices are factored in. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 15.7 percent of gross income — roughly $742 monthly against a national median of $928 — freeing significant cash flow for transportation, food, and discretionary spending. However, the goods index near parity means a trip to the grocery store or a fill-up at the pump delivers little geographic arbitrage, concentrating real savings in categories like medical visits or utility bills where consumption is less flexible. Local demographics sharpen the picture of who captures this value. An unemployment rate of 3.3 percent signals a tight labor market that likely supports wage stability, yet educational attainment remains modest at 16.3 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, capping median earnings well below the national $65,047 benchmark. The median age of 38.8 years suggests a workforce in prime earning years, but the income trajectory — up 28.9 percent since 2010 versus a 25.5 percent rent increase — shows wages have historically outpaced shelter costs, a dynamic that has improved real living standards even as the overall RPP drifted downward from 92.99 to 89.91 over the same period. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. Housing consumes a minor slice of the paycheck, and services such as childcare, insurance, and healthcare carry deep discounts, but the goods basket — food, fuel, clothing — offers scant geographic advantage. A household relocating from a high-cost area should not assume proportional savings across the ledger; the effective "raise" delivered by the 63,101 purchasing power metric materializes primarily if consumption patterns align with the cheap service economy. Conversely, families with high medical needs or heavy utility usage capture outsized value, while those with long commutes or large grocery bills find the margin of safety thinner than the headline affordability figure suggests.

What this means for residents of Barton County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63101.2, a median household income of $56,733 in Barton County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $35,799,204 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
