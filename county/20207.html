📊 Local Market Analysis

Woodson County residents operate within a cost environment that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 for 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — represent the highest cost component at 96.5, merely a 3.5 percent discount to the national baseline, while Services plummet to 68.6, a staggering 31.4 percent below average. Rental costs sit at 90.8, suggesting housing affordability is relative rather than absolute; the median monthly rent of $597 consumes only 14.9 percent of the median household income of $48,152, a burden significantly lighter than the national median rent of $928 against a national median income of $65,047. The consumption breakdown indicates that daily life is inexpensive primarily due to depressed service-sector pricing — healthcare, utilities, and dining — rather than cheap staples. A household allocating a standard share of expenditure to groceries and transportation experiences near-national price levels, meaning the "discount" is largely captured by categories with lower frequency or discretionary control. This dynamic elevates the Purchasing Power Index to $53,557, effectively granting the median earner $5,405 in additional real purchasing capacity compared to their nominal income. However, that surplus evaporates quickly if consumption patterns skew heavily toward goods; a family with high fuel and grocery needs realizes far less benefit than a retiree leveraging low-cost local services. Demographic and labor market fundamentals reinforce a low-pressure economic environment. An unemployment rate of 2.5 percent signals a tight labor market, yet educational attainment remains modest at 15.9 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and a median age of 49.9 years points to an aging workforce with limited in-migration of prime-age earners. This structure likely suppresses wage growth potential while keeping service labor costs — and thus service prices — depressed. The labor market suggests stability over dynamism; residents benefit from job security but face an income ceiling that constrains upward mobility relative to national benchmarks. Over the past decade, the RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 to 89.908, even as nominal median income surged 34.6 percent from $35,787 to $48,152, vastly outpacing a 7.0 percent rise in median rent from $558 to $597. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have decoupled from local inflation, expanding real purchasing power considerably. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: fixed housing costs are remarkably manageable, but variable goods expenses — particularly fuel for long rural commutes and grocery baskets — will track national prices closely. The financial advantage resides almost entirely in the service column; households should calibrate expectations around healthcare access and utility reliability, as the low price index may reflect limited provider density or infrastructure investment rather than pure efficiency.

What this means for residents of Woodson County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 53557.0, a median household income of $48,152 in Woodson County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $25,788,767 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
