📊 Local Market Analysis

O’Brien County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 88.8, meaning consumer prices are roughly 11 percent below the national average of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel and fuel—registers at 96.6, only 3.4 points under the national level, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 66.0, a 34‑point discount. Rental costs follow the broader pattern, with a housing index of 87.3, indicating rents are about 13 percent lower than the countrywide norm. The dominant driver of the county’s price structure is the services sector, which is the lowest‑cost pillar at 66.0. This deep discount on utilities, medical care and restaurant meals translates into a daily life that feels inexpensive, especially when contrasted with the modestly higher goods index. Even though groceries and fuel are only slightly below national levels, the overall expense profile remains favorable because the bulk of household outlays—energy bills, health expenses and discretionary dining—are substantially reduced. Consequently, residents experience a net cost advantage despite paying near‑average prices for tangible goods. Median household income in O’Brien County stands at $68,216, surpassing the national median of $65,047 by 4.9 percent. After accounting for the median monthly rent of $770, which represents just 13.5 percent of household earnings, the median family retains roughly $61,446 of annual disposable income. The Purchasing Power Index of 76,846.6 quantifies this advantage: residents can purchase nearly 23 percent more goods and services than the average American with comparable earnings. The low rent burden—well under the 30 percent threshold commonly used to flag housing stress—frees additional income for non‑housing consumption, reinforcing the county’s strong purchasing power. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of only 1.2 percent, suggesting near‑full employment and stable wage conditions. However, only 17.5 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in sectors that typically offer modest wages but also lower cost structures. The median age of 39.8 years aligns closely with the national median, implying a balanced mix of earners and dependents. This composition supports the observed low rent burden and high disposable income, as households are less likely to face the premium housing costs associated with younger, entry‑level earners in high‑growth metros. Over the past decade, the regional price‑performance (RPP) index slipped from 91.139 in 2010 to 88.769 in 2023, a 2.6 percent decline, reflecting a modest easing of consumer prices relative to the nation. In the same period, median household income rose from $55,227 to $68,216, a 23.5 percent gain, while median rent increased from $584 to $770, a 31.8 percent jump. Although rent growth outpaced income growth, the absolute rent level remains well below the national median of $928, preserving the county’s affordability cushion. Prospective residents should budget with the expectation that groceries and fuel will cost near national averages, while utilities, health care and dining will be markedly cheaper. With a median rent of $770, housing will consume roughly one‑eighth of gross earnings, leaving ample room for discretionary spending. Allocating roughly 30 percent of income to non‑housing essentials—food, transportation, health services—will likely cover most household needs comfortably, given the low services index. A prudent monthly plan would therefore prioritize the modest goods expense, leverage the inexpensive services sector, and maintain a rent‑to‑income ratio well under 15 percent to preserve the county’s historically strong purchasing power.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of O'Brien County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76846.6, a median household income of $68,216 in O'Brien County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $52,421,677 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
