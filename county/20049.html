📊 Local Market Analysis

Elk County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percent below the national average, reflected in a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 89.9, though this aggregate masks significant divergence across spending categories. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — run only 3.5 percent below the national benchmark at 96.5, making everyday consumables the primary driver of local outlays. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a steep 31.4 percent discount at 68.6, while rental costs sit 9.2 percent below average at 90.8. This composition creates a distinctive budgeting environment: households feel national-level pressure at the checkout counter but capture substantial relief on recurring service obligations, a dynamic that defines the local consumer experience more than the headline index suggests. The median household income of $56,087 trails the national median of $65,047 by 13.8 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of $62,383 reveals that local wages stretch considerably further once the 89.9 RPP is applied. Housing affordability is exceptional; median monthly rent of $534 consumes only 11.4 percent of gross income, a fraction of the 30 percent burden threshold and dramatically lower than the national median rent of $928. This shelter discount effectively subsidizes the near-parity pricing on goods, leaving discretionary capacity intact for a population where 14.4 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher and the median age sits at 49.6 years. The 3.5 percent unemployment rate signals a tight labor market, but the educational profile suggests earnings growth may remain constrained to sectors less sensitive to high-skill premiums. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.99 to 89.91, indicating that local consumer prices fell relative to the national basket even as nominal median income surged 53.8 percent from $36,458 to $56,087. Rent remained virtually flat, inching up just 0.4 percent from $532 to $534, a stagnation that contrasts sharply with national rental inflation. This divergence means real purchasing power has expanded aggressively; the median worker today commands significantly more command over local goods and services than a decade ago, with the bulk of gains derived from income growth rather than deflation. The services deflation — likely tied to healthcare and utility cost containment in a sparse, aging county — has been the silent engine of this improvement. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror national averages, as the 96.5 Goods RPP offers little relief at the pump or supermarket aisle. The 68.6 Services index implies lower monthly outlays for electricity, medical visits, and restaurant meals, but access and quality may correlate with the low index value in a county of this demographic vintage. Renters enter a market where a median lease requires roughly $534, freeing cash flow for transportation or savings, though the older housing stock may carry hidden maintenance or efficiency costs. With incomes rising faster than the local cost curve, the ledger favors the earner, provided employment aligns with the limited high-wage opportunities suggested by the educational attainment data.

What this means for residents of Elk County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 62382.7, a median household income of $56,087 in Elk County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $34,988,585 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
