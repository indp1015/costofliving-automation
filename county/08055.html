📊 Local Market Analysis

Huerfano County presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline at 101.9 on the BEA Regional Price Parity index, yet this aggregate figure masks a severe structural distortion in local consumer economics. The overall index is inflated almost entirely by a Services component registering 130.5—thirty percent above the national average—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses that disproportionately burden fixed-income households. Conversely, Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track slightly below the national norm at 99.2, while Rental Costs offer significant relief at 86.8, translating to a median monthly rent of $739 against a national median of $928. This divergence creates a bifurcated reality: securing shelter is comparatively affordable, but the recurring operational costs of daily life—keeping lights on, accessing medical care, or eating out—carry a steep premium that the headline index obscures. Purchasing power dynamics reveal a population struggling to leverage that housing discount. The median household income of $52,139 trails the national figure of $65,047 by twenty percent, yielding a Purchasing Power Index of 51,189 that signals real earnings are effectively eroded by the local price level. Although rent consumes only 17.0 percent of median income—a strikingly low burden compared to national averages—the high Services RPP of 130.5 implies that disposable income remaining after rent is rapidly absorbed by non-discretionary service costs. The labor market context sharpens this picture: an 11.1 percent unemployment rate combined with a mere 14.9 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate suggests a workforce concentrated in lower-wage service sectors, while a median age of 55.1 years indicates a demographic skew toward retirees or near-retirees likely reliant on fixed Social Security or pension income that does not adjust for local service inflation. Over the past decade, the economic trajectory has favored income growth over cost acceleration, though from a depressed base. The RPP drifted upward only modestly from 100.59 in 2010 to 101.86 in 2023, a cumulative increase of 1.3 percent, while median household income surged 64.4 percent from $31,715 to $52,139. Median rent lagged significantly behind income growth, rising just 23.8 percent from $597 to $739 over the same period. This divergence suggests that wage gains—potentially driven by tight labor markets in specific sectors or policy adjustments—have outpaced both shelter inflation and the broader cost index, improving the theoretical standard of living. However, the persistence of double-digit unemployment implies these gains are unevenly distributed, leaving a segment of the population detached from the income growth necessary to offset the 30.5 percent service premium. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. A household relocating here captures immediate savings on lease obligations, freeing roughly $189 monthly versus the national median rent, but must immediately allocate that margin toward a services basket that costs roughly $1.30 for every dollar of national average value. Healthcare access and utility reliability become critical line-item risks; a standard medical procedure or a winter heating cycle in this high-altitude county will consume a larger share of the budget than in 70 percent of U.S. counties. The low goods index softens the blow for pantry staples and transportation fuel, yet the demographic profile—older, less credentialed, with high joblessness—signals a community where service-sector employment offers limited wage mobility. Residents should model their cash flow assuming housing is a solved variable, but treat healthcare, utilities, and local services as the primary volatility drivers in the monthly ledger.

What this means for residents of Huerfano County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 51188.9, a median household income of $52,139 in Huerfano County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $26,689,381 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
