📊 Local Market Analysis

Coffee County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 96.5 against a baseline of 100. Goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register a price index of 97.7, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 88.3. Rental costs, measured by the housing component of the RPP, are 92.3, reflecting a median monthly rent of $713, well under the national median of $928. These figures collectively signal that everyday expenses for most categories are 3‑12 % cheaper than the average American household, with the most pronounced discount appearing in services. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the goods segment, which, at 97.7, is the highest of the three components. Although still below the national average, it outpaces both services and rental costs, suggesting that price advantages are strongest for health‑related and utility expenses rather than for consumables. For a typical household, the rent burden sits at 17.1 % of median income, well under the 30 % threshold often cited as a affordability stress point. After covering rent, a median earner—earning $50,175 annually or $4,181 monthly—retains roughly $3,468 for all other expenditures. When adjusted for the local price environment, the Purchasing Power Index of 51,998.1 places residents at about 52 % of the national purchasing power level, indicating that while nominal income trails the national median of $65,047, the lower cost of services and housing partially offsets the gap. Demographically, the county’s labor market features a 4.9 % unemployment rate and a modest educational attainment profile, with only 8.5 % holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 36.6 years aligns with a working‑age population, yet the limited share of highly educated workers suggests a concentration in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. This composition reinforces the modest income level and explains the relatively low rent burden; wages are sufficient to cover basic needs without excessive strain, but limited high‑skill employment may constrain upward mobility and discretionary spending. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023—a 2.0 % increase—while median household income surged 47.7 % from $33,965 to $50,175. Rent grew 30.8 % in the same period, from $545 to $713. Income growth outpaced both price inflation and rental increases, expanding real purchasing power for residents. However, the modest rise in the goods index suggests that price pressures on consumables have remained relatively subdued, preserving the affordability advantage in daily necessities. Prospective residents should allocate roughly 17 % of their earnings to rent, leaving about $41,600 annually for food, transportation, healthcare, and discretionary items. Given the sub‑national service index, budgeting for medical visits, utilities, and dining out will be less burdensome than in many other regions. Nonetheless, the higher goods index implies that grocery bills and fuel costs will consume a larger share of the remaining budget than services. A prudent household plan would therefore prioritize cost‑effective grocery shopping and vehicle fuel management while taking advantage of the lower utility and healthcare expenses that characterize Coffee County’s consumer environment.

What this means for residents of Coffee County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 51998.1, a median household income of $50,175 in Coffee County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $26,090,047 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
