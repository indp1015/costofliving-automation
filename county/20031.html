📊 Local Market Analysis

Coffey County residents operate in a cost environment roughly 10 percent below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 89.9 against a U.S. average of 100. The composite figure masks significant divergence across categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel sit at 96.5, nearly parity with the nation, while services — healthcare, utilities, and dining — plummet to 68.6, the lowest component and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs, indexed at 90.8, track closer to goods than to services, leaving daily household budgets heavily influenced by the price of tangible consumables rather than shelter or labor-intensive services. That purchasing power dynamic translates into tangible household latitude. The median household income of $67,377 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 3.6 percent, while median monthly rent of $842 runs 9.3 percent below the national $928, producing a rent burden of just 15 percent of gross income. When the RPP adjustment is applied, the purchasing power index rises to 74,939.9, indicating that the median earner commands roughly $7,500 more in real consumption capacity than the nominal income suggests. Local labor market conditions reinforce this cushion: a 6.0 percent unemployment rate sits above the national norm, yet the older median age of 44 and a modest 14.3 percent bachelor’s attainment rate point to a workforce anchored in established, often non-degree occupations that benefit disproportionately from the low service-cost structure. Over the past decade, the affordability gap has widened. The overall RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, even as nominal median income grew 17.3 percent from $57,433 to $67,377. Rent, however, surged 45.7 percent from $578 to $842, outpacing income growth by a factor of 2.6. Despite that pressure, the rent-to-income ratio remains low by national standards because the starting base was exceptionally cheap. The divergence suggests that while wage gains have broadly outstripped the general price level, the fastest-rising line item — shelter — is eroding the margin, particularly for newer entrants to the rental market. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly urban, with goods costing 96.5 percent of the national average, while expecting steep discounts on healthcare visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals at roughly two-thirds the U.S. price. The 15 percent rent burden leaves ample room for savings or debt service, but the 45.7 percent decade rent trajectory signals that lease renewals may climb faster than wages. Households reliant on service-sector consumption — childcare, professional services, dining out — capture the largest arbitrage, whereas those with high goods-intensive needs (commuting fuel, household supplies) see far less relief.

What this means for residents of Coffey County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 74939.9, a median household income of $67,377 in Coffey County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $50,492,256 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
