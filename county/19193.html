📊 Local Market Analysis

Woodbury County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 88.8, meaning the average consumer price bundle is 11.2 % cheaper than the national benchmark of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel and fuel—registers at 96.6, only 3.4 % above the national level, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly low at 66.0, a 34 % discount. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $946, translate to a rental index of 87.3, roughly 12.7 % below the national average rent of $928 when adjusted for the national cost‑of‑living baseline. Together, these figures paint a picture of a market where everyday essentials are affordable, but the price of tangible goods remains modestly elevated. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 66.0 pulls the overall composite down. Even though goods are priced near the national norm, the steep discount on services—healthcare, utilities and restaurant meals—means that routine daily life is inexpensive for residents. For example, a household spending $200 per month on utilities would effectively pay only $132 in Woodbury County terms, while a comparable grocery bill of $400 would be only slightly higher than the national average. Rental costs, while below the national figure, are higher than they were a decade ago, rising 39.5 % from $678 to $946; nevertheless, the rent‑burden metric of 16.2 % of income indicates that housing remains affordable relative to earnings. Median household income in the county stands at $70,147, 7.5 % above the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the composite cost‑of‑living index, the Purchasing Power Index (PPI) of 79,022 reflects that residents retain roughly 79 % of the purchasing power of an average American household. In practical terms, a median earner’s disposable income—after covering the average rent of $946 and a proportional share of goods and services—exceeds $45,000 annually, leaving ample room for discretionary spending. The low services index amplifies this effect, allowing households to allocate a larger share of income to savings or non‑essential consumption. Demographically, the county’s labor market is modestly robust: an unemployment rate of 4.3 % sits below the national average, while only 15.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors such as manufacturing, retail and health services that typically command lower wages but also benefit from the low service‑price environment. The median age of 36.2 years aligns with a prime‑working‑age population, reinforcing steady labor‑force participation and supporting the observed income growth. The combination of relatively low unemployment and a younger age profile contributes to a stable demand for affordable goods and services, reinforcing the county’s low overall cost of living. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parities (RPP) index slipped from 91.139 to 88.769, a 2.6 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Meanwhile, median household income surged 50.1 % from $46,720 to $70,147, outpacing the 39.5 % rise in median rent. This divergence shows that wages have more than kept pace with housing costs and have outstripped the modest inflation in goods and services, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. Prospective movers should budget for a monthly rent of roughly $946, allocate about $300–$350 for groceries and apparel (reflecting the near‑national goods price), and anticipate utility and healthcare expenses that are roughly one‑third of the national average. With a rent‑burden well under 20 % of median income, a typical household can comfortably cover essential costs and retain a sizable discretionary margin for savings or leisure.

What this means for residents of Woodbury County, Iowa
