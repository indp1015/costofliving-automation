📊 Local Market Analysis

Fulton County residents operate within a cost structure 13.2 percent below the national average, reflected in a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 86.8, yet the composition of that discount reveals sharp internal disparities. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—sit at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. baseline, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9, meaning the primary friction point for daily household budgets is not shelter or services, but the essential consumables that track closer to national pricing. That pricing dynamic translates into a Purchasing Power Index of $44,857.7, effectively granting the median household income of $38,941 the spending capacity of a significantly higher earner in a median-cost metro. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 18.4 percent of gross income—$596 monthly versus the national median of $928—leaving substantial residual income for other outlays. However, the median income itself trails the national figure of $65,047 by 40 percent, a gap rooted in local labor market characteristics where only 9.4 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree and the median age sits at 48.6 years, suggesting an economy reliant on lower-wage sectors despite a tight 3.8 percent unemployment rate. Over the past decade, the affordability calculus has shifted subtly against wage earners. The overall RPP declined 2.0 percent from 88.6 to 86.8, signaling mild deflationary pressure on the broad basket, yet median rent surged 17.3 percent from $508 to $596, outpacing the 5.1 percent growth in median income. This divergence indicates that while the service-heavy deflation helps offset goods inflation, the fastest-rising essential cost—shelter—is eroding the income gains of the last thirteen years. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is defined by a low fixed-cost floor but a variable ceiling on necessities. The negligible rent burden provides immediate cash flow relief, but the goods index near parity means grocery and fuel bills will feel familiar to anyone relocating from a major metro. The deep discount on services reduces the cost of dining out, utilities, and routine healthcare, yet the aging demographic profile and low educational attainment suggest limited upward pressure on wages, making the current purchasing power advantage heavily dependent on maintaining that low rental baseline.

What this means for residents of Fulton County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 44857.7, a median household income of $38,941 in Fulton County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $17,468,037 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
