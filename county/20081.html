📊 Local Market Analysis

Haskell County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percent below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity registering 89.9 for 2023 against a U.S. baseline of 100. The composition of that discount reveals an unusual inversion: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 96.5, only marginally cheaper than the national average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 68.6, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 90.8, meaning housing consumes a smaller share of the household ledger than in most metros, yet daily consumables offer scant relief. The median monthly rent of $776 claims just 13.5 percent of the $69,138 median household income, a burden roughly half the national median rent-to-income ratio implied by the $928 U.S. figure. Purchasing power translates that price advantage into tangible capacity. The Purchasing Power Index of 76,898.6 indicates the median household commands roughly $7,850 more real purchasing capacity than the nominal median income of $65,047 nationally, a 12.1 percent premium after adjusting for local prices. That surplus reflects a labor market where a 4.9 percent unemployment rate sits alongside modest educational attainment — only 14.3 percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher — and a median age of 38.0 years suggesting a workforce in its prime earning years but with limited upward mobility through credentialing. The service-sector discount likely stems from lower wage floors in healthcare, personal care, and hospitality, sectors that employ a disproportionate share of non-degreed workers in rural Kansas. Over the 2010 to 2023 window, the RPP declined from 92.991 to 89.908, a 3.3 percent drop in relative price levels, while median income surged 22.4 percent from $56,484 to $69,138. Median rent increased only 11.0 percent, from $699 to $776, lagging income growth by more than half. The divergence means real housing affordability improved materially over the decade, even as goods prices remained sticky near national levels. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror coastal or urban pricing — the 96.5 goods index offers no meaningful discount — while allocating significantly less for medical visits, utility deposits, and restaurant meals. The rent burden at 13.5 percent leaves unusual latitude for savings or debt service, but the goods floor means discretionary spending on tangible items erodes faster than the headline affordability numbers suggest.

What this means for residents of Haskell County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76898.6, a median household income of $69,138 in Haskell County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $53,166,154 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
