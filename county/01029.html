📊 Local Market Analysis

Cleburne County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices register at 94.6, still under the national average, while the services index—covering healthcare, utilities and dining—drops to a stark 61.6, indicating that everyday non‑housing expenses are markedly cheaper. Rental costs, measured by the housing component of the RPP, are 89.9, translating to a median monthly rent of $792, roughly 15 % lower than the national median of $928. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index is the lowest of the three components. Because services are so inexpensive, residents experience a generally affordable daily life despite goods prices that are only modestly below national levels. Housing costs, while not the cheapest category, remain competitive; the rent‑burden metric shows that renters allocate just 17.8 % of household income to rent, far under the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a stress point. Consequently, the median household in Cleburne County retains a sizable share of earnings for discretionary spending. Purchasing power, measured by the index of $59,843.8, reflects the combined effect of lower prices and modest incomes. After deducting the median rent of $792, a household earning $53,319 annually has roughly $45,735 left for all other expenses, equating to about $3,811 per month. Compared with the national median income of $65,047, Cleburne residents enjoy a higher real‑income margin relative to local costs, though absolute disposable income remains lower than the national average. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of 1.5 % and a median age of 42.3 years. The low educational attainment—only 11.1 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—suggests a concentration of jobs in sectors that do not command premium wages, reinforcing the modest median income figure. Nevertheless, the extremely low unemployment rate indicates stable employment, which helps cushion households against the modest price increases observed over the past decade. From 2010 to 2023 the RPP slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8 % decline, while median household income rose 40.1 % from $38,056 to $53,319 and median rent climbed 42.2 % from $557 to $792. Income growth has slightly outpaced the modest rise in rental costs, preserving purchasing power despite higher nominal rents. Prospective residents should budget around $800 per month for rent, allocate a larger share of income to goods (groceries, fuel, apparel) than to services, and expect utilities and healthcare to consume a fraction of national averages. A realistic monthly household budget would therefore emphasize modest spending on non‑housing services, while allowing for average‑priced groceries and transportation costs, leaving a comfortable cushion for savings or discretionary items.

What this means for residents of Cleburne County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 59843.8, a median household income of $53,319 in Cleburne County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $31,908,116 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
