📊 Local Market Analysis

The overall cost of living in Cleburne County, Alabama, stands at 89.1 on the BEA RPP scale, meaning consumer prices are roughly 11 percent below the national benchmark of 100. Housing costs track the county’s general affordability, with a rental‑cost index of 89.9, while goods—encompassing groceries, apparel and fuel—are essentially at parity at 94.6. Services, however, are markedly cheaper, registering at 61.6, the lowest component of the local price structure. This distribution shows that daily expenses are driven more by the near‑national pricing of goods than by housing or services, making routine consumption slightly more expensive than the national average while everyday services remain a cost advantage for households. Median household income of $53,319 leaves a modest rent burden of 17.8 percent, well within the conventional 30 percent threshold, and the purchasing power index of 59,843.8 reflects a strong capacity to afford local goods and services relative to income. The local labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of just 1.5 percent, yet educational attainment is limited—only 11.1 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—and the median age of 42.3 suggests a population skewed toward established households rather than young professionals. These demographic factors help explain why wages are modest; the low education level constrains higher‑paying sectors, while the low unemployment rate indicates stable, albeit lower‑skill, employment opportunities that support the observed income levels. Over the past decade, the county’s cost‑of‑living index has edged down from 89.783 in 2010 to 89.097 in 2023, a marginal –0.8 percent change. In contrast, median income has risen 40.1 percent, from $38,056 to $53,319, while median rent has increased 42.2 percent, from $557 to $792. The parallel growth of wages and housing costs suggests that residents have not yet outpaced rent inflation, but the overall RPP decline indicates that other components—particularly the low‑cost services—have offset housing price rises. The modest decline in the cost‑of‑living index underscores that, despite rent pressures, the broader expense environment remains slightly more affordable than it was a decade ago. Prospective residents should budget approximately $792 for monthly rent, which consumes less than one‑fifth of the median household income, leaving substantial discretionary income for goods and services. Because the goods index is near the national level, grocery and apparel expenses will be comparable to what families pay elsewhere, while utilities, healthcare and dining are roughly 38 percent cheaper than the average U.S. household. The low unemployment rate signals job stability, yet the limited educational attainment suggests that higher‑wage opportunities may be scarce, reinforcing the importance of aligning lifestyle expectations with the prevailing income profile. In sum, Cleburne County offers a cost structure where housing and services are affordable, goods are modestly priced, and overall purchasing power remains solid for median earners.

What this means for residents of Cleburne County, Alabama

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
