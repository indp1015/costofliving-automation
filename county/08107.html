📊 Local Market Analysis

Routt County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 101.9 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a severe structural distortion in local consumer pricing. The composite index is suppressed by a rental cost component of 86.8—thirteen points below the national average—while the services category surges to 130.5, marking the highest cost driver in the local budget. Goods, encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel, track near parity at 99.2. Consequently, daily life is expensive for residents consuming healthcare, utilities, and dining services, a dynamic that decouples the local experience from the seemingly moderate headline number; the 30.5 percent premium on services exerts disproportionate pressure on household cash flow because these categories are less substitutable than discretionary goods. Purchasing power metrics confirm that the median household income of $104,803 translates into a real purchasing power index of 102,893.3, indicating that nominal wages more than offset the elevated price level. Rent burden stands at a manageable 21.1 percent of gross income against a median monthly rent of $1,845, a stark contrast to the national median rent of $928 and national median income of $65,047. This favorable rent-to-income ratio is the primary mechanism preserving disposable income, effectively subsidizing the 30.5 percent services premium. The labor market reinforces this capacity: a 2.5 percent unemployment rate and a workforce where 33.1 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher suggest a high-skill, high-wage equilibrium that sustains the income premium necessary to absorb above-average service costs. Historical trends reveal a tightening margin over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023. The RPP climbed modestly from 100.59 to 101.856, a 1.3 percent increase, while median income surged 61.3 percent from $64,963 to $104,803 and median rent accelerated 62.3 percent from $1,137 to $1,845. Although income growth has slightly lagged rent growth on a percentage basis, the absolute dollar gap has widened significantly in favor of the household budget, expanding the cushion against the persistent services inflation embedded in the 130.5 index. The median age of 42.6 years implies a population in peak earning years, which supports the current income trajectory but introduces demographic risk should the labor market soften or the age cohort shift toward fixed-income retirement. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the services-housing trade-off. The below-average rental index (86.8) provides immediate monthly relief relative to major metropolitan areas, freeing capital for the unavoidable 30.5 percent premium on healthcare, utilities, and dining. A household relocating from a national-average cost environment should model a baseline budget where housing savings of roughly 13 percent are reallocated entirely to service categories. Groceries and fuel offer no meaningful arbitrage at 99.2. The critical variable remains labor market attachment; the 2.5 percent unemployment rate signals tight demand for the skilled labor that commands the $104,803 median, and any disruption to that income stream would immediately expose the household to the full weight of the services index without the offsetting shelter discount.

What this means for residents of Routt County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 102893.3, a median household income of $104,803 in Routt County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $107,835,265 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
