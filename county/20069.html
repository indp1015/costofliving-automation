📊 Local Market Analysis

Gray County, Kansas sits 10.1 percentage points below the national price level with an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9, positioning it among the least expensive jurisdictions in the United States for daily consumer expenditures. The composite figure masks significant divergence across categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — register at 96.5, only marginally cheaper than the national benchmark, while rental costs come in at 90.8. The dominant deflationary force is services, which include healthcare, utilities, and dining, priced at a striking 68.6, or 31.4 percent below the U.S. average. This services discount drives the overall affordability narrative; a household's recurring monthly obligations for electricity, medical visits, and restaurant meals cost substantially less than in metropolitan areas, even as the weekly grocery bill and fuel fill-up track near parity with national norms. The median household income of $77,885 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 19.7 percent, and when adjusted for the local price level, the purchasing power index rises to $86,627 — a 23.3 percent real income premium over the national baseline. Housing consumes just 12.8 percent of gross income at the median rent of $833, well below the 30 percent burden threshold and far lighter than the national median rent of $928. The labor market reinforces this advantage: unemployment stands at 2.1 percent, signaling tight demand for workers, while a median age of 36.6 and a bachelor's degree attainment rate of 20.5 percent suggest a workforce anchored in prime earning years with moderate educational specialization. These demographics support stable wage growth without the cost-push pressures seen in high-skill, high-cost metros. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined from 93.0 to 89.9, a 3.3 percent drop in relative price levels, while nominal median income climbed 27.7 percent from $61,000 to $77,885. Median rent increased 30.2 percent from $640 to $833, slightly outpacing income growth, yet the rent-to-income ratio remains low in absolute terms. The deceleration in local consumer prices relative to the national basket — driven primarily by the widening services gap — has amplified real wage gains. A prospective resident should budget for goods expenditures that mirror national averages, particularly at the supermarket and gas pump, while anticipating meaningful savings on utilities, healthcare copays, and dining out. The rent advantage persists but warrants monitoring given its faster trajectory relative to earnings; the current 12.8 percent burden leaves limited cushion if rental inflation accelerates without corresponding wage adjustment.

What this means for residents of Gray County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 86627.4, a median household income of $77,885 in Gray County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $67,469,750 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
