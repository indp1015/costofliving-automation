📊 Local Market Analysis

Pope County presents a distinct cost-of-living profile where the overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 sits marginally below the national baseline, masking a sharp bifurcation between consumption categories. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — run 1.6 percent above the national average at 101.6, marking the highest cost component in the local basket. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in at 92.4, while rental housing costs plummet to 80.4, nearly twenty points below the national figure. This divergence creates a daily expense environment where filling a cart at the supermarket or a tank at the pump extracts a slight premium, yet the recurring monthly nut for shelter and utilities remains substantially discounted relative to the broader Midwest. The rental housing metric acts as the primary anchor pulling the aggregate index down, with the median monthly rent of $438 consuming only 8.4 percent of the $62,500 median household income — a rent burden roughly half the national standard. This extreme affordability on the shelter side fuels a Purchasing Power Index of 63,289.9, effectively granting the median earner roughly $780 in additional real income compared to their nominal wage. However, the goods penalty means discretionary spending on tangible items — vehicle maintenance, clothing for a household, weekly grocery trips — erodes that advantage faster than in comparable low-rent jurisdictions. Residents effectively trade higher variable costs for lower fixed overhead, a dynamic that favors households with stable, low-mileage routines but penalizes those with long commutes or high consumption needs. Demographic and labor market fundamentals reinforce this structure. An unemployment rate of 3.0 percent signals a tight local labor market, yet educational attainment remains low at 11.0 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age of 50.8 suggests a workforce skewed toward late-career or fixed-income status. This composition likely suppresses wage pressure on the service side — keeping haircuts, repairs, and dining affordable — while the goods premium reflects the logistical costs of servicing a sparse, aging population in a rural supply chain. The labor market appears stable but not dynamic; income growth has been robust historically, rising 59.3 percent since 2010 against a national median that grew more slowly, yet the absolute median of $62,500 still trails the national $65,047. Over the past decade, the RPP has declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, indicating the county has grown relatively cheaper even as nominal incomes surged. Median rent actually fell 3.7 percent from $455 to $438 during that span, a rare deflationary trend in shelter costs that has effectively transferred purchasing power directly to tenants. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is clear: allocate roughly $5,250 annually for rent — a figure that leaves substantial room for the elevated grocery and fuel bills — but do not expect the same discount on services to persist if local wages continue tightening. The household ledger here balances on cheap walls and expensive shelves.

What this means for residents of Pope County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63289.9, a median household income of $62,500 in Pope County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $39,556,188 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
