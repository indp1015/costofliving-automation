📊 Local Market Analysis

Woodford County residents operate within a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that index reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the primary inflationary pressure at 101.6, exceeding the national average by 1.6 percent and functioning as the highest cost component in the local consumption basket. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in at a substantial 92.4, while rental housing costs sit at a deep discount of 80.4, nearly twenty points below the national figure. This divergence creates a bifurcated experience: the recurring, non-discretionary outlays for transportation and food erode paychecks faster than the national norm, even as the largest fixed expense—shelter—offers significant relief relative to the broader U.S. market. The median household income of $85,629 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 86,711.2, indicating that after adjusting for local price levels, the typical household commands roughly $21,664 more real purchasing capacity than the national median income of $65,047. Rent burden remains exceptionally low at 12.7 percent of gross income, with the median monthly rent of $907 undercutting the national median of $928 despite local incomes exceeding the national figure by over 31 percent. This dynamic suggests that while housing affordability is a structural advantage, the marginal dollar is increasingly consumed by goods priced above parity. The labor market reinforces this stability; a 3.0 percent unemployment rate signals tight conditions that support wage integrity, while a median age of 40.5 and a 23.7 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate describe a workforce anchored in prime earning years with moderate educational specialization, likely concentrated in sectors that tether wages to regional rather than metropolitan scales. Over the past decade, the trajectory has favored the consumer. The RPP declined from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent improvement in relative affordability, even as nominal median income surged 30.0 percent from $65,852 to $85,629. Median rent increased only 23.1 percent over the same span, moving from $737 to $907, meaning wage growth outpaced shelter costs by nearly seven percentage points. This decoupling of income growth from housing inflation is rare in the current economic climate and suggests that the county’s cost curve has flattened relative to national pressures, largely driven by the persistent discount in rental markets and services. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the volatility of the goods basket. A household relocating from a metro area where rents consume 30 percent of income will experience immediate cash flow liberation in Woodford County, but that surplus is partially offset at the grocery store and the pump. The 1.6 percent premium on goods compounds across weekly trips, making vehicle efficiency and food sourcing strategy material line items. Utilities and healthcare, priced at 92.4, provide a modest tailwind, but the net daily expense profile demands vigilance on variable consumption. The data indicates a high-floor, low-ceiling cost structure: the baseline for shelter is cheap, but the ceiling for daily survival costs is slightly higher than the national average, requiring disciplined management of discretionary goods spending to fully capture the income advantage.

What this means for residents of Woodford County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 86711.2, a median household income of $85,629 in Woodford County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $74,249,933 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
