📊 Local Market Analysis

Fountain County residents operate within a cost structure running 7.9 percent below the national baseline, reflected in an overall Regional Price Parity of 92.1, yet the composition of that discount reveals a market where daily consumables barely register a savings. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—carry an index of 95.6, the highest local component and only 4.4 percent cheaper than the U.S. average, meaning the weekly trip to the supermarket or the commute to work delivers minimal fiscal relief. The true affordability lever sits in services, priced at 71.3, a staggering 28.7 percent discount that depresses the aggregate index; healthcare, utilities, and dining out cost dramatically less than in metropolitan centers. Rental costs, indexed at 84.7, provide a moderate 15.3 percent shelter savings, positioning housing as the second-most impactful discount behind services but ahead of goods in shaping the household budget. The median household income of $62,526 trails the national median of $65,047 by 3.9 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 67,891.5 indicates that local earnings effectively command 8.6 percent more goods and services than their nominal value suggests. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 16.1 percent of gross income against a median monthly rent of $837, well under the national median of $928, leaving substantial residual income for the 95.6-indexed grocery runs and fuel purchases that dominate variable spending. Demographic undercurrents frame this dynamic: a median age of 42.8 and a bachelor’s attainment rate of only 8.5 percent signal a labor force weighted toward established, non-degreed occupations, while the 4.5 percent unemployment rate suggests a market tight enough to support wage floors but loose enough to cap aggressive bidding on rental stock. The labor market appears to sustain a equilibrium where service-sector wages align with deeply discounted local service prices, preventing the cost-push inflation seen in higher-skill corridors. Over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023, the RPP contracted marginally from 93.0 to 92.1, a 1.0 percent improvement in relative affordability, while nominal median income surged 33.7 percent from $46,760 to $62,526. Median rent advanced 31.0 percent from $639 to $837, nearly matching income growth, yet the rent-to-income ratio improved because the denominator expanded slightly faster. Critically, the goods index near parity (95.6) implies that the 33.7 percent income gain translated into genuine purchasing gains for durable and non-durable consumables, whereas the services deflation of the past decade amplified real wage growth for healthcare and utility outlays. The data depict a rare decade where local wage growth outpaced the primary shelter cost driver while the overall price level drifted incrementally cheaper relative to the nation. A prospective resident should budget for a grocery and fuel bill that feels nearly identical to national norms, allocating roughly 95 cents on the dollar compared to the average American household, while capturing outsized savings on insurance premiums, medical co-pays, and utility bills that collectively run at roughly 71 cents on the dollar. The sub-17 percent rent burden creates a cash-flow cushion unusual for a county with sub-$65,000 median income, permitting higher allocation to transportation or savings vehicles without triggering housing instability. However, the low educational attainment and elevated median age suggest limited upward wage mobility; the current purchasing power advantage rests heavily on static service-sector discounts rather than income escalation. Households relocating from higher-cost regions should not assume the goods discount will widen—historical RPP stability indicates the 95.6 goods index is structural, not cyclical—and should model long-term budgets around an income trajectory that historically tracks closer to rent inflation than to the broader consumer basket.

What this means for residents of Fountain County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 67891.5, a median household income of $62,526 in Fountain County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $42,449,839 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
