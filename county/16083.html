📊 Local Market Analysis

Twin Falls County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 92.2, meaning consumer prices are roughly 8 % below the national average of 100. Goods prices register at 95.9, just 4 % under the nation, while services are markedly cheaper at 86.9, a 13 % discount. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component at 68.1, translating to rents that are 31 % lower than the national median. The median monthly rent of $1,011 still exceeds the national median of $928, but the index reflects that overall housing expenses—including utilities and related services—remain substantially below average. The structure of local expenses is driven primarily by the low housing cost component. Although goods are only modestly cheaper than the nation, the pronounced discount in services and especially rentals pulls the overall cost profile down. Daily life for a typical household therefore leans toward affordability, particularly in shelter and utility bills, while grocery and apparel costs are only slightly below national levels. The rent‑burden figure of 18.6 % of income underscores this advantage: residents allocate less than one‑fifth of earnings to housing, well under the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a stress point. Disposable income calculations reveal a purchasing‑power index of 70,837.9, indicating that the median household’s real buying capacity is roughly 70 % of a baseline national figure of 100. With a median household income of $65,338, subtracting the average rent of $1,011 leaves $54,327 for all other expenses. After accounting for the service index (86.9) and goods index (95.9), the remaining funds stretch farther than in higher‑cost regions, reinforcing the modest rent burden and the relatively low service prices. The purchasing‑power index therefore reflects a net advantage: residents retain more real income for discretionary spending despite a median income that is only marginally above the national $65,047. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with unemployment at 3.2 %, well below the national rate. A relatively young median age of 35.8 years suggests a sizable working‑age population, yet only 13.9 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a labor force concentrated in sectors that may not command premium wages. This educational profile aligns with the lower service cost index, as wages in service‑intensive occupations tend to be modest, keeping consumer prices subdued. The combination of low unemployment and limited higher‑education attainment produces a stable but modest income environment, which the cost structure accommodates through cheaper housing and services. Over the past decade, the regional price‑performance (RPP) index slipped from 93.2 to 92.2, a 1 % decline, while median household income surged from $44,048 to $65,338, a 48 % increase. Rent climbed from $704 to $1,011, a 43.6 % rise, outpacing the modest 1 % dip in overall price pressure. The disparity shows that wages have outpaced most expense categories, especially housing, which remains the cheapest component despite its absolute increase. Consequently, residents have experienced a real improvement in purchasing power over the ten‑year span. Prospective residents should budget with housing as the primary line item, allocating roughly 19 % of income to rent. The remaining 81 % must cover goods (groceries, apparel, fuel) and services (healthcare, utilities, dining). Given the goods index of 95.9, a typical grocery basket will cost close to national levels, so budgeting for food and transportation should mirror national averages. Utilities and healthcare, however, are 13 % cheaper, allowing modest savings in those categories. A realistic monthly budget for a median household would therefore consist of $1,011 rent, $600–$650 for groceries and fuel, $300 for utilities and health services, and $400 for discretionary dining and personal expenses, leaving a comfortable cushion for savings or non‑essential spending.

What this means for residents of Twin Falls County, Idaho

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70837.9, a median household income of $65,338 in Twin Falls County, Idaho has the equivalent buying power of $46,284,067 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 68.1 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
