📊 Local Market Analysis

Bureau County’s cost of living sits just below the national average, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 98.8 compared with the benchmark of 100. Goods prices are marginally higher than the countrywide norm at 101.6, while services are noticeably cheaper at 92.4. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component, registering an index of 80.4—nearly 20 % below the national level. This mix produces a price environment that is modestly inexpensive for everyday expenses, even though grocery and apparel bills run slightly above average. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the goods sector, which pushes the overall index upward despite the low housing index. Daily life in Bureau County is therefore characterized by inexpensive shelter and services but a modest premium on consumables. For a household earning the median income of $65,894, the median monthly rent of $838 consumes only 15.3 % of earnings, well under the conventional rent‑burden threshold of 30 %. After subtracting rent, a median earner retains roughly $61,056 annually for food, transportation, healthcare, and discretionary spending. The Purchasing Power Index of 66,726.7 quantifies this advantage: residents can purchase about 6.7 % more with their income than the average American, reflecting the combined effect of lower housing costs and modest service prices. Demographic factors reinforce the affordability picture. The county’s unemployment rate of 5.0 % aligns closely with the national average, indicating a stable labor market, while only 14.3 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. A median age of 44.1 years points to a mature population, likely with established household budgets and reduced propensity for high‑cost consumption. The modest educational attainment and older age profile help keep demand for premium services and upscale goods subdued, which in turn sustains the lower service and housing indices. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline that signals a gradual easing of price pressures. In the same period, median household income rose from $50,423 to $65,894, a 30.7 % increase, while median rent climbed from $640 to $838, a 30.9 % rise. Income growth outpaced the modest drop in overall price parity, and the near‑parallel rise in rent and income preserves the low rent‑burden ratio. The data suggest that wages have kept pace with, and slightly exceeded, the pace of consumer price changes, preserving disposable income for most households. Prospective residents should anchor their monthly budget around the $838 median rent figure and allocate roughly 15 % of gross earnings to housing. With goods prices marginally above national levels, a realistic grocery and apparel budget will be slightly higher than the $300‑$350 range typical in lower‑cost regions. Services such as healthcare and utilities can be expected to cost about 8 % less than the national average, providing a cushion for discretionary spending. Factoring in the 5 % unemployment rate and the county’s lower educational attainment, job seekers may encounter fewer high‑skill, high‑pay opportunities, reinforcing the importance of budgeting conservatively and leveraging the county’s affordable housing and service costs to maintain a comfortable standard of living.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Bureau County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 66726.7, a median household income of $65,894 in Bureau County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $43,968,892 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
