📊 Local Market Analysis

The overall cost of living in Bartow County remains modestly below the national benchmark, with the 2023 Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 96.5 against a national baseline of 100, indicating a 3.5 percent discount for local consumers. Within this composite, goods—including groceries, apparel, and fuel—are priced at 97.7, essentially on par with the national average, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 88.3. Housing costs, measured by rental rates, sit at 92.3, also below the national level. The divergence between the relatively high goods index and the low services index shapes the county’s consumption profile, making daily expenses slightly more expensive in the grocery and apparel categories but noticeably cheaper in health and leisure services, which together keep the overall cost structure just under the national average. Median household income of $79,431 positions Bartow County well above the national median of $65,047, providing a robust income base for residents. After allocating the median monthly rent of $1,202—equivalent to $14,424 annually—rent consumes 18.2 percent of household earnings, leaving a substantial residual income for other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index of 82,317.0 reflects the relative capacity of local residents to acquire goods and services, suggesting that despite a modest cost disadvantage in goods, the higher income level offsets the expense gap. The labor market, with an unemployment rate of 4.6 percent, indicates a relatively stable employment environment, though the educational profile—only 15.4 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher—may constrain high‑skill, high‑wage opportunities, influencing the composition of consumer spending. Over the past decade, consumer costs have risen incrementally, with the RPP moving from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest 2.0 percent increase. In contrast, median household income has surged 62.5 percent, climbing from $48,893 to $79,431, while median rent has advanced 46.2 percent, from $822 to $1,202. This divergence demonstrates that wage growth has outpaced housing cost inflation, preserving household purchasing power. The relatively low unemployment rate and the steady rise in earnings suggest that residents experience a gradual improvement in real income, even as the broader cost environment edges upward. Prospective residents should budget for rent that occupies roughly one‑fifth of typical earnings, leaving ample room for discretionary spending on goods that are essentially at national price levels. Services, however, offer a cost advantage, with utilities and healthcare priced about 12 percent below the national index. When planning a household budget, the combination of higher median income and comparatively lower service costs means that daily living expenses are generally manageable, though attention to grocery and apparel categories is advisable to align with the near‑parity pricing observed locally.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Bartow County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 82317.0, a median household income of $79,431 in Bartow County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $65,385,216 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
