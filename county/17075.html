📊 Local Market Analysis

Iroquois County’s overall cost‑of‑living index of 98.8 places consumer prices slightly below the national benchmark of 100, indicating modest affordability for the average household. Within this composite, goods (groceries, apparel, fuel) register at 101.6, making them the most expensive component, while services (healthcare, utilities, dining) sit at 92.4 and rental costs at 80.4, both well under the national average. The divergence between a relatively cheap housing market and a premium goods segment shapes the local cost structure, meaning daily life is inexpensive in shelter and services but carries a higher price tag for everyday consumption. Median household income of $62,439 falls short of the national median of $65,047, yet the rent burden of 15.2 %—$793 per month—remains comfortably low, leaving substantial discretionary income. The Purchasing Power Index of 63,228.1 reflects this gap, suggesting that after covering essential housing and utilities, the typical family retains a sizable portion of earnings for non‑essential spending. The local labor market, with an unemployment rate of 4.8 % and only 11.9 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, limits high‑wage opportunities and may constrain wage growth, but the modest joblessness rate indicates stable employment for many residents. The median age of 44.4 years points to an older population that may prioritize fixed‑cost items such as healthcare and utilities, reinforcing the observed low service index. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity (RPP) declined from 101.778 to 98.752, a -3.0 % shift that signals a slight easing of overall consumer costs. In contrast, median income rose 30.5 % from $47,834 to $62,439, while median rent increased 30.0 % from $610 to $793, indicating that earnings have outpaced housing expenses. The parallel growth in income and rent suggests that wage gains have helped offset rising shelter costs, preserving purchasing power despite the higher goods index. Prospective residents should budget for a relatively inexpensive housing environment, with rent consuming roughly one‑sixth of median income, but anticipate higher out‑of‑home expenditures for groceries and fuel. Services such as healthcare and utilities are below national averages, providing additional savings. The combination of modest living costs, a stable job market, and an older demographic profile creates a financial landscape where household budgets can be managed without excessive strain, though attention to everyday consumption items is advisable to maintain balanced spending.

What this means for residents of Iroquois County, Illinois
