📊 Local Market Analysis

Macon County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the overall Relative Price Parity (RPP) index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Prices for goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 94.6, still under the national benchmark but the highest component locally. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheap at 61.6, while rental costs track close to the overall index at 89.9. The median household earns $45,951, roughly 70 % of the national median income of $65,047, yet the median rent of $709 is 24 % lower than the national median of $928, resulting in a rent‑burden of 18.5 % of income—well under the 30 % threshold that defines housing stress. The expense structure is driven primarily by goods, which lift the overall cost profile despite the deep discount on services. Daily life feels inexpensive in categories that dominate discretionary spending, such as dining out and utility bills, but the modest premium on essential goods tempers the overall affordability. With a Purchasing Power Index of 51,574.1, residents retain roughly half the buying power of an average American household when all price differentials are accounted for. After subtracting the median rent, a typical household still has about $42,000 of annual income to cover food, transportation, healthcare, and other necessities, a figure that translates into a modest discretionary margin given the lower service costs. Local demographics shape this picture. Only 11.2 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the unemployment rate stands at 10.7 %, both well above national averages. The relatively young median age of 37.8 suggests a labor force still transitioning into higher‑earning careers, which constrains household income growth. The elevated unemployment rate depresses wage pressure, limiting the ability of residents to translate lower service costs into higher disposable income. Consequently, while housing and services are affordable, limited educational attainment and job scarcity keep overall purchasing power modest. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped marginally from 89.8 to 89.1, a 0.8 % decline, indicating a slight easing of price pressures. Median household income surged 49.5 % from $30,738 to $45,951, outpacing the 19.8 % rise in median rent. This divergence suggests that wages have historically outstripped housing cost growth, enhancing real disposable income. However, the persistent unemployment gap and low educational attainment temper the benefits of higher earnings. Prospective residents should budget tightly around the $709 monthly rent, allocate a larger share of income to groceries and fuel given the 94.6 goods index, and expect substantially lower utility and healthcare bills. With a rent‑burden well below 30 % and services priced at roughly 62 % of national levels, a household can achieve a comfortable, if modest, standard of living provided it manages the higher relative cost of essential goods and navigates a labor market that offers limited high‑skill opportunities.

What this means for residents of Macon County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 51574.1, a median household income of $45,951 in Macon County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $23,698,815 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
