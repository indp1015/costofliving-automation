📊 Local Market Analysis

Owen County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percent below the national benchmark, reflected in an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest local cost component at 94.5, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. average, while Services, covering healthcare, utilities, and dining, plummet to 62.9, the lowest segment and a full 37.1 points below parity. Rental costs settle at 80.9, providing meaningful shelter savings relative to the national median rent of $928; the local median of $775 consumes just 15.6 percent of the median household income of $59,706, a burden significantly lighter than the 30-percent affordability threshold. This architecture makes daily life inexpensive in aggregate, driven primarily by deeply discounted services rather than cheap goods, meaning a household budget stretches furthest on recurring professional and utility outlays while grocery and fuel bills remain near national norms. The purchasing power index of 66,421.2 quantifies the real value of local earnings after adjusting for this price environment, effectively granting the median household roughly $6,700 in additional buying capacity compared to the nominal income gap against the national median of $65,047. Demographics reinforce this dynamic: a median age of 42.5 and a bachelor's attainment rate of only 12.8 percent suggest a workforce concentrated in trades, healthcare support, and service roles rather than high-wage knowledge sectors, while a 5.2 percent unemployment rate indicates modest labor slack. The labor market appears to price wages to local costs rather than national benchmarks, creating an equilibrium where nominal incomes lag the country but real disposable income holds ground, particularly for renters insulated from coastal housing pressures. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward a marginal 0.6 percent, from 90.433 to 89.89, signaling remarkable price stability in a decade marked by national inflation. Meanwhile, median household income surged 42.4 percent, from $41,934 to $59,706, outpacing the 37.2 percent climb in median rent from $565 to $775. Real wage growth has been substantive; the income-to-rent ratio improved, and the broad cost basket barely moved, implying that local earners have captured genuine purchasing power gains rather than merely keeping pace with shelter inflation. The divergence between goods inflation — likely tracking national supply chains — and the persistent services discount suggests structural factors, such as lower provider density and regulated utility rates, continue to suppress the service-side cost curve. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly identical to metropolitan areas, with the 94.5 goods index offering little relief at the checkout counter. The primary fiscal advantage arrives in monthly service obligations: electricity, water, internet, and routine healthcare encounters cost roughly three-fifths of the national average, freeing cash flow for savings or discretionary spending. Rent at $775 leaves roughly $4,200 per month before taxes for a median earner, a cushion that absorbs transportation and childcare more comfortably than in higher-RPP regions. However, the low educational attainment and elevated unemployment hint at limited upward mobility; households relying on a single median income should maintain larger emergency reserves given the narrower local labor market and the absence of high-wage fallback options.

What this means for residents of Owen County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 66421.2, a median household income of $59,706 in Owen County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $39,657,442 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
