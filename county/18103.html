📊 Local Market Analysis

Miami County’s cost of living sits at 92.1 on the BEA RPP scale, meaning consumer prices are roughly 8 percent below the national benchmark of 100. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel and fuel—register at 95.6, just 4.4 percent under the national average, while Services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 71.3, 28.7 percent lower than the country as a whole. Even Rental Costs come in at 84.7, 15.3 percent less than the national median. The overall index suggests that everyday expenses are modestly discounted relative to the United States, though the discount is driven primarily by services rather than housing or necessities. Among the three major categories, Goods exert the strongest influence on the local cost structure. At 95.6, they hover closest to the national baseline, indicating that households allocate a relatively large share of their budget to groceries, clothing and fuel. Services, by contrast, are the cheapest component, allowing residents to manage healthcare, utilities and dining at a fraction of the national cost. This dichotomy makes daily life in Miami County affordable in many discretionary areas but still requires careful management of essential goods, which are not substantially reduced. The median household income of $61,130 is 5.3 percent lower than the national median of $65,047, yet the rent burden remains modest at 15.6 percent of income, with a median rent of $794 versus the national $928. The Purchasing Power Index of 66,375.7 reflects the relative ability of local earners to cover living expenses, suggesting that after housing costs, the typical household retains a meaningful portion of income for other consumption. The index underscores that while earnings are below the national norm, the lower cost of services and housing preserves a degree of discretionary capacity. Local demographics reinforce the observed cost dynamics. Only 11.6 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, a figure well below the national average, which tends to limit higher‑wage employment opportunities and can suppress income growth. The median age of 41.0 years points to an older population that may prioritize stable, lower‑cost services over luxury consumption. An unemployment rate of 6.5 percent, above the typical national low, adds pressure on wage growth and can affect household budgeting, though the modest rent burden suggests that housing costs have not escalated in tandem with labor market conditions. Historical trends reveal that Miami County’s cost environment has shifted only slightly over the past decade. The RPP index fell by 1.0 percent, from 93.022 in 2010 to 92.097 in 2023, indicating a marginal decline in overall price levels. Median household income rose sharply by 35.3 percent, outpacing the 22.0 percent increase in median rent. This divergence implies that wage growth has kept pace with—or even exceeded—housing cost inflation, supporting household financial stability. For prospective residents, budgeting should emphasize the near‑national price of goods, the low cost of services, and the manageable rent of $794, which together create a cost structure that is modestly below the national average while still requiring attention to essential consumption items.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Miami County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 66375.7, a median household income of $61,130 in Miami County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $40,575,465 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
