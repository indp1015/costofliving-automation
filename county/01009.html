📊 Local Market Analysis

Blount County’s cost‑of‑living index of 89.1 places consumer prices roughly 11 % below the national average of 100, indicating a generally affordable environment for household budgets. The goods component sits at 94.6, still under the national benchmark but only 5 % cheaper, while services are markedly low at 61.6, reflecting a 38 % discount on healthcare, utilities, and dining. Rental costs, measured by the BEA RPP housing sub‑index, are 89.9, translating to rents that are about 10 % less than the national norm. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index of 61.6 pulls the overall cost down far more than the modestly lower goods index. Daily life in Blount County is therefore inexpensive in terms of recurring outlays such as medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals, while grocery and fuel bills remain only slightly below national levels. The median monthly rent of $743, 20 % lower than the national median of $928, further eases the budget pressure, especially given that rent consumes just 14.6 % of median household income—a rent‑burden well beneath the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag affordability issues. Disposable income calculations reveal a purchasing‑power index of 68,572.5, meaning that a median household can buy roughly 31 % more with its earnings than the average American family. After covering the median rent of $743, the remaining monthly income is $4,173, leaving $3,430 for all other expenses. When juxtaposed with the goods index (94.6) and services index (61.6), this residual income comfortably covers higher‑priced items such as groceries and fuel while still affording a sizable surplus for discretionary spending, underscoring the county’s strong purchasing power relative to its cost base. Demographic factors reinforce this affordability narrative. With only 10 % of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market is skewed toward lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations, yet the unemployment rate of 5.8 % remains modestly above the national average, suggesting a stable, if not booming, job environment. The median age of 40.9 years points to a mature population likely to prioritize steady income and predictable expenses, which aligns with the low rent‑burden and high disposable income. The limited educational attainment may constrain wage growth, but the current income level of $61,096 already exceeds the national median of $65,047 by a modest margin when adjusted for cost of living. Over the past decade, the regional price parity (RPP) index slipped from 89.78 in 2010 to 89.10 in 2023, a 0.8 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened slightly relative to the nation. Median household income surged 33.4 % from $45,813 to $61,096, outpacing the 23.6 % rise in median rent (from $601 to $743). This divergence shows that wages have grown faster than housing costs, enhancing real purchasing power and reinforcing the county’s affordability advantage. Prospective residents should allocate roughly 15 % of income to rent, a figure that leaves ample room for the higher‑priced goods category. A realistic monthly budget might assign $400–$450 to groceries and fuel, $300 to utilities and healthcare, and $200–$250 to dining and personal services, with the balance directed toward savings or discretionary activities. The low services index suggests that even modest spending on health and utilities will stretch further than in most states, while the near‑national goods index warns that grocery bills will not be dramatically cheaper. Understanding these allocations enables newcomers to plan a household budget that leverages the county’s low service costs and strong purchasing power while accounting for the modestly higher expense of everyday goods.

What this means for residents of Blount County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 68572.5, a median household income of $61,096 in Blount County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $41,895,055 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
