📊 Local Market Analysis

Meade County residents navigate a cost environment running 10.1 percent below the national average, reflected in a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 89.9, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — sit at 94.5, merely a 5.5 percent discount to the U.S. baseline, meaning daily consumables offer limited relief compared to broader indices. The primary deflationary force stems from Services, priced at a striking 62.9, or 37.1 percent below national levels, driven by lower healthcare, utility, and dining-out costs. Rental housing, indexed at 80.9, provides a 19.1 percent advantage, though the gap narrows considerably against the national median rent of $928; local median rent of $976 actually exceeds the U.S. figure, absorbing 16.7 percent of the $70,163 median household income. This consumption structure creates a paradox where fixed shelter costs pressure cash flow while variable service expenses remain remarkably cheap. The Purchasing Power Index of 78,054.3 illustrates the net benefit: the median earner commands roughly $13,000 more in real goods and services than their nominal income suggests, a 17.8 percent boost over the $65,047 national median income. However, that surplus relies heavily on the suppressed Services index. A household with above-average medical needs or high utility consumption captures outsized value, whereas families allocating larger shares to groceries and fuel — categories tracking near parity — experience a cost reality much closer to the national norm. Demographic undercurrents reinforce this bifurcation. An 11.8 percent bachelor’s attainment rate and a 3.7 percent unemployment rate signal a labor market anchored in trades, logistics, and the Fort Knox-adjacent defense sector rather than high-wage knowledge work. The median age of 39.3 suggests a workforce in prime earning years, yet the educational profile caps wage growth potential. Over the 2010–2023 window, nominal median income surged 40.7 percent from $49,884, outpacing a 26.4 percent rent climb from $772, while the overall RPP barely budged, slipping a marginal 0.6 percent from 90.4. Real wage growth has been substantial, but the rent-to-income ratio has tightened from roughly 18.6 percent to 16.7 percent, indicating shelter affordability has improved even as absolute rent levels crossed above the national median. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the divergence between shelter and goods. Signing a lease at or above $976 immediately aligns housing outlays with or above national benchmarks, eroding the county’s primary affordability claim. Grocery and fuel budgets should be modeled at near-national prices, offering no meaningful geographic arbitrage. The genuine savings reservoir lies in healthcare premiums, utility bills, and restaurant meals — line items where the 62.9 Services index translates to tangible monthly retention. Households relocating from high-service-cost metros capture immediate cash-flow relief on those specific categories, provided they do not offset gains by over-consuming housing square footage or accepting longer commutes that inflate the 94.5 Goods index via fuel expenditure.

What this means for residents of Meade County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 78054.3, a median household income of $70,163 in Meade County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $54,765,239 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
