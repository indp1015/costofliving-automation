📊 Local Market Analysis

Lincoln County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percentage points below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 86.8 for 2023 against a U.S. baseline of 100. This aggregate figure masks sharp divergence across consumption categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — command an index of 93.1, the highest local component and only marginally discounted from national pricing, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, a staggering 43.3-point discount that anchors the overall affordability profile. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9, translating to a median monthly outlay of $672 versus the $928 national median, a gap that directly suppresses the shelter burden to just 15.6 percent of median household income. That median income of $51,667 trails the national $65,047 by 20.6 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 59,517.3 reveals that local wages stretch 15.2 percent further than their nominal value suggests once the depressed price level is absorbed. The labor market reinforces this dynamic: a 2.3 percent unemployment rate signals tight demand for workers, though the 7.0 percent bachelor's attainment rate and median age of 40.1 years indicate an economy anchored in occupations that do not typically command premium compensation. Low educational attainment correlates with the modest income ceiling, while the tight labor market prevents wage erosion — a combination that sustains the current purchasing power equilibrium without generating rapid real income growth. Over the 2010–2023 horizon, the RPP declined modestly from 88.561 to 86.81, a 2.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while median household income surged 58.6 percent from $32,580 to $51,667. Median rent increased only 13.3 percent over the same span, from $593 to $672, meaning housing costs grew at roughly one-quarter the pace of earnings. This divergence has progressively lowered the rent-to-income ratio and expanded real disposable income, a trajectory that contrasts sharply with metropolitan areas where shelter inflation routinely outpaces wage growth. The goods category's persistence near national pricing — 93.1 versus 100 — represents the primary constraint on further purchasing power gains, as food and fuel expenditures resist the deep discounts evident in services. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and transportation costs that track within 7 percent of national averages, making bulk purchasing and fuel-efficient vehicles meaningful levers for household savings. Healthcare and utility bills, by contrast, operate at roughly half the national price level, freeing cash flow for discretionary spending or savings. The $672 median rent implies that a two-earner household at median income allocates roughly $8,064 annually to shelter — approximately $3,000 less than the national equivalent — while the low services index suggests that routine medical visits, electricity, and dining out impose minimal friction on monthly cash management. The critical variable remains goods inflation: any sustained rise in food or energy prices would erode the purchasing power advantage faster than local wage growth can offset it.

What this means for residents of Lincoln County, Arkansas

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
