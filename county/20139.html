📊 Local Market Analysis

Osage County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the closest to the benchmark at 96.5, while services are markedly cheaper at 68.6, and rental costs register at 90.8. The composite picture shows a community where everyday purchases—groceries, apparel, fuel—cost roughly 3.5 % less than the average American, whereas health care, utilities and dining out are nearly one‑third cheaper. Housing, measured by median monthly rent of $810, is 12.7 % lower than the national median rent of $928, reinforcing the affordability narrative. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index of 68.6 pulls the overall cost down. Even though goods are only modestly below national levels, the steep discount on services translates into a cheaper daily life for residents. For a household spending $200 per month on utilities, $150 on health care and $100 on restaurant meals—a typical service basket—the county’s pricing would reduce that outlay to roughly $137, $103 and $69 respectively, a combined saving of over $200 each month. Rental costs, while not the lowest in the state, remain comfortably affordable; at $810 per month the rent burden is 13.8 % of the median household income, far beneath the 30 % threshold that defines housing stress. Disposable income calculations illustrate the purchasing power advantage. The median household earns $70,216 annually, 7.9 % higher than the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median rent of $810 (9,720 % of annual income) and accounting for the lower service and goods prices, the Purchasing Power Index reaches 78,097.6—approximately 22 % above the national baseline. In practical terms, a family earning the median income can afford a broader basket of goods and services, or allocate the surplus to savings, education, or discretionary spending, without compromising basic needs. Demographic factors reinforce this affordability. With an unemployment rate of 2.5 %, the labor market is tight, suggesting stable earnings and limited wage pressure on prices. However, only 14.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill occupations that typically command modest wages. The median age of 42.8 years points to a mature population, likely with established household budgets and less reliance on high‑cost, youth‑oriented consumption. These traits combine to produce a cost environment where essential expenses remain low, but limited high‑skill employment may cap income growth for a segment of the populace. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, a 3.3 % decline, while median household income rose from $49,915 to $70,216, a 40.7 % increase. Rent climbed 29.0 % from $628 to $810. Income growth outpaced both the modest deflation in overall prices and the steeper rise in housing costs, indicating that wages have more than kept pace with expenses, enhancing real purchasing power for most residents. Prospective residents should budget around $1,200 per month for core household costs: $810 for rent, $150 for utilities, $100 for groceries, $80 for transportation fuel, and $60 for health‑care co‑pays. With a median income of $5,851 per month before taxes, the rent burden stays under 14 %, and the remaining $4,041 can cover discretionary spending, debt service, and savings. The low service‑price index means dining out or using local health services will stretch the budget further than in higher‑cost regions. Awareness of the modest rent increase relative to income growth helps set realistic expectations for long‑term affordability in Osage County.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Osage County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 78097.6, a median household income of $70,216 in Osage County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $54,837,011 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
