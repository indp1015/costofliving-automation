📊 Local Market Analysis

Hancock County’s cost of living sits well below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 94.5, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 62.9. Housing costs, measured by rental‑price parity, are 80.9, indicating that a typical apartment costs roughly 19 % less than the national norm. These figures translate into a daily life that feels inexpensive for most categories, especially health care, utilities, and dining, which together drive the services index down to less than two‑thirds of the national level. The consumption structure is dominated by goods, which account for the highest relative price index. Although groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced only about 5 % below the national average, they still represent the primary cost driver because services are substantially cheaper. Rent, at a median of $752 per month, is 19 % lower than the national median of $928, and the rent‑burden ratio of 13.5 % of household income underscores the affordability of housing. After covering rent, a median household earning $66,836 retains roughly $61,336 annually for other expenses, yielding a Purchasing Power Index of 74,353.1. This index, expressed in 2023 dollars, shows that residents can purchase about 12 % more goods and services than the average American household with comparable income, reflecting the combined effect of lower prices and modest rent pressure. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of 3.9 %, well under the national average. However, only 10.9 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 41.5 years indicates a mature population, likely with established earning patterns and stable consumption habits. The low educational attainment may limit upward wage mobility, yet the current unemployment figure points to sufficient job availability, which helps maintain household income levels despite modest skill premiums. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 90.433 to 89.89, a 0.6 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened slightly relative to the nation. Median household income surged 32.4 % from $50,476 to $66,836, outpacing the 39.5 % rise in median rent, which grew from $539 to $752. Although rent increased faster than income, the absolute rent level remains low, and the rent‑burden ratio stayed comfortably under 30 %. Consequently, wages have generally kept pace with, and in many respects exceeded, the modest inflation in local costs, preserving disposable income for residents. Prospective residents should budget with the understanding that housing will consume a relatively small slice of income—approximately 13.5 %—leaving ample room for groceries and transportation, which are only slightly below national price levels. Allocating roughly 30 % of take‑home pay to goods (food, clothing, fuel) and another 20 % to services (healthcare, utilities, dining) aligns with the county’s price structure and preserves the purchasing power advantage reflected in the 74,353.1 index. Careful tracking of utility bills and healthcare expenses will be especially rewarding, given that these services are priced at less than two‑thirds of the national average, allowing households to stretch their discretionary income further than in most parts of the country.

What this means for residents of Hancock County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 74353.1, a median household income of $66,836 in Hancock County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $49,694,638 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
