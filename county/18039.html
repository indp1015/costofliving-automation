📊 Local Market Analysis

Elkhart County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 92.1, meaning consumer prices are roughly 8 percent below the national average of 100. Goods prices—groceries, apparel and fuel—register an index of 95.6, only 4.4 percent under national levels, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 71.3, a 28.7 percent discount. Rental costs, measured by the BEA Regional Price Parities, are 84.7, indicating rents are 15.3 percent lower than the nation as a whole. The median household income of $65,617 in Elkhart County barely exceeds the national median of $65,047, but the lower rent of $1,023 per month (national median $928) and modest service costs combine to produce a purchasing‑power index of 71,247.7, well under the national benchmark of 100. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the service sector, whose index of 71.3 pulls the overall figure down despite goods being relatively close to national pricing. Daily life therefore feels inexpensive, especially for utilities, medical care and restaurant meals, which together offset the near‑national cost of groceries and fuel. Rental costs, while still below the national average, have risen faster than income—rent climbed 42.3 percent since 2010 versus a 37.0 percent rise in median earnings—yet the rent‑burden metric remains modest at 18.7 percent of household income, well under the 30 percent threshold that defines affordability stress. A median earner in Elkhart County retains roughly $54,000 of annual income after subtracting the average annual rent expense of $12,276, leaving about $41,724 for all other consumption. When adjusted for the region’s lower service prices, this translates into a real purchasing‑power advantage of roughly 12 percent relative to a comparable national household, as reflected in the Purchasing Power Index of 71,247.7. The labor market, with a 3.4 percent unemployment rate, supports steady earnings, while the low educational attainment—only 12.8 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—suggests a concentration of jobs in manufacturing and trade, sectors that typically offer wages aligned with regional price levels. The median age of 35.5 indicates a relatively young workforce, likely to prioritize rent, transportation and family‑related expenses over luxury services. Over the past decade the RPP slipped from 93.022 to 92.097, a 1.0 percent decline, signaling that consumer prices have softened relative to the national basket. Income, however, surged 37.0 percent from $47,913 to $65,617, outpacing the modest 1.0 percent dip in the price index, thereby expanding real disposable income. Rent, by contrast, accelerated 42.3 percent from $719 to $1,023, slightly outstripping wage growth and eroding part of the income gain, though the overall rent‑burden remains low. Prospective residents should allocate roughly 15 percent of gross earnings to rent, a figure comfortably below national stress levels, and anticipate service expenses that are substantially cheaper than elsewhere. Budgeting for groceries and fuel should follow national norms, while utilities, healthcare and dining out will stretch the dollar further. A prudent household plan would earmark about 30 percent of after‑rent income for combined goods and services, leaving a sizable margin for savings or discretionary spending, given the region’s favorable service‑price environment and modest rent pressure.

What this means for residents of Elkhart County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71247.7, a median household income of $65,617 in Elkhart County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $46,750,603 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
