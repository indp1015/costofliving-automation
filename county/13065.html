📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Clinch County, Georgia, sits modestly below the national benchmark, with the overall Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 96.5 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 97.7, while services are markedly cheaper at 88.3, and housing—measured by median monthly rent—registers at 92.3. Compared with the national median rent of $928, the county’s $505 average rent reflects a 45.6% discount, and the rent burden of 10.9% of household income underscores a relatively light housing cost pressure for residents. Goods dominate the local cost structure, but the advantage in services offsets much of that pressure. The 12.4‑point gap between goods (97.7) and services (88.3) indicates that everyday expenditures such as healthcare, utilities, and dining out are substantially cheaper than the national average. Consequently, while grocery bills and fuel costs are near‑national, the lower price of services makes daily life comparatively affordable. The median household income of $55,549—15.2% below the national median of $65,047—combined with the low rent burden yields a Purchasing Power Index of 57,567.3, suggesting that a typical household retains roughly 57.6% of its income for discretionary spending after covering basic housing costs. In dollar terms, after paying $505 in rent, a median family still has about $46,000 annually for other expenses, a figure that translates to roughly $3,800 per month for food, transportation, health, and entertainment. Demographically, the county’s labor market is characterized by a 3.4% unemployment rate, well under the national average, and a relatively young median age of 38.8 years. However, only 9.1% of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. This educational profile helps explain the modest income level and reinforces the importance of the low service‑price environment for maintaining household solvency. The labor market’s strength, reflected in low unemployment, mitigates income volatility but does not offset the limited earning potential associated with the education gap. Over the past decade, the RPP index rose from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest 2.0% increase, while median household income surged 131.3% from $24,015 to $55,549. Rental costs grew 21.1% in the same period, from $417 to $505. Income growth has far outpaced price inflation, expanding real purchasing power and allowing residents to allocate a larger share of earnings to non‑housing consumption. Nevertheless, the slower rise in service prices keeps discretionary spending relatively inexpensive. Prospective residents should budget around $505 for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 per month for utilities and healthcare, and expect grocery and fuel expenses near national averages—approximately $400–$450 combined. With a rent burden of only 10.9%, the remaining 89.1% of income can be distributed across transportation, personal care, and entertainment, yielding a comfortable margin for savings or occasional leisure. Understanding that services are the cheapest component while goods remain near‑national levels enables a realistic household plan that leverages the county’s low housing and service costs to offset higher grocery and fuel bills.

What this means for residents of Clinch County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57567.3, a median household income of $55,549 in Clinch County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $31,978,059 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
