📊 Local Market Analysis

Sierra County’s cost of living is markedly above the national benchmark, with an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index of 112.2—12.2 percent higher than the national average of 100. This elevation is driven by disproportionate expenses in services (157.8) and housing (147.3), while goods (106.8) remain only modestly elevated. The disparity underscores that everyday consumption in the county is more expensive than the typical American household experience, primarily because services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining command a premium relative to the nation. Among the three consumption categories, services dominate the local cost structure, registering the highest index at 157.8—roughly 50 percent above the national baseline. Housing follows closely at 147.3, indicating that rental costs are also a significant burden, whereas goods sit near parity at 106.8. Consequently, daily life in Sierra County is expensive not because of grocery or apparel prices, but because routine service expenditures—utilities, medical care, and restaurant meals—consume a larger share of household budgets. The median monthly rent of $1,181, while higher than the national median of $928, represents 23.6 percent of the median household income of $60,000, suggesting that housing alone is not the primary cost driver; rather, the broader service sector inflates overall living expenses. Purchasing power analysis reveals a mixed picture. The Purchasing Power Index of 53,478.3, when contextualized against the national median income of $65,047, indicates that the median Sierra County household retains less real purchasing ability despite a nominal income of $60,000. After allocating $1,181 for rent, roughly $48,819 remains for other expenses. Adjusting for the county’s 112.2 cost index, the effective disposable income is reduced by about 12.2 percent, leaving roughly $42,800 in real terms for goods, services, and discretionary spending. This margin is constrained by the high cost of services, which already exceed the national average by nearly 58 percent. Local demographic and labor market conditions shape this cost environment. An unemployment rate of 12.2 percent is notably higher than the national average, limiting wage growth potential and reinforcing a labor market where high‑skill, higher‑paying jobs are scarce—reflected in only 14.9 percent of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 56 suggests a population inclined toward fixed incomes and lower mobility, which can suppress demand for certain services but also increase reliance on healthcare and utilities, both of which are priced above the national norm. The combination of a relatively older, less educated workforce and elevated unemployment pressures keep wages from rising as rapidly as service costs. Trend analysis over the past decade shows that incomes have outpaced cost increases. Median household income rose 40.1 percent, from $42,833 in 2010 to $60,000 in 2023, while the overall RPP index climbed only 2.3 percent, from 109.703 to 112.195. Median rent increased 25.9 percent, from $938 to $1,181, which is substantial but still below the income growth trajectory. This divergence suggests that, historically, wages have kept pace with—or even exceeded—rising living costs, though the disproportionate rise in service prices continues to strain household budgets. Prospective residents should budget for a cost structure where services dominate expenses. A typical household should allocate roughly one‑quarter of income to rent, but anticipate that utility, healthcare, and dining bills will be 50‑60 percent higher than national averages. While groceries remain near the national level, the overall expense profile demands careful planning to preserve discretionary income. Understanding the local labor market’s constraints and the demographic tilt toward older, less‑educated residents helps explain why service prices remain elevated and why income growth, though positive, may not fully offset the premium paid for daily living in Sierra County.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Sierra County, California

With a Purchasing Power Index of 53478.3, a median household income of $60,000 in Sierra County, California has the equivalent buying power of $32,086,980 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 147.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
