📊 Local Market Analysis

Nez Perce County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 92.2, meaning the average consumer price is 7.8 % below the national benchmark of 100. Goods are the most expensive component at 95.9, only 4.1 % under the national level, while services register at 86.9, a full 13.1 % discount. Housing, measured by rental costs, is markedly cheap at 68.1, translating to a 31.9 % reduction versus the nation’s average. The disparity between categories shows that everyday purchases such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are only modestly cheaper than elsewhere, whereas utilities, healthcare and dining out are substantially less costly, and rent is dramatically lower. Consequently, routine household spending on non‑housing items feels only slightly cheaper, while the dominant savings stem from the housing market, which cushions overall expenses. The median household earns $71,466, outpacing the national median of $65,047 by 10 %. After deducting the median monthly rent of $936—just 1.0 % above the national median rent of $928—the rent burden falls to 15.7 % of income, well under the conventional 30 % affordability threshold. The Purchasing Power Index of 77,481.7 indicates that, after adjusting for the county’s lower price levels, a resident’s effective buying power is roughly 9.5 % higher than the national average. In practical terms, a median earner retains enough disposable income to cover essential goods and services with a comfortable margin, especially given the pronounced housing discount. The modest rent premium relative to the national average is more than offset by the deep housing index gap, leaving a net surplus for discretionary spending. Demographically, the county’s unemployment rate of 2.9 % signals a tight labor market, supporting wage growth that has propelled median income up 48.4 % since 2010. Yet only 18.1 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting that much of the income gains arise from sectors not requiring advanced credentials, such as manufacturing, agriculture, or service industries. The median age of 40.9 years points to a mature workforce, likely with established household budgets and lower propensity for high‑cost consumption. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity slipped from 93.2 to 92.2 (a 1 % decline), while median rent rose 41 % and income rose 48 %, indicating that wages have outpaced housing cost inflation. The modest rise in the overall cost‑of‑living index, coupled with stronger income growth, confirms that residents have experienced real gains in purchasing power. Prospective residents should allocate roughly $936 per month for rent, representing a modest 15.7 % of the median household income. With goods priced only slightly below national levels, budgeting for groceries, clothing and fuel should follow national averages, while utilities, healthcare and restaurant meals can be expected to cost roughly 13 % less. The low housing index provides a sizable buffer, allowing households to direct a larger share of income toward savings, education, or leisure. A realistic monthly budget might therefore consist of $300‑$350 for groceries, $150 for transportation, $200 for utilities and healthcare combined, and $150‑$200 for dining out, leaving a discretionary pool of $1,000‑$1,200 after essential expenses. This structure underscores that while daily living costs are modest, the primary advantage for Nez Perce County dwellers lies in the affordability of rent, which amplifies overall purchasing power and supports a resilient household budget.

What this means for residents of Nez Perce County, Idaho

With a Purchasing Power Index of 77481.7, a median household income of $71,466 in Nez Perce County, Idaho has the equivalent buying power of $55,373,072 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 68.1 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
