📊 Local Market Analysis

Macoupin County’s cost‑of‑living index sits just below the national benchmark, with an overall RPP of 98.8 versus 100 nationally, indicating that a typical basket of goods and services costs roughly 1.2 % less than the U.S. average. The goods component is the outlier, scoring 101.6, which means groceries, apparel and fuel are marginally pricier than the countrywide norm. Services—including healthcare, utilities and dining—are 7.6 % cheaper (92.4), while housing, measured by median rental costs, is the most affordable segment at 80.4, a full 19.6 % discount relative to the national rental price level. The structure of local expenses is driven primarily by the goods sector. Because goods exceed the national index while services and housing fall below it, everyday life in Macoupin County feels mixed: essential consumables such as food and gasoline impose a modest premium, whereas routine expenditures on utilities, medical care and restaurant meals are noticeably lower. The dominant housing advantage further softens the overall cost picture; median rent of $789 is 15 % below the national median of $928, and rent burden accounts for only 13.8 % of median household income, well under the 30 % threshold that typically signals affordability stress. Consequently, while residents may notice higher grocery bills, the savings on rent and services more than offset that pressure, rendering daily life comparatively inexpensive. Disposable income calculations reinforce the affordability narrative. Median household earnings in the county stand at $68,518, 5.3 % above the national median of $65,047. After deducting the median monthly rent of $789 (annualized to $9,468), the median family retains $59,050 of pre‑tax earnings, translating to a post‑rent income share of 86.2 %—well above the rent‑burdened benchmark. The Purchasing Power Index of 69,383.9 quantifies this advantage: residents can acquire roughly 30 % more goods and services than the average American household with comparable earnings, a direct result of the low housing cost and cheaper services. Demographic and labor‑market characteristics shape this cost environment. With only 13.2 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the local economy leans toward lower‑skill occupations, which historically command modest wages. Yet the unemployment rate of 4.4 % aligns closely with the national rate, suggesting a relatively stable labor market. The median age of 44.3 years points to a mature population, often associated with higher home‑ownership rates and lower consumption of youth‑oriented discretionary goods, further dampening demand‑driven price pressures. The combination of steady employment and a sizable proportion of retirees or near‑retirees contributes to the modest goods price premium while preserving the low service and housing costs. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline that signals a gradual easing of price pressures. In the same period, median household income surged from $51,206 to $68,518, a 33.8 % rise, outpacing the 27.7 % increase in median rent (from $618 to $789). The divergent trajectories indicate that wages have not only kept pace with, but have exceeded, the growth in housing costs, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. The modest decline in the overall cost index, coupled with robust income growth, underscores an improving affordability landscape for households. Prospective residents should anchor budgeting on three concrete figures: a monthly rent of $789, a goods expense slightly above the national average (approximately 1–2 % higher), and service costs roughly 8 % below national levels. Allocating around 13–14 % of income to rent leaves roughly 86 % for all other expenditures, a ratio that comfortably exceeds typical affordability thresholds. Planning for a modest premium on groceries and fuel while capitalizing on cheaper utilities and healthcare will maximize disposable income. The data suggest that a household earning the county median can comfortably meet essential needs, enjoy a lower overall cost burden than most U.S. locales, and retain a meaningful cushion for discretionary spending.

What this means for residents of Macoupin County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69383.9, a median household income of $68,518 in Macoupin County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $47,540,461 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
