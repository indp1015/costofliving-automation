📊 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 86.8, meaning consumer prices are roughly 13 % lower than the national average of 100. Goods prices are the closest to the benchmark at 93.1, while services are markedly cheaper at 56.7, and rental costs lag the nation at 81.9. The disparity between categories signals that everyday necessities such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are only modestly discounted, whereas healthcare, utilities, and dining out are less than two‑thirds of typical U.S. costs. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 56.7 pulls the overall figure down. Even though goods are priced near national levels, the low cost of services offsets higher grocery bills, making day‑to‑day living comparatively inexpensive. Housing, measured by median monthly rent of $662, is 28.6 % below the national median rent of $928, reinforcing the affordability narrative. However, the rent‑burden metric—19.3 % of median household income—suggests that housing costs remain manageable for most residents, well under the conventional 30 % affordability threshold. Disposable income can be gauged through the Purchasing Power Index (PPI) of 47,477.2. When the median household earns $41,215, the adjusted purchasing power after accounting for the local price environment translates to roughly $47.5 k in national‑price dollars. In practical terms, a median earner retains about $6,300 more purchasing power than a comparable household in an average‑price county. After deducting the median rent of $662 (≈$7,944 annually), the remaining income of $33,271 still exceeds the national median after housing costs, underscoring a relatively comfortable margin for discretionary spending. Demographically, the county’s labor market is constrained: an unemployment rate of 9.7 % exceeds the national average, while only 8.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 40.4 years indicates a mature workforce, likely skewed toward lower‑skill occupations that align with the modest income level. High unemployment depresses wage growth, yet the low cost of services and housing cushions the impact on household budgets. The limited educational attainment may also restrict access to higher‑paying sectors, reinforcing reliance on the cheaper service‑oriented consumption mix. Over the past decade, the regional price parity (RPP) index slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median household income rose 31.8 % from $31,261 to $41,215. Rental rates increased 22.4 % from $541 to $662. Income growth outpaced both price and rent inflation, expanding real purchasing power and confirming that wages have kept pace with, and indeed surpassed, cost pressures. The modest downward shift in RPP further amplifies the net gain in affordability for residents. Prospective residents should budget around $662 for rent, allocate a larger share of their budget to groceries and fuel given the 93.1 goods index, and anticipate low utility and healthcare expenses consistent with the 56.7 services index. With a median income of $41,215, a household can expect to spend roughly 19 % of earnings on rent, leaving ample capacity for food, transportation, and modest discretionary items. The combination of low service costs and affordable housing makes Jackson County a viable option for households prioritizing budget‑friendly daily expenses, provided they are comfortable with a labor market that exhibits higher unemployment and limited advanced‑education opportunities.

What this means for residents of Jackson County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 47477.2, a median household income of $41,215 in Jackson County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $19,567,728 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
