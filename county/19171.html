📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Tama County sits well below the national benchmark, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 88.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are only modestly higher than the national average at 96.6, while services are markedly cheaper at 66.0, and rental costs trail at 87.3. This composite picture means that everyday expenses—groceries, fuel, apparel, health care, utilities, and dining—are collectively 11.2 % less expensive than in a typical American market, though the relative cheapness of services pulls the overall index down further. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, which registers the lowest index at 66.0, indicating that health care, utilities, and restaurant meals cost roughly one‑third less than elsewhere. Goods remain near parity with the nation, suggesting that a resident’s grocery bill will not differ dramatically from the average American household. Rental costs, while below the national median rent of $928, are higher than a decade ago (rising from $672 to $865, a 28.7 % increase). Because rent consumes only 15.3 % of median household income, the burden is relatively light; a typical family can afford housing without sacrificing a large share of earnings. Consequently, daily life in Tama County is inexpensive in service‑related categories but neutral in goods, producing an overall affordable environment for most households. Median household income in the county stands at $67,955, surpassing the national median of $65,047 by 4.5 %. After accounting for the median monthly rent of $865, a typical household retains roughly $61,000 of annual income for discretionary spending, utilities, food, and transportation. The Purchasing Power Index of 76,552.6 quantifies this advantage: residents can purchase about 15 % more with their earnings than the average American consumer. The low rent‑to‑income ratio and the substantial service‑price discount together amplify disposable income, allowing households to allocate a larger share of earnings to savings or non‑essential consumption. Local demographics reinforce the affordability narrative. With a bachelor’s‑degree attainment rate of 13.7 %—well below the national average—the labor market is anchored in sectors that typically offer modest wages but also lower price pressures, especially in services. The unemployment rate of 4.0 % aligns closely with the national rate, indicating a stable job environment. A median age of 41.8 years suggests a mature workforce, likely with established spending patterns and limited reliance on high‑cost amenities, further dampening demand for premium services. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 91.139 to 88.769, a 2.6 % decline, while median income rose 23.1 % from $55,203 to $67,955. Rental costs, however, outpaced income growth, climbing 28.7 % from $672 to $865. The divergence implies that while overall price pressures eased, housing affordability tightened modestly. Nonetheless, the net effect remains positive: rising wages more than offset the modest increase in living costs, preserving purchasing power for most residents. Prospective residents should budget with the service discount in mind, allocating a smaller share of income to health care, utilities, and dining out than they might expect elsewhere. Housing costs, though rising, remain manageable at 15.3 % of income, leaving ample room for groceries (aligned with national prices) and transportation. A realistic monthly budget might allocate roughly $300 to utilities, $400 to food, $200 to health‑care expenses, and $865 to rent, leaving over $1,200 for discretionary spending or savings. Understanding these allocations helps new households gauge the true cost of everyday life in Tama County and plan accordingly.

What this means for residents of Tama County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76552.6, a median household income of $67,955 in Tama County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $52,021,319 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
