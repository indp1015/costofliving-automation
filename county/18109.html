📊 Local Market Analysis

Morgan County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national benchmark at an index of 92.1, indicating that consumer prices are roughly 8 percent cheaper than the average U.S. household faces. Within this composite score, the goods component registers at 95.6, services at 71.3, and rental costs at 84.7, each measured against a national baseline of 100. The goods index is the highest of the three, reflecting relatively higher prices for groceries, apparel, and fuel, while services are the most affordable category, driven by lower costs for healthcare, utilities, and dining out. Housing costs, though still below the national average, are closer to parity, suggesting that rent burdens are not the primary driver of overall affordability. The consumption breakdown shows that daily life in Morgan County is shaped more by the expense of goods than by services or shelter. Because the goods index exceeds 90, households allocate a larger share of their budgets to food, clothing, and transportation compared with the national average, whereas the services index of 71.3 indicates that healthcare, utilities, and restaurant meals are comparatively inexpensive. Rental costs at 84.7 are also below the national level, but the median monthly rent of $988 consumes 15 percent of the median household income of $79,088, a manageable rent burden that does not disproportionately strain budgets. The combination of higher goods prices and lower service costs creates a mixed affordability picture: residents pay more for everyday consumables but benefit from cheaper personal services. Local demographics reinforce the observed cost structure. An unemployment rate of 3.8 percent signals a relatively tight labor market, while a bachelor’s degree attainment of 13.9 percent suggests a workforce skewed toward occupations that may not command premium wages, potentially limiting discretionary spending power. The median age of 41.6 years points to a mature population that typically prioritizes essential consumption over luxury items, aligning with the observed emphasis on goods and the lower demand for high‑priced services. The purchasing power index of 85,874.7 reflects the adjusted income after local price differentials, indicating that the median household retains a substantial portion of its earnings for non‑essential purchases despite the higher goods costs. Trend analysis reveals that Morgan County’s cost environment has remained relatively stable while incomes have risen sharply. The regional price parity (RPP) declined by just 1.0 percent from 93.022 in 2010 to 92.097 in 2023, whereas median household income jumped 42.7 percent from $55,432 to $79,088 over the same period. What this means for residents of Morgan County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 85874.7, a median household income of $79,088 in Morgan County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $67,916,583 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
