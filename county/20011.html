📊 Local Market Analysis

Bourbon County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 96.5, nearly converging with national pricing and leaving minimal margin for savings on daily necessities. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a steep 31.4 percent discount to the national benchmark at 68.6, while rental costs sit modestly below average at 90.8. This configuration creates a bifurcated reality: recurring service obligations are remarkably affordable, but the unavoidable weekly expenditure on tangible goods absorbs a disproportionate share of disposable income relative to the overall index suggestion. The median household income of $67,555 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 3.9 percent, and when adjusted for the local price level, purchasing power swells to an effective $75,138, a 15.5 percent premium over nominal earnings. Rent burden stands at a notably low 13.9 percent, with median monthly rent of $784 running 15.5 percent below the national median of $928, freeing significant cash flow for other categories. However, the local labor market context tempers this advantage; an unemployment rate of 3.8 percent signals a tight market, yet educational attainment remains low with only 16.6 percent of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting wage growth may be concentrated in sectors that do not fully leverage the purchasing power arbitrage. The median age of 38.8 indicates a workforce in its prime earning years, but the skills gap implied by the education data may cap upward mobility for the median earner. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.99 to 89.91, indicating that local consumer prices have fallen relative to the national basket even as nominal costs rose. Median income surged 76.5 percent from $38,280 to $67,555, vastly outpacing the 33.8 percent increase in median rent from $586 to $784. This divergence confirms that wage acceleration has substantially outstripped shelter inflation, a rare dynamic that has progressively improved the real standard of living for renters. The declining RPP trajectory suggests the county’s cost advantage has structural roots—likely tied to the low services index—rather than cyclical weakness, reinforcing the durability of the purchasing power gains observed in the current data. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly identical to metropolitan areas, as the 96.5 goods index offers little relief at the checkout counter. The primary financial leverage lies in housing and services: a rental budget of roughly $784 secures shelter at a fraction of the national cost, while healthcare premiums, utility deposits, and dining out carry meaningful discounts. The critical variable remains income trajectory; with local degree attainment lagging, the median earner’s ability to maintain the current 15.5 percent real income premium depends on whether the tight labor market continues to bid up wages for non-degreed roles faster than the goods index reverts toward parity.

What this means for residents of Bourbon County, Kansas

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
