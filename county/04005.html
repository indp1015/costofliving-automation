📊 Local Market Analysis

Coconino County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 100.7 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a severe structural distortion in the local consumer basket. Services dominate the inflationary pressure at an index of 108.6, pushing healthcare, utilities, and dining costs roughly 8.6 percent above the U.S. average, while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run slightly cheaper at 97.9. The most striking deviation appears in rental costs, which carry an index of only 83.3—16.7 percent below the national level—creating a rare environment where shelter acts as a deflationary anchor rather than a budgetary strain. This composition fundamentally reshapes daily household economics. Because housing consumes a smaller share of the wallet, the median rent of $1,406 absorbs just 24.2 percent of the $69,748 median household income, a burden significantly lighter than the national median rent of $928 would imply against local earnings. However, the savings on shelter are largely offset by the service premium; residents effectively trade lower rent for higher recurring costs in electricity, medical care, and restaurant meals. The resulting Purchasing Power Index of 69,234.3 indicates that after adjusting for local prices, the median household retains real purchasing power roughly equivalent to its nominal income, suggesting the service surcharge and goods discount roughly neutralize each other for the typical earner. Demographic and labor dynamics reinforce this tension. An unemployment rate of 6.6 percent sits well above the national norm, signaling slack in the labor market that likely suppresses wage growth for the 76.6 percent of adults without a bachelor’s degree, while the relatively youthful median age of 32.4 suggests a workforce heavy on early-career service and tourism-sector employment. Over the 2010–2023 period, nominal median income rose 38.8 percent to $69,748, yet median rent climbed 40.2 percent to $1,406, narrowly outpacing earnings growth. Meanwhile, the overall RPP barely budged, inching from 100.558 to 100.742, implying that the county’s affordability squeeze stems less from broad inflation and more from the specific trajectory of rental markets relative to local paychecks. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the service-heavy cost structure. A household moving from a high-rent metro area will experience immediate relief on lease payments but should anticipate monthly utility and healthcare outlays running roughly 9 percent above what they previously paid. Grocery bills offer modest relief at 2 percent below average, yet the 6.6 percent unemployment rate warrants a larger emergency fund to buffer against income volatility. The data suggests that while the rent-to-income ratio appears favorable on paper, the elevated service floor raises the baseline cost of simply maintaining a household, compressing discretionary spending for anyone earning near the local median.

What this means for residents of Coconino County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69234.3, a median household income of $69,748 in Coconino County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $48,289,540 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
