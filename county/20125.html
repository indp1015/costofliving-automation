📊 Local Market Analysis

Montgomery County’s cost of living index of 89.9 in 2023 indicates that consumer prices are roughly 10% below the national benchmark of 100, making the locale comparatively inexpensive for overall expenses. The breakdown shows that goods—encompassing groceries, apparel and fuel—drive the local cost structure at 96.5, just above the national average, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 68.6. Housing costs, measured by rental rates, sit at 90.8, also below the national norm, suggesting that shelter expenses are modest relative to the broader market. The disparity between relatively higher goods costs and low service costs creates a mixed affordability picture: everyday items like food and clothing are slightly pricier, but routine services and utilities provide a cushion for household budgets. Median household income of $53,242 yields a rent burden of 17.1%, with median monthly rent at $759. After allocating rent, the typical household retains roughly $44,200 in discretionary income, and the purchasing power index of 59,218.3 reflects strong consumer capacity relative to the local price environment. The index, when contextualized against the 89.9 cost‑of‑living score, suggests that residents can stretch their earnings further than the average American, especially in categories where the county scores well below 100. This surplus is not merely a function of low housing costs; it also stems from the inexpensive service component, which reduces routine expenditures. Local demographics shape this affordability dynamic. An unemployment rate of 3.9% signals a tight labor market, yet only 14.4% of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, which may limit higher‑wage opportunities and influence wage distribution. The median age of 40.3 years points to a mature population that typically carries established household budgets, often prioritizing stable, lower‑cost living arrangements. These factors combine to keep wage pressures modest while maintaining a cost structure that remains attractive to middle‑income families. Over the past decade, consumer costs have trended downward, with the RPP slipping from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, a 3.3% decline. In contrast, median income has risen sharply, climbing 29.6% from $41,083 to $53,242, while median rent has increased at a slower pace of 21.6% from $624 to $759. The data show that earnings growth has outpaced housing cost inflation, reinforcing the county’s relative affordability. Residents therefore benefit from a widening gap between income and essential expenses, a trend that supports sustainable household budgeting. Prospective residents should budget approximately $759 for housing, anticipate goods costs that run slightly above the national average, and expect utilities, healthcare and dining to be notably cheaper. The rent‑to‑income ratio of 17.1% remains well within comfortable thresholds, and the overall cost‑of‑living index suggests that discretionary spending can be allocated to non‑essential categories without excessive strain. Understanding these dynamics enables households to plan for stable, predictable monthly outflows while preserving a margin for savings or irregular expenses.
