📊 Local Market Analysis

Livingston County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark at an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 98.8, meaning the average consumer price is roughly 1.2 % cheaper than the U.S. average of 100. The local price structure is uneven: goods such as groceries, apparel and fuel register at 101.6, edging above the national level, while services—including healthcare, utilities and dining—are markedly cheaper at 92.4. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component at 80.4, well under the national index. The disparity shows that daily life in the county is modestly inexpensive overall, but the higher cost of goods offsets the savings from low rents and utilities, shaping a consumption pattern where households allocate a larger share of their budget to food and personal items. Median household income of $70,796 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 8.9 %, providing a solid earnings base. After accounting for the median monthly rent of $855, annual housing expenses total $10,260, which represents 14.5 % of median income—a relatively low rent burden. The Purchasing Power Index of 71,690.7 reflects the county’s strong net buying capacity relative to price levels, indicating that the typical household retains sufficient discretionary funds after essential costs. The local labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of 3.6 %, suggesting stable job availability, yet only 12.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, which may limit high‑skill wage growth. The median age of 41.9 points to an older population that tends to prioritize stable, lower‑turnover employment and may favor fixed‑cost consumption patterns, reinforcing the observed low rent burden and modest service expenditures. What this means for residents of Livingston County, Illinois
