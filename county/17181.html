📊 Local Market Analysis

Union County residents operate within a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet the aggregate figure masks a pronounced bifurcation in household expenditure patterns. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—run 1.6 percent above the national index at 101.6, making daily consumables the most expensive relative component of the local budget. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a 7.6 percent discount at 92.4, while rental housing costs represent the deepest value proposition at 80.4, nearly twenty points below the national average. This structure indicates that daily life is cheap for those anchored by a lease or mortgage but grows progressively more expensive at the checkout counter and the gas pump, a dynamic that disproportionately affects households without fixed housing costs. The median household income of $56,420 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 57,133, effectively granting the median earner roughly $713 in additional real purchasing capacity compared to the nominal wage after adjusting for local prices. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 14.0 percent of gross income—median rent of $658 versus the national $928—freeing substantial cash flow for other categories. However, the labor market context complicates this picture: a 5.2 percent unemployment rate exceeds the national norm, and only 14.1 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower-wage sectors. The median age of 44.3 years further implies a population with elevated healthcare utilization, a category where the 92.4 services index provides modest relief but cannot fully offset the goods premium for an older demographic. Over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023, the RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, signaling a gradual improvement in relative affordability. Nominal median income surged 32.4 percent from $42,606 to $56,420, yet median rent accelerated slightly faster at 34.6 percent, climbing from $489 to $658. While wages have outpaced the broad cost basket, the narrowing gap between income growth and shelter cost inflation suggests the county’s historic housing affordability advantage is eroding at the margins. The goods index remaining stubbornly above 100 throughout this period indicates that transportation and food supply chains have not benefited from the same deflationary pressures affecting services and rents. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel expenses that track at or slightly above metropolitan averages, allocating roughly $350–$400 monthly per adult for food and transportation to maintain parity with national consumption patterns. Utility and healthcare line items will likely run 5–8 percent below national benchmarks, offering a buffer of perhaps $100–$150 monthly versus a comparable household in a median-cost metro. The critical variable remains housing security: at $658 median rent, a two-earner household at median income retains over 85 percent of gross pay after shelter, but any disruption to that rental arrangement—whether through displacement or quality deterioration—would force entry into a market where the rent-to-income calculus deteriorates rapidly.

What this means for residents of Union County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57133.0, a median household income of $56,420 in Union County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $32,234,439 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
