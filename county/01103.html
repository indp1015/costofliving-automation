📊 Local Market Analysis

Morgan County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 89.1, meaning consumer prices are roughly 11 % below the national average of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel and fuel—registers at 94.6, still modestly cheaper than the nation but the closest to parity. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities and dining, are markedly low at 61.6, indicating that everyday non‑housing expenses are about 38 % cheaper than elsewhere in the United States. Rental costs, measured by the BEA’s housing index, stand at 89.9, only 10 % under the national benchmark, while the median monthly rent of $835 is 10 % lower than the national median of $928. The cost structure is driven primarily by the services sector, whose index is the lowest of the three categories. Because services are so inexpensive, daily life in Morgan County feels relatively affordable despite a goods index that is nearer to national levels. Housing costs, while still below average, are the second‑largest driver; the rent‑burden figure of 15.4 % of household income confirms that most residents spend a modest share of earnings on shelter. By contrast, the goods index suggests that grocery bills and fuel purchases will be only slightly cheaper than in higher‑cost metros, limiting the overall savings potential from the low services cost. Disposable income can be gauged through the Purchasing Power Index of 72,795, which translates to roughly $73 k of purchasing power for the median household. After subtracting the median rent of $835 (about $10,020 annually), the median household retains roughly $54,800 for all other expenses. Compared with the national median income of $65,047, Morgan County’s median household income of $64,858 is essentially on par, yet the lower cost environment yields a higher real‑income buffer. The 15.4 % rent burden is well under the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag housing stress, reinforcing the view that residents have considerable leeway for discretionary spending. Demographically, the county’s unemployment rate of 4.0 % aligns closely with the national average, supporting a stable labor market. However, only 15.3 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 40.3 years points to a mature population, likely with established household budgets and less reliance on high‑cost childcare or education services. These factors combine to keep demand for high‑priced services muted, reinforcing the low services index. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity (RPP) slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a modest 0.8 % decline, indicating that price growth has been slower than the national pace. Median household income surged 41.8 % from $45,751 to $64,858, outpacing the 39.4 % rise in median rent ($599 to $835). The near‑parity of income and rent growth suggests that wages have kept slightly ahead of housing costs, preserving affordability. Goods prices have risen in line with national trends, limiting the upside of the low services cost. Prospective residents should allocate a larger share of their budget to goods—particularly groceries and fuel—than to services or rent. With a median rent of $835, a household earning the county median can comfortably afford housing while retaining roughly $4,600 per month for food, transportation, healthcare and discretionary items. The low services index means utilities and medical expenses will be substantially cheaper than in most states, but the modest goods index implies that price savings will not be as pronounced in those categories. Budget planners should therefore prioritize grocery and fuel budgeting, anticipate modest utility bills, and expect a rent burden well below national stress levels, creating a generally favorable cost‑of‑living environment for median earners in Morgan County.

What this means for residents of Morgan County, Alabama

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
