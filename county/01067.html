📊 Local Market Analysis

Henry County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 89.1, meaning the average consumer price level is roughly 11 % below the national benchmark of 100. Goods prices—groceries, apparel and fuel—register at 94.6, only 5 % under the national average, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 61.6, a 38 % discount. Rental costs, measured by the median monthly rent of $770, translate to an index of 89.9, also about 10 % lower than the national median rent of $928. The combined effect is a price environment that is modestly inexpensive, driven chiefly by the deep discount in services rather than by a uniformly low price structure across all categories. The dominant cost driver in Henry County is housing, which, despite being below national levels, still consumes a larger share of household budgets than services. Rent accounts for 15.4 % of median household income ($60,135), leaving roughly $50,000 of annual earnings for other expenses. After subtracting the average rent payment, the median resident retains about $44,000 for goods, services, taxes and savings. The Purchasing Power Index of 67,493.9 quantifies this residual buying capacity: residents can purchase roughly 67 % of the goods and services that a typical American household can afford with the national median income. The low services index amplifies disposable income, allowing households to stretch their earnings further on everyday necessities and discretionary spending. Demographically, the county’s labor market is anchored by a relatively low unemployment rate of 3.3 % and a median age of 45, suggesting a mature, stable workforce. However, only 14.2 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a concentration in lower‑skill occupations that typically command modest wages. This educational profile aligns with the modest median income, which trails the national median by $4,900 (7 %). The age structure also implies higher healthcare and retirement expenditures, yet the services index remains low, tempering those costs for residents. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8 % decline, while median household income surged 43.2 % from $41,997 to $60,135. Rental rates rose 29.4 % in the same period, from $595 to $770. Income growth outpaced both price inflation and rent increases, expanding real purchasing power and reinforcing the county’s affordability advantage. Prospective residents should budget around $770 per month for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 for utilities and healthcare combined, and expect grocery and fuel costs to be only slightly below national averages. With services markedly cheaper, discretionary spending on dining and entertainment can be proportionally higher than in many other regions, provided the household maintains the median income level.

What this means for residents of Henry County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 67493.9, a median household income of $60,135 in Henry County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $40,587,457 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
