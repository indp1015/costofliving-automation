📊 Local Market Analysis

Pratt County’s cost of living sits comfortably below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 96.5, still modestly under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 68.6. Housing costs, measured by the rental‑cost sub‑index, register at 90.8, indicating that rents are roughly 9 % lower than the countrywide norm. The median monthly rent of $810 compares with the national median of $928, reinforcing the picture of a relatively affordable rental market. The consumption profile is dominated by goods, which drive the overall index upward relative to services and housing. Daily necessities such as groceries, apparel, and fuel cost about 3.5 % less than the national average, yet they constitute the highest share of the local price structure. Services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are the cheapest segment, falling 31.4 % below the national level, which cushions household budgets for discretionary spending. Rental costs, while not the cheapest, remain well under national rates, contributing to a low rent‑burden figure of 15.1 % of median household income. Consequently, everyday life in Pratt County is inexpensive in terms of services and housing, but modestly higher in goods, producing a balanced cost environment that favors residents who spend a larger share of income on tangible consumer items. Disposable income calculations reveal that the median household, earning $64,348 annually, retains a purchasing power index of 71,570.9. After allocating $9,720 per year to rent (15.1 % of income), roughly $54,628 remains for all other expenses. When adjusted for the local price level—89.9 % of the national cost base—this residual income translates to an effective purchasing power comparable to $61,500 in a typical U.S. market, confirming that median earners can afford a standard basket of goods and services with a modest surplus. The purchasing power index, therefore, signals that residents enjoy a real‑income advantage despite the modestly higher goods index, because the savings in services and housing more than offset the slight premium on consumables. Demographically, Pratt County’s labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of 3.7 %—well below the national average—suggesting stable job prospects and limited wage pressure. However, only 17 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in sectors that typically offer modest wages. The median age of 38.2 years points to a mature, working‑age population, which aligns with the observed median income of $64,348, just 1 % below the national median of $65,047. The combination of low unemployment and a relatively low educational attainment level implies that while jobs are plentiful, they may not command premium salaries, reinforcing the importance of the county’s lower service and housing costs in preserving household purchasing power. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 92.991 to 89.908, a 3.3 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. In the same period, median household income rose from $49,550 to $64,348, a 29.9 % increase, outpacing the modest rise in the rental market, which climbed 27.0 % from $638 to $810. The divergence between income growth and price inflation suggests that wages have more than kept pace with living‑cost pressures, enhancing real disposable income for residents. This trend underscores the county’s capacity to sustain a higher standard of living without proportionate cost escalations. Prospective residents should budget around $810 per month for rent, allocate roughly $1,200 annually for utilities and healthcare (reflecting the low services index), and plan for groceries and fuel expenditures that hover near the national average, translating to about $400–$450 per month. With rent consuming only 15 % of median earnings, the remaining budget can comfortably cover transportation, modest discretionary spending, and savings. Emphasizing the cheap services sector and the modest goods premium will help newcomers calibrate expectations and maintain a balanced household budget in Pratt County.

What this means for residents of Pratt County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71570.9, a median household income of $64,348 in Pratt County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $46,054,443 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
