📊 Local Market Analysis

Jasper County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark at an index of 98.8, indicating that the average resident faces slightly lower overall expenses than the typical American household. Goods, however, register at 101.6, meaning groceries, apparel and fuel are about 1.6 percent more expensive than the national average, while services (healthcare, utilities, dining) are markedly cheaper at 92.4 and housing (rental costs) is the most affordable component at 80.4. The divergence between the high‑cost goods category and the low‑cost housing category shapes the county’s consumption profile, making daily life modestly expensive in the grocery and fuel segment but overall inexpensive when shelter is considered. The consumption breakdown shows that goods drive the local cost structure, as the 101.6 index is the only category above parity with the nation. Residents therefore allocate a larger share of their budget to food and clothing than to housing, which is reflected in the rent burden of 12.9 percent of median household income. With a median rent of $766 per month, the annual shelter cost amounts to roughly $9,200, leaving the median household earning $71,094 with about $62,000 of discretionary income before other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 71,992.5 underscores that, after accounting for local price levels, the median earner retains a purchasing capacity that is roughly 28 percent above the national median income of $65,047, suggesting a relatively strong ability to afford non‑housing consumption. Local demographics reinforce the observed cost dynamics. Only 14.0 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, which tends to limit high‑wage employment opportunities and may keep wage growth moderate despite the 31.1 percent increase in median income over the past decade. The median age of 44.2 years points to a mature population that typically has lower housing demand and reduced consumer spending on education‑related goods. Meanwhile, an unemployment rate of 3.7 percent signals a stable labor market, though the modest educational attainment suggests that wage gains are driven more by overall employment growth than by skill‑premium effects. Trend analysis reveals divergent movements in income and costs. The Regional Price Parity (RPP) fell from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3.0 percent decline that indicates a modest improvement in affordability. Median household income rose 31.1 percent from $54,209 to $71,094, outpacing the 42.6 percent increase in median rent from $537 to $766. Although rent grew faster than wages, the overall cost of living index fell, suggesting that price adjustments in other categories, particularly services, offset housing cost pressures. The net effect is a gradual erosion of cost‑of‑living pressure despite higher rental expenses. For a prospective resident, budgeting should emphasize the relatively low housing cost as a primary advantage, while recognizing that goods expenses are slightly above the national average. Allocating roughly $800–$900 per month for rent leaves room for a grocery budget that is about 1.5–2 percent higher than the typical household’s food spend. Utility and dining costs, being below the national index, can be expected to be modest. Overall, the combination of affordable shelter, stable employment and a cost‑of‑living index just under 100 suggests that daily household expenses are manageable, provided that the higher price of goods is factored into weekly and monthly planning.

What this means for residents of Jasper County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71992.5, a median household income of $71,094 in Jasper County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $51,182,348 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
