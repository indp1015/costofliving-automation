📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Orange County, California, remains markedly above the national benchmark, with the 2023 BEA Regional Price Parity (RPP) index sitting at 112.2—12.2 percent higher than the national average of 100. Within this composite, services dominate at an index of 157.8, followed closely by housing (rental costs) at 147.3, while goods such as groceries, apparel and fuel sit modestly higher than the national baseline at 106.8. The divergence between services and goods underscores a consumption pattern where everyday expenses are driven more by health care, utilities and dining than by basic merchandise. Services are the principal driver of the local cost structure, accounting for roughly one‑third of total household outlays despite housing’s comparable index. The 157.8 figure reflects the premium placed on health care, electricity, water and restaurant meals, all of which outpace the national average by more than half a point. In contrast, goods remain only slightly elevated, suggesting that a resident’s grocery bill or clothing purchase is not the primary pressure point. The median household income of $113,702 yields a rent burden of 24.8 percent—$2,352 per month—leaving roughly $85,000 of annual discretionary income before other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 101,343.2 indicates that, after accounting for local price levels, the median earner retains a substantial surplus relative to the national median income of $65,047. Local demographics reinforce the observed cost dynamics. A 27.3 percent share of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher cultivates a high‑skill labor market that supports elevated wages, yet also fuels demand for premium services. The median age of 39.1 years suggests a population in peak earning and consumption years, willing to allocate more of their budget to health care and dining. Meanwhile, an unemployment rate of 5.3 percent is modestly above the national norm, implying a labor market that is not overly tight but still competitive for higher‑skill positions. Over the past decade, consumer costs have risen slowly, with the RPP climbing from 109.703 in 2010 to 112.195 in 2023—a 2.3 percent increase. Median household income, however, has surged 48.6 percent to $113,702, outpacing the overall cost‑of‑living index. Rental costs have risen even faster, jumping 51.9 percent to $2,352 monthly, narrowing the gap between wage growth and housing expenses. The divergence suggests that while overall living costs remain stable, housing affordability has eroded relative to earnings. Prospective residents should budget for a services‑heavy expense profile, allocating roughly one‑quarter of income to rent and anticipating that health care, utilities and dining will consume a larger share of discretionary funds than in most other regions. Goods‑related spending can be managed within a modest 6.8 percent premium over national levels. By recognizing that the local economy supports higher wages but also commands premium service prices, households can plan a sustainable budget that reflects the true cost structure of daily life in Orange County.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Orange County, California

With a Purchasing Power Index of 101343.2, a median household income of $113,702 in Orange County, California has the equivalent buying power of $115,229,245 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 147.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
