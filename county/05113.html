📊 Local Market Analysis

Polk County, Arkansas presents a cost of living profile that sits 13.2 percent below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 for 2023. This aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in household expenditure patterns: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel carry an index of 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. average, while services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—plummet to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9, meaning a median monthly rent of $699 consumes just 17.0 percent of the median household income of $49,252, a burden significantly lighter than the national median rent of $928 against a national median income of $65,047. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is structurally inexpensive primarily because the service economy is deeply discounted, likely reflecting lower local labor costs for healthcare practitioners, personal care, and hospitality staff. However, the goods index near parity suggests residents capture minimal savings on tradable essentials like food and fuel, which are priced largely by national supply chains. Consequently, a household moving from a metropolitan area would experience immediate relief on rent and service-intensive bills but would find the grocery bill and gas pump stubbornly familiar. The Purchasing Power Index of 56,735.4 quantifies this dynamic, indicating the median earner commands roughly $7,500 more in real goods and services than their nominal income suggests, a 15.2 percent boost over the raw dollar figure. Local demographics sharpen the context: an unemployment rate of 8.4 percent sits well above national norms, and only 11.8 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, signaling a labor market anchored in lower-wage sectors that suppresses service pricing but caps income growth. The median age of 44.4 years implies a population skewing older, potentially increasing per-capita healthcare consumption precisely where the 56.7 services index offers the deepest discount. This alignment between demographic demand and local price structure mitigates the impact of lower nominal wages, though the elevated jobless rate suggests slack in the labor market that limits upward pressure on earnings. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative price levels, while median income surged 46.1 percent from $33,702 to $49,252 and median rent rose 44.4 percent from $484 to $699. Income growth has marginally outpaced rent inflation, improving the rent-to-income ratio over the decade, yet the near-lockstep movement of wages and housing costs indicates that real gains remain fragile. For a prospective resident, the budgeting takeaway is clear: allocate capital toward goods and transportation at near-national rates, but expect substantial relief on housing, utilities, and healthcare services; the primary financial risk lies not in runaway living costs but in an employment landscape where wage growth depends on a tightening labor market that has yet to materialize.

What this means for residents of Polk County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 56735.4, a median household income of $49,252 in Polk County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $27,943,319 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
