📊 Local Market Analysis

Pottawatomie County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest cost component at 96.5, nearly converging with national pricing and leaving little margin for savings on daily necessities. Conversely, the Services category, covering healthcare, utilities, and dining, plunges to 68.6, the lowest index value in the profile and the primary driver of the county’s overall affordability advantage. Rental costs sit at 90.8, moderately below the national benchmark but notably higher than the services index, creating a bifurcated environment where fixed shelter expenses consume a larger share of the budget than variable service consumption. This consumption breakdown dictates that daily life is inexpensive for service-intensive households but tight for those with high goods dependency. The median household income of $87,694 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 34.8 percent, translating into a Purchasing Power Index of 97,537.5 that signals significant real wage strength. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 13.8 percent of income against a median monthly rent of $1,011, compared to the national median of $928, confirming that shelter costs are highly manageable relative to local earnings. However, because goods prices track close to national levels, the effective disposable income gain is partially offset at the checkout counter; a dollar earned here buys substantially more healthcare or restaurant meals but roughly the same amount of gasoline or groceries as it would in a median U.S. county. Demographic and labor market fundamentals reinforce this purchasing power dynamic. An unemployment rate of 1.9 percent indicates an exceptionally tight labor market that likely sustains wage premiums, while a median age of 36.2 years suggests a workforce in prime earning years. The 23.3 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate, though below national averages, aligns with an economy anchored in sectors that reward specialized labor without requiring dense concentrations of advanced degrees. These factors combine to support the 45.6 percent nominal income growth recorded between 2010 and 2023, which outpaced the 38.5 percent rise in median rent and the 3.3 percent decline in the overall RPP. Real income growth has been robust, expanding the buffer against goods-price parity. Over the decade, the widening gap between income trajectory and cost trajectory has improved the resident balance sheet considerably. The RPP decline from 92.991 to 89.908 signals that local inflation has run cooler than the national rate, effectively boosting real wages beyond the nominal 45.6 percent gain. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between categories: housing and services offer deep discounts relative to earnings, while goods demand near-national outlays. A household relocating from a high-cost metro area would likely experience immediate relief on rent and healthcare premiums but should not assume proportional savings on weekly grocery trips or vehicle fuel. The data suggests the optimal household profile for this county is one that consumes heavily from the local services basket—utilities, local healthcare, childcare—while minimizing exposure to nationally priced goods.

What this means for residents of Pottawatomie County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 97537.5, a median household income of $87,694 in Pottawatomie County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $85,534,535 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
