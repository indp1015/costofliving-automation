📊 Local Market Analysis

Dallas County, Alabama’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 94.6, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 61.6. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $749, are 19.3 % lower than the national median rent of $928, reinforcing the county’s affordability profile. The expense structure is driven primarily by goods, which account for the highest index score, but even that figure remains modest relative to the national average. Services—healthcare, utilities, and dining—are the dominant source of savings, falling more than a third below the national level. Rental costs, while higher than the 2010 level (up 32.8 % over the decade), remain modest in absolute terms and represent 24.4 % of median household income. After rent, a typical household retains roughly $31,060 of its $36,810 median income, leaving a sizable margin for discretionary spending, though the Purchasing Power Index of 41,314.5 signals that the dollar stretches less than half as far as in the average U.S. market. Demographic factors temper the affordability picture. The unemployment rate of 10.9 % exceeds the national average, limiting earnings potential for a sizable share of residents. Only 11.2 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor pool concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑pay occupations. The median age of 40.3 years indicates a mature population, likely with established household budgets but also potentially higher healthcare needs—an area where the low services index may mask limited access or quality. Together, these traits create a scenario where low prices coexist with constrained income growth, reducing the net disposable income available for non‑essential goods and services. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped marginally from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8 % decline, while median household income rose 34.8 % from $27,306 to $36,810. Rent increased 32.8 % in the same period, keeping pace with income growth and preserving the 24‑percent rent‑burden ratio. The near‑parallel rise in wages and housing costs indicates that, despite modest price inflation, residents have not experienced a real increase in purchasing power; the Purchasing Power Index remains well below the national figure. Prospective residents should budget for a rent payment that consumes roughly a quarter of household earnings, allocate a larger share of expenses to goods than to services, and anticipate that limited local employment prospects may constrain income growth. A prudent household plan would prioritize essential goods, leverage the low cost of services, and maintain a cushion for the relatively high unemployment risk inherent in the county’s labor market.

What this means for residents of Dallas County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 41314.5, a median household income of $36,810 in Dallas County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $15,207,867 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
