📊 Local Market Analysis

Buena Vista County presents a cost-of-living profile significantly below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 88.8 in 2023, meaning local consumer prices run roughly 11.2 percent cheaper than the U.S. average. This aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in the consumption basket: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel sit at 96.6, only marginally discounted from national levels, while services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—plummet to an index of 66.0, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs, indexed at 87.3, track closer to the overall average but remain substantially cheaper than the national median rent of $928, settling locally at $835 per month. The service sector’s deep discount drives the county’s affordability narrative, effectively subsidizing daily life for residents despite near-parity pricing on tangible goods. A median household income of $66,564 translates into a purchasing power index of 74,985.6, indicating that after adjusting for local price levels, the typical household commands roughly $8,400 more in real consumption capacity than their nominal earnings suggest. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 15.1 percent of gross income, a figure that underscores the dominance of housing affordability in the local budget calculus; for context, the national median rent consumes a significantly larger share of the national median income of $65,047. Demographic and labor market characteristics reinforce this low-cost structure. An unemployment rate of 5.0 percent sits above the national norm, signaling labor market slack that likely suppresses wage growth and service-sector pricing power. Educational attainment remains modest, with only 15.1 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, correlating with an occupational mix weighted toward production and agriculture rather than high-wage knowledge work. The median age of 35.7 years suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the income distribution reflects the region’s industrial composition rather than metropolitan-scale compensation. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8, even as nominal median income surged 38.1 percent from $48,195 to $66,564. Median rent increased 40.8 percent over the same span, slightly outpacing income growth, yet the starting base was low enough to keep absolute shelter costs manageable. The widening gap between income appreciation and the modest dip in overall price levels has expanded real purchasing power considerably. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is clear: discretionary spending on goods offers little arbitrage opportunity compared to national prices, but the steep discount on services—particularly healthcare and utilities—combined with minimal rent pressure creates a household budget environment where fixed costs consume a unusually small slice of take-home pay.

What this means for residents of Buena Vista County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 74985.6, a median household income of $66,564 in Buena Vista County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $49,913,415 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
