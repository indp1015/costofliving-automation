📊 Local Market Analysis

Kodiak Island Borough residents face a cost of living 3.3 percent above the national baseline, driven almost entirely by a rental market that sits 13.3 percent higher than the U.S. average, while services such as healthcare and utilities run 3.3 percent below the benchmark at 96.7. Goods, encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel, track slightly above parity at 103.7, creating a bifurcated expense profile where shelter costs dominate household budgets. The median monthly rent of $1,669 consumes 23.9 percent of the $83,716 median household income, a burden that appears manageable on paper but masks the erosion of discretionary spending power when compared to the national median rent of $928 against a national median income of $65,047. The rental category is the singular engine of local inflation, pushing the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) to 103.3 despite the moderating influence of cheaper services. Daily life is expensive specifically because housing absorbs a disproportionate share of earnings; the Purchasing Power Index of 81,044 indicates that the median dollar buys roughly 3.2 percent less in Kodiak than the national average after adjusting for local prices. This gap means a household earning the local median effectively operates on a real income significantly lower than the nominal figure suggests, constraining capacity for savings, debt repayment, or non-essential consumption even with a rent-to-income ratio below the standard 30 percent stress threshold. Local demographics sharpen this pressure. An unemployment rate of 4.8 percent sits above the national norm, signaling a labor market with slack that may suppress wage growth, while only 20.4 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, limiting access to the high-wage remote or professional roles that could offset the housing premium. The median age of 35.4 years suggests a population in prime earning and family-formation years, precisely the cohort most sensitive to rental costs and childcare expenses embedded in the services index. The labor market structure—likely anchored by seasonal fishing, government, and tourism—creates income volatility that compounds fixed high shelter costs. Over the past decade, the RPP has remained remarkably stable, edging up just 0.2 percent from 103.1 to 103.3, yet the lived experience has deteriorated sharply. Median income rose 18.1 percent, from $70,887 to $83,716, but median rent surged 70.1 percent, climbing from $981 to $1,669. Wages have not kept pace with the primary cost driver; rent growth outpaced income growth by a factor of nearly four. A prospective resident must budget for a housing floor roughly 80 percent above the national median, while grocery and fuel bills will run only marginally higher. The critical budgeting consideration is not the overall index, but the rigidity of the rental market: with services cheap but shelter dear, fixed costs are high and non-negotiable, leaving limited room for financial shock absorption in an economy prone to seasonal fluctuation.

What this means for residents of Kodiak Island Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 81044.0, a median household income of $83,716 in Kodiak Island Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $67,846,795 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
