📊 Local Market Analysis

Kodiak Island Borough’s cost of living sits modestly above the national norm, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) indexed at 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—track closely at 103.7, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining register a modest discount at 96.7. Housing, measured by median monthly rent, is the outlier: a rent index of 113.3 reflects a $1,669 average rent, more than 80 % higher than the national median of $928. The rental market dominates the local price structure. Although everyday commodities are only 3–4 % pricier than the countrywide average, the steep 70 % jump in median rent since 2010 inflates the overall cost burden. Rent consumes 23.9 % of median household income, leaving a larger share of earnings for discretionary spending than in many high‑cost metros where rent can exceed 30 % of income. Nonetheless, the elevated housing expense pushes the borough’s cost profile into the “expensive” tier, driven primarily by the island’s limited housing stock and logistical constraints on construction. Disposable income calculations underscore a nuanced picture. Median household income in Kodiak stands at $83,716, 28.7 % above the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median rent of $1,669, a typical household retains roughly $75,000 annually for other expenses, taxes, and savings. The Purchasing Power Index of 81,044 quantifies this residual buying strength: residents can purchase about 19 % fewer goods and services than the average American with the same nominal income, reflecting the combined drag of higher housing costs and the modest discount on services. Demographic factors shape these dynamics. With 20.4 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the borough’s educational attainment lags the national average, limiting the pool of high‑skill, high‑wage occupations. The median age of 35.4 suggests a relatively young workforce, which aligns with the 4.8 % unemployment rate—slightly below the national rate and indicative of a stable labor market anchored by fisheries, tourism, and government employment. The youthful, moderately educated labor force sustains median earnings growth but does not fully offset the rapid escalation in housing costs. Over the past decade, the RPP rose a mere 0.2 % (from 103.099 to 103.297), signaling price stability for most consumer categories. In contrast, median household income surged 18.1 % (from $70,887 to $83,716), outpacing the modest price increase. However, median rent exploded 70.1 % (from $981 to $1,669), dwarfing both income and overall price growth. The divergence indicates that while wages have improved, they have not kept pace with the specific surge in housing expenses, compressing discretionary budgets. Prospective residents should anchor their budgeting around the high rent component. Allocating roughly one‑quarter of gross income to housing leaves about 75 % for utilities, groceries, transportation, and healthcare, which are priced near or slightly above national levels. A realistic monthly budget might allocate $300–$350 to utilities and internet (reflecting the 3 % service premium), $500–$600 to groceries and apparel (aligned with the 3.7 % goods index), and $200–$250 to discretionary dining and health services (benefiting from the 3.3 % service discount). Understanding that the primary cost pressure stems from housing, rather than everyday consumables, enables households to plan cash flow with confidence, preserving savings potential despite the island’s elevated rent environment.

What this means for residents of Kodiak Island Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 81044.0, a median household income of $83,716 in Kodiak Island Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $67,846,795 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
