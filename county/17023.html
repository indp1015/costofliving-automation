📊 Local Market Analysis

Clark County’s cost of living sits just below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 98.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are modestly higher than average at 101.6, while services are 7.6 points cheaper (92.4) and housing—measured by rental costs—is the most affordable component at 80.4. The median household income of $70,625 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 8.5%, yet the median monthly rent of $828 is 10.8% lower than the national median of $928, reinforcing the picture of a region where housing costs are a clear outlier on the low side. The consumption profile is dominated by the goods sector. With a goods index of 101.6, everyday items such as groceries, apparel, and fuel cost about 1.6% more than the national average, offsetting the savings generated by cheaper services and rentals. Daily life therefore feels mixed: residents benefit from low housing and utility bills, but must allocate a slightly larger share of their budget to tangible consumer goods. The rent‑burden figure of 14.1% of income illustrates that housing consumes a modest slice of earnings, well under the 30% threshold often cited as a stress point, while the lower services index suggests reduced spending on healthcare, utilities, and dining out. Disposable income calculations reveal a relatively strong purchasing position. After deducting the median rent of $828 (≈ $9,936 annually) from the median household income, roughly $60,689 remains for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 71,517.5—derived from the RPP and income data—indicates that, adjusted for local price levels, residents can purchase about 71.5% of the national basket of goods and services with their income. In real terms, the county’s lower housing and services costs translate into a net disposable income advantage of roughly $5,000 to $6,000 compared with a typical American household. Demographic characteristics shape this advantage. With only 14.3% of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market leans toward occupations that may not command premium wages, yet the unemployment rate of 4.0% aligns with the national average, suggesting a stable job environment. The median age of 42 points to a mature workforce, often associated with higher earnings and more established consumption patterns, which helps sustain the higher median income despite the modest educational attainment. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3.0% decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Meanwhile, median household income rose from $50,517 to $70,625, a 39.8% increase, and median rent grew from $638 to $828, a 29.8% rise. Income growth outpaced both the overall price index and the housing cost increase, confirming that wages have kept pace with, and indeed exceeded, the pace of inflation in the county. Prospective residents should budget around $828 per month for rent, allocate roughly 12%–15% of income to goods—recognizing the slight premium— and expect lower expenditures on services such as healthcare and utilities. With a rent burden of only 14.1% of earnings, a typical household can comfortably cover housing and retain a sizable margin for discretionary spending, making Clark County a comparatively affordable environment for everyday consumption despite the modest uptick in goods prices.

What this means for residents of Clark County, Illinois
