📊 Local Market Analysis

Cassia County’s cost of living index of 92.2 (national = 100) signals that consumer prices are roughly 8 % below the national average, with goods priced at 95.9, services at 86.9 and rental costs at a markedly lower 68.1. The overall index reflects a modestly cheaper environment, driven primarily by the steep discount in housing expenses, while services remain the most economical component. Although goods sit just under parity with the nation, the low rental cost index underscores a significant advantage for household budgets. The local cost structure is dominated by goods, which register the highest index among the three categories, suggesting that everyday items such as groceries, apparel and fuel consume a larger share of spending than housing or services. Daily life in Cassia County is therefore relatively inexpensive, especially when compared with national benchmarks, because residents allocate a smaller proportion of income to shelter and utility expenses. The low rent burden of 16.5 %—$920 monthly rent against a median household income of $67,042—further illustrates how affordable housing cushions overall expenditure. Median household income of $67,042 exceeds the national median of $65,047, providing a modest income premium. The Purchasing Power Index of 72,685.3 quantifies the relative purchasing capacity of local earnings after accounting for price differences, indicating that the typical Cassian household can command more real consumption than a comparable national counterpart. After allocating $920 to rent, roughly $5,200 remains for other necessities, leaving a surplus that supports discretionary spending and savings. Local demographics reinforce the affordability narrative: an unemployment rate of 2.5 % reflects a tight labor market, while a median age of 33.1 suggests a workforce concentrated in prime earning years. However, only 13.9 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, which may limit high‑skill wage growth despite the current income advantage. The low jobless rate signals stable employment opportunities, yet the education profile hints at potential constraints on future income expansion. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity (RPP) has edged down 1.0 % from 93.2 to 92.236, while median household income has risen 46.7 % and median rent has climbed 56.2 %. Although wages have grown faster than inflation in real terms, rent increases have outpaced income gains, narrowing the gap between earnings and housing costs. The modest decline in the overall cost‑of‑living index suggests that broader price pressures remain subdued, but the faster rent trajectory warrants attention in long‑term budgeting. Prospective residents should anticipate that housing expenses will consume a relatively small share of income, allowing more flexibility for other consumption categories. Goods will be priced near the national average, so grocery and apparel budgets should align with typical expectations. Services, being significantly cheaper, reduce the cost of healthcare, utilities and dining out. When constructing a household budget, emphasizing the low rent burden and modest service costs provides a realistic baseline, while monitoring rent trends ensures that future rent escalations do not erode the current affordability advantage.

What this means for residents of Cassia County, Idaho
