📊 Local Market Analysis

Washington County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 7.9 percent below the national average, reflected in an overall Regional Price Parity of 92.1, yet the composition of that advantage reveals sharp internal contrasts. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run only 4.4 percent cheaper than the U.S. baseline at 95.6, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining out collapse to 71.3, a staggering 28.7 percent discount that anchors the county's affordability profile. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 84.7, or 15.3 percent below national levels, making shelter the second-most influential component after the deeply discounted services basket. The median household income of $61,358 trails the national median of $65,047 by 5.7 percent, but the Purchasing Power Index of 66,623.2 — derived by adjusting nominal income for local price levels — indicates that the typical worker effectively commands 2.4 percent more real purchasing capacity than the national average after accounting for the county's lower cost structure. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 14.9 percent of gross income, with median monthly rent of $760 comparing favorably against the $928 national figure. Local labor dynamics reinforce this calculus: a 3.6 percent unemployment rate signals a tight market, while a median age of 41.4 and a bachelor's attainment rate of just 9.9 percent suggest an economy anchored in skilled trades, manufacturing, and service employment rather than knowledge-sector wages, keeping upward pressure on nominal incomes modest. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward from 93.0 to 92.1, a marginal 1.0 percent decline in relative price levels, while median household income surged 48.6 percent from $41,289 to $61,358. Median rent increased only 22.2 percent over the same span, from $622 to $760, meaning wage growth has outpaced shelter costs by a factor of more than two. This divergence has progressively improved real living standards for incumbent households, though the low educational attainment ceiling may constrain future income trajectory absent structural economic shifts. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel expenses that track near national norms, while allocating significantly less for healthcare visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals than in most U.S. counties. The 14.9 percent rent-to-income ratio leaves unusually wide discretionary bandwidth for transportation, childcare, or savings, but the services discount likely reflects limited provider density and fewer consumption options rather than pure efficiency. Households relocating from higher-cost metros will experience immediate relief on recurring service outlays, though they should verify that local healthcare networks and broadband infrastructure meet their specific needs before assuming the index advantage translates uniformly across all consumption categories.

What this means for residents of Washington County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 66623.2, a median household income of $61,358 in Washington County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $40,878,663 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
