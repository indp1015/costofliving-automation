📊 Local Market Analysis

Montgomery County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices are the most elevated component of the local basket, registering a goods index of 93.1, still modestly under the national average but markedly higher than the county’s service and housing scores. Services—healthcare, utilities and dining—are the cheapest segment at 56.7, and rental costs follow at 81.9, indicating that everyday expenses for non‑housing items are the primary driver of the county’s price structure. A median rent of $734, 21 % lower than the national median of $928, further underscores the affordability of shelter, while the rent‑burden figure of 18 % of income suggests that most households allocate a modest share of earnings to housing. Disposable income calculations reveal a constrained purchasing environment despite the low price levels. The median household income of $49,015 is 25 % below the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index—56,462.4—places the county’s effective buying power at roughly 86 % of a typical U.S. household. After covering the median rent, a typical family retains about $42,000 annually, or $3,500 per month, to meet all other expenses. Given the services index of 56.7, spending on health, utilities and dining is substantially cheaper than elsewhere, but the higher goods index erodes some of the savings, especially for households with larger grocery bills or fuel consumption. Demographic factors amplify the cost experience. With only 12.7 % of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher and an unemployment rate of 7.0 %, the labor market offers limited high‑skill, high‑wage opportunities. The median age of 50.8 years points to a sizable retiree and near‑retiree cohort, whose income sources often rely on fixed wages or pensions, further tightening discretionary budgets. The modest educational attainment and elevated unemployment rate together depress wage growth, reinforcing the reliance on low‑cost services and affordable housing to maintain living standards. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median household income surged 48 % from $33,037 to $49,015. Rental prices rose 36 % in the same period, from $538 to $734. Income growth outpaced both the modest deflation in overall prices and the steeper rise in rents, suggesting that wages have generally kept pace with, and even exceeded, cost pressures. Prospective residents should budget heavily on groceries and fuel, allocate roughly 18 % of income to rent, and expect substantially lower expenditures on healthcare, utilities and dining. A realistic monthly budget would reserve $300–$350 for utilities, $250–$300 for food, and $150–$200 for transportation, leaving discretionary funds modest but manageable given the county’s overall affordability.

What this means for residents of Montgomery County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 56462.4, a median household income of $49,015 in Montgomery County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $27,675,045 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
