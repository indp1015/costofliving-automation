📊 Local Market Analysis

Scott County residents navigate a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) registering 98.8 against the 100 national average in 2023. This aggregate figure masks a distinct bifurcation in household expenditure: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—run 1.6 percent above the national index at 101.6, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in significantly cheaper at 92.4. The dominant deflationary force remains rental housing, priced at just 80.4 on the index, a near 20 percent discount to the U.S. mean that effectively subsidizes the overall cost structure for the median household earning $73,013 annually. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is inexpensive primarily because shelter costs are remarkably low; median monthly rent of $669 consumes only 11.0 percent of gross income, a burden roughly half the national standard and a stark contrast to the $928 national median rent. However, the goods penalty means residents pay a premium at the grocery checkout and the pump, partially offsetting the housing windfall. This dynamic creates a purchasing power index of 73,935.7, indicating that the median dollar stretches approximately 1.3 percent further than the nominal income suggests, a tangible advantage driven almost entirely by the rental discount rather than broad-based affordability across all consumption categories. Local demographics reinforce this economic profile. An unemployment rate of 2.6 percent signals a tight labor market that likely supports the 54.6 percent nominal income growth observed since 2010, a period during which the RPP actually declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752. Wages have significantly outpaced both inflation and the 22.1 percent rise in median rent over the same horizon. Yet the relatively low educational attainment—only 17.7 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—combined with a median age of 44.3 years suggests an economy anchored in established, non-degree occupations where the low cost of living acts as a retention mechanism rather than a magnet for high-skill migration. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the trade-off between cheap shelter and dear consumables. The rental discount provides immediate cash-flow relief, freeing discretionary income for savings or the higher recurring costs of food and fuel. However, the goods premium is inescapable and regressive, impacting fixed budgets disproportionately. While the historical trajectory shows real income gains accelerating, the reliance on a single cheap component—housing—means any upward pressure on rents would erode the purchasing power advantage faster than in diversified low-cost metros. Households should model budgets using the 101.6 goods index as the baseline for variable expenses rather than the headline 98.8 RPP.

What this means for residents of Scott County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 73935.7, a median household income of $73,013 in Scott County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $53,982,673 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
