📊 Local Market Analysis

Floyd County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 3.5 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 96.5 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest relative cost component at 97.7, nearly converging with national pricing, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining offer the steepest discount at 88.3, a full 11.7 points below the benchmark. Rental costs sit at 92.3, providing moderate shelter relief compared to the broader U.S. market. This configuration suggests daily essentials like food and transportation consume a disproportionate share of local wages, whereas discretionary service spending stretches further than in most metros. The median household income of $62,540 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 3.8 percent, but the Purchasing Power Index of 64,812.3 indicates that local prices effectively boost real earnings by approximately 3.6 percent above the nominal figure. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 18.6 percent of gross income—median rent of $971 against the national $928—freeing significant cash flow for other categories. However, with only 14.2 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher and an unemployment rate of 4.3 percent, the labor market skews toward sectors where wage growth often lags productivity gains, limiting the ability of many households to capitalize fully on the favorable services index. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP climbed from 94.574 to 96.494, a cumulative 2.0 percent increase in the relative price level. During that same window, nominal median income surged 50.5 percent from $41,549 to $62,540, outpacing the 44.1 percent rise in median rent from $674 to $971. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have expanded real purchasing power considerably, though the accelerating goods index—now nearly at parity with the nation—threatens to erode those gains if income growth decelerates. The demographic profile, marked by a median age of 37.8, suggests a workforce in prime earning years, yet the educational attainment gap implies vulnerability to automation and sectoral shifts that could stall the income trajectory responsible for the current comfort margin. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel virtually identical to higher-cost metros, while allocating significantly less for healthcare visits, utility deposits, and restaurant meals. The low rent burden creates a buffer, but the high goods index means inflation at the pump or checkout register transmits directly to the household ledger with minimal local offset. Transportation costs in a county with limited transit alternatives amplify the goods exposure, and the services discount, while real, applies to categories often consumed irregularly. Households moving from higher-wage regions will find their dollars stretch further on rent and services, but the weekly grocery run and daily commute offer little geographic arbitrage.

What this means for residents of Floyd County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64812.3, a median household income of $62,540 in Floyd County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $40,533,612 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
