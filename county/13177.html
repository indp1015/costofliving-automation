📊 Local Market Analysis

Lee County residents navigate a cost structure that sits modestly below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) for 2023 registering 96.5 against a national index of 100. This overall discount masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—track near parity at 97.7, while rental costs come in at 92.3. The most pronounced deviation appears in services, where healthcare, utilities, and dining aggregate to an index of just 88.3, marking the lowest cost component and the primary driver keeping the composite figure below the national average. Consequently, daily life leans inexpensive relative to the broader U.S., though the near-parity pricing of essential goods tempers the relief offered by cheaper services. Purchasing power metrics confirm that the median household captures substantial real income gains from this price environment. The median household income of $88,024 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 35.3 percent, translating into a Purchasing Power Index of 91,222.3—effectively meaning the median dollar stretches roughly 3.5 percent further locally than a dollar at the national average price level. Housing affordability underscores this advantage: median monthly rent of $1,147 consumes only 15.6 percent of gross income, a stark contrast to the national median rent of $928 which, against the lower national income, imposes a significantly heavier burden. The labor market reinforces this stability; an unemployment rate of 3.6 percent signals tight conditions, while a median age of 37.2 years and an 18.2 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate suggest a workforce anchored in prime earning years, albeit with a skill distribution that may limit upward wage pressure in high-skill sectors. Over the past decade, the inflation-adjusted trajectory reveals income growth decisively outpacing both rent and general price appreciation. The RPP edged up only 2.0 percent from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, indicating remarkably contained local inflation. Meanwhile, median household income surged 43.0 percent from $61,537 to $88,024, dwarfing the 23.2 percent increase in median rent from $931 to $1,147. This divergence has progressively lowered the rent-to-income ratio, expanding discretionary capacity for the median household. The data suggests that wage growth has not merely kept pace with expenses but has substantially widened the buffer against cost-of-living erosion, a dynamic increasingly rare in comparable metropolitan fringes. For a prospective resident budgeting daily household expenses, the ledger presents clear trade-offs. Grocery bills and fuel costs will feel familiar to anyone relocating from a national average market, offering little arbitrage opportunity. The meaningful savings accumulate in recurring service obligations—utility contracts, healthcare out-of-pocket costs, and restaurant meals—where the 11.7 percent discount to the national index compounds monthly. Rent, while up 23 percent since 2010, remains a low-friction line item at roughly one-sixth of gross income. The critical variable remains goods inflation; should the 97.7 index converge toward 100 or beyond, the current purchasing power cushion would narrow rapidly given the category’s weight in essential spending.

What this means for residents of Lee County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 91222.3, a median household income of $88,024 in Lee County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $80,297,517 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
