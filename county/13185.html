📊 Local Market Analysis

Lowndes County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national benchmark, with the overall Relative Price Parity (RPP) index at 96.5 versus 100 for the United States. The goods component—groceries, apparel, and fuel—registers at 97.7, indicating that everyday purchases are roughly 2.3 % cheaper than the average American market. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are the most affordable segment at 88.3, a full 11.7 % discount to the national level. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $1,002, translate to a rental index of 92.3, meaning renters pay about 7.7 % less than the national median rent of $928. The composite picture is one of a region where essential consumer goods are slightly cheaper, services are markedly cheaper, and housing is modestly below average, suggesting that daily life is generally inexpensive relative to the broader economy. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 88.3 pulls the overall RPP down. While goods remain close to parity, the low price of utilities, medical care, and restaurant meals offsets the modestly higher rent. Rent burden sits at 21.5 % of median household income, well under the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag affordability concerns, reinforcing the view that housing pressure is limited. After accounting for rent, a median household earning $55,887 retains $46,885 of pre‑tax income for other expenses, a disposable pool that exceeds the national median income by roughly $10,000 but is tempered by the county’s lower price environment. The Purchasing Power Index of 57,917.6 quantifies this advantage: residents can acquire roughly 57.9 % more goods and services than a household with the same nominal income in a region where the RPP equals 100. Demographic factors shape this affordability. With only 16.5 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market leans toward lower‑skill occupations that typically command modest wages, yet the unemployment rate of 5.7 % remains close to the national average, indicating a relatively stable job base. A median age of 31.3 years points to a youthful population, often associated with higher consumption of goods such as apparel and fuel, but also with lower healthcare utilization, which dovetails with the low services index. The combination of a young, less‑educated workforce and modest unemployment suggests that while wages are not high, the cost structure—especially in services—mitigates the impact on household budgets. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 94.574 to 96.494, a 2.0 % increase, while median household income surged 51.7 % from $36,834 to $55,887. Rental costs climbed 32.2 % from $758 to $1,002. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rent escalation, expanding real purchasing power for residents. The gap between wage growth and price growth indicates that, historically, residents have enjoyed improving standards of living, with disposable income expanding faster than the cost of everyday items. Prospective residents should allocate a larger share of their budget to goods—particularly transportation and food—because those categories hover near national price levels, while budgeting conservatively for services, which are notably cheaper. With rent consuming roughly one‑fifth of household income, the remaining 80 % can be distributed across utilities, healthcare, and discretionary spending without breaching typical affordability thresholds. A realistic monthly budget might allocate $300–$350 to groceries, $150 to fuel, $200 to utilities and healthcare combined, and $200–$250 for dining and entertainment, leaving a comfortable margin for savings or debt service. Understanding these allocations helps newcomers gauge the true cost of daily life in Lowndes County, where lower service prices and modest housing costs combine to produce a comparatively affordable living environment.

What this means for residents of Lowndes County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57917.6, a median household income of $55,887 in Lowndes County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $32,368,409 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
