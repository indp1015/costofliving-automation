📊 Local Market Analysis

Perry County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parity index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices are only modestly lower than average, registering a goods index of 93.1, while the services index—covering healthcare, utilities and dining—drops to 56.7, the county’s most affordable component. Rental costs, measured by the housing index, are 81.9, reflecting a median monthly rent of $734, roughly 21 % cheaper than the national median of $928. The expense profile is driven chiefly by services, which are nearly half the national price level, cushioning household budgets despite a goods index that remains close to the national figure. Daily life therefore leans toward affordability: essential non‑housing outlays such as medical care, electricity and restaurant meals cost substantially less, while food and fuel costs are only slightly below the countrywide average. The rent‑to‑income ratio of 14.7 % underscores a light housing burden; most households spend well under the 30 % threshold that typically signals affordability. Disposable income calculations reveal a purchasing‑power advantage. The median household earns $60,078, 7.6 % below the national median, yet after subtracting the average rent the median family retains roughly $53,000 in annual earnings. The Purchasing Power Index of 69,206 places Perry County residents at about 69 % of the national standard, meaning that each dollar stretches farther on everyday goods and services. This advantage stems from the low services cost and modest rent, offsetting the slightly higher goods prices. Demographic factors reinforce the cost picture. With 12.2 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher and an unemployment rate of 4.5 %, the labor market is modest but stable, supporting steady income growth. The median age of 44.1 suggests a mature population, likely with established consumption patterns focused on essential services rather than discretionary spending. Over the past decade, the RPP fell 2 % from 88.6 to 86.8, while median household income surged 41.9 % from $42,346 to $60,078, outpacing the 18.2 % rise in median rent. Wage growth has therefore outstripped price inflation, enhancing real purchasing power. Prospective residents should budget around $734 for rent, allocate roughly 10‑12 % of income to groceries and fuel, and expect utilities, healthcare and dining costs to be markedly lower than elsewhere. With a rent burden under 15 % and services costs at just 57 % of the national level, a typical household can allocate a larger share of its earnings to savings or discretionary items, provided income remains near the county median.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Perry County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69206.3, a median household income of $60,078 in Perry County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $41,577,761 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
