📊 Local Market Analysis

Morgan County residents navigate a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) registering 98.8 against the 100 national average in 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run 1.6 percent above the national index at 101.6, making daily necessities the most expensive component of the local budget. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a substantial 7.6 percent discount at 92.4, while rental housing costs represent the deepest value proposition at 80.4, nearly twenty percent below the national norm. This structure creates a distinctive consumer profile where the marginal dollar spent on recurring shelter and services stretches considerably further than in the average U.S. county, even as the weekly trip to the grocery store or gas station extracts a slight premium. The median household income of $65,175 aligns almost precisely with the national median of $65,047, yet the local purchasing power index of 65,998.7 reveals a meaningful real-income advantage derived almost entirely from the housing discount. Median monthly rent of $710 consumes only 13.1 percent of gross income, a burden roughly half the national standard implied by the $928 national median rent, freeing substantial cash flow for discretionary spending, savings, or the elevated goods basket. However, the labor market context introduces friction: a 5.6 percent unemployment rate exceeds typical full-employment thresholds, and an educational attainment rate of just 16.1 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher suggests a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not command wage premiums sufficient to offset the goods penalty over time. The median age of 41.5 indicates a stable, established population, but the unemployment figure signals that income stability remains a variable for a non-trivial share of households. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the county’s cost trajectory has improved relative to the nation, with the RPP declining 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752. During that same window, nominal median income surged 41.8 percent from $45,978, vastly outpacing the 17.7 percent increase in median rent from $603. This divergence indicates that wage growth has not merely kept pace with shelter costs but has dramatically expanded the real resource envelope for renters. The goods category remains the outlier; because the RPP for goods sits above 100, the purchasing power gains from cheap housing and services are partially eroded every time a household purchases food, clothing, or gasoline. Inflation in these tradeable categories is largely imported, meaning local policy or wage dynamics have limited influence on the highest-cost component of the consumer basket. For a prospective resident constructing a household budget, the calculus centers on the trade-off between cheap shelter and dear consumables. A renter securing the median $710 unit locks in a housing cost roughly $2,600 below the national annual median, a surplus that can absorb the 1.6 percent goods premium—amounting to perhaps a few hundred dollars annually on a typical grocery and fuel budget—with ample margin remaining. The risk profile shifts for households with high medical utilization or childcare needs, where the 92.4 services index offers relief, but for those with long commutes or large families driving high grocery and fuel volumes, the 101.6 goods index acts as a persistent drag on disposable income. The data suggests the county functions as a high-purchasing-power environment for renters whose consumption patterns weight heavily toward housing and services, while heavy goods consumers capture a smaller net benefit.

What this means for residents of Morgan County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 65998.7, a median household income of $65,175 in Morgan County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $43,014,653 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
