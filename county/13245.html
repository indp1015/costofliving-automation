📊 Local Market Analysis

Richmond County residents navigate a cost environment that sits 3.5 percent below the national average, reflected in a 2023 Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 96.5, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 97.7, nearly matching the national baseline, while Services (healthcare, utilities, dining) offer the steepest relief at 88.3, a full 11.7 points below the U.S. average. Rental costs, indexed at 92.3, provide moderate savings relative to the country, but the near-parity pricing on daily necessities means the typical consumer feels the pinch at the checkout counter more acutely than in the service economy, creating a bifurcated experience where discretionary spending on dining or healthcare stretches further than the weekly grocery run or commute. Purchasing power metrics confirm that nominal income gains have translated into real wage growth over the last decade, though a significant gap persists against national benchmarks. The median household income of $53,197 yields a Purchasing Power Index of 55,129.9, indicating that after adjusting for local prices, the median earner commands roughly $1,933 more in real goods and services than their nominal paycheck suggests. However, this adjusted figure remains roughly 15 percent below the national median income of $65,047. Rent burden sits at a manageable 24.5 percent of gross income—with median monthly rent at $1,087 against a national median of $928—suggesting housing affordability is a relative bright spot, yet the 7.9 percent unemployment rate and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 14.7 percent signal a labor market tilted toward lower-wage sectors, limiting upward mobility for the median worker aged 35. Historical trends underscore a decade of improving real terms for residents, though the trajectory warrants scrutiny. Since 2010, the RPP has drifted upward modestly from 94.6 to 96.5, a 2.0 percent increase in the cost level, while median income surged 42.1 percent from $37,424 to $53,197. Median rent climbed 37.6 percent over the same span, from $790 to $1,087, lagging income growth by a meaningful margin. This divergence suggests that, on aggregate, wage growth has outpaced both inflation and shelter costs, expanding the disposable income cushion for households that remained continuously employed. The data implies a widening of real purchasing power for incumbents, though the rising RPP indicates the county is slowly converging toward national price levels, eroding some of the historical cost advantage. For a prospective resident constructing a monthly budget, the immediate takeaway is that shelter costs consume roughly one-quarter of gross income, leaving approximately $3,000 per month for all other expenses before taxes. The 11.7 percent discount on services provides tangible relief on utility bills and healthcare outlays, but the 97.7 index on goods demands careful allocation for food and transportation, categories where national pricing largely prevails. A household relocating from a higher-cost metro should anticipate a net increase in discretionary capacity driven primarily by the rental discount and service savings, provided their income scales proportionally; those entering the local labor market at prevailing wages, however, will contend with an income floor that sits well below the national median, making the goods-price parity the dominant constraint on daily financial flexibility.

What this means for residents of Richmond County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55129.9, a median household income of $53,197 in Richmond County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $29,327,453 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
