📊 Local Market Analysis

Glades County residents face a cost of living that sits 3.6 percent above the national baseline, a figure driven almost entirely by a services sector that runs 23.2 percent higher than the U.S. average while rental housing costs remain 6.3 percent below it. The Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registers an overall index of 103.6, masking a sharp bifurcation: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel come in at 98.2, offering modest relief on daily consumables, but healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses inflate the services component to 123.2. This structure makes daily life expensive in practice despite cheap rents; a household spending heavily on medical care, electricity, or prepared food absorbs the full weight of that premium, whereas the 93.7 rental index only benefits those actively leasing. The median monthly rent of $927 aligns almost exactly with the national median of $928, yet the local median household income of $38,905 trails the national figure of $65,047 by 40 percent, rendering the nominal rent parity misleading for local earners. Purchasing power calculations underscore the strain: the adjusted Purchasing Power Index of 37,540 indicates that after accounting for the 103.6 price level, the median household commands real resources roughly equivalent to $37,540 in a median-cost metro. Rent burden consumes 28.6 percent of gross income, a manageable ratio on paper but one that leaves little margin given the depressed wage floor. The labor market context explains much of this compression. An unemployment rate of 6.2 percent sits well above national norms, while only 9.9 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, limiting access to higher-wage service or professional roles. A median age of 45.8 years suggests an older workforce potentially reliant on fixed incomes or physically demanding sectors where wage growth lags, reinforcing the gap between local earnings and the elevated service costs that dominate the consumption basket. Over the past decade, the pressure has intensified. The RPP climbed from 100.4 in 2010 to 103.6 in 2023, a 3.2 percent cumulative increase in the price level. During the same window, median income rose 11.5 percent from $34,877 to $38,905, but median rent surged 36.1 percent from $681 to $927. Rent growth has outpaced income growth by a factor of more than three, eroding the affordability advantage that once characterized the county’s housing market. While goods prices remain slightly below national levels, the services index—typically stickier and less substitutable—has likely anchored the overall RPP ascent, meaning the cost of simply maintaining a household (insurance, electricity, medical visits) has risen faster than the nominal wage gains suggest. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on the services-housing trade-off. The below-average rental index provides genuine savings on shelter, but the 23.2 percent services premium acts as a regressive tax on essential consumption. A household budget must allocate disproportionately to health insurance premiums, utility deposits, and vehicle maintenance—costs that scale with the 123.2 index rather than the 93.7 rent index. Commuting distances in a rural county amplify fuel and vehicle wear expenses, partially offsetting the 98.2 goods advantage. Anyone relocating should model cash flows against the 37,540 purchasing power benchmark, not the nominal $38,905 income, and maintain liquid reserves to absorb service-sector price volatility that has historically outpaced local wage adjustments.

What this means for residents of Glades County, Florida

With a Purchasing Power Index of 37540.0, a median household income of $38,905 in Glades County, Florida has the equivalent buying power of $14,604,937 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 93.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
