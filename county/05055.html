📊 Local Market Analysis

Greene County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 86.8, meaning that a typical consumer basket costs roughly 13 % less than the national average of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel, and fuel—registers at 93.1, only 7 % below the U.S. norm, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 56.7, a 43 % discount. Housing, measured by median monthly rent of $852, is 8.2 % lower than the national median rent of $928, yielding a rental‑cost index of 81.9. Together these figures paint a picture of a region where everyday essentials are affordable, especially on the services side, but where price advantages are modest for tangible goods and housing. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 56.7 pulls the overall composite down far more than the relatively higher goods index of 93.1. Daily life in Greene County is therefore inexpensive in categories that consume a large share of household budgets—energy bills, medical visits, and restaurant meals. By contrast, the price of groceries and fuel remains only slightly below national levels, limiting the overall affordability advantage. The rent burden of 18 % of median household income ($56,793) confirms that housing costs are manageable; residents spend less than one‑fifth of their earnings on rent, well under the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a stress point. After deducting the average rent, a median household retains roughly $52,941 of annual income, translating to a purchasing‑power index of 65,422.2. This index, expressed as a dollar amount, indicates that the median resident can purchase the same basket of goods and services that a national consumer would buy with about $65,400, underscoring a modest but tangible advantage in disposable income. Demographically, Greene County’s labor market is characterized by a 7.5 % unemployment rate and a low educational attainment—only 10.4 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 37.8 suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the limited share of highly educated workers may constrain wage growth and keep the income distribution anchored near the current median of $56,793, which lags the national median of $65,047 by 12.5 %. Over the past decade, the regional price‑performance index slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median household income rose 37.6 % from $41,286 to $56,793. Rental costs climbed 27.2 % from $670 to $852 in the same period. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rent increases, indicating that wages have generally kept pace with, and even exceeded, the rise in living expenses. Prospective residents should budget for a modestly higher spend on groceries and fuel relative to other rural markets, but can expect substantial savings on utilities, health care, and dining. Allocating roughly 18 % of income to rent leaves about 70 % for all other expenses, a ratio that supports a comfortable standard of living provided that discretionary spending remains aligned with the relatively low services cost environment.

What this means for residents of Greene County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 65422.2, a median household income of $56,793 in Greene County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $37,155,230 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
