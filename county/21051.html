📊 Local Market Analysis

Clay County residents navigate a cost structure sitting 10.1 percent below the national average, reflected in a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 89.9, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest cost component at 94.5, nearly converging with national pricing and preventing the overall index from falling further. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining register a strikingly low 62.9, the lowest component by a wide margin, while rental costs sit at 80.9. This configuration means daily life is inexpensive regarding shelter and professional services, but the essential, high-frequency purchases of food and transportation offer scant relief compared to metropolitan benchmarks, effectively anchoring the cost floor for working families. The Purchasing Power Index of 44,061.6 indicates that the median household income of $39,607 stretches considerably further than nominal figures suggest, yielding a real income advantage of roughly $4,450 above the nominal median after adjusting for local prices. Rent burden stands at a manageable 21.1 percent, with the median monthly rent of $698 comparing favorably against the national median of $928. However, this purchasing power operates within a constrained labor market defined by a 10.2 percent unemployment rate and an educational attainment rate of just 7.5 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher. The median age of 40.6 years suggests a settled workforce, yet the elevated unemployment figure signals limited wage mobility and a reliance on sectors that may not fully capitalize on the low service-cost environment, capping absolute disposable income despite the favorable price parity. Over the past decade, the economic trajectory has favored income growth over cost escalation. The RPP remained virtually flat, edging down only 0.6 percent from 90.433 in 2010 to 89.89 in 2023, indicating remarkable price stability. Meanwhile, median household income surged 83.8 percent from $21,549 to $39,607, significantly outpacing the 47.6 percent increase in median rent, which rose from $473 to $698. This divergence confirms that wage growth has substantially outstripped both inflation and housing cost appreciation, improving the real standard of living for incumbent residents, though the absolute income level remains 39 percent below the national median of $65,047. A prospective resident should budget for a bifurcated expense profile: allocate near-national budgets for groceries and fuel given the 94.5 goods index, while anticipating significant savings on healthcare, utilities, and dining out relative to the national baseline. The low rent burden of 21.1 percent frees cash flow for other obligations, but the $39,607 median income leaves thin margins for savings, debt service, or unexpected emergencies, particularly in a county where one in ten workers faces unemployment.
