📊 Local Market Analysis

Greenlee County’s cost of living sits just above the national baseline, with an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 100.7 versus the United States average of 100. The goods index registers at 97.9, indicating that groceries, apparel and fuel are roughly 2 percent cheaper than the countrywide norm. Services—healthcare, utilities and dining—are the outlier, priced at 108.6, or 8.6 percent above the national level. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component, recorded at 83.3, meaning rents are 16.7 percent lower than the national median. The structure of local consumer spending is dominated by services. While everyday necessities such as food and fuel cost less than elsewhere, the premium on health care, utility bills and restaurant meals pushes the overall expense profile upward. A resident’s monthly budget will therefore feel the pinch primarily in recurring service bills rather than in the price of groceries or gasoline. The low rental index offsets this pressure: median monthly rent stands at $570, only 38.6 percent of the national median rent of $928, and represents just 9.1 percent of median household income. Consequently, housing does not dominate the cost picture; instead, the higher service costs drive the modest overall premium. Median household income in Greenlee County is $75,239, 15.6 percent above the national median of $65,047. After deducting the median rent of $570, a typical household retains $74,669 of annual earnings for other expenses, yielding a Purchasing Power Index of 74,684.8. This figure confirms that, despite higher service prices, residents enjoy greater disposable income than the average American because of higher earnings and substantially lower housing costs. The net effect is a purchasing power advantage that compensates for the service premium, allowing households to allocate more to discretionary spending or savings than a comparable national household. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight: unemployment sits at 3.5 percent, well below the national rate, indicating robust job availability. However, only 15.7 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not require advanced credentials, such as mining, agriculture or service industries. The median age of 36.1 years points to a relatively young population, likely in the prime earning phase, which reinforces the higher median income and low rent burden. The combination of low unemployment and modest educational attainment implies that wages are competitive enough to attract workers despite the service cost premium. Over the past decade, the RPP rose marginally from 100.558 in 2010 to 100.742 in 2023, a 0.2 percent increase, indicating price stability. In the same period, median household income surged 45.7 percent, from $51,628 to $75,239, while median rent climbed 37.3 percent, from $415 to $570. Income growth outpaced both overall price changes and rent increases, expanding real purchasing power and reducing rent burden from roughly 8 percent of income in 2010 to 9.1 percent today. Prospective residents should budget with service expenses as the primary cost driver. Allocating a larger share of income to healthcare, utilities and dining out is prudent, while housing will consume a relatively small slice of the budget. With a median rent of $570, a household earning the county median can comfortably cover rent and still retain over $60,000 annually for other needs. Planning for higher utility bills and medical costs than the national average will ensure a realistic household budget that leverages the county’s strong earnings and low housing costs.

What this means for residents of Greenlee County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 74684.8, a median household income of $75,239 in Greenlee County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $56,192,097 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
