📊 Local Market Analysis

Cochise County presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 100.7 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a pronounced bifurcation in household expenditure patterns. The primary inflationary pressure stems from the services sector, which clocks in at 108.6—8.6 percent above the national average—driven by elevated outlays for healthcare, utilities, and dining. Conversely, the goods basket, encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel, offers a modest discount at 97.9, while rental housing costs represent the county’s most significant structural advantage at 83.3, or 16.7 percent below the national mean. This composition dictates that daily life is expensive for non-discretionary service consumption but comparatively affordable for shelter and tangible goods, a dynamic that fundamentally reshapes the local consumer basket relative to metropolitan Arizona. The interaction between this price structure and local income levels yields a distinctive purchasing power calculus. The median household income of $58,970 trails the national median of $65,047 by 9.3 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 58,535.7 indicates that the lower cost environment—specifically the deep discount on rents—largely neutralizes the nominal wage gap. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 19.1 percent of gross income against a median monthly rent of $939, significantly undercutting the national median rent of $928 and the standard 30 percent affordability threshold. This surplus in shelter affordability effectively subsidizes the 8.6 percent premium on services, allowing the median worker to retain disposable income that would otherwise be absorbed by housing in higher-cost jurisdictions, though the 7.3 percent unemployment rate suggests this calculus applies unevenly across the labor force. Demographic and labor market characteristics further contextualize these household economics. A median age of 42.9 years and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 16.9 percent—well below the national average—signal a workforce concentrated in sectors that typically command lower wage premiums, consistent with the sub-national median income. The elevated unemployment rate of 7.3 percent introduces income volatility that complicates budgeting for the services-heavy cost structure; households facing job disruption lose the wage buffer needed to cover the 108.6 service index, where costs for healthcare and utilities are less elastic than discretionary goods. This labor market fragility means the low rent burden, while statistically favorable, may not translate to financial resilience for a significant portion of the population. Over the 2010–2023 period, the economic trajectory has been broadly favorable for resident purchasing power. The RPP remained essentially flat, inching up only 0.2 percent from 100.558 to 100.742, indicating remarkable price stability in the aggregate. Meanwhile, median income surged 30.8 percent from $45,075 to $58,970, outpacing the 17.1 percent rise in median rent from $802 to $939. This divergence confirms that wage growth has comfortably exceeded the primary cost driver—shelter—while the sticky services premium has been contained by the static overall RPP. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: allocate capital for above-average recurring service costs, particularly healthcare and utilities, but expect housing expenditure to consume a substantially smaller share of gross income than the national norm, provided employment stability is secured.

What this means for residents of Cochise County, Arizona

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58535.7, a median household income of $58,970 in Cochise County, Arizona has the equivalent buying power of $34,518,502 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 83.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
