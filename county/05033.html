📊 Local Market Analysis

Crawford County sits 13.2 percent below the national price level with an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, but the aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods—covering groceries, apparel, and fuel—run at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, a staggering 43.3 percent discount. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9, pushing the median monthly rent to $828 against a national benchmark of $928. For a household managing day-to-day cash flow, the low services index is the primary driver of affordability, effectively subsidizing the relatively stickier cost of physical goods. That price advantage translates into tangible purchasing power. The median household income of $60,362 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 7.2 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 69,533.5 indicates that local earnings stretch significantly further once the 86.8 RPP is applied. Rent burden stands at a modest 16.5 percent of gross income, well below the 30 percent threshold that typically signals stress, leaving discretionary capacity for the higher relative cost of goods. The labor market context reinforces this dynamic: a 5.2 percent unemployment rate sits above the national norm, and only 13.7 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors where wage growth may lag professional hubs but living costs remain compressed. Over the last decade, the cost curve has bent favorably for residents. The RPP declined 2.0 percent from 88.6 in 2010 to 86.8 in 2023, even as median income surged 46.3 percent from $41,268 to $60,362. Median rent rose a comparatively modest 30.0 percent over the same span, from $637 to $828. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have widened the gap against shelter costs, a rarity in many regional economies. The median age of 39.1 implies a stable, prime-working-age population unlikely to generate sudden demand spikes that would destabilize the current rental equilibrium. A prospective resident should budget for a grocery and fuel bill that feels nearly national in magnitude while enjoying deep discounts on healthcare visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals. The 16.5 percent rent-to-income ratio offers a buffer against goods inflation, but the low educational attainment rate and elevated unemployment hint at limited high-wage mobility. Household budgets here hinge on managing the 93.1 goods index—where substitution effects are limited—rather than the service categories where the county’s structural cost advantage is most pronounced.

What this means for residents of Crawford County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69533.5, a median household income of $60,362 in Crawford County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $41,971,811 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
