📊 Local Market Analysis

Wilcox County presents a cost of living profile that sits 10.9 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.1 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distorted consumer landscape where the price of tangible necessities barely budges while service costs collapse. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — carry an index of 94.6, only a modest 5.4 percent discount to the national baseline, meaning residents pay near-national prices for daily physical consumption. By contrast, the Services index plunges to 61.6, a staggering 38.4 percent below average, reflecting deeply depressed pricing for healthcare, utilities, and dining that likely signals limited provider density and suppressed local wage floors rather than genuine affordability. Rental costs, indexed at 89.9, align closely with the overall figure, placing shelter modestly below the national median of $928 at $699 per month. This structure makes Goods the primary driver of the local cost burden, forcing households to allocate a disproportionate share of nominal income toward non-discretionary physical items that resist the deep discounts found in services. The median household income of $42,236 — 35.1 percent below the national median of $65,047 — translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 47,404.5, indicating that after adjusting for local prices, the typical household commands roughly 73 percent of the national real income standard. Rent burden sits at a manageable 19.9 percent of gross income, significantly lighter than the national average, yet this relief is offset by the high relative cost of goods; a dollar earned in Wilcox County purchases a standard basket of groceries and fuel nearly as expensively as in a major metro area, while the savings on services often reflect unavailable or lower-quality options rather than true surplus. Demographic and labor market fundamentals reinforce the constrained purchasing environment. An unemployment rate of 12.1 percent — more than double typical national levels — combined with an educational attainment rate of just 8.0 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher, suggests a labor market dominated by low-wage, low-skill employment that caps income growth. The median age of 42.0 years indicates an older workforce with limited mobility, potentially locking households into the existing earnings paradigm. Over the 2010–2023 period, nominal median income surged 77.8 percent from $23,750 to $42,236, outpacing the 64.9 percent rise in median rent from $424 to $699, while the overall RPP slipped marginally from 89.783 to 89.097. This trajectory shows real income gains have occurred, but the starting base was so low that the absolute dollar improvement — roughly $18,500 over thirteen years — barely lifts households above subsistence thresholds. A prospective resident should budget for a daily experience where shelter costs are genuinely low but the checkout lane at the grocery store and the gas pump offer little geographic arbitrage. The 94.6 Goods index means food and fuel budgets will resemble those in far higher-income counties, consuming a larger slice of a $3,520 monthly paycheck than the national average would suggest. Conversely, healthcare and utility outlays may run significantly cheaper, though access to specialists or high-speed broadband may be limited. The effective disposable income after rent — approximately $2,821 — must stretch to cover near-national prices for physical necessities, leaving minimal margin for savings, vehicle maintenance, or emergency expenses in a county where the labor market offers few pathways to wage advancement.

What this means for residents of Wilcox County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 47404.5, a median household income of $42,236 in Wilcox County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $20,021,765 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
