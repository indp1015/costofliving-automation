📊 Local Market Analysis

Floyd County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 11.2 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 96.6, nearly converging with national pricing and leaving little margin for savings on daily necessities. Conversely, the services category, covering healthcare, utilities, and dining, plunges to 66.0, the lowest index value and the primary driver of the county’s overall affordability advantage. Rental costs sit at 87.3, moderately below the national benchmark but notably higher than the services index, creating a bifurcated environment where keeping a roof overhead consumes a larger share of the wallet than maintaining the lifestyle inside it. The median household income of $64,500 tracks slightly below the national median of $65,047, but the purchasing power index of 72,660.5 illustrates how effectively the low price level amplifies local earnings. A median monthly rent of $672—substantially lower than the $928 national figure—translates to a rent burden of just 12.5 percent of gross income, a ratio that signals significant shelter affordability relative to coastal or metropolitan centers. This dynamic effectively grants the median earner roughly $8,160 in additional real purchasing power annually compared to their nominal wage, a buffer that absorbs the near-parity pricing of goods. However, the 13.2 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate and a median age of 44.2 years suggest a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not command wage premiums sufficient to offset the goods penalty if national inflation accelerates in food or energy. Over the past decade, the county’s economic trajectory has favored the earner. The RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.139 in 2010 to 88.769 in 2023, indicating that local consumer prices fell relative to the national basket even as the broader economy inflated. Meanwhile, median household income surged 44 percent from $44,797 to $64,500, vastly outpacing the 20.9 percent climb in median rent from $556 to $672. This divergence has widened the real income gap considerably; a decade ago, rent consumed approximately 14.9 percent of median income, compared to the current 12.5 percent. The 3.5 percent unemployment rate signals a tight labor market that has supported this wage growth, though the demographic profile hints at potential labor supply constraints that could pressure service-sector pricing—currently the county’s deepest discount—moving forward. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. Housing consumes a predictably low slice of the paycheck, and services—from medical visits to utility bills—offer profound discounts relative to the rest of the country. The friction point arrives at the checkout counter: grocery bills, gasoline, and clothing purchases will feel nearly identical in nominal dollars to higher-cost regions, eroding the theoretical advantage of the low RPP. A household relocating from a metro area should not assume the full 11.2 percent index gap materializes as disposable cash; rather, the surplus concentrates in housing and services line items, while the goods column demands near-national outlays. Understanding this split is essential for accurate cash-flow forecasting in a county where the cost of living is cheap, but the cost of living *well*—stocking a pantry and fueling a commute—remains stubbornly standard.

What this means for residents of Floyd County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 72660.5, a median household income of $64,500 in Floyd County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $46,866,022 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
