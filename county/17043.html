📊 Local Market Analysis

DuPage County presents a distinctive cost-of-living profile where the overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 sits marginally below the national baseline, masking significant divergence across consumption categories that directly shape household budgets. The composite index is pulled down by rental costs indexed at 80.4—nearly 20 percent below the national average—while the goods basket, encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel, runs at 101.6, making daily consumables the most expensive component relative to the rest of the country. Services, including healthcare, utilities, and dining, register at 92.4, offering modest relief on recurring monthly obligations. This structure creates a paradox for residents: the headline affordability suggested by the sub-100 overall index obscures the reality that frequent, non-discretionary purchases at the grocery store or gas station carry a premium, whereas the typically dominant budget line item—shelter—costs substantially less than the national norm. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life in DuPage is defined by cheap housing relative to expensive goods. Median monthly rent of $1,628 consumes only 17.7 percent of the $110,502 median household income, a rent-burden ratio that is exceptionally low compared to the national median rent of $928 against a $65,047 national income. This dynamic frees significant cash flow for other categories, yet the goods index of 101.6 ensures that the savings on shelter are partially offset every time a household restocks the pantry or fills a tank. The services index at 92.4 further supports disposable income retention, particularly for healthcare and utility outlays, but the goods premium acts as a persistent drag on the marginal dollar. Consequently, the local cost structure favors established households with stable housing arrangements, while the inflationary exposure remains concentrated in volatile, high-frequency categories. Purchasing power metrics confirm the net benefit of this configuration. The Purchasing Power Index of 111,898.5 indicates that the median household commands roughly 11.9 percent more real consumption capacity than the nominal national median income would suggest, effectively translating the $110,502 nominal wage into six-figure real purchasing strength. Local demographics reinforce this advantage: a 30.5 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate and a median age of 40.0 years signal a workforce concentrated in prime earning years with high human capital, while the 4.8 percent unemployment rate suggests a labor market tight enough to sustain wage growth but slack enough to prevent overheating. These factors underpin the historical trajectory where median income surged 38.7 percent from $79,658 to $110,502 between 2010 and 2023, outpacing the 42.4 percent rise in median rent but occurring alongside a 3.0 percent decline in the overall RPP from 101.778 to 98.752, signaling that real wage growth has been robust and broad-based. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: the primary financial leverage point lies in housing, where costs are structurally depressed relative to income, but the operational budget must absorb above-average prices for goods. A household relocating from a higher-cost metro should anticipate grocery and fuel bills that run roughly 1.6 percent above the national average, while enjoying rental outlays roughly 20 percent below it. The services discount of 7.6 percent provides a buffer for healthcare and utilities, yet the goods premium is inescapable and recurring. With the RPP trending downward over the last decade while incomes climbed, the trajectory favors the consumer, though the concentration of cost pressure in goods means any supply-side shock to food or energy would disproportionately impact the DuPage household budget compared to jurisdictions where shelter dominates the index.

What this means for residents of DuPage County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 111898.5, a median household income of $110,502 in DuPage County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $123,650,080 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
