📊 Local Market Analysis

Cherokee County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 96.5, still modestly under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 68.6. Housing costs, measured by the rental‑cost sub‑index, register at 90.8, indicating that renters pay roughly nine percent less than the average American household. The combined effect is a price environment that is 10 % cheaper than the countrywide average, though the savings are uneven across expense categories. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index of 68.6 reflects substantially lower costs for healthcare, utilities, and dining out. By contrast, goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are only slightly below national levels, suggesting that everyday consumables do not provide the same discount. Rental costs, while below the national median rent of $928, are higher than they were a decade ago (median rent rose from $657 to $801, a 21.9 % increase). Consequently, a typical resident experiences cheap everyday services but faces relatively tighter budgets for food and transportation, where price differentials are modest. Disposable income can be gauged through the Purchasing Power Index, which stands at 64,815.1 for Cherokee County. With a median household income of $58,274, the median earner retains roughly $58,274 × (1 – 0.165) ≈ $48,600 after accounting for the median rent burden of 16.5 % of income. This residual income translates into a purchasing power that is about 64 % of the national average, underscoring that lower prices are partially offset by lower earnings. The gap between income and cost is evident when the county’s purchasing power index is compared with the national median income of $65,047; residents earn about 10 % less while paying roughly 9 % less for housing and significantly less for services. Demographically, the county’s labor market is characterized by a 3.6 % unemployment rate, modestly below the national average, and a relatively low educational attainment—only 13.9 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 42 suggests a mature workforce, potentially limiting high‑wage, skill‑intensive occupations. These factors reinforce a cost environment where lower wages and limited high‑skill employment keep overall purchasing power modest, even as essential services remain inexpensive. The labor market’s stability, however, does provide a reliable income base for the majority of households. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 92.991 to 89.908, a 3.3 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Median household income surged 40.8 % from $41,388 to $58,274, outpacing the 21.9 % rise in median rent. The disparity between income growth and price growth suggests that wages have more than kept pace with living costs, enhancing real disposable income despite the county’s lower price levels. Prospective residents should allocate a larger share of their budget to goods—particularly food and fuel—than to services or rent. With median rent at $801, a household earning the county median would spend roughly $1,300 per month on housing, utilities, and groceries combined, leaving about $3,200 for discretionary spending, transportation, and healthcare. Budgeting for the higher relative cost of goods while leveraging the inexpensive service sector will be essential for maintaining a balanced household budget in Cherokee County.

What this means for residents of Cherokee County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 64815.1, a median household income of $58,274 in Cherokee County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $37,770,351 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
