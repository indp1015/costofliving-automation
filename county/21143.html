📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Lyon County, Kentucky sits well below the national benchmark, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 89.9 versus the United States average of 100. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 94.5, still under the national level, while services are markedly cheaper at 62.9. Housing costs, measured by the rental‑cost index, register at 80.9, indicating that renters face roughly 19 % lower prices than the countrywide norm. This pricing structure translates into a daily‑life environment that is inexpensive in the service sector—healthcare, utilities, and dining out cost less than two‑thirds of what they would in an average U.S. city—while the modest premium on goods keeps grocery and apparel bills close to, but still below, national levels. Disposable income calculations reveal a modest cushion for the median household. Median annual earnings stand at $64,464, just 0.9 % shy of the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the median monthly rent of $809 (15.1 % of income), a typical household retains roughly $56,800 in pre‑tax earnings for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 71,714.3 quantifies this residual buying strength: residents can acquire about 71.7 % of the goods and services that a national median‑income household can purchase, reflecting the combined effect of lower service costs and slightly higher goods prices. The rent‑burden figure—well under the 30 % affordability threshold—underscores that housing does not erode income as aggressively as in many parts of the country. Demographic characteristics shape this affordability profile. Unemployment is low at 3.5 %, suggesting a stable labor market, yet only 9.2 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 47.8 years points to a mature population, often associated with higher savings rates and reduced discretionary spending on items such as child‑care or higher‑education tuition. Together, these factors produce a cost‑of‑living experience where essential expenses—housing, utilities, and basic consumption—are restrained, but limited educational attainment may cap upward mobility and the ability to benefit fully from the lower price environment. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped marginally from 90.433 in 2010 to 89.89 in 2023, a 0.6 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have held steady relative to the national basket. Median household income, however, surged 37.4 % from $46,931 to $64,464, outpacing the 93.1 % rise in median rent (from $419 to $809). While rent growth outstripped income gains, the absolute rent level remains modest, and the broader wage increase more than compensates for the slight uptick in overall price levels, preserving purchasing power. Prospective residents should budget with the following concrete figures in mind: allocate roughly 15 % of gross income to rent, leaving about 85 % for all other categories. Expect grocery and apparel costs to be near national averages, while utilities, medical visits, and restaurant meals will be substantially cheaper. A household earning the median income can comfortably cover these expenses, but limited local high‑skill employment opportunities may constrain long‑term income growth. Careful planning around discretionary spending and savings will be essential to fully leverage the county’s low‑service‑cost advantage.

What this means for residents of Lyon County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71714.3, a median household income of $64,464 in Lyon County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $46,229,906 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
