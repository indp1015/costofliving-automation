📊 Local Market Analysis

Clark County’s cost of living sits well below the national benchmark, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 92.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 95.6, still under the national average, while services are markedly cheaper at 71.3. Housing, measured by rental costs, registers an index of 84.7, indicating that renters pay roughly 15 % less than the countrywide norm. The median household income of $72,298 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 11 %, reinforcing the county’s relative affordability despite modest price differentials. The consumption profile is dominated by goods, which account for the highest index reading. However, the low services index pulls the overall cost structure down, making everyday expenses such as healthcare, utilities, and dining out considerably cheaper than elsewhere. Rental costs, while higher than the services component, remain 15 % below the national average, with median monthly rent at $1,058 compared with $928 nationally; the rent‑burden figure of 17.6 % of income confirms that housing consumes a modest share of earnings. Consequently, daily life in Clark County can be characterized as inexpensive in the service sector and moderately priced in goods, yielding a net cost environment that is favorable for households that allocate a larger portion of spending to necessities rather than discretionary services. Purchasing power is quantified by an index of 78,502, reflecting the dollar amount a median earner can spend after accounting for local price levels. Subtracting median rent ($1,058 × 12 = $12,696) from the median income leaves $59,602 of pre‑tax earnings. Applying the services index (71.3) to typical non‑housing expenditures suggests that a median household retains roughly $42,000 of real purchasing power for groceries, apparel, fuel, and other goods, well above the national average disposable income after cost adjustments. This surplus is amplified by the county’s low rent burden, allowing residents to allocate a larger fraction of income to savings or discretionary spending. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with an unemployment rate of 3.8 %, well under the national average, indicating robust job availability. Yet only 17.5 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, potentially lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 39.8 years points to a mature, working‑age population, which typically enjoys stable earnings but may also face higher healthcare costs; the low services index mitigates this concern. The combination of strong employment and modest educational attainment creates a scenario where wages are sufficient to cover basic expenses without generating excessive disposable income. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.022 to 92.097, a 1 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Median household income rose from $51,699 to $72,298, a 39.8 % increase, outpacing the 40.1 % rise in median rent ($755 to $1,058). The near‑parallel growth of income and rent preserves the rent‑burden ratio, while the modest drop in the RPP index enhances real purchasing power. In effect, wages have kept pace with, and slightly exceeded, the pace of price growth, reinforcing the county’s affordability trajectory. Prospective residents should budget around $1,058 per month for rent, allocate roughly 10‑12 % of income to utilities and healthcare given the low services index, and expect grocery and fuel costs to align closely with national averages. With a rent burden of 17.6 % of income, a household earning the median $72,298 can comfortably cover housing and retain a sizable margin for other necessities, reinforcing Clark County’s reputation as a cost‑effective locale for everyday living.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Clark County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 78502.0, a median household income of $72,298 in Clark County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $56,755,376 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
