📊 Local Market Analysis

Searcy County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. While services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—come in at a striking 56.7, less than half the national price level, and rental costs track 18.1 percent below average at 81.9, the goods category tells a different story. At 93.1, the price of groceries, apparel, and fuel trails the national benchmark by a mere 6.9 percent, making daily consumables the primary driver of out-of-pocket expense for local households. This asymmetry defines the local purchasing power dynamic. The median household income of $41,978 translates to a Purchasing Power Index of $48,356.2, effectively granting residents a 15.2 percent boost in real terms relative to their nominal earnings. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 17.0 percent of income—median rent is $596 against a national $928—freeing cash flow that higher goods prices partially absorb. However, the gap between income growth and shelter costs over the last decade signals pressure: median income rose 26.9 percent since 2010 while median rent climbed 41.6 percent, outpacing wage gains and slowly eroding the rental affordability advantage. Demographic and labor market characteristics amplify these trends. An unemployment rate of 5.3 percent exceeds the national norm, and an educational attainment rate of just 9.8 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher suggests a labor market anchored in lower-wage sectors, limiting upward pressure on the median income. The median age of 48.5 years indicates an older population likely drawing on fixed incomes or retirement savings, for whom the steep discount on services—particularly healthcare and utilities—provides material relief, while the near-national pricing on goods consumes a disproportionate share of disposable income. Over the thirteen-year window, the overall RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative price levels that has not fully offset the rent-to-income divergence. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is clear: housing costs offer deep savings relative to urban centers, and service expenses are exceptionally low, but the weekly grocery bill and fuel costs will feel nearly identical to national averages. The net effect is a cost environment where fixed shelter costs are cheap, but variable consumption costs demand careful management, particularly for households reliant on a single median income.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Searcy County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 48356.2, a median household income of $41,978 in Searcy County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $20,298,966 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
