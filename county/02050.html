📊 Local Market Analysis

Bethel’s cost of living sits modestly above the national baseline, with the Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 103.3 compared with the United States average of 100. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—track closely at 103.7, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are slightly cheaper at 96.7. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the outlier: the rental index of 113.3 signals that rents are 13.3 % higher than the national norm. The median monthly rent of $1,454 reinforces this premium, outpacing the national median of $928 by 56.7 %. The rental component dominates the local cost structure. Although everyday goods are only marginally pricier and services are marginally cheaper than the countrywide average, the elevated rent inflates the overall expense profile. A household earning the median income of $70,616 faces a rent burden of 24.7 % of its earnings, a figure that, while below the conventional 30 % affordability threshold, still represents a sizable slice of disposable income. After allocating rent, the median resident retains roughly $61,000 annually, or $5,083 per month, for other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index of 68,362.1—well under the national benchmark of 100—captures this squeeze: despite a higher nominal income than the national median of $65,047, the combination of elevated housing costs and a relatively low services index depresses real purchasing power. Demographic and labor‑market conditions amplify the cost experience. Unemployment stands at 14.1 %, far above the national average, limiting the pool of supplemental earnings that could offset high rents. Only 5.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor force concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 28.7 years points to a youthful population, often earlier in career trajectories and less likely to command premium wages. Together, these factors constrain household budgets, making the high rental premium more burdensome for a workforce with limited earning power. Over the past decade, RPP has risen a mere 0.2 % (from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023), indicating price stability relative to the nation. In contrast, median household income surged 38.4 % (from $51,012 to $70,616), while median rent climbed 24.4 % (from $1,169 to $1,454). Income growth outpaced rent inflation, yet the modest rise in overall price levels means that the net gain in disposable resources is modest once housing costs are deducted. Prospective residents should budget heavily for rent, allocate a smaller share to services, and anticipate that everyday goods will not differ dramatically from national prices. A realistic monthly plan would earmark roughly 25 % of income for housing, 15–20 % for food and transportation, and the remaining balance for utilities, healthcare, and discretionary spending, leaving limited cushion for savings in a market where wages are modest and unemployment remains high.

What this means for residents of Bethel Census Area, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 68362.1, a median household income of $70,616 in Bethel Census Area, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $48,274,581 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
