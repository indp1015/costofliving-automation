📊 Local Market Analysis

Saline County residents navigate a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 98.8 against a 100 national average. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run 1.6 percent above the national level at 101.6, marking the highest cost component in the local basket. Services, including healthcare, utilities, and dining, come in substantially cheaper at 92.4, while rental housing costs represent the most pronounced discount at 80.4, nearly twenty percentage points below the national benchmark. The net effect is a daily cost structure where discretionary goods purchases exert upward pressure on household budgets, but the steep shelter discount more than offsets that premium, rendering overall consumer prices modestly favorable for residents. The median household income of $54,945 translates into a purchasing power index of 55,639.4 when adjusted for local prices, indicating that the typical worker retains slightly more real purchasing capacity than the nominal wage suggests. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 15.5 percent of income, with median monthly rent of $711 comparing favorably against the national median of $928. However, the local labor market context complicates this picture: a 5.9 percent unemployment rate exceeds national norms, and educational attainment remains modest with only 14.5 percent of adults holding a bachelor's degree or higher. The median age of 43.2 years signals an older workforce, potentially limiting wage mobility. These demographics suggest that while housing costs are low, the income ceiling constrains the ability to absorb the above-average goods prices, particularly for households reliant on single earners or lower-wage service employment. Over the past decade, the RPP has declined from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent drop in relative price levels that contrasts with a 43.6 percent surge in median income from $38,258 to $54,945. Median rent increased just 23.7 percent over the same period, from $575 to $711, meaning wage growth has significantly outpaced shelter cost inflation. This divergence has progressively improved real living standards for incumbent residents, though the goods category's persistent premium above the national average—holding at 101.6—represents a structural cost floor that limits further gains. The declining RPP trajectory suggests the county has become relatively more affordable even as nominal incomes rise, a dynamic driven largely by housing costs failing to keep pace with broader inflation. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel expenses that track slightly above national averages, with a typical household likely spending 3-5 percent more on weekly consumables than in a median-cost metro. Utility and healthcare outlays will run roughly 7-8 percent below national norms, providing monthly relief on essential services. The rental market offers substantial value: a two-bedroom unit at the median $711 consumes roughly one-sixth of the median pre-tax income, leaving considerable latitude for transportation, insurance, and savings. However, the elevated unemployment rate and limited high-wage employment density mean income volatility is a material risk; households should maintain larger emergency reserves than the low rent burden alone would suggest, particularly given the goods-price premium that cannot be easily substituted away.

What this means for residents of Saline County, Illinois

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
