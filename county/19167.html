📊 Local Market Analysis

Sioux County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — run only 3.4 percent cheaper than the U.S. baseline at 96.6, meaning a trip to the supermarket or a fill-up at the pump delivers minimal savings relative to coastal or metropolitan centers. Conversely, the services category, which captures healthcare, utilities, and dining, plunges to 66.0, a staggering 34 percent discount that acts as the primary engine suppressing the overall index. Rental costs, indexed at 87.3, occupy the middle ground, contributing to a profile where the highest cost component is actually the unavoidable daily spend on goods, while the lowest — services — provides the most significant budgetary relief. This dynamic translates into exceptional purchasing power for the median household earning $84,736, a figure that already exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 30.3 percent. When adjusted for local prices, the Purchasing Power Index climbs to $95,456.7, effectively granting residents a 12.6 percent raise in real terms simply by living in the county. Housing affordability underscores this advantage: median monthly rent of $888 consumes only 12.6 percent of gross income, a burden roughly half the national standard where median rent of $928 claims a larger slice of a smaller income pie. The labor market reinforces this stability; a 2.0 percent unemployment rate signals a tight, demand-driven economy where workers possess leverage, while a median age of 34.0 suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, though the 21.5 percent bachelor’s attainment rate indicates an economy likely anchored in skilled trades, agriculture, and manufacturing rather than high-density knowledge sectors. Over the past decade, the trajectory has favored the household balance sheet, albeit with a caveat on housing velocity. The overall RPP drifted downward from 91.1 to 88.8, a 2.6 percent decline in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 37.5 percent from $61,627 to $84,736. However, median rent accelerated faster at 42.8 percent, climbing from $622 to $888, narrowing the gap between income growth and shelter costs. This rent trajectory, while currently manageable at 12.6 percent of income, represents the single fastest-moving expense line item and the primary risk vector for future budget compression should income growth decelerate. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. The services discount — particularly on healthcare and utilities — functions as a structural subsidy on monthly overhead, freeing cash flow for savings or discretionary spend. However, the near-parity pricing on goods means grocery and transportation budgets will feel familiar to anyone relocating from higher-cost regions; there is no "Iowa discount" at the checkout counter. The tight rental market, implied by sub-3 percent vacancy pressure in a 2.0 percent unemployment environment, suggests limited negotiating power on lease renewals. A prudent household budget should anchor on the favorable services baseline while allocating a generous margin for goods inflation and potential rent escalation, recognizing that the current 12.6 percent rent-to-income ratio, while historically low, has limited room to absorb shocks without eroding the county’s considerable real income advantage.

What this means for residents of Sioux County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 95456.7, a median household income of $84,736 in Sioux County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $80,886,189 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
