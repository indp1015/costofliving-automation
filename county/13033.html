📊 Local Market Analysis

Burke County’s overall cost of living index of 96.5 indicates consumer prices are modestly below the national benchmark, yet the local price structure is skewed toward higher expenses in the goods category, which sits at 97.7, while services remain the most affordable component at 88.3 and rental costs sit just under the national average at 92.3. This distribution means that everyday purchases such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are the primary drivers of the local cost structure, making routine consumption slightly more expensive than the national norm, whereas healthcare, utilities, and dining are comparatively cheap, offsetting some of the higher goods costs. The housing component, though not the lowest, is also cheaper than the national median rent of $928, with local median rent at $763, suggesting that shelter costs are not a major pressure point for household budgets. The median household income of $50,739 provides a baseline for disposable income analysis, and the rent burden of 18.0% reflects a manageable housing expense relative to earnings. The Purchasing Power Index of 52,582.5 further illustrates that residents retain a significant portion of income after essential costs, especially given that services are inexpensive. However, the relatively low educational attainment—only 10.9% hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—may constrain higher‑wage opportunities, while the median age of 38.4 years points to a workforce that is still in peak earning years. The unemployment rate of 5.8% is modestly above the national average, indicating a labor market that is not overly tight but also not generating excessive wage competition, which helps keep wage growth steady without inflating living costs. Historical trends reveal that the Regional Price Parity (RPP) has risen by 2.0% from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest upward drift. Median household income has surged 50.8% over the same period, climbing from $33,641 to $50,739, outpacing the 39.7% increase in median rent from $546 to $763. This divergence suggests that wage growth has largely kept pace with, and in fact exceeded, housing cost inflation, helping maintain affordability despite the slight rise in the overall cost index. The goods component, which remains the highest cost driver, has also moved in tandem with broader price trends, but the overall index remains below the national baseline, reflecting a balanced cost environment. For a prospective resident, budgeting should prioritize the goods category, where expenses are near the national average, while recognizing that services and housing are comparatively favorable. Allocating roughly 18% of a $50,739 income to rent leaves ample room for discretionary spending, especially given the low cost of utilities and dining. The local labor market, while not dominated by high‑skill sectors, offers stable employment opportunities that support the observed income levels. Consequently, households can anticipate a cost structure that is slightly below the national average overall, with the primary expense concentration in everyday consumer items rather than shelter or essential services.

What this means for residents of Burke County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 52582.5, a median household income of $50,739 in Burke County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $26,679,835 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
