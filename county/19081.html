📊 Local Market Analysis

Hancock County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—command an index of 96.6, nearly converging with national pricing, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 66.0, the lowest component and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs occupy a middle ground at 87.3, meaning a household allocating $1,000 toward the national median rent basket would spend roughly $873 locally for comparable shelter, a tangible savings that nonetheless trails the deeper discounts found in the service economy. The median household income of $70,212 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by roughly 8.0 percent, translating into a Purchasing Power Index of 79,095 that signifies substantial real wage strength after adjusting for local prices. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 13.4 percent of gross income—calculated against a median monthly rent of $784 versus the national $928—leaving a disproportionately large share of earnings available for discretionary spending, savings, or the near-national-cost goods basket. This dynamic is reinforced by a labor market posting a 2.2 percent unemployment rate, indicating tight demand for labor that likely supports wage resilience, even as the relatively low bachelor’s degree attainment of 17.0 percent and a median age of 44.6 years suggest an economy anchored in established, non-degree-intensive sectors rather than high-growth knowledge industries. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8, meaning the county became incrementally cheaper relative to the nation even as nominal costs rose. Median income surged 32.5 percent from $52,981 to $70,212, yet median rent accelerated faster at 42.3 percent, climbing from $551 to $784. While income growth comfortably outpaced the overall cost-of-living trajectory, the faster appreciation in shelter costs signals a gradual erosion of the rental affordability edge, a trend that warrants monitoring for households on fixed incomes or those entering the rental market anew. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on a trade-off: housing and services—healthcare, utilities, restaurant meals—deliver deep, structural savings, but the weekly grocery run and fuel fill-up offer little refuge from national price levels. A household budgeting $800 for rent locally captures a standard of living that would cost significantly more in a metro area, yet the $70,212 median income must stretch to cover goods at 96.6 percent of the national index. The low service cost index of 66.0 effectively subsidizes daily living—lower utility bills and cheaper healthcare visits function as an implicit income boost—but the goods floor ensures that the "floor" of essential spending remains high relative to the overall discount.

What this means for residents of Hancock County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 79095.2, a median household income of $70,212 in Hancock County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $55,534,322 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
