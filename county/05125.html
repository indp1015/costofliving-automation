📊 Local Market Analysis

Saline County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023. The composition of that discount is heavily skewed: services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—come in at a striking 56.7, nearly 43 points below the national baseline, while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track much closer to parity at 93.1. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9. This divergence means daily life is distinctly inexpensive for service-intensive consumption, yet households see far less relief at the grocery store or gas pump, creating a bifurcated budget reality where discretionary service spending stretches far but essential goods demand near-national outlays. Purchasing power data confirms the arithmetic favors the median earner. With a median household income of $76,534—17.7 percent above the national median of $65,047—and median rent of $980 consuming only 15.4 percent of gross income, the typical household retains significant disposable capacity. The Purchasing Power Index of 88,162.7 quantifies this advantage, indicating that after adjusting for local price levels, the median income commands roughly $11,600 more in real goods and services than the nominal figure suggests. An unemployment rate of 3.8 percent and a median age of 40.4 years point to a stable, prime-working-age labor force, though the 19.3 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate signals a workforce tilted toward trades and service sectors rather than high-wage knowledge industries, potentially capping future income growth relative to coastal metros. Over the past decade, the economic trajectory has widened the gap between earnings and expenses. The RPP declined modestly from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while median income surged 37.1 percent from $55,817 to $76,534. Rent appreciation lagged at 21.3 percent, rising from $808 to $980. This divergence indicates that wage growth has substantially outpaced both inflation and housing cost acceleration, improving the real standard of living for incumbent households. However, the goods index at 93.1 suggests that the categories most resistant to local discounting—food and energy—remain tethered to national supply chains, limiting the full pass-through of regional affordability to the weekly household budget. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the goods-services split. Housing costs are low relative to income, freeing cash flow for savings or debt service, but the grocery bill and transportation fuel will feel familiar to anyone relocating from a median-cost metro. Healthcare and utility expenses, indexed at 56.7, represent the primary source of fiscal relief, effectively subsidizing the cost of living for families and older adults with higher service consumption. The data suggests a household moving from a national-average area could maintain an identical lifestyle while banking the difference, provided their consumption basket leans toward locally priced services rather than nationally priced goods.

What this means for residents of Saline County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 88162.7, a median household income of $76,534 in Saline County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $67,474,441 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
