📊 Local Market Analysis

Little River County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2% below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the national baseline, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, the lowest segment and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs sit at 81.9, roughly 18% below the national figure, translating to a median monthly outlay of $812 against a national median of $928. The consumption breakdown indicates that daily life is inexpensive relative to the country, but the savings are heavily concentrated in service categories that households cannot easily substitute or defer. A median household income of $52,241 yields a purchasing power index of $60,179, meaning the typical earner commands roughly $8,000 more in real goods and services than the nominal wage suggests. Rent burden stands at a manageable 18.7% of gross income, well under the 30% stress threshold, leaving significant residual income for the goods basket where prices track closer to national levels. However, the 9.0% unemployment rate—substantially above national norms—signals that this purchasing power is unevenly distributed and potentially volatile for the 87.8% of adults without a bachelor’s degree in a labor market that likely skews toward lower-wage service and goods-producing sectors. Over the past decade, the affordability calculus has shifted against the earner. While the RPP improved modestly from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0% decline in relative price levels, median rent accelerated 53.2% from $530 to $812, outpacing the 44.3% growth in median income from $36,205 to $52,241. This divergence has compressed the shelter-cost advantage that once defined the county’s budget profile, even as the services discount deepened. The median age of 42.2 suggests a population approaching peak earning years but also facing rising healthcare utilization, a category where the local 56.7 index provides substantial insulation against national medical inflation trends. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on a favorable rent-to-income ratio and cheap services offset by goods prices that offer little arbitrage. The primary variable is employment stability; with nearly one in ten workers unemployed, the median income figure masks a bimodal distribution where many households operate on wages significantly below $52,241. In that scenario, the 93.1 goods index becomes the binding constraint—groceries and fuel consume a disproportionate share of take-home pay precisely because they cannot be downsized like discretionary services. The county offers genuine purchasing power expansion for those with secure income, but the labor market data suggests that security is the scarce resource.

What this means for residents of Little River County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 60178.6, a median household income of $52,241 in Little River County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $31,437,902 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
