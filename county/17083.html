📊 Local Market Analysis

Jersey County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark at an index of 98.8, indicating that the average consumer price is about 1.2 percent lower than the United States as a whole. The Goods component registers at 101.6, modestly above parity, while Services weigh in at 92.4 and Rental Costs at 80.4, both well under the national average. Because Goods are the sole category above 100, they drive the local cost structure, making everyday items such as groceries, apparel and fuel slightly more expensive than the typical American household would pay, even though housing remains the most affordable element of the budget. The median household income of $79,104 places Jersey County residents well above the national median of $65,047, providing a solid income base for consumption. Rent at $794 consumes only 12.0 percent of that income, leaving ample discretionary cash flow. The Purchasing Power Index of 80,103.7 reflects the relative ability of local earners to purchase goods and services after accounting for the modest price differentials, suggesting that the typical worker retains a higher real income than a comparable national peer. The local labor market, with an unemployment rate of 4.4 percent, is stable, though the low share of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher (13.5 percent) may limit high‑wage opportunities and keep wage growth modest relative to income gains elsewhere. Over the past decade, consumer costs have trended downward, with the Regional Price Parity (RPP) slipping from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023—a decline of 3.0 percent. In contrast, median household income has risen sharply, climbing 48.5 percent from $53,271 to $79,104, while median rent has increased more slowly, up 17.6 percent from $675 to $794. The divergence shows that wage growth has outpaced housing cost inflation, reinforcing the purchasing power advantage for median earners. The aging population, reflected in a median age of 43.2 years, tends to favor services that are already below the national index, while the modest unemployment rate suggests a stable job market that supports consistent consumption patterns. Prospective residents should budget for a rent payment of roughly $794 per month, which represents a manageable 12 percent of the median household income. Goods, however, require careful planning, as they sit 1.6 percent above the national average, meaning a typical grocery or apparel purchase will cost slightly more than the average American household would pay. Services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are about 7.6 percent cheaper than the national norm, offering savings on routine expenses. The combination of relatively low housing costs, modestly higher goods prices and a strong income base creates a net environment where daily household expenses are generally affordable, though attention to the higher cost of goods is advisable for accurate budgeting.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Jersey County, Illinois
