📊 Local Market Analysis

Harvey County presents a cost of living profile significantly below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 89.9 in 2023, meaning local consumer prices sit roughly 10.1 percent under the U.S. average. This discount is heavily anchored by the services category, which clocks in at a strikingly low 68.6 — over 31 percentage points below parity — encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses that typically weigh heavily on household budgets. Rental costs follow at 90.8, offering modest savings relative to the national median rent of $928, while the goods category, covering groceries, apparel, and fuel, runs closest to the national level at 96.5, acting as the primary friction point preventing an even lower aggregate index. The consumption breakdown reveals a daily economic life where the cost structure is defined by cheap services rather than cheap goods. Residents benefit from substantially lower outlays for recurring service obligations — medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals — which creates a structural advantage for fixed budgets. However, the goods index at 96.5 indicates that trips to the grocery store or gas station deliver far less discount, meaning the "basket of necessities" purchased frequently tracks near national pricing. Consequently, while the overall environment is inexpensive, the marginal savings are concentrated in infrequent or contracted service payments rather than the daily transactional economy, a dynamic that shapes cash flow management for the median household earning $73,269 annually. Purchasing power analysis confirms the income advantage is real and widening. The Purchasing Power Index of 81,493.3 implies the median household commands the equivalent of over $81,000 in national-average goods and services, a premium of roughly $8,200 over the nominal median income. Rent burden sits at a remarkably low 14.6 percent of gross income — the median rent of $891 consumes a fraction of the paycheck compared to national norms — leaving substantial discretionary capacity. Over the last decade, this cushion has expanded: median income surged 42.7 percent from $51,327 to $73,269, while median rent rose only 30.5 percent from $683 to $891, and the overall RPP actually declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 to 89.908, signaling that wage growth has decisively outpaced local inflation. Local demographics reinforce this affordability narrative. An unemployment rate of 4.2 percent suggests a stable, if not tight, labor market supporting those income gains, while a median age of 40.2 years indicates a workforce in prime earning years. The educational attainment figure — 21.1 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher — aligns with an economy likely anchored in skilled trades, healthcare, and manufacturing rather than high-wage knowledge sectors, which helps explain why incomes exceed the national median of $65,047 without triggering the rental inflation seen in degree-dense metros. For a prospective resident, the budgeting takeaway is clear: housing and services offer deep value, but grocery and fuel budgets should be calibrated to near-national prices; the surplus generated by low shelter and service costs represents the primary mechanism for wealth accumulation or lifestyle upgrade in this market.

What this means for residents of Harvey County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 81493.3, a median household income of $73,269 in Harvey County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $59,709,326 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
