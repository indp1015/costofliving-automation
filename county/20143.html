📊 Local Market Analysis

Ottawa County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 96.5, still under the national average but only 3.5 points shy of parity. Services are markedly cheaper, recorded at 68.6, indicating that healthcare, utilities, and dining out cost roughly 31 % less than elsewhere in the country. Rental costs, measured by the housing sub‑index, are 90.8, translating into a median monthly rent of $738—23 % lower than the national median of $928. The combined effect is a cost structure that is modest overall, with the greatest pressure coming from everyday consumables rather than housing or discretionary services. Disposable income calculations reveal a robust purchasing position for the median household. Median annual earnings stand at $76,755, 18 % above the national median of $65,047. After allocating the median rent of $738 per month ($8,856 annually), rent consumes only 11.5 % of household income, well under the conventional 30 % affordability threshold. Subtracting rent leaves roughly $67,900 for all other expenses. When this residual income is weighted against the local price indices, the Purchasing Power Index of 85,370.6—about 15 % higher than the national baseline of 100,000—confirms that residents can acquire more goods and services for each dollar earned than the average American. The low services index further amplifies this advantage, allowing households to stretch discretionary spending on health and recreation. Demographic factors reinforce the affordability narrative. Unemployment is a modest 3.2 %, indicating a tight labor market that supports steady earnings. However, only 18.4 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not demand advanced credentials, yet still command wages above the national median. The median age of 42.5 years points to a mature population likely past early‑career salary growth phases, which aligns with the higher median income and lower rent burden. The combination of low unemployment and a seasoned labor pool contributes to stable household budgets and mitigates cost‑of‑living pressure. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, a 3.3 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Median household income surged from $52,479 to $76,755, a 46.3 % increase, while median rent rose from $606 to $738, a 21.8 % gain. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and housing cost escalation, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. The decoupling of wage growth from price pressures suggests that Ottawa County has become increasingly affordable for its earners. Prospective residents should budget with the knowledge that housing will consume a relatively small slice of income—approximately $739 per month—leaving ample room for groceries, apparel, and fuel, which sit near national price levels. The pronounced discount on services means that utilities, medical visits, and restaurant meals will stretch further than in most markets. A prudent monthly budget might allocate 10–12 % to rent, 15–20 % to transportation and groceries, and the remaining balance to savings, healthcare, and leisure, capitalizing on the county’s low services index and high purchasing power.

What this means for residents of Ottawa County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 85370.6, a median household income of $76,755 in Ottawa County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $65,526,204 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
