📊 Local Market Analysis

Gove County residents experience a cost of living that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 89.9 for 2023 against a U.S. average of 100. The composite figure masks significant variation across expenditure categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 96.5, only marginally cheaper than the national norm, while rental costs come in at 90.8. The dominant discount emerges in services, where healthcare, utilities, and dining out collectively price at 68.6, a full 31.4 points below the national average and the single largest driver of the county's affordability advantage. Daily life leans inexpensive primarily because service-intensive consumption — medical visits, electricity, restaurant meals — costs substantially less than in metropolitan areas, even as households pay near-national prices for tangible goods at the checkout counter. The median household income of $63,203 trails the national median of $65,047 by 2.8 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 70,297.4 reveals that local earnings stretch considerably further once the 89.9 RPP is applied. Rent burden illustrates the gap: median monthly rent of $730 consumes just 13.9 percent of gross income, compared with a national median rent of $928 that would claim a far larger share of the typical paycheck. Labor market conditions reinforce this dynamic — unemployment stands at 3.5 percent, indicating a tight but not overheated market — while demographic factors shape consumption patterns. A median age of 41.7 years and a bachelor's degree attainment rate of 15.4 percent suggest a workforce concentrated in skilled trades, agriculture, and service occupations rather than high-wage knowledge sectors, aligning with an economy where lower service costs benefit households that allocate a disproportionate share of spending to utilities, healthcare, and local amenities. Over the thirteen-year span from 2010 to 2023, the RPP declined from 92.991 to 89.908, a 3.3 percent drop signaling that Gove County became incrementally cheaper relative to the nation even as nominal prices rose elsewhere. Median household income surged 36.3 percent from $46,364 to $63,203, outpacing the 30.6 percent increase in median rent from $559 to $730. Wage growth has not merely kept pace with shelter costs; it has widened the cushion between earnings and housing outlays, a rare trajectory in an era when many rural counties have seen rents accelerate faster than incomes. The divergence suggests that local labor demand — likely tied to agricultural commodity cycles and regional healthcare employment — has supported income gains that exceed the modest inflationary pressure on the county's largest fixed expense. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror national averages, with the 96.5 goods index implying a weekly supermarket trip for a family of four will cost roughly $280 to $310, comparable to suburban Kansas City or Denver. The real savings materialize in recurring service obligations: electricity and natural gas for a 1,500-square-foot home may run $150 to $180 monthly versus $220-plus in larger metros, while a routine primary-care visit or dental cleaning often costs 20 to 30 percent less than the national mean. Dining out follows the same pattern — a mid-range dinner for two rarely exceeds $55. The trade-off appears in limited retail variety and longer drives for specialized medical procedures, but for households prioritizing cash flow over consumption breadth, the arithmetic favors Gove County: after rent, utilities, and groceries, the median earner retains roughly $3,800 per month in discretionary capacity, a figure that would require a $75,000-plus salary in a 100 RPP environment.

What this means for residents of Gove County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70297.4, a median household income of $63,203 in Gove County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $44,430,066 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
