📊 Local Market Analysis

Morgan County residents navigate a cost structure that sits marginally above the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 101.9 for 2023 against a national average of 100. This aggregate figure masks a stark bifurcation in daily expenses: services command a premium of 30.5 percent above the national norm at 130.5, while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run slightly cheaper at 99.2. The most significant relief for household budgets comes from rental costs, which sit 13.2 percent below the national average at an index of 86.8, translating to a median monthly rent of $1,092 compared to the national $928. The services category acts as the primary driver of the county’s elevated composite index, making routine expenditures on healthcare, utilities, and dining disproportionately expensive relative to the rest of the country. A median household income of $75,407 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by roughly 16 percent, yet the purchasing power index of 74,032.9 indicates that local price levels erode a portion of that nominal advantage. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 17.4 percent of income, a figure that suggests housing affordability is a distinct strength, freeing cash flow for the inflated service costs that dominate the local consumption basket. Demographic and labor market characteristics reinforce this dynamic. An unemployment rate of 4.2 percent signals a reasonably tight labor market, though educational attainment remains modest with only 11.4 percent of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 36.9 years points to a workforce in prime earning years, likely concentrated in sectors that do not require advanced credentials but benefit from the region’s industrial or agricultural base. This composition suggests that while wage growth has been robust—median income surged 55.6 percent from $48,450 in 2010 to current levels—the workforce remains vulnerable to shifts in service-sector pricing, particularly healthcare and utilities, which outpace goods inflation. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted upward only modestly, from 100.59 to 101.86, a cumulative increase of 1.3 percent. In contrast, median rents climbed 50.4 percent over the same period, moving from $726 to $1,092, yet income growth of 55.6 percent slightly outpaced that trajectory. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the trade-off: significantly discounted shelter costs against a persistent, structural premium on services. Households should allocate a larger share of monthly expenditure to health insurance, utility bills, and prepared food than they might expect in a typical U.S. county, while enjoying relative insulation from housing cost volatility that plagues metropolitan fronts.

What this means for residents of Morgan County, Colorado
