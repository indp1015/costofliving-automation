📊 Local Market Analysis

Randolph County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 94.6, still modestly under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 61.6. Housing costs, measured by the rental‑cost index, register at 89.9, indicating that rents are roughly 10 % lower than the average American market. The median monthly rent of $646 corroborates this, representing a 30 % discount to the national median of $928. The expense profile is dominated by goods, which account for the highest share of the cost structure, but even that category remains under national levels. Services—healthcare, utilities, and dining—are the primary driver of the county’s affordability, falling to roughly two‑thirds of the national price. Daily life for a typical household is therefore inexpensive relative to most U.S. locales, especially in discretionary spending on services. The low rent burden of 15 % of income underscores the affordability; renters allocate a smaller slice of earnings to housing than the national average, where rent typically consumes about 30 % of median income. Disposable income after covering basic housing and service costs remains modest but above the national average when adjusted for local price levels. The Purchasing Power Index of 57,859.4 points to a real‑income advantage: residents can purchase nearly $8,000 more in goods and services than a median American household with comparable nominal income. With a median household income of $51,551, the county’s workers retain roughly $41,000 in inflation‑adjusted purchasing power after accounting for the lower cost of services and housing, a figure that reflects the combined effect of cheap services and modest rent. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, evidenced by a 2.8 % unemployment rate, well below the national average. However, only 12.1 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age of 44 suggests a mature workforce. The limited educational attainment may constrain high‑skill wage growth, yet the low unemployment keeps labor supply tight, supporting wage stability. The modest educational profile aligns with the lower services index, as demand for premium service offerings is muted. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8 % decline, while median income surged 39.6 % from $36,924 to $51,551. Rental costs rose only 7.1 % in the same period, from $603 to $646. Income growth has outpaced both overall price inflation and rent increases, expanding real purchasing power for residents. Prospective renters should budget $646 for monthly housing, allocate roughly $150–$200 for utilities and basic healthcare, and expect grocery and apparel expenses to hover near national averages but still undercut them. With services costing less than two‑thirds of the national level, discretionary spending on dining and personal care can stretch further, allowing a median household to maintain a comfortable standard of living despite modest nominal wages.

What this means for residents of Randolph County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57859.4, a median household income of $51,551 in Randolph County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $29,827,099 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
