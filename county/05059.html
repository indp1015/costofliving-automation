📊 Local Market Analysis

Hot Spring County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices are only modestly lower than average, reflected in a Goods index of 93.1, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly cheaper at 56.7. Rental costs, measured by the housing component of the RPP, register at 81.9, indicating that a typical lease is roughly 18 % cheaper than the national benchmark. The median monthly rent of $763 confirms this gap, trailing the national median of $928 by $165, or 18 %. The dominant driver of the county’s price structure is the Goods category, which, at 93.1, is the highest sub‑index and accounts for most of the remaining price premium over services. Nonetheless, daily life remains inexpensive because the low service index offsets higher goods prices, and housing costs are also subdued. A household earning the local median income of $52,644 faces a rent burden of 17.4 %—well under the 30 % threshold that defines affordability—leaving roughly $43,000 of pre‑tax earnings for other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 60,642.8 translates to a disposable‑income‑adjusted cost of living that is about 39 % lower than the national average, meaning residents can stretch each dollar farther despite modest wages. Demographically, the county’s labor market is tight, with unemployment at 3.6 %, well below the national rate. However, only 10 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age of 43 suggests a mature workforce that may prioritize stability over high‑growth sectors. The limited educational attainment can constrain wage growth, yet the low unemployment rate and modest rent burden keep household budgets relatively comfortable. The combination of affordable services and housing offsets the higher relative cost of goods, producing a balanced cost profile for a population that is less likely to experience rapid income escalation. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median household income rose from $40,000 to $52,644, a 31.6 % increase. Rental prices climbed 16.1 % from $657 to $763. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rent increases, expanding real purchasing power. For a prospective resident, budgeting should allocate a larger share to groceries and transportation, given the Goods index, while expecting modest utility and healthcare bills. With rent consuming less than one‑fifth of income, the remaining budget can comfortably cover food, fuel and discretionary spending, provided that household size and personal consumption patterns align with the county’s price structure.

What this means for residents of Hot Spring County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 60642.8, a median household income of $52,644 in Hot Spring County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $31,924,796 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
