📊 Local Market Analysis

Rice County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 89.9 against a U.S. average of 100. This discount is not uniform across the consumption basket. Services — encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining — represent the primary deflationary force at an index of 68.6, a full 31.4 points below parity, while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track much closer to the national mean at 96.5. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 90.8, meaning a household paying the local median rent of $717 per month spends roughly 23 percent less on shelter than the national median of $928. The data reveals that daily life is inexpensive primarily because service-intensive categories — medical visits, electricity, restaurant meals — carry substantially lower price tags than in metropolitan corridors, whereas the weekly grocery run or fuel stop offers only marginal savings. That price advantage translates into measurable purchasing power. The median household income of $63,947 falls slightly below the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 71,124.9 indicates that after adjusting for local price levels, the typical earner commands roughly $7,200 more in real goods and services than the nominal wage suggests. Rent burden sits at a remarkably low 13.5 percent of gross income, less than half the 30 percent threshold commonly used to define affordability stress. The labor market reinforces this stability: unemployment stands at 3.3 percent, signaling tight conditions that support wage growth, while a median age of 39.0 and a bachelor's degree attainment rate of 16.0 percent describe a workforce that is experienced but not heavily credentialed, consistent with an economy anchored in agriculture, manufacturing, and service provision rather than knowledge-intensive sectors. Over the past decade, the trajectory has favored the consumer. The overall RPP declined from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, a 3.3 percent drop in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 32.0 percent from $48,460 to $63,947. Median rent increased 39.0 percent over the same span, from $516 to $717, outpacing income growth on a percentage basis but remaining low in absolute terms. Because the rent share of income has stayed contained — the 13.5 percent burden reflects that the dollar increase in rent ($201) was absorbed by a much larger dollar increase in income ($15,487) — real disposable income has expanded meaningfully. The divergence between goods inflation (holding near national rates) and services deflation (widening) suggests the county's cost advantage is structural, rooted in lower labor and overhead costs for local service providers rather than a temporary cyclical dip. A prospective resident should budget for a grocery bill and fuel costs that mirror national averages, but expect healthcare premiums, utility bills, and restaurant checks to run 25 to 35 percent below what they would pay in a typical U.S. city. Housing costs, while rising faster than wages in percentage terms, remain an order of magnitude cheaper than coastal or major metro markets, leaving ample room in the monthly ledger for savings, debt repayment, or discretionary spending. The low rent burden means a household earning the median income retains roughly $4,600 per month after shelter, a cushion that absorbs the near-parity pricing of goods. However, the modest educational attainment and service-sector orientation of the local economy imply that wage growth may remain tethered to productivity gains in traditional industries, so the current purchasing power premium depends on the persistence of the services cost gap rather than rapid income escalation.

What this means for residents of Rice County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 71124.9, a median household income of $63,947 in Rice County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $45,482,240 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
