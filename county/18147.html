📊 Local Market Analysis

Spencer County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 7.9 percent below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 92.1 for 2023. The composite figure masks sharp divergence across categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run only 4.4 percent cheaper than the U.S. average at 95.6, while services — healthcare, utilities, and dining — plummet to 71.3, a 28.7 percent discount that anchors the county's affordability advantage. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 84.7, or 15.3 percent below national levels, reinforcing a profile where daily essentials carry near-average price tags but the service economy operates at a substantial discount. The services component drives the local cost structure downward, creating an environment where a median household income of $70,890 translates into a purchasing power index of 76,973.2 — roughly 18.4 percent above the nominal dollar figure. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 11.8 percent of income, with median monthly rent of $697 comparing favorably against the $928 national median. A worker earning the local median retains significant disposable income after housing, though the goods index near parity means grocery trips and fuel stops deliver less relief than the headline number suggests. The unemployment rate of 3.4 percent signals a tight labor market, yet the 13.4 percent bachelor's degree attainment rate and median age of 44.2 years indicate an economy weighted toward established, non-degreed workers in sectors that may not command premium wages. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP edged down marginally from 93.022 to 92.097, a 1.0 percent decline that barely registers against a 28.3 percent surge in median income from $55,247 to $70,890. Median rent climbed 18.7 percent from $587 to $697 over the same span, meaning wage growth outpaced housing cost acceleration by nearly ten percentage points. This divergence suggests the county's affordability position has strengthened in real terms, though the goods category's stubborn proximity to national averages — 95.6 versus 93.0 a decade ago — implies limited progress on the expenses households encounter most frequently. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and transportation costs that mirror national norms while capturing meaningful savings on healthcare, utilities, and restaurant meals. The low rent burden creates flexibility for debt repayment or savings, but the services discount may reflect limited provider networks or longer travel times for specialized care. With median age above 44 and educational attainment below national averages, the local consumption basket likely skews toward practical necessities over discretionary services, a pattern that reinforces the current price dynamics without guaranteeing their persistence.

What this means for residents of Spencer County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76973.2, a median household income of $70,890 in Spencer County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $54,566,301 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
