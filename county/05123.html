📊 Local Market Analysis

St. Francis County sits 13.2 percentage points below the national cost-of-living baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023. This discount is heavily skewed by a services index of just 56.7 — the lowest component by a wide margin — reflecting substantially cheaper healthcare, utilities, and dining relative to the U.S. average. Goods, at 93.1, track much closer to national levels, meaning groceries, apparel, and fuel offer limited savings. Rental costs, indexed at 81.9, provide moderate relief but do not drive the overall affordability narrative; the county’s cost structure is defined by an unusually deep services discount that makes daily household operations notably inexpensive. The median household income of $41,784 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 48,132.7, effectively granting residents a 15.2% boost in real income after adjusting for local prices. Rent consumes 22.7% of gross income at a median of $791, well below the 30% burden threshold and significantly lighter than the national median rent of $928. However, this purchasing power gain exists against a backdrop of structural labor market weakness: a 7.4% unemployment rate and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of only 7.3% suggest limited high-wage employment density. The median age of 39.7 indicates a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the educational profile constrains upward mobility and wage growth potential. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined marginally from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0% drop in relative price levels, while nominal median income rose 30.1% from $32,105. Median rent, however, accelerated 37.3% from $576, outpacing income growth and compressing the shelter affordability cushion. Although the overall price level has eased slightly, the faster trajectory of housing costs signals that the primary inflationary pressure for households is shelter, not the broader consumption basket where services remain deeply discounted. A prospective resident should budget for near-national prices on tangible goods — food and fuel savings will be marginal — while capturing significant monthly savings on services such as insurance, medical visits, and utility bills. The rent-to-income ratio remains manageable at current levels, but the decade-long trend of rent appreciation exceeding wage gains warrants scrutiny; any further acceleration in housing costs would erode the real income advantage quickly. The local economy’s reliance on lower-wage sectors, evidenced by the education and unemployment metrics, implies that household budget resilience depends heavily on maintaining the current services cost advantage rather than expecting rapid nominal income growth.

What this means for residents of St. Francis County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 48132.7, a median household income of $41,784 in St. Francis County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $20,111,767 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
