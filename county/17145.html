📊 Local Market Analysis

Perry County residents navigate a cost environment that sits marginally below the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) registering 98.8 for 2023 against a national index of 100. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run 1.6 percent above the national average at 101.6, marking the county’s most expensive consumption category, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in at a substantial 7.6 percent discount (92.4). The dominant deflationary force remains rental housing, priced at 80.4 on the index, nearly 20 percent cheaper than the U.S. norm and the primary anchor pulling the overall cost structure down. The consumption breakdown reveals a daily life where discretionary spending on tangible goods carries a slight premium, likely reflecting supply-chain logistics in a rural setting, yet the heavy weight of shelter costs in household budgets renders the overall experience materially affordable. Median monthly rent of $714 consumes only 14.5 percent of the $59,286 median household income, a rent-burden ratio roughly half the 30-percent threshold commonly used to define affordability stress. This dynamic translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 60,035.2, indicating that after adjusting for local price levels, the median household commands real resources modestly exceeding its nominal earnings, a rare advantage compared to the national median income of $65,047 which faces a national median rent of $928. Local demographics frame the economic constraints within which these budgets operate. An unemployment rate of 5.1 percent sits above typical full-employment levels, while educational attainment remains low, with only 8.2 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor market concentrated in lower-wage sectors that caps income growth potential. The median age of 42.2 years signals an aging workforce with limited in-migration of younger, higher-skilled workers, a structural factor that suppresses wage pressure but also limits the upward mobility necessary to outpace even modest inflation in goods. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted downward from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent decline in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 39.6 percent from $42,478 to $59,286. However, median rent accelerated faster, climbing 49.1 percent from $479 to $714, eroding a portion of the real income gains. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is clear: shelter costs remain a profound bargain relative to the broader Midwest, but the weekly grocery bill and fuel stops will feel slightly more expensive than national averages suggest, and the local labor market offers limited insulation against future goods inflation.

What this means for residents of Perry County, Illinois
