📊 Local Market Analysis

Pike County registers an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8, placing the local cost of living marginally below the national baseline, yet the aggregate figure masks a distinct bifurcation in household expenditure patterns. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run 1.6 percent above the U.S. average at 101.6, representing the county’s most expensive consumption category and exerting upward pressure on daily discretionary budgets. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining sit 7.6 percent below the national norm at 92.4, while rental housing costs offer the steepest discount at 80.4, nearly twenty points under the benchmark. This structure creates a consumer environment where recurring fixed costs for shelter are exceptionally low, but the variable basket of tangible necessities remains stubbornly aligned with or slightly above metropolitan pricing, a dynamic that disproportionately affects households with limited flexibility to substitute away from physical goods. The median household income of $58,029 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 10.8 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 58,762.4 indicates that local wages stretch further than the nominal gap suggests, primarily due to the profound rental advantage. Median monthly rent of $659 consumes only 13.6 percent of gross income, a burden roughly half the national standard implied by the $928 median rent, freeing substantial cash flow for other obligations. However, the 14.1 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate and a median age of 42.9 years signal a labor market tilted toward established, lower-wage employment sectors, reflected in a 4.6 percent unemployment rate that sits above the national lows seen in recent cycles. This demographic profile suggests the low housing costs may reflect limited demand pressure rather than pure affordability policy, and the workforce’s lower educational density likely caps earnings growth potential relative to high-skill hubs. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, indicating a subtle deflationary trend in local consumer prices relative to the national basket. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 43.0 percent from $40,588 to $58,029, significantly outpacing the 27.5 percent rise in median rent from $517 to $659. This divergence confirms that wage growth has comfortably absorbed housing inflation, improving the rent-to-income ratio over the decade. Yet the persistence of goods prices above the national index (101.6) suggests that transportation and food supply chains — critical in a rural geography — have not shared in the relative price declines seen in shelter and services, anchoring the cost floor for essential consumption. A prospective resident should budget for a daily expense profile where the savings on lease payments — approximately $269 less per month than the national median rent — are partially offset by grocery and fuel bills that meet or exceed urban averages. The low service cost index (92.4) provides relief on utilities and healthcare access, but the goods premium (101.6) implies that vehicle dependence and food security require careful allocation. With the purchasing power index effectively neutralizing the nominal income deficit, the median household retains discretionary capacity, though the narrow industrial base and elevated unemployment rate introduce income volatility risk that a static budget cannot capture. Households relocating from higher-cost metros will experience immediate shelter arbitrage, but must recalibrate expectations for the price stability of physical necessities.

What this means for residents of Pike County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58762.4, a median household income of $58,029 in Pike County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $34,099,233 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
