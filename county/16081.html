📊 Local Market Analysis

Teton County, Idaho, registers a cost‑of‑living profile that sits comfortably below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 92.2 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 95.9, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 86.9. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable pillar at 68.1, indicating that renters in the county pay roughly 32 % less than the national norm. This pricing structure means that everyday expenditures on groceries, apparel, and fuel are only modestly higher than the countrywide median, whereas utilities, healthcare, and dining out are substantially cheaper, and the rent gap is the widest. The consumption breakdown shows that the modestly elevated goods index drives the local cost structure, but the dominant savings stem from housing. Median monthly rent of $1,196 translates to 15.8 % of the median household income of $90,740, well under the conventional rent‑burden threshold of 30 %. By contrast, the national median rent of $928 consumes about 14.3 % of the national median income of $65,047, but the relative affordability in Teton County is amplified by the higher local earnings. After accounting for rent, a median household retains roughly $84,544 of annual income, or $7,045 per month, for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 98,378.1 underscores that residents can purchase nearly 98 % of the goods and services that a typical American household can buy with a national average income, reflecting a net advantage despite the slightly higher goods prices. Local demographics reinforce this purchasing advantage. With an unemployment rate of 1.5 %, the labor market is exceptionally tight, supporting wage growth and job security. The 34.2 % share of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher signals a skilled workforce that commands premium earnings, contributing to the median income’s 69.7 % rise from $53,474 in 2010 to $90,740 today. The median age of 39.8 years suggests a mature, financially stable population, likely to prioritize stable employment and discretionary spending. These factors combine to create a cost‑of‑living environment where high earnings offset modest price increases, especially in housing. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.2 to 92.2, a 1.0 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the national average. Meanwhile, median household income surged 69.7 %, outpacing the 41.7 % rise in median rent. The disparity between income growth and price inflation signals that wages have more than kept pace with living costs, enhancing real disposable income for residents. Prospective renters should budget for a baseline housing expense of $1,196 per month, allocate roughly $500–$600 for utilities and healthcare (reflecting the 86.9 services index), and anticipate grocery and fuel costs near $400–$500 monthly, given the 95.9 goods index. With rent consuming less than one‑sixth of earnings, the remaining income comfortably covers transportation, education, and leisure, allowing a balanced household budget that leverages the county’s low housing costs and strong earning power.

What this means for residents of Teton County, Idaho

With a Purchasing Power Index of 98378.1, a median household income of $90,740 in Teton County, Idaho has the equivalent buying power of $89,268,288 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 68.1 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
