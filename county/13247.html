📊 Local Market Analysis

Rockdale County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits modestly below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 96.5 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. While services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining carry a significant 11.7 percent discount to the national average at an index of 88.3, the category consuming the largest share of daily expenditure—goods covering groceries, apparel, and fuel—runs nearly at parity with the nation at 97.7. Rental costs, indexed at 92.3, offer relative relief compared to the broader housing crisis, but the goods index identifies the primary driver of the local cost structure; residents feel the national price level most acutely at the checkout counter and the pump, making daily life marginally cheaper than the U.S. average only because service costs are deeply suppressed, not because the weekly shop is a bargain. Purchasing power metrics confirm that the median household retains meaningful real income after local costs are absorbed. With a median household income of $72,349—exceeding the national median of $65,047 by 11.2 percent—and a Purchasing Power Index calculated at 74,977.7, the typical worker commands roughly $9,930 more in real purchasing capacity than the nominal national median suggests. The rent burden stands at a manageable 22.2 percent of gross income, derived from a median monthly rent of $1,341 against the national median of $928, indicating that shelter costs, while 44.5 percent higher in nominal terms than the national average, consume a smaller slice of the local paycheck because wages have scaled more aggressively. This dynamic suggests disposable income is robust for those employed locally, though the 5.8 percent unemployment rate signals that access to those wages is not universal. Demographic and labor market characteristics frame the affordability experience. A median age of 39.2 years places the county in prime earning years, yet an educational attainment rate of just 17.6 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher implies a workforce concentrated in sectors where wage growth may not consistently outpace goods inflation. The unemployment rate of 5.8 percent, sitting above typical full-employment thresholds, introduces income volatility for a segment of the population that the aggregate median income figure obscures; for those households, the 97.7 goods index represents a binding constraint rather than a minor discount. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP drifted upward only 2.0 percent, moving from 94.574 to 96.494, a pace that signals relative price stability compared to many Sun Belt metros. However, nominal median income surged 43.4 percent from $50,455 to $72,349, while median rent accelerated 46.4 percent from $916 to $1,341. Rent growth has marginally outpaced income growth over the decade, narrowing the shelter affordability cushion, though the current 22.2 percent burden ratio remains healthy by historical standards. Prospective residents should budget for grocery and fuel bills that track the national average closely, anticipate utility and healthcare costs that run roughly 12 percent below typical U.S. cities, and recognize that while the median rent demands $1,341 monthly, the local labor market’s elevated unemployment rate and lower college attainment rate suggest income stability varies significantly across the workforce.

What this means for residents of Rockdale County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 74977.7, a median household income of $72,349 in Rockdale County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $54,245,616 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
