📊 Local Market Analysis

Muhlenberg County sits 10.1 percent below the national price level with an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9, masking a stark bifurcation in household expenditure categories. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run only 5.5 percent below the U.S. average at 94.5, representing the highest cost component in the local basket. Conversely, services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 62.9, a staggering 37.1 percent discount to the national baseline. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 80.9, roughly 19 percent cheaper than the national median rent of $928, settling locally at $712 per month. This structure renders daily life inexpensive for service-intensive households but offers limited relief on the tangible goods that dominate weekly shopping trips. The median household income of $52,672 trails the national figure of $65,047 by 19 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 58,596 reveals that the local cost advantage effectively boosts real income by roughly $5,900 annually. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 16.2 percent of gross income, less than half the standard 30 percent affordability threshold, freeing significant cash flow for transportation, food, and discretionary spending. However, the goods-heavy consumption profile means residents capture less of that advantage at the checkout counter; a dollar spent on groceries or gasoline stretches only marginally further here than in a major metro, while savings on healthcare visits or utility bills accrue more substantially. Demographic and labor indicators frame the economic constraints within which these budgets operate. An unemployment rate of 4.1 percent suggests a tight labor market, yet educational attainment remains low with only 7.1 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, likely capping wage growth in higher-paying service sectors. The median age of 42.8 years indicates an established, aging workforce with potentially fixed healthcare needs — a category where the 62.9 service index provides meaningful relief. This combination of modest educational credentials and an older population creates a consumer base highly sensitive to goods inflation but insulated from the service-price spikes plaguing coastal markets. Over the past decade, the RPP has remained remarkably stable, slipping just 0.6 percent from 90.4 to 89.9, while nominal median income surged 35.2 percent from $38,961 and median rent rose 34.1 percent from $531. Wage growth has effectively matched shelter cost escalation, preserving the low rent-burden ratio, but the persistent elevation of the goods index near 95 means the bulk of weekly variable expenses — fuel, food, clothing — have not benefited from the same relative price stability. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly national in magnitude while enjoying substantial discounts on rent, utilities, and medical services, a dynamic that favors households with low service consumption but high mobility and nutritional needs.

What this means for residents of Muhlenberg County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58596.1, a median household income of $52,672 in Muhlenberg County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $30,863,738 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
