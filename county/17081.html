📊 Local Market Analysis

Jefferson County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark, with an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index of 98.8—meaning consumer prices are roughly 1.2 % cheaper than the average U.S. basket. Goods and services diverge sharply from this average: the goods component (groceries, apparel, fuel) registers at 101.6, indicating a 1.6 % premium over the national baseline, while services (healthcare, utilities, dining) come in at 92.4, a 7.6 % discount. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable segment at 80.4, suggesting rent is about 19.6 % lower than the national median. This pattern shows that daily expenses are modestly inexpensive overall, but the higher price tag on goods offsets the savings in shelter and services. The consumption breakdown is driven primarily by the goods category, which is the highest cost component in the local index. Although housing is the cheapest of the three pillars, the elevated cost of everyday items makes routine spending relatively expensive for households. Services remain the most affordable, providing some relief for households that rely heavily on healthcare, utilities, and dining out. Consequently, residents experience a mixed cost environment: they benefit from low rent and utility expenses but face above‑average prices for food, clothing, and fuel, which can strain discretionary budgets. Purchasing power in Jefferson County is reflected by the median household income of $61,102, about 6 % below the national median of $65,047. The Purchasing Power Index of 61,874.2 underscores that, after adjusting for the local price structure, the median earner retains a comparable share of real income to the national average. With rent consuming 16.9 % of income—$862 monthly against a $61,102 annual figure—housing costs are well within a comfortable range. The relatively low rent burden, combined with cheaper services, leaves a larger portion of disposable income for other expenditures, despite the higher cost of goods. Local demographics shape this cost profile in notable ways. Only 11.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, a level that tends to correlate with lower‑wage employment opportunities and can suppress overall income growth. The median age of 41.1 years suggests a mature population that may prioritize stable, lower‑cost housing and essential services over discretionary spending. Meanwhile, an unemployment rate of 6.6 % introduces modest pressure on wage growth, keeping household incomes in check even as the broader economy expands. These labor‑market conditions help explain why wages have risen but not at a pace that fully matches the higher price of goods. Trend analysis reveals a favorable shift for local residents. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 101.778 to 98.752, a 3.0 % decline that indicates a modest reduction in overall consumer costs. Median household income surged 41.3 % from $43,247 to $61,102, outpacing the 46.1 % increase in median rent ($590 to $862). Although rent rose faster than income, the broader cost index moved downward, suggesting that non‑housing expenses have become relatively cheaper. The net effect is an improvement in real purchasing power, with households retaining more income after essential expenses. For prospective residents, budgeting should prioritize the higher cost of goods, which now represent the most significant expense category. Allocating roughly 10‑12 % of the median income to groceries, apparel, and fuel is realistic, while housing costs remain the most affordable component of the budget. Services, including healthcare and utilities, are comparatively low, offering additional savings. By recognizing that rent consumes less than one‑fifth of earnings and that overall living costs are slightly below the national average, new households can plan a balanced budget that accounts for the premium on everyday items while taking advantage of the county’s affordable rental market.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Jefferson County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 61874.2, a median household income of $61,102 in Jefferson County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $37,806,374 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
