📊 Local Market Analysis

Washington County residents operate within a cost structure that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet the composition of that index reveals distinct friction points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—represent the primary inflationary pressure at 101.6, pushing daily consumables 1.6 percent above the U.S. average, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining provide significant relief at 92.4. The dominant deflationary force remains rental housing, priced at 80.4, a full 19.6 percent below the national level, which anchors the overall index despite the premium paid at the checkout counter and the pump. This rental discount fundamentally reshapes the local consumption profile, driving the median gross rent to $776 against a national median of $928 and compressing the median rent burden to just 12.3 percent of household income. Consequently, the median household income of $75,652—already 16.3 percent above the national median of $65,047—translates into exceptional purchasing power, quantified by a Purchasing Power Index of 76,608.1. A worker earning the local median effectively commands a real income surplus relative to national price levels because the largest fixed expense, shelter, consumes a disproportionately small share of the paycheck, freeing discretionary capacity for the above-average goods costs or savings accumulation. Demographic and labor market characteristics reinforce this dynamic. An unemployment rate of 4.1 percent signals a reasonably tight labor market supporting wage growth, while a median age of 44.2 years and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of 16.4 percent suggest a workforce concentrated in established, non-degree-intensive occupations such as skilled trades, healthcare support, and public administration. These sectors typically offer stable compensation without the volatility of high-tech hubs, aligning with an environment where housing supply has remained adequate enough to prevent the rent spikes seen in metropolitan corridors, thereby preserving the low shelter-cost advantage. Over the 2010–2023 period, the economic trajectory has favored the resident earner decisively. The RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 to 98.752, indicating that local consumer prices grew slower than the national average, while nominal median income surged 42.2 percent from $53,202 to $75,652. Median rent increased only 26.2 percent over the same span, lagging income growth by a substantial 16 percentage points. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is clear: allocate roughly 1.5 to 2 percent more of the weekly budget for groceries and fuel than in a typical U.S. county, but expect to direct roughly 7 to 8 percent less of gross income toward rent, creating a structural surplus that defines the county’s standard of living.

What this means for residents of Washington County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76608.1, a median household income of $75,652 in Washington County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $57,955,560 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
