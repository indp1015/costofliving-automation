📊 Local Market Analysis

Montezuma County’s cost‑of‑living index sits just above the national benchmark at 101.9, indicating that the average consumer price level is 1.9 % higher than the United States overall. The goods component, measured at 99.2, is marginally below the national average, suggesting that groceries, apparel and fuel are priced roughly 0.8 % cheaper than elsewhere. Services, however, are markedly higher at 130.5, a 30.5 % premium that drives the county’s overall cost structure. Housing, represented by rental costs, is the most affordable element with an index of 86.8, meaning rents are 13.2 % lower than the national norm. This combination produces a mixed picture: everyday necessities such as food and fuel are modestly inexpensive, but health care, utilities and dining out impose a substantial surcharge that outweighs the housing advantage. The service premium dominates household budgeting. With median monthly rent at $974—$46 above the national median of $928—but a rent‑burden ratio of only 18.6 % of income, housing costs consume a relatively small slice of earnings. By contrast, the 130.5 service index translates into higher out‑of‑pocket expenses for medical care, electricity, water and restaurant meals. For a median household earning $63,005 annually, the Purchasing Power Index of 61,856.9 reveals that after accounting for the county’s price levels, residents retain roughly 98 % of the purchasing power of a typical American household (national index = 100). In dollar terms, the median family’s disposable income after rent and typical service expenses is approximately $45,000, compared with a national disposable estimate near $47,000. The gap reflects the service surcharge more than housing savings, confirming that daily life feels expensive in categories that affect health and leisure, while basic consumables remain affordable. Local demographics reinforce this cost profile. An unemployment rate of 4.5 % aligns closely with the national average, indicating a stable labor market, yet only 18.7 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, well below the national proportion. The median age of 44.5 years points to a mature population, often associated with higher health‑care utilization, which dovetails with the elevated services index. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity rose modestly from 100.59 to 101.856 (+1.3 %), while median household income surged 44.7 % from $43,553 to $63,005. Rent increased 25.7 % in the same period, from $775 to $974. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rental escalation, suggesting that wages have generally kept pace with, and even exceeded, rising costs. Prospective residents should allocate a larger share of their budget to health‑related and utility expenses than to food or clothing, anticipate a rent payment near $1,000 per month, and recognize that despite lower housing costs, the high service index will compress discretionary spending. A realistic monthly budget might reserve $300‑$350 for utilities and health care, $200 for groceries, $150 for transportation, and $250‑$300 for dining and personal services, leaving approximately $1,200 for savings, debt repayment or other variable costs. This allocation reflects the county’s unique price composition and ensures that households can maintain a comfortable standard of living without excessive rent strain.

What this means for residents of Montezuma County, Colorado

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
