📊 Local Market Analysis

Logan County residents navigate a cost structure that sits marginally above the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) registering 101.9 for 2023 against a national index of 100. This aggregate figure masks a stark bifurcation in household budgets: while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track slightly below average at 99.2, and rental housing costs offer significant relief at 86.8, the services category—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—surges to 130.5, representing the single largest inflationary pressure on local disposable income. Consequently, daily life is defined by cheap shelter and affordable commodities offset by disproportionately expensive essential services, a dynamic that forces households to allocate a larger share of their budget toward non-discretionary service consumption than peers in lower-cost metros. The median household income of $55,074 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 15.3 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 54,070.5 indicates that the lower cost environment partially bridges this nominal gap. Rent burden stands at a manageable 22.4 percent of gross income, with median monthly rent of $1,027 comparing favorably against the national $928 when adjusted for local income levels. However, the 130.5 services index suggests that savings on housing are likely absorbed by healthcare premiums, utility bills, and transportation services, compressing true discretionary spending. The labor market context reinforces this constraint: a 5.6 percent unemployment rate exceeds typical full-employment thresholds, and an educational attainment rate of just 11.3 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher signals a workforce concentrated in lower-wage sectors, limiting upward pressure on earnings relative to service-sector inflation. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP drifted upward a modest 1.3 percent, moving from 100.59 to 101.856, suggesting relative price stability in the aggregate. Beneath this stability, however, a significant divergence emerged: median income rose 30.1 percent from $42,319 to $55,074, while median rent accelerated 52.6 percent from $673 to $1,027. Although rent remains below the national RPP benchmark, its growth trajectory has outpaced wage gains by a factor of nearly 1.75, eroding the historical affordability advantage. If service costs continue to track at 30 percent above national averages, the gap between nominal wage growth and the true cost of maintaining a household standard of living will likely widen, particularly for the 39.1-year median age cohort entering peak earning years without the credential premium associated with higher education attainment. A prospective resident should budget for a household ledger where housing savings are real but insufficient to offset service-sector premiums. The 86.8 rental index translates to tangible monthly relief, yet the 130.5 services index implies that a routine medical visit, monthly utility bundle, or restaurant meal carries a structural surcharge relative to the national norm. With goods pricing near parity at 99.2, grocery and fuel budgets will feel familiar, but the overall consumption basket remains tilted toward high-cost services. Households relocating from lower-service-cost regions should stress-test budgets against healthcare and utility estimates rather than relying on rent differentials alone, as the purchasing power advantage of 54,070.5 is heavily contingent on consumption patterns that minimize service intensity.

What this means for residents of Logan County, Colorado
