📊 Local Market Analysis

DeKalb County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits 7.9 percent below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 92.1 for 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run only 4.4 percent below the national average at 95.6, while rental costs sit 15.3 percent lower at 84.7. The most dramatic deviation appears in services, where healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses plummet to 71.3, a full 28.7 percent discount to the U.S. mean. This structural composition indicates that daily life is fundamentally inexpensive, driven primarily by a services sector that costs roughly three-quarters of the national rate, offsetting the relatively modest savings on tangible goods. The consumption breakdown reveals that the services category acts as the primary anchor suppressing the overall index, creating a budget environment where recurring monthly outlays for healthcare and utilities exert far less pressure than in typical metropolitan areas. Median monthly rent of $828 consumes just 14.2 percent of the $70,080 median household income, a rent-burden ratio that signals exceptional housing affordability relative to national norms where the median rent of $928 claims a larger share of a lower $65,047 median income. Translating these dynamics into real purchasing power, the Purchasing Power Index of 76,093.7 implies the median household commands roughly $6,000 more in real goods and services than their nominal income suggests, effectively granting residents a 9.3 percent raise in living standard purely through geographic arbitrage. Local demographics reinforce this affordability narrative while sketching a labor market characterized by moderate specialization. An unemployment rate of 4.1 percent sits near structural norms, suggesting a balanced labor market without acute slack or overheating. Educational attainment remains modest, with only 14.1 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, a factor that likely caps wage growth in high-skill sectors but simultaneously suppresses service-sector pricing through lower labor input costs. The median age of 38.4 years indicates a workforce in its prime earning years, supporting the observed income trajectory without the fiscal drag of a rapidly aging dependency ratio. Over the 2010 to 2023 window, the RPP drifted marginally lower from 93.0 to 92.1, a negligible 1.0 percent decline that signals remarkable price stability. In contrast, nominal median household income surged 45.0 percent from $48,341 to $70,080, vastly outpacing the 26.0 percent climb in median rent from $657 to $828. This divergence confirms that wage growth has not merely kept pace with shelter costs but has expanded the real resource envelope for the median earner. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between categories: grocery and fuel bills will feel familiar to national transplants, while healthcare premiums, utility bills, and restaurant tabs will arrive at a steep discount. The primary financial risk lies not in runaway housing costs but in the potential volatility of goods prices—specifically energy and food—which remain tethered to national supply chains and offer less local insulation than the deeply discounted service economy.

What this means for residents of DeKalb County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76093.7, a median household income of $70,080 in DeKalb County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $53,326,465 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
