📊 Local Market Analysis

Izard County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, reflected in an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distorted consumer landscape where the price of tangible goods barely registers a discount while services plunge to 56.7 on the index. The Goods component, encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel, carries an RPP of 93.1, meaning households pay nearly full national freight for daily necessities despite the rural setting; conversely, the Services category—healthcare, utilities, and dining—collapses to nearly half the national price level, a dynamic that suppresses the headline index but offers limited relief to budgets weighted heavily toward physical consumption. Rental costs, indexed at 81.9, provide moderate shelter savings, though the median monthly rent of $626 still consumes 15.7 percent of the $47,728 median household income, a burden that appears manageable only because the denominator remains 26.6 percent below the $65,047 national median. The purchasing power calculus translates that income into a real value of $54,979.80, effectively granting the median household an $7,251.80 arbitrage over their nominal earnings, but this gain is almost entirely attributable to the depressed Services index rather than broad-based affordability. A household allocating a standard share of expenditure to groceries and transportation experiences prices only 6.9 percent cheaper than the national baseline, while the steep discount on services—often driven by limited provider density and lower wage inputs for local labor—does not proportionally reduce out-of-pocket costs for essential goods. The labor market context reinforces this friction: a 7.0 percent unemployment rate paired with a 12.8 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate signals a workforce concentrated in lower-wage sectors, limiting the ability of households to leverage the purchasing power premium for wealth accumulation or discretionary spending. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative prices, while nominal median income surged 46.3 percent from $32,630 to $47,728, significantly outpacing the 23.2 percent rise in median rent from $508 to $626. This divergence suggests that, on paper, local wage growth has absorbed housing inflation comfortably, yet the persistent gap between the Goods RPP (93.1) and the national average implies that the real cost of maintaining a standard consumption basket has not fallen in step with the headline index. The aging demographic profile, with a median age of 47.5 years, further complicates the outlook; older households typically weight healthcare and utilities more heavily—categories where the local index is exceptionally low—but may face access constraints that nominal price data fails to capture. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on a trade-off: shelter and services are genuinely inexpensive, but the weekly trip to the grocery store and the fuel required to navigate a sparse retail environment deliver minimal savings. A household relocating from a metro area should not extrapolate the 13.2 percent overall discount to their entire budget; instead, they should model expenses assuming near-national pricing for goods and fuel, while capturing significant discounts on rent and utilities. The low rent burden of 15.7 percent frees cash flow for transportation and healthcare access costs, which are likely higher in time and distance than the RPP suggests, making vehicle reliability and fuel efficiency critical line items in any household ledger.

What this means for residents of Izard County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 54979.8, a median household income of $47,728 in Izard County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $26,240,759 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
