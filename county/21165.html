📊 Local Market Analysis

Menifee County sits 10.1 percentage points below the national price level, with a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 89.9, yet the aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in household spending. Goods — groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 94.5, only a modest discount to the U.S. average, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 62.9, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs settle at 80.9, meaning a median monthly rent of $634 claims just 17.0 percent of the $44,672 median household income, a burden far lighter than the national benchmark where $928 median rent consumes a larger share of a $65,047 median income. The cost structure is therefore defined not by housing pressure but by the relative expense of tangible goods, which anchors daily outlays closer to parity even as service-intensive categories deliver deep savings. That dynamic translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 49,696.3, effectively granting the median earner roughly $5,000 in additional real income compared with the nominal figure. The local labor market, however, frames this advantage within constraints: a 4.9 percent unemployment rate sits above the national norm, educational attainment remains thin at 7.3 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and a median age of 45.1 years signals an older, likely fixed-income population. These demographics suggest limited wage mobility and a reliance on transfer payments or service-sector employment that rarely scales with productivity gains, so the purchasing power cushion, while material, may be fragile for households facing unexpected medical or vehicle expenses. Over the 2010–2023 window, the RPP barely moved, slipping from 90.433 to 89.89, a negligible 0.6 percent decline, while nominal median income surged 41.8 percent from $31,503 to $44,672 and median rent rose only 24.1 percent from $511 to $634. Real income growth has therefore been substantial, outpacing both the national inflation trajectory and local shelter costs. The divergence reflects a county where wage gains — possibly driven by minimum-wage adjustments, commuter earnings, or sectoral shifts — have not been absorbed by housing inflation, preserving a widening gap between earnings and the cost of keeping a roof overhead. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the goods-services split. Grocery trips and fuel fills will feel familiar to anyone relocating from a metro area, with prices only 5.5 percent below the national index, whereas utility bills, restaurant meals, and outpatient care deliver consistent 37 percent discounts. Rent consumes a low share of income, but the low educational attainment and elevated unemployment rate imply that job loss or hours reduction could erode the rent-to-income ratio quickly. Households should allocate contingency funds for goods-price volatility — particularly energy and food — because the service-side savings, while structural, cannot offset a spike at the pump or the checkout lane.

What this means for residents of Menifee County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 49696.3, a median household income of $44,672 in Menifee County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $22,200,331 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
