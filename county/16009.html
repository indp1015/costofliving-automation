📊 Local Market Analysis

Benewah County residents experience a cost of living that is modestly below the national benchmark, with an overall index of 92.2—meaning consumer prices are roughly 8 percent lower than the U.S. average. The breakdown of that index reveals a nuanced picture: goods (including groceries, apparel and fuel) sit at 95.9, slightly above the national baseline, while services (healthcare, utilities and dining) are markedly cheaper at 86.9, and housing (rental costs) is the most affordable component at 68.1. Compared with national median rent of $928, local monthly rent averages $853, reinforcing the relatively low cost of shelter in the county. The consumption profile shows that goods drive the local cost structure. Because the goods index exceeds 100, households must allocate a larger share of their budget to everyday items such as food and clothing. In contrast, services are inexpensive, and housing is the cheapest category, which offsets some of the higher goods expenses. As a result, daily life is mixed: residents pay a premium for essentials but benefit from low rent and utility costs, creating a balanced but not uniformly cheap experience. Median household income of $56,553 falls short of the national median of $65,047, yet the rent burden of 18.1 percent—derived from $853 monthly rent—remains well within typical affordability thresholds. Local demographics shape the cost‑of‑living experience. Only 13.1 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age is 46 years, suggesting a workforce that may be concentrated in lower‑skill, stable occupations. The unemployment rate of 3.9 percent reflects a tight labor market, but the limited educational attainment can constrain higher‑wage opportunities, keeping income growth moderate. These factors contribute to a consumer environment where wages are steady but not rapidly expanding. Over the past decade, the regional price parity (RPP) has edged down by 1.0 percent to 92.236, while median income has risen by 41.9 percent to $56,553 and median rent has increased by 30.0 percent to $853. The data indicate that earnings have outpaced rent growth, helping to preserve purchasing power despite a slight decline in the overall cost‑of‑living index. Prospective residents should budget for higher‑than‑average goods expenses, anticipate affordable rental housing, and plan for moderate service costs while recognizing that the local labor market offers stable but modestly compensated employment opportunities.
