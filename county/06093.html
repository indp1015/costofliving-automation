📊 Local Market Analysis

Siskiyou County’s cost of living sits at 112.2 on the BEA RPP scale, meaning consumer prices are roughly 12 percent above the national benchmark of 100. The services component leads the local price structure at 157.8, followed closely by housing (rental costs) at 147.3, while goods remain modestly elevated at 106.8. This pattern indicates that everyday expenses are driven more by service‑related items—such as healthcare, utilities, and dining—than by the purchase of tangible goods. Among the three major categories, services dominate the cost profile, accounting for the highest index value and therefore imposing the greatest pressure on household budgets. Even though rental costs are also well above the national average, they rank just behind services, suggesting that a typical resident’s monthly outlay for housing, while significant, is slightly less burdensome than the combined expense of utilities, medical care, and restaurant meals. Consequently, daily life in Siskiyou County is relatively expensive, not because of grocery bills or clothing, but because the cost of maintaining a standard quality of life through services exceeds the national norm by nearly 58 percent. The median household income of $55,499 provides a baseline for purchasing power, yet the rent burden of 22.6 percent—$1,043 per month—already consumes a sizable share of that earnings. The Purchasing Power Index of 49,466.6 reflects the relative ability of local residents to afford goods and services after accounting for cost‑of‑living adjustments, and it underscores that, despite a higher-than‑average income, the elevated service costs erode disposable income. Compared with the national median income of $65,047, Siskiyou households earn less, and the higher service index further reduces their effective purchasing capacity. Local demographics shape this economic picture in several ways. Only 15.8 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, a figure that likely limits the presence of high‑skill, higher‑wage occupations and may contribute to the 7.7 percent unemployment rate. The median age of 47.4 years suggests a population that tends to be older, which can increase demand for healthcare and other service provisions while potentially reducing the labor force participation rate. Together, these factors reinforce a labor market that offers fewer premium employment opportunities and sustains upward pressure on service prices. Over the past decade, consumer costs have risen modestly, with the RPP increasing from 109.703 in 2010 to 112.195 in 2023—a gain of 2.3 percent. In contrast, median household income has grown substantially, climbing from $37,170 to $55,499, a 49.3 percent increase, while median rent has risen from $806 to $1,043, a 29.4 percent jump. This divergence shows that wages have outpaced the growth of living expenses, offering some relief for residents whose earnings are tied to local job markets. However, the persistent premium in services means that even with higher incomes, households must allocate a larger portion of their budgets to maintain their standard of living. Prospective residents should budget for a cost structure where services dominate monthly outlays, with rent representing roughly one‑fifth of total income. While goods remain relatively affordable, planning for higher utility bills, medical expenses, and dining costs is essential. The unemployment rate, though moderate, suggests the need for a financial cushion, and the relatively low educational attainment level indicates that income growth may be uneven across sectors. Understanding these dynamics enables realistic household budgeting and highlights the importance of monitoring local labor market conditions when evaluating long‑term residency.

What this means for residents of Siskiyou County, California

With a Purchasing Power Index of 49466.6, a median household income of $55,499 in Siskiyou County, California has the equivalent buying power of $27,453,468 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 147.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
