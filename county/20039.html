📊 Local Market Analysis

Decatur County, Kansas presents a cost of living profile that sits 10.1 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 89.9 in 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run only 3.5 percent below the national baseline at 96.5, while rental costs track 9.2 percent lower at 90.8. The dominant force suppressing the overall index is the services component—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—which clocks in at a striking 68.6, or 31.4 percent below the national average. This structural anomaly suggests that while residents pay near-national prices for tangible necessities at the register, the intangible overhead of daily life, particularly medical services and utility provision, carries a substantial discount relative to metropolitan benchmarks. The consumption breakdown reveals that goods act as the primary anchor preventing the cost structure from falling further, functioning as the highest-cost component in the local basket. A household allocating a standard share of expenditures to food, fuel, and clothing experiences pricing pressure nearly equivalent to the national norm, meaning the perceived "cheapness" of the county is largely confined to service-intensive categories. Consequently, daily life is not uniformly inexpensive; a resident filling a prescription or contracting a repair service captures significant savings, but the weekly grocery run or fuel stop offers minimal arbitrage. This dynamic creates a bifurcated budget reality where discretionary service consumption is highly affordable, but non-discretionary goods consumption remains sticky. Purchasing power metrics confirm that median income stretches considerably further here than the raw dollar figure suggests. The median household income of $52,030 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 57,870.3, effectively granting residents a 11.2 percent real income premium over their nominal earnings. Housing affordability underscores this advantage: median monthly rent of $695 consumes only 16.0 percent of gross income, a burden roughly half the national standard where median rent of $928 weighs heavier against a national median income of $65,047. This low shelter cost acts as the primary engine of disposable income retention, freeing capital for savings or goods consumption that carries a higher relative price tag. Demographic and labor market context sharpens the picture of who benefits from this structure. With a median age of 52.1 years and only 21.9 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the population skews older and less credentialed than the national workforce, yet unemployment sits at a tight 3.7 percent. This suggests a labor market reliant on established, possibly trade-based or public-sector employment rather than high-wage knowledge industries. Over the 2010–2023 period, nominal median income rose 28.0 percent from $40,657 while median rent increased 24.8 percent from $557, indicating wages have modestly outpaced shelter costs. Simultaneously, the overall RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991, meaning the cost of living actually fell in real terms. For a prospective resident, the budgeting takeaway is clear: allocate near-national budgets for groceries and fuel, expect significant relief on rent and medical services, and recognize that the local wage floor supports this arithmetic only if household earnings align with the county’s $52,000 median.

What this means for residents of Decatur County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57870.3, a median household income of $52,030 in Decatur County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $30,109,917 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
