📊 Local Market Analysis

Ketchikan Gateway Borough’s cost of living sits modestly above the national baseline, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Prices for everyday goods—groceries, apparel, and fuel—track closely at 103.7, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are actually cheaper than the national average at 96.7. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the outlier, registering an index of 113.3, the highest component in the local price basket. The rental market drives the borough’s cost structure. Median monthly rent of $1,388 exceeds the national median of $928 by 49.5%, and rent consumes 18.7% of median household income. Even though goods are only 3.7% pricier and services are 3.3% cheaper than the nation, the elevated housing expense pushes the overall cost of living above average. For a typical household, $1,388 in rent leaves $5,592 of the median $89,155 annual income for all other expenditures, equating to a disposable income share of roughly 81% after housing. The Purchasing Power Index of 86,309.4 reflects this balance: residents enjoy higher earnings but the premium on rent erodes a portion of that advantage, yielding purchasing power that is still above the national median income level but tempered by housing costs. Local demographics reinforce the cost picture. Unemployment sits at a low 3.5%, indicating a relatively tight labor market, while only 18.2% of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not command premium wages. The median age of 40.8 years points to a mature population, often associated with higher household formation and stable income streams, yet the limited educational attainment may constrain upward wage mobility. Together, these factors create a scenario where incomes are robust—median household income is $24,108 (38.8%) above the national median—but the earnings are largely allocated to housing, leaving less flexibility for discretionary spending. Over the past decade, the RPP rose marginally from 103.099 to 103.297, a 0.2% increase, indicating that general price pressures have been relatively flat. In contrast, median household income surged 38.8% from $64,222 to $89,155, and median rent climbed 34.4% from $1,033 to $1,388. Wage growth has outpaced overall price inflation, but the gap between income and rent growth remains narrow, meaning the affordability gain from higher earnings is largely offset by steeper housing costs. Prospective residents should budget rent as the dominant expense, allocate roughly 19% of gross income to housing, and anticipate that everyday goods will be only slightly pricier than elsewhere while services may be modestly cheaper. Planning for a household budget that accommodates a higher rent burden, while leveraging the comparatively lower cost of services, will be essential for maintaining a comfortable standard of living in Ketchikan Gateway Borough.

What this means for residents of Ketchikan Gateway Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 86309.4, a median household income of $89,155 in Ketchikan Gateway Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $76,949,146 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
