📊 Local Market Analysis

Ketchikan Gateway Borough residents face a cost of living 3.3 percent above the national baseline, driven almost entirely by rental housing costs that sit 13.3 percent higher than the U.S. average. The BEA’s 2023 Regional Price Parity index shows goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel running 3.7 percent above national levels, while services — including healthcare, utilities, and dining — actually come in 3.3 percent below the benchmark at 96.7. This divergence makes daily life a study in contrasts: a household’s variable expenses for food and fuel are moderately elevated, but the fixed burden of shelter dominates the budget. With median monthly rent at $1,388 versus a national median of $928, housing consumes 18.7 percent of the local median household income of $89,155, a ratio that remains manageable only because wages here significantly outpace the national median of $65,047. The purchasing power index of 86,309.4 reflects this dynamic, indicating that after adjusting for local prices, the median household retains real purchasing power roughly equivalent to $86,309 in national-average dollars — a 3.2 percent haircut from nominal income. Local labor market conditions reinforce this cushion: a 3.5 percent unemployment rate signals tight demand for workers, while a median age of 40.8 and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 18.2 percent suggest an economy reliant on experienced trades and service-sector roles rather than high-skill knowledge work. That composition likely limits upward wage pressure for entry-level positions but supports the above-average median income that keeps rent burdens from becoming acute. Over the past thirteen years, the overall RPP has been remarkably stable, inching up just 0.2 percent from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023. In that same span, median household income surged 38.8 percent from $64,222 to $89,155, while median rent climbed 34.4 percent from $1,033 to $1,388. Wage growth has not only kept pace with shelter costs but has modestly outrun them, improving the rent-to-income ratio slightly over the decade. The stability of the broader price index suggests that non-housing inflation has been contained, allowing income gains to translate into genuine standard-of-living improvements rather than being eroded by across-the-board price increases. A prospective resident should budget for a housing premium that is immediate and unavoidable: rent runs roughly $460 above the national median each month, and that gap persists regardless of lifestyle choices. Grocery and fuel bills will be roughly 4 percent higher, but utility and healthcare costs offer a modest offset at 3 percent below average. The 18.7 percent rent-to-income ratio assumes the median earner; households earning below $75,000 would see that burden exceed 22 percent, a threshold where discretionary spending compresses noticeably. With services cheaper than the national norm, families can partially recoup housing costs through lower medical and utility outlays, but the calculus hinges on securing income at or above the local median to maintain the purchasing power advantage the data currently shows.

What this means for residents of Ketchikan Gateway Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 86309.4, a median household income of $89,155 in Ketchikan Gateway Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $76,949,146 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
