📊 Local Market Analysis

Pope County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—are 93.1, still under the national level but the closest to parity among the three components. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheaper at 56.7, reflecting a 43 % discount relative to the country average. Rental costs, measured by the housing index, register at 81.9, meaning a typical apartment costs roughly 18 % less than the national benchmark. The median monthly rent of $855 further underscores affordability, trailing the national median of $928 by $73, or 7.9 %. The consumption structure is dominated by goods, which account for the highest relative price index. Although services are substantially cheaper, the modestly elevated goods index offsets some of the savings, producing a mixed affordability profile. Daily life for most residents is inexpensive in the service sector—healthcare visits and utility bills are likely to be a fraction of those in urban centers—yet grocery bills and fuel expenditures remain closer to national levels, limiting the breadth of cost advantage. The rent burden of 18.8 % of household income confirms that housing costs are manageable; the figure sits comfortably below the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag affordability stress. Disposable income calculations reveal that the median household, earning $54,606 annually, retains a purchasing power index of 62,902.9. After deducting the median rent of $855 per month ($10,260 annually), the remaining income is $44,346, or 81 % of pre‑tax earnings. When adjusted for the overall cost‑of‑living index of 86.8, the effective purchasing power translates to roughly $48,400 in national‑equivalent dollars, indicating that a typical family can buy more than its nominal income suggests. This surplus is amplified by the low service costs, allowing residents to allocate a larger share of their budget to discretionary spending or savings. Demographically, the county’s labor market is modestly robust: an unemployment rate of 4.7 % sits just below the national average, and a median age of 36.7 years points to a relatively young workforce. However, only 16.2 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, implying a concentration in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The limited educational attainment may constrain wage growth, yet the current employment environment keeps income levels rising, as evidenced by a 37.2 % increase in median household income since 2010. The combination of steady jobs and affordable services mitigates the impact of lower educational premiums on household budgets. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median income surged 37.2 % from $39,810 to $54,606. Rental prices climbed 33.6 % from $640 to $855. Income growth outpaced both the modest deflation in overall price levels and the rise in rent, suggesting that wages have kept pace with, and even exceeded, the pace of consumer expenses. The gap between income and rent remains wide, preserving affordability despite higher nominal rents. Prospective residents should plan budgets around three pillars: a modest rent of $855, a grocery and fuel bill that aligns closely with national averages, and significantly lower utility and healthcare costs. Allocating roughly 18–20 % of income to housing leaves ample room for goods expenditures, while the cheap services sector frees additional cash for transportation, education, or leisure. A realistic monthly budget might allocate $855 to rent, $400–$500 to groceries and fuel, $200 to utilities and healthcare, and the remainder to discretionary items, preserving a comfortable cushion given the county’s strong purchasing power relative to its cost structure.

What this means for residents of Pope County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 62902.9, a median household income of $54,606 in Pope County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $34,348,758 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
