📊 Local Market Analysis

Atchison County presents a cost of living profile significantly below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 89.9 in 2023 against a national index of 100. This 10.1 percent discount is structurally driven by a pronounced divergence in service costs, which sit at just 68.6 — over 31 points below the national average — while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track much closer to parity at 96.5. Rental costs, indexed at 90.8, occupy a middle ground, offering modest savings relative to the broader market. The data indicates that daily life is distinctly inexpensive regarding recurring service obligations like healthcare, utilities, and dining, though residents realize minimal relief at the checkout counter for tangible goods. The consumption breakdown reveals that the low service index is the primary engine suppressing the overall RPP, effectively decoupling the local cost structure from national inflationary pressures on labor-intensive sectors. However, the goods index at 96.5 signals that supply-chain-dependent expenses — fuel for the 36.4-year-old median resident’s commute, groceries, and household necessities — remain stubbornly aligned with national pricing. This dynamic creates a bifurcated budget reality: a household earning the median $62,164 allocates only 14.6 percent of gross income to median rent of $756, a rent burden roughly half the national standard, yet faces near-national prices for the weekly grocery run or a tank of gas. The Purchasing Power Index of 69,141.8 quantifies this advantage, suggesting the median dollar stretches roughly 11 percent further here than the nominal income gap against the national median of $65,047 would imply. Local demographics and labor conditions reinforce this affordability narrative but highlight structural wage constraints. An unemployment rate of 4.2 percent sits near full employment, yet educational attainment remains low, with only 14.0 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. This composition suggests a labor market anchored in sectors that do not command premium wages, capping income growth potential even as the cost of living remains suppressed. The workforce is effectively trading lower nominal earnings for disproportionately lower shelter and service costs, a trade-off reflected in the purchasing power metric that exceeds raw income differentials. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the economic trajectory has favored the resident. The RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.991 to 89.908, indicating local consumer prices fell relative to the national basket. Simultaneously, median household income surged 42.6 percent from $43,581 to $62,164, vastly outpacing the 29.0 percent rise in median rent from $586 to $756. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have expanded the real income buffer considerably. For a prospective resident, the budgeting takeaway is clear: housing and services offer deep, structural discounts that insulate the household budget, but the near-parity pricing of goods demands careful management of variable expenses like transportation and food to fully capture the county’s purchasing power advantage.

What this means for residents of Atchison County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 69141.8, a median household income of $62,164 in Atchison County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $42,981,309 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
