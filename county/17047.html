📊 Local Market Analysis

Edwards County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 98.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the only segment that exceeds the national level, registering at 101.6, while services are 7.6 points cheaper (92.4) and housing—measured by rental costs—is 19.6 points below the national average (80.4). The median household income of $59,386 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 9 %, yet the county’s rent burden remains modest at 15.1 % of income, far lower than the 30 % threshold commonly cited as stressful. Monthly rent of $746 compares with the national median of $928, reinforcing the affordability of housing in the area. The composition of expenses reveals that goods dominate the local price structure. With groceries, apparel, and fuel priced 1.6 % above the national norm, everyday consumables impose a slight premium on residents. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining out are 7.6 % cheaper, and the rental market is 19.6 % less expensive. For a typical household, the net effect is a modestly cheap daily life: lower housing and service costs offset the higher price of goods, resulting in a disposable income pool that remains relatively robust. After covering the median rent of $746, a household retains approximately $2,470 per month for other expenses, equating to roughly 49 % of its gross income. Demographic factors shape this affordability profile. Only 8.9 % of the adult population holds a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor market concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 44.6 years points to a mature workforce, often with established spending patterns and reduced demand for high‑cost amenities. Unemployment stands at 6.1 %, modestly above the national rate, indicating some labor market slack that can suppress wage growth but also keep wage pressure on prices limited. The combination of modest earnings and low housing costs yields a Purchasing Power Index of 60,136.5, meaning residents can purchase roughly 60 % of the goods and services that an average American can with the same nominal income. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline, while median household income rose 33.5 % from $44,497 to $59,386. Rent, however, surged 58.4 % from $471 to $746, outpacing income growth. The divergence suggests that while overall price pressures have eased, housing costs have become a more significant share of household budgets. Nonetheless, the rent burden remains low because absolute rent levels are still well beneath national averages. Prospective residents should budget with the higher cost of goods in mind, allocating a larger share of their monthly spend to groceries, fuel, and apparel than they might in a typical U.S. county. Housing will consume a relatively small slice of income—approximately 15 %—leaving ample room for discretionary spending on services that are already cheaper than average. A prudent household plan would earmark roughly 30 % of net income for goods, 15 % for services, and retain the remaining 55 % for savings, transportation, and occasional discretionary items, ensuring a comfortable standard of living despite the modest income gap with the national average.

What this means for residents of Edwards County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 60136.5, a median household income of $59,386 in Edwards County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $35,712,662 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
