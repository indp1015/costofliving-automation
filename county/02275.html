📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Wrangell City and Borough runs modestly above the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced at 103.7, while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are 96.7, a full 3.3 points below the national average. Housing, measured by median rental costs, is the outlier at 113.3, reflecting a 13.3‑percent premium over the countrywide benchmark. The rental market dominates the local cost structure. Median monthly rent stands at $1,047, 13.0 % higher than the national median of $928, and accounts for 19.5 % of the median household income of $64,545. By contrast, the lower‑priced services sector offsets some of the housing premium, keeping overall consumer expenses from spiraling. Daily life for a typical household is therefore mixed: essential non‑housing outlays—groceries and fuel—track closely with national levels, while discretionary spending on services is slightly cheaper. The net effect is a cost profile that feels “moderately expensive” because the housing surcharge outweighs the modest savings in services. Disposable income after rent can be approximated by subtracting the annualized rental burden ($12,564) from the median household earnings ($64,545), leaving roughly $51,981 for all other expenses. This translates to a Purchasing Power Index of 62,484.9, which is 62.5 % of the national reference level (set at 100). In practical terms, a median resident can afford only about two‑thirds of the goods and services that a median American can purchase with the same nominal income, largely due to the rent premium. The index underscores that even with a median income that is 0.8 % below the national figure, the higher housing cost erodes real purchasing capacity. Local demographics reinforce the budgetary picture. The unemployment rate of 3.5 % is well below the national average, suggesting a relatively tight labor market that supports steady earnings. However, only 14.4 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not command premium wages. The median age of 44.3 years points to a mature population, often associated with higher household formation rates and greater demand for stable rental housing, which in turn sustains the elevated rent levels. The combination of low unemployment and modest educational attainment creates a scenario where wages are sufficient to cover basic needs but leave limited room for discretionary spending. Over the past decade, the RPP index rose marginally from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023, a 0.2 % increase that signals price stability relative to the national basket. Median household income, however, surged 32.8 % from $48,603 to $64,545, outpacing the slight price uptick. Median rent climbed 43.0 % from $732 to $1,047, outstripping both income growth and overall price inflation. The divergence indicates that while wages have improved substantially, housing costs have accelerated faster, compressing real disposable income despite the modest overall cost‑of‑living index. Prospective residents should anchor their budgeting around the rent burden. With $1,047 monthly for housing, allocating roughly 20 % of income to rent is realistic, leaving about $4,300 per month for utilities, groceries, transportation, and healthcare. Given that services are priced 3.3 % below the national average, utilities and medical expenses may be slightly cheaper, but the higher grocery and fuel index (103.7) suggests modestly elevated food and transportation bills. A prudent budget would therefore earmark roughly 30 % of net income for food and fuel, 15 % for utilities and healthcare, and the remaining 35 % for discretionary items and savings. Understanding this allocation helps a newcomer gauge the true cost of daily household management in Wrangell, where housing dominates the expense profile while other categories remain comparatively affordable.

What this means for residents of Wrangell City and Borough, Alaska

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
