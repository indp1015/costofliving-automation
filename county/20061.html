📊 Local Market Analysis

Geary County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—represent the highest local cost component at 96.5, nearly converging with the national average and limiting the savings typically associated with non-metro Midwestern living. Conversely, services, including healthcare, utilities, and dining, plummet to 68.6, the lowest segment and the primary driver of the county’s overall affordability advantage. Rental costs occupy a middle ground at 90.8, modestly below the national figure but elevated relative to the deep discount found in services, creating a bifurcated expense profile where discretionary service consumption is cheap but essential goods and shelter retain significant price parity with the broader country. The median household income of $57,344 trails the national median of $65,047 by 11.8 percent, a gap that narrows considerably when adjusted for local prices. The Purchasing Power Index of 63,780.8 indicates that after accounting for the 89.9 RPP, the typical household commands real resources roughly equivalent to a $63,781 income in an average-cost jurisdiction, effectively closing 70 percent of the nominal income deficit. Rent burden stands at a manageable 23.4 percent of gross income, based on a median monthly rent of $1,119 against the national median of $928; while local rents exceed the national dollar figure, the lower overall price level preserves disposable income for other categories. This dynamic suggests that while shelter consumes a larger nominal slice of the paycheck than the national average might imply, the steep discount on services—particularly utilities and healthcare—provides a meaningful offset for daily household cash flow. Demographic and labor market characteristics sharpen the context for these budget dynamics. A median age of 27.1 years signals a population heavily weighted toward early-career workers and military-affiliated households connected to Fort Riley, a factor consistent with the 6.0 percent unemployment rate that exceeds typical national lows. Educational attainment remains modest, with only 15.5 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, correlating with the below-average median wage and limiting upward pressure on the local service economy’s price floor. The labor market’s slack, combined with a transient demographic profile, likely sustains the suppressed service costs—particularly in personal care, recreation, and dining—while the goods index remains tethered to national supply chains and transportation costs that the local economy cannot influence. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 3.3 percent from 92.99 to 89.91, signaling that local consumer prices have grown slower than the national basket. Nominal median income surged 30.4 percent from $43,992 to $57,344, vastly outpacing the 9.0 percent climb in median rent from $1,027 to $1,119. This divergence has expanded real purchasing power substantially; a decade ago, the rent-to-income ratio hovered near 28 percent, compared to the current 23.4 percent, freeing roughly $250 per month in real terms for the median renter. For a prospective resident, the budgeting takeaway is clear: allocate near-national prices for the weekly grocery run and fuel stops, anticipate below-average utility and healthcare bills, and recognize that while rent demands a higher absolute dollar outlay than the national median, the surrounding cost environment absorbs that hit more effectively than in most comparable jurisdictions.

What this means for residents of Geary County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63780.8, a median household income of $57,344 in Geary County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $36,574,462 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
