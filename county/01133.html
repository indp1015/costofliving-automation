📊 Local Market Analysis

Winston County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 89.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 94.6, still under the national average but the highest component within the county. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheap at 61.6, while rental costs are modestly lower than the countrywide figure, posting an index of 89.9. The median household income of $48,032 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 26%, yet the median monthly rent of $744 is 20% below the national median of $928, leaving a rent‑burden ratio of 18.6% of income—well under the 30% threshold that defines affordability. The expense profile is driven primarily by goods, which account for the bulk of price differentials, while the services sector provides a substantial cost cushion. Daily life in Winston County is therefore inexpensive in categories that dominate household budgets, such as utilities and health care, but consumers still feel the pinch of higher relative grocery and fuel costs. After covering the median rent, a typical household retains $44,288 of annual income, equivalent to $3,690 per month. The Purchasing Power Index of 53,909.8 reflects that, after adjusting for local price levels, residents can purchase roughly 54% of the goods and services that a median American can buy with the same nominal income. In practical terms, a family of four can expect to spend a larger share of its budget on food and transportation than on discretionary services. Demographically, the county’s labor force is older (median age 45.3) and less educated, with only 8.3% holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. Unemployment sits at 4.2%, modestly above the national rate but low enough to sustain a stable labor market for low‑skill occupations. The limited educational attainment constrains wage growth, reinforcing the reliance on lower‑cost services and keeping household budgets anchored to essential spending. The older population also tends to have lower consumption of high‑priced discretionary services, further depressing the services index. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 89.78 to 89.10, a 0.8% decline, indicating a slight easing of overall price pressure. Median household income surged 44.7% from $33,194 to $48,032, while median rent rose 44.5% from $515 to $744, essentially preserving the rent‑burden ratio. Wage growth has kept pace with housing costs, but both lag behind national income growth, leaving residents with less discretionary cash than their counterparts elsewhere. Prospective residents should budget conservatively for groceries and fuel, allocate a modest portion of income to utilities and health care, and anticipate a relatively low rent expense that will not dominate their monthly outlays. The combination of low services costs and affordable housing creates a budget environment where essential expenses consume a smaller slice of income, allowing limited flexibility for non‑essential spending.

What this means for residents of Winston County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 53909.8, a median household income of $48,032 in Winston County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $25,893,955 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
