📊 Local Market Analysis

Carroll County presents a cost of living profile that sits 11.2 percent below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 for 2023, a modest improvement from 91.1 in 2010. The composite figure masks significant internal divergence: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track near parity at 96.6, while rental costs come in at 87.3. The dominant deflationary force is the services sector, which clocks in at a striking 66.0—34 percent below the national average—driven by markedly lower outlays for healthcare, utilities, and dining. This structural anomaly makes daily operational expenses unusually inexpensive for residents, particularly those whose consumption baskets weight heavily toward services rather than tradable goods. The consumption breakdown reveals that the median household income of $68,528 translates into exceptional purchasing power, indexed at 77,198.1, effectively granting residents a 12.6 percent premium over their nominal earnings when adjusted for local prices. Rent burden stands at a mere 12.6 percent of gross income, with the median monthly rent of $722 comparing favorably against the national median of $928. Over the past decade, nominal income growth of 35.5 percent has slightly outpaced rent appreciation of 33.2 percent, while the overall RPP declined 2.6 percent, indicating that real wage growth has been robust. The labor market reinforces this dynamic; an unemployment rate of 2.1 percent signals a tight market that supports wage stability, even as the relatively low bachelor’s degree attainment of 17.8 percent and a median age of 41.8 years suggest an economy anchored in established, non-degree-intensive industries rather than high-skill knowledge sectors. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implications are concrete: the primary friction point in the household ledger will be goods, where prices offer almost no discount relative to metropolitan areas, meaning grocery bills and fuel costs will feel familiar. Conversely, the services discount acts as a persistent tailwind, lowering the marginal cost of healthcare access, utility bills, and restaurant meals. The demographic profile implies a community with stable, if not rapidly escalating, earnings trajectories, where the cost of shelter consumes a minor share of cash flow. Residents effectively retain a larger slice of disposable income for savings or discretionary spending than national averages would predict, a dynamic sustained by the persistent gap between local service pricing and national income norms.

What this means for residents of Carroll County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 77198.1, a median household income of $68,528 in Carroll County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $52,902,314 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
