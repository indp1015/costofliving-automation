📊 Local Market Analysis

Lee County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2% below the national average, with an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest relative expense at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. baseline, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs sit at 81.9, translating to a median monthly outlay of $638 that consumes 20.8% of the median household income of $36,860, a burden significantly lighter than the national rent-to-income dynamic where the median rent of $928 weighs on a $65,047 income. The purchasing power index of 42,460.5 illustrates that the median dollar stretches considerably further here than in higher-cost metros, effectively granting residents the consumption capacity of a $42,460 income in an average-cost environment. However, this arithmetic advantage is mediated by a labor market exhibiting structural friction: an unemployment rate of 17.7% and an educational attainment rate of just 5.4% for bachelor’s degrees or higher suggest limited wage mobility and a reliance on lower-wage sectors. The median age of 41.9 years indicates a workforce with established consumption patterns but potentially constrained upskilling pathways, reinforcing the gap between local earnings and national income benchmarks. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined 2.0% from 88.6 to 86.8, signaling a modest relative cheapening of the consumption basket even as nominal costs rose. Median income grew 29.8% from $28,393 to $36,860, outpacing the 19.7% increase in median rent from $533 to $638, a divergence that has incrementally improved the rent-burden ratio over the decade. This wage-rent decoupling provides a buffer against shelter-cost inflation, though the persistently high goods index (93.1) means daily essentials like food and transportation fuel have not shared in the same relative deflation, anchoring the floor of household expenditure. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on a sharp bifurcation: housing and services offer deep discounts relative to national norms, but the weekly grocery bill and fuel costs track near parity with the rest of the country. A household earning the local median retains roughly 79% of pre-tax income after rent, a high retention rate that evaporates quickly when factoring in goods priced at 93% of the national level and a services sector where access—particularly healthcare—may be geographically limited despite low price indices. The economic calculus favors those with portable or fixed incomes, while workers dependent on the local labor market face a ceiling on earnings growth that limits long-term discretionary capacity.

What this means for residents of Lee County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 42460.5, a median household income of $36,860 in Lee County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $15,650,940 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
