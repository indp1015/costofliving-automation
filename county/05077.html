📊 Local Market Analysis

Lee County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 93.1, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheap at 56.7. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $638, translate to a rental index of 81.9, again lower than the national median rent of $928. The composite picture is one of a region where everyday expenses—particularly health care, utilities, and dining—are substantially less costly than the countrywide average, offset slightly by modestly higher prices for groceries, apparel, and fuel. The consumption structure is dominated by goods, which account for the highest relative price level among the three categories. Despite this, the overall cost burden remains light because services, which typically consume a large share of household budgets, are priced at barely more than half the national level. Rent, while higher than the 2010 figure of $533, still represents only 20.8 % of median household income, well under the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag affordability stress. Consequently, daily life in Lee County can be characterized as inexpensive, especially for discretionary spending on health and leisure, while essential goods consume a larger slice of the budget than in many other locales. Median household income in the county stands at $36,860, roughly 56 % of the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the average rent of $638, a typical household retains about $30,860 in annual discretionary income, or $2,572 per month. The Purchasing Power Index of 42,460.5 quantifies this gap: residents can purchase roughly 42 % of the goods and services that a median American can with the same nominal income. The low rent‑to‑income ratio cushions the income shortfall, but the modest earnings limit the ability to absorb price spikes in the goods sector, where costs are relatively higher than services. Demographically, the county’s labor market is constrained. An unemployment rate of 17.7 % far exceeds the national average, and only 5.4 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 41.9 suggests a mature workforce with limited inflow of higher‑skill labor. These factors depress wage growth and reinforce reliance on low‑cost services and affordable housing. The high unemployment environment also means that a sizable share of the population may be dependent on public assistance, further emphasizing the importance of the county’s cheap service sector for basic living standards. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 88.56 to 86.81, a 2 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. Median income, however, rose 29.8 % from $28,393 to $36,860, outpacing the 19.7 % increase in median rent. The divergence suggests that wages have kept pace with, and even exceeded, housing cost growth, enhancing affordability despite the persistent high unemployment rate. Prospective residents should budget around $638 for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 for utilities and health‑related services, and expect grocery and fuel expenditures to be modestly higher than the national average, perhaps $400–$450 per month for a family of four. With a rent burden of just over one‑fifth of income, the remaining 80 % of earnings must cover transportation, food, and discretionary items, leaving limited room for savings. Careful monitoring of goods prices and maintaining a tight discretionary budget will be essential for sustaining a comfortable standard of living in Lee County.

What this means for residents of Lee County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 42460.5, a median household income of $36,860 in Lee County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $15,650,940 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
