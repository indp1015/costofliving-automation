📊 Local Market Analysis

Rockcastle County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 94.5, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 62.9. Housing, measured by rental costs, registers at 80.9, indicating that renters pay roughly 19 % less than the average American household. The composite picture is one of a region where everyday expenses are modest, especially in the service sector, even though grocery and fuel costs are closer to the national level. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services category, which depresses the overall index. With services priced at just 62.9, residents enjoy low utility bills, affordable healthcare, and inexpensive dining out. Goods remain the only segment approaching parity, reflecting the county’s reliance on transportation and food items that track broader market trends. Rental costs, while lower than the national median rent of $928, are higher than a decade ago (up 29.9 % from $522 to $678). The rent‑burden metric—16.8 % of median household income—suggests that housing remains affordable; the national threshold for affordability is commonly set at 30 %. Consequently, daily life is inexpensive relative to the national average, driven primarily by cheap services and modest rent, while goods costs temper the overall savings. Median household income in Rockcastle County stands at $48,367, 26 % below the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median monthly rent of $678 (8.4 % of annual income), a typical household retains roughly $46,000 for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 53,806.9 quantifies this residual buying capacity, indicating that the median resident can afford about 54 % of the goods and services that a median national household can purchase with its income. In practical terms, a family can cover essential groceries, fuel, and modest discretionary spending, but the gap in disposable income limits higher‑end consumption and savings potential. Demographically, the county’s labor market is constrained. An unemployment rate of 6.1 % exceeds the national average, and only 5.9 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, reflecting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 42.8 suggests a mature population, likely with established household budgets but limited upward mobility. These factors combine to keep demand for low‑cost services high while limiting pressure on wages, reinforcing the low‑price environment for utilities and healthcare. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped marginally from 90.433 to 89.89, a 0.6 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have barely shifted relative to the nation. In contrast, median household income surged 53.3 % from $31,555 to $48,367, outpacing the modest rise in rental costs (29.9 %). Wage growth has therefore outstripped price inflation, expanding real purchasing power for most residents, though the absolute income level remains below national standards. Prospective residents should budget around $678 per month for rent, allocate a larger share of their income to goods such as groceries and fuel (prices near 95 % of national levels), and expect utilities, healthcare, and dining to cost roughly two‑thirds of comparable national expenses. With services accounting for the greatest savings, a household can stretch its median income to cover essential needs comfortably, but discretionary spending will be constrained by the lower overall income ceiling. Careful allocation toward essential goods and leveraging the inexpensive service sector will be key to maintaining a balanced household budget in Rockcastle County.

What this means for residents of Rockcastle County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 53806.9, a median household income of $48,367 in Rockcastle County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $26,024,783 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
