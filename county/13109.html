📊 Local Market Analysis

Evans County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national benchmark, with an overall RPP of 96.5 against the United States average of 100. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 97.7, while services are markedly cheaper at 88.3, and housing costs—measured by median monthly rent of $720—register a 92.3 index, well under the national median rent of $928. The lower service index reflects cheaper utilities, healthcare, and dining out, whereas the near‑national goods index indicates that groceries, apparel, and fuel are only slightly cheaper than the countrywide norm. The expense structure is driven primarily by goods, which account for the highest index value and therefore dominate daily outlays such as food and transportation. Housing, while still below national levels, represents a larger share of the budget because rent consumes 16.0 % of median household income, a proportion that remains comfortably under the national rent‑burden threshold of 30 %. Services, being the cheapest segment, provide a cushion for discretionary spending; residents can expect lower bills for electricity, water, and medical services, which offsets the relatively higher price of consumables. Consequently, everyday life in Evans County is inexpensive in the service sphere but only modestly cheaper for essential goods, yielding a mixed affordability profile. Median household income of $53,908 translates to a monthly pre‑tax earnings pool of $4,492. After deducting the median rent of $720, a household retains $3,772 for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 55,866.7 quantifies this residual buying capacity, indicating that Evans County residents can purchase roughly 44 % of the national basket of goods and services with their after‑rent income. By contrast, the national median income of $65,047 leaves a larger absolute dollar amount for discretionary spending, but the higher national cost indices erode that advantage. The local index therefore signals a modest but tangible purchasing advantage for Evans County households, especially in service‑related categories. Demographically, the county’s labor market is characterized by a 5.5 % unemployment rate and a low educational attainment—only 9.3 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 36.9 aligns with a typical working‑age population, suggesting a stable labor supply but limited high‑skill wage premiums. The modest educational profile contributes to the lower income ceiling, while the unemployment figure, though slightly above the national rate, does not dramatically inflate cost pressures because service prices remain suppressed. The labor market thus sustains a cost environment where basic necessities are affordable, yet upward mobility through higher‑paid positions remains constrained. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 94.574 to 96.494, a 2.0 % increase, while median household income surged 42.4 % from $37,865 to $53,908. Rent climbed 17.3 % from $614 to $720. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and housing cost escalation, expanding real disposable income and reinforcing the county’s relative affordability. However, the modest rise in goods prices suggests that future wage gains will be needed to maintain the current purchasing advantage, particularly if national inflation accelerates. Prospective residents should allocate roughly 16 % of their income to rent, leaving about $3,770 per month for groceries, transportation, utilities, and healthcare. Given the low service index, budgeting for utilities and medical expenses can be conservative, while grocery bills should be planned at near‑national levels. A prudent monthly budget might reserve 30 % for food and fuel, 15 % for transportation, 10 % for healthcare, and the remaining 29 % for discretionary items, savings, and occasional dining out. Understanding this allocation helps newcomers gauge the true cost of daily life in Evans County and align their spending with the county’s distinctive price landscape.

What this means for residents of Evans County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55866.7, a median household income of $53,908 in Evans County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $30,116,621 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
