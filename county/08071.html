📊 Local Market Analysis

Las Animas County presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline at 101.9 on the BEA Regional Price Parity index, yet this aggregate figure masks a profound bifurcation in household expenditure patterns. While goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track slightly below the national average at 99.2, and rental housing costs offer significant relief at 86.8, the services category—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—surges to 130.5, marking it as the primary driver of local inflationary pressure. This 30.5 percent premium on services effectively neutralizes the savings found in housing and goods, creating an environment where fixed, non-discretionary outlays consume a disproportionate share of the median household income of $50,408, which itself trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 22 percent. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is expensive precisely where consumers have the least flexibility. A median monthly rent of $875 translates to a rent burden of 20.8 percent, a figure that appears manageable against the national median rent of $928, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 49,489.5 indicates that after adjusting for the full local price level, the real value of local earnings erodes significantly. Residents effectively lose over $900 in annual purchasing power relative to their nominal wages because the high cost of services—medical visits, electricity, insurance—cannot be easily substituted or downsized. This dynamic forces a rigid budget structure where discretionary spending is compressed not by shelter costs, but by the operational costs of maintaining a household in a service-expensive region. Local demographics sharpen the picture of economic vulnerability. An unemployment rate of 5.6 percent exceeds typical national lows, while a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 11.5 percent suggests a labor market concentrated in lower-wage sectors with limited bargaining power for wage growth. The median age of 46.5 years implies a population with rising healthcare utilization needs, directly exposing the typical household to the county’s most inflated cost category. This demographic overlay suggests the labor market is not generating the income velocity required to offset the structural service-cost premium, leaving a workforce that is older, less credentialed, and more susceptible to service-sector price hikes than the national average. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted upward modestly from 100.59 to 101.86, a 1.3 percent cumulative increase, while nominal median income rose 11.9 percent from $45,067 to $50,408. However, median rent accelerated faster at 15.9 percent, climbing from $755 to $875, signaling that shelter costs are tightening even within a generally affordable rental market. Although nominal wage growth has outpaced the broad price index, the outsized weight of services in the local basket means the effective inflation rate for a typical household likely exceeds the headline RPP change. A prospective resident should budget for healthcare and utility line items that run roughly 30 percent above national norms, understanding that the apparent affordability of rent is largely offset by the inescapable cost of keeping the lights on and accessing medical care in this specific economic geography.

What this means for residents of Las Animas County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 49489.5, a median household income of $50,408 in Las Animas County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $24,946,667 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
