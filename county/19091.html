📊 Local Market Analysis

Humboldt County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits 11.2 percentage points below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 in 2023, a modest decline from 91.1 in 2010. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel track near parity at 96.6, while services—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—plummet to 66.0, the lowest component and the primary driver of the county’s affordability advantage. Rental costs occupy a middle ground at 87.3, translating to a median monthly gross rent of $731, substantially lower than the national median of $928. Consequently, daily life is inexpensive relative to the broader U.S., but the discount is heavily concentrated in service consumption rather than the tangible goods households purchase most frequently. The median household income of $66,994 slightly exceeds the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 75,470 reveals that local earnings, when adjusted for the specific consumption basket, command significantly less real value than the nominal figure suggests. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 13.1 percent of gross income, freeing substantial cash flow for other categories; however, the near-national pricing for goods (96.6) means grocery and fuel bills absorb a disproportionate share of that disposable income compared to service-heavy budgets in higher-cost metros. The labor market reinforces this dynamic: a 3.1 percent unemployment rate signals tight conditions, but an educational attainment rate of just 14.8 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher suggests a workforce concentrated in sectors where wage growth may not structurally outpace goods inflation. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted downward by 2.6 percent while nominal median income surged 41.8 percent from $47,252 and median rent climbed 26.9 percent from $576. Wages have demonstrably outpaced shelter costs in nominal terms, and the declining RPP indicates that the local price level has fallen relative to the national average, a rare combination that has expanded real purchasing power for incumbent residents. The median age of 41.8 years implies a stable, established population base rather than a transient workforce, which typically correlates with steadier demand for local services and less speculative pressure on rental markets. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and transportation expenses that mirror national averages almost dollar-for-dollar, while anticipating steep discounts on healthcare visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals. The 13.1 percent rent-to-income ratio offers a wide margin for savings or debt service, but the goods-heavy consumption basket means inflation at the pump or the supermarket checkout erodes real wages faster here than in service-dominated economies. Housing quality at the $731 median rent level warrants scrutiny, as the low service RPP may reflect older housing stock with higher per-square-foot energy costs that partially offset the headline rental discount.

What this means for residents of Humboldt County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 75470.0, a median household income of $66,994 in Humboldt County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $50,560,372 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
