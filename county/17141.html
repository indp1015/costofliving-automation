📊 Local Market Analysis

Ogle County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits marginally below the national baseline, with a 2023 Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 98.8 against a national index of 100. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: residents pay a premium for tangible goods, registering a goods RPP of 101.6, while services come in substantially cheaper at 92.4. The most pronounced discount appears in rental housing, where the RPP of 80.4 indicates shelter costs roughly one-fifth below the national average. This structure creates a distinctive consumption environment where daily expenditures on groceries, apparel, and fuel exert upward pressure on budgets, but the low cost of shelter and services—healthcare, utilities, and dining—more than offsets those premiums for the typical household. The rental housing component acts as the primary anchor pulling the overall index down, with median monthly rent of $901 comparing favorably against the national median of $928. This translates to a rent burden of just 13.6 percent of the median household income of $79,244, a figure that signals exceptional housing affordability relative to national standards. Consequently, the consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is effectively inexpensive for established residents because the largest recurring line item—shelter—is deeply discounted. The goods premium of 1.6 percent above national prices is a manageable friction when measured against the 19.6 percent discount on rents and the 7.6 percent discount on services, resulting in a Purchasing Power Index of 80,245.5 that exceeds the nominal median income, indicating that local wages stretch further here than the raw dollar amount suggests. Local demographics and labor dynamics reinforce this affordability narrative while highlighting structural constraints. A median age of 42.0 years and a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of only 15.5 percent suggest a workforce concentrated in trades, manufacturing, or service sectors rather than high-wage knowledge industries. The 5.4 percent unemployment rate sits above typical full-employment thresholds, implying slack in the labor market that may suppress wage growth but also reduces competitive pressure on rental inventory. This demographic profile aligns with the observed cost structure: a population with moderate educational attainment benefits disproportionately from low shelter costs, while the goods penalty affects all income cohorts equally at the checkout counter. Over the past decade, the economic trajectory has favored the resident consumer. The RPP declined from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 44.5 percent from $54,849 to $79,244. Median rent increased only 33.7 percent over the same period, from $674 to $901, lagging income growth by a substantial margin. This divergence confirms that wages have not merely kept pace with expenses but have significantly outpaced the primary cost driver. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: allocate expectations for grocery and fuel bills to run slightly above national norms, but anticipate substantial surplus capacity relative to housing and service costs, a dynamic that supports higher discretionary spending or savings rates than comparable nominal incomes would afford in higher-RPP regions.

What this means for residents of Ogle County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 80245.5, a median household income of $79,244 in Ogle County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $63,589,744 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
