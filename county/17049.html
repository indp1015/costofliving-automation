📊 Local Market Analysis

Effingham County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits marginally below the national baseline, with a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 98.8, yet the aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—run 1.6 percent above the U.S. average at 101.6, marking the highest cost component for local households. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining register at 92.4, while rental costs sit deeply discounted at 80.4, the lowest index value in the dataset. This structure creates a bifurcated daily expense environment: residents pay a slight premium at the pump and the checkout counter but capture substantial savings on shelter and service-intensive spending, a dynamic that distinguishes the county from typical Midwestern metros where housing often anchors the budget. The rental advantage fundamentally reshapes the household ledger. Median monthly rent of $734 consumes only 11.7 percent of the $75,380 median household income, a burden roughly half the national standard where median rent of $928 weighs heavier against a lower $65,047 median income. This shelter discount propagates through the Purchasing Power Index, which adjusts nominal income to 76,332.6, effectively granting the median earner an additional $1,300 in real purchasing capacity relative to their nominal wage. With unemployment holding at a tight 2.2 percent, the labor market appears capable of sustaining wage pressure, yet the low educational attainment rate—only 17.8 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—suggests the local wage premium may be concentrated in specific sectors rather than broadly distributed across the workforce. Historical trajectory confirms that income growth has decisively outpaced both inflation and shelter costs over the past decade. The RPP declined 3.0 percent from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, indicating the county became relatively cheaper even as nominal median income surged 44.3 percent from $52,224 to $75,380. Rent appreciation lagged significantly at 26.3 percent over the same period, moving from $581 to $734. This divergence implies that the median worker’s real disposable income expanded substantially, a rare occurrence in an era where housing costs nationally have frequently outstripped earnings. The median age of 39.5 suggests a workforce in prime earning years, potentially supporting continued income resilience. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the trade-off between elevated goods pricing and the shelter windfall. A household relocating from a national-average market should anticipate grocery and fuel bills that run slightly hotter, but the rental discount—roughly $194 below the national median monthly—frees capital for savings, debt service, or discretionary services. The low services index further reduces the cost of healthcare access and utilities, compounding the shelter advantage. However, the goods penalty is inescapable and recurrent; unlike housing, which is a fixed contract, fuel and food volatility transmits directly to weekly cash flow. Residents effectively trade higher variable costs for lower fixed costs, a profile that favors households with stable transportation needs and moderate space requirements.

What this means for residents of Effingham County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76332.6, a median household income of $75,380 in Effingham County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $57,539,514 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
