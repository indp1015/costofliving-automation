📊 Local Market Analysis

Decatur County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 96.5 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—register an index of 97.7, still under the national average but the highest component locally. Services, encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining, are markedly cheaper at 88.3, while rental housing costs are 92.3, indicating that renters pay roughly 7.7 % less than the countrywide benchmark. The median monthly rent of $860 further underscores the affordability gap, falling $68 below the national median of $928. The cost structure is driven primarily by the goods sector, which, at 97.7, contributes the most to the overall index. However, the pronounced discount on services (88.3) offsets higher goods prices, resulting in a net cost environment that is relatively inexpensive for everyday life. Residents spend less on healthcare, utilities, and restaurant meals, which together represent a sizable share of household outlays. Rental costs, while below national levels, are the second‑largest expense driver, yet they remain affordable enough that rent burden accounts for only 20.4 % of median household income—well under the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag housing stress. Disposable income calculations reveal a constrained purchasing power despite the lower price environment. The median household income of $50,485 translates to an annual after‑rent income of $42,805 after subtracting the $10,320 annual rent bill. The Purchasing Power Index of 52,319.3—roughly 80 % of the national average—reflects that, after accounting for local price levels, residents retain less real buying power than the typical American household. The gap widens when non‑housing expenses are considered, as the cheap services offset only part of the higher goods costs. Demographic factors shape this economic portrait. With only 8.9 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor pool leans toward lower‑skill occupations, which typically command lower wages. The unemployment rate of 6.9 % exceeds the national rate, limiting income growth and reinforcing the modest purchasing power. A median age of 38.7 years suggests a mature workforce, yet the combination of limited higher‑education attainment and elevated unemployment dampens wage‑setting power, keeping household budgets tight despite affordable prices. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest 2.0 % increase, indicating that price pressure has been relatively tame. In contrast, median household income surged 61.4 % from $31,284 to $50,485, outpacing inflation and cost growth. Rental costs, however, climbed 35.9 % from $633 to $860, narrowing the gap between income gains and housing expense. The divergent trajectories suggest that wages have generally kept pace with, and even exceeded, the modest rise in consumer prices, though the faster rent escalation erodes part of that advantage. Prospective residents should structure budgets around three pillars: allocate roughly 20 % of income to rent, anticipate slightly higher grocery and fuel bills relative to services, and leverage the low cost of healthcare and utilities to stretch discretionary spending. A household earning the median income would need to reserve about $860 monthly for housing, $400–$500 for groceries and fuel, and $250–$300 for utilities and medical expenses. The remaining funds must cover transportation, education, and savings, leaving limited room for luxury consumption. Understanding these allocations equips newcomers to navigate Decatur County’s affordable yet income‑constrained environment without overextending their finances.

What this means for residents of Decatur County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 52319.3, a median household income of $50,485 in Decatur County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $26,413,399 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
