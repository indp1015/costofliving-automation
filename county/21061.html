📊 Local Market Analysis

Edmonson County presents a cost of living profile that sits 10.1 percent below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a stark bifurcation in household expenditure patterns. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—command an index of 94.5, merely 5.5 points shy of the U.S. average, while Services plummet to 62.9, a staggering 37.1 percent discount driven by below-average healthcare, utility, and dining costs. Rental housing, indexed at 80.9, occupies the middle ground, reinforcing a consumption landscape where the daily necessities of food and fuel absorb a disproportionate share of the local budget relative to the national experience. This structural imbalance makes Goods the primary driver of the local cost structure, rendering daily life deceptively expensive for a low-income region despite the low headline RPP. The median household income of $54,512 trails the national median of $65,047 by 16.2 percent, yet median rent of $739 consumes only 16.3 percent of gross income, a burden significantly lighter than the national comparison where $928 median rent weighs heavier against higher earnings. The Purchasing Power Index of 60,643 quantifies this advantage: after adjusting for local prices, the typical household commands real resources roughly 11.3 percent above their nominal income, a buffer almost entirely attributable to the deep discount on Services and shelter rather than wage strength. Demographic and labor market fundamentals constrain the translation of that purchasing power into broader economic mobility. An unemployment rate of 6.8 percent signals persistent labor slack well above national norms, while educational attainment remains critically low at 5.6 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, limiting access to higher-wage service sectors that might otherwise benefit from the county’s low operating costs. A median age of 45.3 years suggests an aging workforce with potentially fixed incomes, amplifying the sensitivity to the 94.5 Goods index where pharmaceutical and food inflation cannot be offset by local wage negotiation. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted marginally from 90.4 to 89.9, a negligible 0.6 percent decline indicating stable relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 30.7 percent from $41,710 to $54,512, outpacing a 22.8 percent rise in median rent from $602 to $739. This divergence confirms that wage growth has exceeded shelter cost inflation, improving the rent-to-income ratio over the decade. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality centers on allocating a larger-than-average slice of disposable income to the grocery aisle and the gas pump, while enjoying substantial relief on monthly rent, utility bills, and healthcare co-pays—a trade-off that favors households with low service consumption but high transportation and nutritional needs.

What this means for residents of Edmonson County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 60643.0, a median household income of $54,512 in Edmonson County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $33,057,712 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
