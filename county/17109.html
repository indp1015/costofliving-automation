📊 Local Market Analysis

McDonough County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark, with an overall Relative Price Parity (RPP) of 98.8 against a U.S. index of 100. Goods prices are modestly higher than the countrywide average at 101.6, while services are noticeably cheaper at 92.4, and housing—measured by median rent—registers a substantial discount at 80.4. The county’s median monthly rent of $730 is 21.4 % lower than the national median of $928, reflecting the pronounced housing affordability advantage. The expense structure is dominated by goods. At 101.6, the goods index exceeds both services and housing, indicating that groceries, apparel, and fuel cost slightly more than they do elsewhere. Services, including healthcare, utilities, and dining, are 7.6 % cheaper than the national average, which offsets the higher goods price and yields a net cost environment that feels inexpensive for everyday living. The low housing index further depresses total outlays, making the county attractive for households that prioritize rent savings over higher-priced consumer goods. Disposable income calculations reveal a modest purchasing cushion. The median household earns $50,434 annually, which translates to $4,202 per month before taxes. After subtracting the median rent of $730, 17.4 % of income is devoted to housing, leaving roughly $3,472 for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 51,071.4—derived from the ratio of local income to the national cost of living—confirms that residents retain about 51 % of the purchasing power of an average American household. In practical terms, a family earning the median income can afford a comparable basket of goods and services to a household earning roughly $101,000 nationally, thanks to the county’s cheaper services and housing. Demographic factors shape this affordability profile. With only 16.7 % of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor market leans toward lower-wage occupations, which aligns with the 8.6 % unemployment rate—well above the national average. The relatively young median age of 35.1 suggests a workforce still building earnings potential, reinforcing reliance on lower-cost housing and services. The higher unemployment rate also pressures disposable income, making the low rent and service costs essential for maintaining household stability. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 101.8 in 2010 to 98.8 in 2023, a 3 % decline that signals a gradual easing of price pressures. Median household income rose 25.1 % from $40,314 to $50,434, outpacing the 15.9 % increase in median rent (from $630 to $730). Wage growth therefore outstripped housing cost inflation, enhancing real purchasing power and allowing residents to allocate a smaller share of income to rent than a decade ago. Prospective residents should budget around $730 for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 for utilities and basic services, and expect grocery and fuel expenses to be marginally above national averages. With housing consuming less than one‑fifth of median earnings, the remaining budget can comfortably cover transportation, modest discretionary spending, and modest savings, provided the household can navigate the higher goods prices and the local labor market’s limited high‑skill opportunities.

What this means for residents of McDonough County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 51071.4, a median household income of $50,434 in McDonough County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $25,757,350 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
