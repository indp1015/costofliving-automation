📊 Local Market Analysis

Henry County, Indiana presents a cost-of-living profile that sits 7.9 percent below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 92.1 against a U.S. baseline of 100. The composite figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run only 4.4 percent below the national average at 95.6, while services, a basket covering healthcare, utilities, and dining, plummet to 71.3, the lowest component and 28.7 percent cheaper than the country at large. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 84.7, roughly 15 percent below the national mean. This structure makes daily life notably inexpensive for service-intensive households, yet the relative proximity of goods pricing to the national level means trips to the supermarket or gas station deliver less discount than a visit to a medical provider or a utility bill payment. Purchasing power data confirms the advantage. The median household income of $60,144 translates into a purchasing power index of 65,305.1 once local prices are factored in, effectively granting residents an additional $5,161 in real buying capacity compared with nominal earnings. Rent burden stands at a modest 16.1 percent of gross income, with median monthly rent of $807 comparing favorably to the national median of $928. Local labor dynamics reinforce this affordability: an unemployment rate of 4.1 percent signals a reasonably tight market, while a median age of 42.1 years and a bachelor’s-degree attainment rate of just 10.8 percent suggest a workforce concentrated in occupations that do not command premium wages but also do not face the cost pressures typical of high-skill, high-cost metros. The demographic profile implies that the low service-cost environment — particularly healthcare and utilities — disproportionately benefits an older, less credentialed population that spends a larger share of income on those categories. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward from 93.0 to 92.1, a marginal 1.0 percent decline in relative price levels. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 43.7 percent from $41,855 to $60,144, outpacing a 28.9 percent increase in median rent from $626 to $807. Real income growth has therefore been substantial; the gap between income appreciation and rent inflation widened the affordability cushion, while the slight dip in the overall price index amplified the gain. Goods prices have remained sticky near the national average, limiting the upside for discretionary spending on tangible items, but the persistent services discount has acted as a structural tailwind for household budgets. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly identical to those in average U.S. communities, while allocating significantly less for healthcare visits, electricity, and restaurant meals. The rent-to-income ratio leaves ample room for transportation, childcare, or savings, though the low educational attainment figure hints at limited high-wage job growth locally. Commuters to nearby Indianapolis may capture higher wages while retaining Henry County’s service-cost advantage, but that calculus introduces vehicle depreciation and time costs not captured in the RPP. Households reliant on local earnings will find the budget math favorable for essentials, with the primary friction point remaining the goods basket that refuses to discount.

What this means for residents of Henry County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 65305.1, a median household income of $60,144 in Henry County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $39,277,099 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
