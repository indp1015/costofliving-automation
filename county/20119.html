📊 Local Market Analysis

Meade County, Kansas enjoys a cost of living roughly 10 % below the national benchmark, according to the 2023 BEA Regional Price Parity index of 89.9, meaning the average consumer price here is about $89.90 for every $100 spent nationally. Within this composite, goods (groceries, apparel, fuel) sit at 96.5, essentially at parity with the country, while services (healthcare, utilities, dining) drop to 68.6, and rental costs sit at 90.8, slightly cheaper than the average. The goods component therefore drives the local cost structure, making everyday essentials the most expensive category, whereas the markedly lower services index keeps many discretionary expenses relatively affordable. The median household earns $74,868, outpacing the national median of $65,047 by 15.2 % and providing a robust buffer against the modest rent of $799, which consumes only 12.8 % of income. The Purchasing Power Index of 83,271.8 reflects the relative buying capacity of a typical resident after local price adjustments, indicating that despite a lower overall price level, the higher wage base yields a stronger real income position. The local labor market reinforces this picture: an unemployment rate of 1.2 % signals tight job availability, while a 13.5 % share of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher suggests a modestly educated workforce that may limit high‑skill wage growth but also keeps living costs contained. The median age of 37.5 points to a household composition tilted toward established families, which aligns with the observed demand for services that remain well below national pricing. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted downward, falling from 92.991 in 2010 to 89.908 in 2023, a -3.3 % shift that underscores a gradual easing of price pressures. In contrast, median income has risen sharply, climbing 43.3 % from $52,250 to $74,868, while median rent has increased more modestly, up 22.0 % from $655 to $799. This divergence shows wages outpacing housing cost growth, allowing households to retain a larger share of earnings for other expenditures. The combination of falling overall price levels and rising incomes suggests that the cost‑of‑living trajectory has been favorable for residents. Prospective residents should budget with the expectation that rent will occupy roughly 13 % of a typical household’s cash flow, leaving ample room for other expenses. Because goods sit near the national average, grocery and clothing budgets will mirror the broader market, whereas utilities, healthcare, and dining are substantially cheaper, offering opportunities to stretch discretionary dollars. The low unemployment environment provides job stability, and the higher median income relative to the national figure enhances overall purchasing power. Consequently, a household planning to relocate to Meade County can anticipate a lifestyle that is modestly priced overall, with the primary cost pressure centered on everyday goods rather than shelter or services.
