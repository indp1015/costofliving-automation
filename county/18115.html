📊 Local Market Analysis

Ohio County residents operate within a cost environment that sits 7.9 percentage points below the national average, as measured by the Bureau of Economic Analysis’ 2023 Regional Price Parity of 92.1, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest local cost component at 95.6, nearly converging with national pricing, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining out plunge to 71.3, the lowest segment and the primary driver of the county’s overall affordability advantage. Rental costs, indexed at 84.7, reinforce the discount but sit closer to the national mean than services do. Consequently, daily life is inexpensive for households that consume heavily from the local service economy — medical visits, electricity, restaurant meals — but offers less relief at the checkout counter for food and fuel, where prices track the broader market closely. That structural discount translates into tangible purchasing power for the median household earning $67,942, which exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 4.5 percent. After adjusting for the 92.1 price level, the Purchasing Power Index rises to 73,772.2, indicating that the typical worker retains roughly $5,830 in additional real income compared to a national benchmark earner. Housing absorbs only 15.0 percent of gross income at a median rent of $852, well below the national median of $928 and a fraction of the 30 percent burden threshold. The labor market underscores this stability: unemployment stands at a remarkably low 1.2 percent, suggesting tight employer demand that supports wage growth, while an older median age of 46.0 years and a modest 13.4 percent bachelor’s attainment rate point to an economy anchored in established, non-degree-intensive occupations rather than high-turnover knowledge sectors. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward marginally from 93.0 to 92.1, a 1.0 percent decline that signals consumer prices have softened slightly relative to the nation. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 39.3 percent from $48,780 to $67,942, outpacing the 27.0 percent rise in median rent from $671 to $852. Real wage growth has therefore been substantial; the gap between income appreciation and shelter cost inflation has widened the household budget cushion considerably. The goods index hovering near parity implies that any future inflation in groceries or energy would erode this gain faster than service-sector deflation could offset it. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly identical to metropolitan averages, while anticipating meaningful savings on healthcare premiums, utility bills, and dining expenses. The low rent burden frees cash flow for transportation — critical in a rural county — or accelerated debt repayment. However, the limited share of college-educated workers and the elevated median age suggest fewer high-wage job openings for newcomers seeking rapid career advancement, meaning household income growth may rely more on tenure and overtime than on credential-driven jumps.

What this means for residents of Ohio County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 73772.2, a median household income of $67,942 in Ohio County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $50,122,308 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
