📊 Local Market Analysis

Bradford County presents a cost-of-living profile that sits modestly above the national baseline, registering a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 103.6 against the U.S. average of 100. This aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in consumer expenditure: while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run 1.8 percent below the national index at 98.2, and rental housing costs sit 6.3 percent lower at 93.7, the services category—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—surges to 123.2, marking the highest cost component by a wide margin. Consequently, daily life for residents is defined less by the price of tangible goods or shelter and more by the recurring burden of service-sector inflation, where a routine medical visit or monthly utility bill carries a premium exceeding 23 percent over the national norm. This structural distortion drives the purchasing power calculus. The median household income of $59,740 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 8.2 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 57,644.1 indicates that local wages stretch slightly further than the raw income gap suggests, largely because the 93.7 rental index suppresses the largest line item in most household budgets. Median monthly rent of $838 consumes only 16.8 percent of gross income, a stark contrast to the national median rent of $928 and a level that leaves significant discretionary capacity for the median earner. However, that surplus is rapidly absorbed by the 123.2 services index; a household allocating the national average share of expenditure to healthcare and utilities would find their effective disposable income eroded well below the headline purchasing power figure. Demographic and labor market conditions reinforce this dynamic. An unemployment rate of 6.6 percent signals meaningful labor slack relative to tighter national markets, while a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 9.5 percent—less than half the national average—constrains upward wage mobility and concentrates employment in sectors where service costs are consumed but not easily offset by earnings growth. The median age of 40.5 years suggests a population in prime earning years but also one with escalating healthcare utilization, amplifying exposure to the county’s most inflated cost category. This combination of lower educational attainment, higher joblessness, and an aging workforce creates a feedback loop where the residents least positioned to negotiate higher wages face the steepest service-price escalation. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP climbed 3.2 percent from 100.4 to 103.6, a moderate cumulative increase that pales against the 43.6 percent surge in median income from $41,606 to $59,740. Rent appreciation of 18.9 percent over the same span further underscores that shelter costs have remained remarkably contained relative to earnings growth. For a prospective resident, the budgeting imperative is clear: the apparent affordability signaled by low rents and cheap goods is a necessary but insufficient condition for financial ease. The critical variable is the services basket; a household moving from a lower-cost service environment should model a 20-to-25 percent step-up in healthcare premiums, utility deposits, and dining expenses to avoid a structural deficit that the favorable rent-to-income ratio cannot offset.

What this means for residents of Bradford County, Florida

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57644.1, a median household income of $59,740 in Bradford County, Florida has the equivalent buying power of $34,436,585 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 93.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
