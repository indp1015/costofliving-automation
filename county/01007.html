📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Bibb County, Alabama, registers at 89.1 on the Beacon's Relative Price Index, placing it below the national benchmark of 100. This relative affordability masks a significant structural imbalance where goods and apparel carry the highest weight at 94.6, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining trail at 61.6. The goods category dominates local consumer expenditure, meaning daily household budgets are disproportionately influenced by transportation, groceries, and clothing costs. This concentration of spending power in the goods segment explains why residents must allocate a substantial portion of their income to basic necessities before addressing other life expenses. The service sector's lower weighting suggests relatively affordable healthcare and dining options, though this does not offset the broader financial pressure created by the goods-heavy structure. Median household income of $51,215 significantly trails the national median of $65,047, representing a 32.4% increase from a decade earlier. The median rent of $802 exceeds the national average of $928, though the gap narrows when considering the rent burden of 18.8% of income, indicating that while housing costs remain elevated, they represent a manageable portion of disposable earnings. The purchasing power index of 57,482.3, calculated relative to the national benchmark, demonstrates that residents retain substantially less purchasing power than their national counterparts. This metric, derived from the Census ACS 2023 data, quantifies the reduced ability to purchase goods and services at local prices compared to the national average, confirming that Bibb County residents face a more constrained budget environment despite the county ranking below the national cost of living index. The local economic context reveals a labor market characterized by a 10.0% unemployment rate and only 7.0% of residents holding a bachelor's degree or higher, with a median age of 41.3 years. These demographic factors shape the cost of living experience by constraining the local workforce's earning potential while simultaneously driving demand for goods and services. The relatively low educational attainment and elevated unemployment rate suggest limited upward mobility in the labor market, which prevents median wages from keeping pace with the 23.2% increase in median rent between 2010 and 2023. This divergence between wage growth and housing costs indicates that the cost of living in Bibb County has widened over the past decade, with essential expenses outstripping income gains for a significant portion of the population. Historical trends show the RPP declining from 89.783 in 2010 to 89.097 in 2023, a modest 0.8% decrease, while income and rent have both risen. The modest decline in the cost of living index contrasts with the substantial increase in median income and median rent, suggesting that while relative prices have stabilized, absolute living costs have continued to climb. Residents in Bibb County should budget with the understanding that goods represent the largest expense category, that purchasing power remains constrained by a purchasing power index below 60,000, and that the combination of moderate unemployment, limited educational attainment, and rising housing costs creates a challenging economic environment. Prospective residents must account for daily household expenses that are disproportionately driven by goods spending, with only a portion of income available for savings or discretionary spending after covering rent, utilities, and other essential services.
