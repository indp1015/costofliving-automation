📊 Local Market Analysis

San Juan County’s cost of living sits just above the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 101.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel and fuel are priced marginally lower than the country average at 99.2, while services—including healthcare, utilities and dining—are markedly higher at 130.5. Rental costs are the most affordable component, reflected in a housing index of 86.8, which translates to a median monthly rent of $1,076—still 16 % above the national median rent of $928 but far below the national services cost pressure. The services sector dominates the local price structure. At 130.5, it outpaces both the national average (100) and the county’s own goods index by more than 30 points, indicating that everyday expenses such as medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals consume a disproportionate share of household budgets. Goods remain near parity with the nation, suggesting that grocery bills and basic consumer items do not impose a premium. Housing, despite the higher nominal rent, is comparatively cheap when measured against the national price level, a result of the 86.8 housing index. Consequently, daily life in San Juan County feels expensive in the service realm but relatively affordable for food and shelter. Disposable income for the median household can be approximated by subtracting the rent burden from total earnings. With a median household income of $73,889 and a rent share of 17.5 % ($1,076 × 12 = $12,912), the median family retains roughly $60,977 for all other expenditures. The Purchasing Power Index of 72,542.6 underscores that, after adjusting for local price levels, residents command about 72 % of the purchasing power of an average American consumer. In other words, while nominal income exceeds the national median ($73,889 versus $65,047), the elevated service costs erode roughly one‑quarter of that advantage, leaving households with purchasing power modestly below the national benchmark. Demographically, the county’s 29.9 % share of adults with a bachelor’s degree or higher and a median age of 42.2 suggest a relatively mature, moderately educated labor force. The unemployment rate of 6.7 % exceeds the national average, hinting at a tighter labor market that may constrain wage growth. Higher education levels typically support higher earnings, yet the elevated service costs offset much of that benefit, especially for households reliant on fixed incomes or part‑time work. The age profile also implies greater healthcare utilization, which aligns with the inflated services index. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 100.59 in 2010 to 101.856 in 2023, a modest 1.3 % increase, while median household income more than doubled, climbing 103.4 % from $36,324 to $73,889. Rental rates grew 21.4 % in the same period, from $886 to $1,076. The divergence between income and price growth indicates that wages have outpaced overall consumer costs, yet the steep rise in service prices means that the net benefit is partially absorbed by higher day‑to‑day expenses. Prospective residents should budget carefully around the service sector. Assuming the median rent of $1,076, a household would allocate roughly 17.5 % of income to shelter, leaving about $5,080 per month for all other costs. Given the 130.5 services index, allocating 30–35 % of monthly income to healthcare, utilities and dining is prudent. Grocery spending can be planned at near‑national levels, but discretionary spending should be moderated to offset the service premium. Understanding that housing is comparatively cheap while services are costly allows newcomers to structure a realistic budget that preserves discretionary cash flow despite the county’s higher-than‑average service price environment.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of San Juan County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 72542.6, a median household income of $73,889 in San Juan County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $53,601,002 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
