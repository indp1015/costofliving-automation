📊 Local Market Analysis

Crawford County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national benchmark, with an overall Relative Price Parity (RPP) of 96.5 against the United States average of 100. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 97.7, while services are markedly cheaper at 88.3, and rental costs register at 92.3. The county’s median household income of $60,905 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 6.4%, yet the gap narrows when housing costs are considered: median rent of $751 is 19% lower than the national median rent of $928, translating to a rent‑burden ratio of only 14.8% of income. This combination of lower housing expenses and subdued service costs yields a Purchasing Power Index of 63,117.9, indicating that a typical Crawford household can afford roughly 63% of the goods and services that a median U.S. household can purchase with its income. The cost structure is driven primarily by the goods segment, which, at 97.7, remains the highest of the three categories. However, the relative cheapness of services—healthcare, utilities, and dining—offsets the modest premium on groceries, apparel, and fuel. Daily life therefore leans toward affordability: a resident spending $300 per month on utilities and healthcare would pay roughly $264 (88.3% of the national average), while a comparable grocery bill of $400 would cost about $390 (97.7% of the national level). Housing, at 92.3, adds another layer of savings, making the overall expense profile lighter than many parts of the country despite the slightly higher goods price. Disposable income calculations underscore the modest surplus. After allocating the median rent of $751, a household retains $60,154 of its annual earnings. Applying the overall RPP of 96.5 suggests that the remaining income can purchase approximately 96.5% of the national basket of goods and services, equating to a real disposable income of roughly $57,970. The Purchasing Power Index of 63,117.9 further confirms that, relative to national purchasing power, Crawford residents experience a 36.9% shortfall, largely attributable to lower wages rather than higher costs. Demographic factors shape this economic picture. With only 7.9% of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the labor pool is skewed toward lower‑skill occupations, which typically command lower wages. The median age of 46 signals a mature population, often associated with higher savings rates and reduced discretionary spending. An unemployment rate of 4.1% aligns closely with the national rate, suggesting a stable labor market but not one that drives significant wage growth. The limited educational attainment and older age profile together temper demand for premium services, reinforcing the low service‑price index. Over the past decade, RPP rose from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest 2.0% increase, while median household income surged 45.6% from $41,825 to $60,905. Rental costs grew only 7.1% in the same period, from $701 to $751. The disparity between income growth and price growth indicates that wages have outpaced consumer expenses, enhancing real purchasing power for residents who have remained in the county throughout the period. Prospective residents should budget with the following benchmarks: allocate roughly 12% of gross income to rent ($751 monthly for a median earner), anticipate utilities and healthcare expenses at about $264 per month, and plan grocery and fuel expenditures near $390 monthly. With a rent‑burden well below the 30% threshold commonly cited as affordable, the primary financial pressure will stem from discretionary spending on goods, where prices are nearer to the national norm. Maintaining a modest lifestyle that leverages the county’s low service costs will preserve disposable income and sustain a comfortable standard of living.

What this means for residents of Crawford County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 63117.9, a median household income of $60,905 in Crawford County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $38,441,957 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
