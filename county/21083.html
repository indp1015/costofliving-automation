📊 Local Market Analysis

Graves County presents a cost of living profile that sits 10.1 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a highly uneven consumer landscape. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest cost component at 94.5, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. benchmark, while rental housing costs land at 80.9. The dramatic outlier is the services category, which includes healthcare, utilities, and dining, priced at just 62.9 on the national index. This 37.1 percent discount on services drives the county’s overall affordability, suggesting that while a trip to the supermarket or filling station delivers limited savings, recurring monthly obligations such as electricity, medical visits, and local restaurant meals are substantially less burdensome than in most American communities. The median household income of $50,576 trails the national median of $65,047 by 22.2 percent, but the Purchasing Power Index of 56,264.3 indicates that local wages stretch considerably further once the depressed price level is factored in. Housing consumes a modest 16.9 percent of gross income at the median rent of $713, compared to a national median rent of $928, leaving a larger share of discretionary cash flow for goods, savings, or debt service. Local labor market conditions reinforce this dynamic: a 4.0 percent unemployment rate signals a tight market, yet educational attainment remains low with only 10.0 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and a median age of 40.0 years points to a settled, experienced workforce. The demographic profile suggests an economy anchored in sectors that do not require advanced credentials, which aligns with the modest nominal wage growth but also limits upward pressure on service-sector pricing. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the overall RPP drifted downward from 90.4 to 89.9, a marginal 0.6 percent decline, while median household income surged 27.9 percent from $39,530 to $50,576, outpacing the 24.0 percent rise in median rent from $575 to $713. This divergence confirms that real purchasing power has improved meaningfully for the typical resident, as income growth has exceeded both general inflation and shelter cost escalation. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the goods-services split: grocery and fuel budgets will resemble national norms, requiring no major adjustment, but utility bills, healthcare copays, and dining out will likely come in well below expectations. The low rent burden provides a buffer against goods-price volatility, though the limited high-wage employment base implies that significant income advancement may require commuting or remote work arrangements.

What this means for residents of Graves County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 56264.3, a median household income of $50,576 in Graves County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $28,456,232 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
