📊 Local Market Analysis

Hodgeman County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 for 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — command an index of 96.5, nearly converging with national pricing, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 68.6, the county’s most pronounced discount. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 90.8. The goods category effectively anchors the local cost floor, preventing deeper deflation in the overall index; a household filling a grocery cart or fueling a vehicle experiences prices nearly indistinguishable from the U.S. average, whereas the 31.4 percent discount on services delivers the primary relief to monthly budgets. The median household income of $63,125 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 3 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 70,210.7 reveals that local earnings stretch considerably further than nominal wages suggest. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 17.1 percent of income against a median monthly rent of $900, compared to the national median of $928, freeing substantial discretionary capacity for savings or goods consumption. However, the 58.7 percent surge in median rent since 2010 — from $567 to $900 — has vastly outpaced the 15.1 percent growth in median income over the same period, signaling a structural erosion of housing affordability that the current low burden ratio only partially obscures. The overall RPP declined 3.3 percent over the decade, suggesting broad price stability, but the rent trajectory indicates a specific pressure point for new entrants to the rental market. Demographic and labor dynamics reinforce the county’s economic profile. An unemployment rate of 2.8 percent signals a tight labor market that typically supports wage growth, yet the relatively low educational attainment — 23.7 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher — and a median age of 45.9 years suggest an economy reliant on established, non-degree-intensive sectors. This composition likely caps median income growth potential relative to metropolitan areas, making the low service-cost environment critical for maintaining real living standards. The aging workforce may also amplify demand for the heavily discounted healthcare and utility services captured in the services index, aligning consumption patterns with the county’s primary cost advantage. A prospective resident should budget for goods expenses that mirror national levels, allocating roughly 96.5 cents for every dollar spent on groceries and fuel elsewhere, while anticipating significant savings on insurance premiums, medical visits, and utility bills. The rental market, while currently absorbing only 17.1 percent of median income, carries momentum; the historical rent acceleration implies that lease renewals or new agreements may reset at higher thresholds faster than income adjusts. Households relocating from higher-cost regions will experience immediate relief on service-intensive line items but should not extrapolate the current rent-to-income ratio indefinitely without verifying local wage trajectory against the established decade-long rent appreciation trend.

What this means for residents of Hodgeman County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70210.7, a median household income of $63,125 in Hodgeman County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $44,320,504 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
