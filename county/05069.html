📊 Local Market Analysis

Jefferson County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 for 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest relative expense at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the U.S. baseline, while Services plummet to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs sit at 81.9, offering meaningful shelter savings versus the national median rent of $928, with the local median settling at $849. This bifurcation means daily essentials like food and gasoline absorb a disproportionate share of disposable income compared to healthcare, utilities, or dining out, which are deeply discounted. The median household income of $48,552 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 55,929, effectively granting residents a 15.2 percent boost in real buying capacity relative to their nominal earnings. Rent burden stands at a manageable 21.0 percent of gross income, well below the 30 percent stress threshold, leaving theoretical headroom for other outlays. However, the labor market context complicates this picture: a 7.5 percent unemployment rate signals significant slack, and an educational attainment rate of just 14.4 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher suggests a workforce concentrated in lower-wage sectors. With a median age of 39.5, the population skews toward prime working years, yet the elevated jobless rate implies that the favorable rent-to-income ratio may not be accessible for a sizable segment of the community. Over the 2010–2023 period, nominal median income surged 32.1 percent from $36,747, outpacing the 26.9 percent climb in median rent from $669. Simultaneously, the overall RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2.0 percent decline in relative price levels. This combination indicates that local wage growth has not only kept pace with shelter inflation but has also benefited from a slight improvement in general affordability versus the nation. The divergence between Goods inflation (sticky near parity) and Services deflation (deepening discount) likely underpins the RPP decline, favoring households that consume heavily in the latter category. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality hinges on the Goods anomaly. While the $79 monthly rent discount against the national median provides immediate relief, the near-national pricing for groceries and fuel—93.1 versus 100—erodes that advantage at the checkout counter. A household relocating from a high-cost metro would experience sharp relief on housing and services but should not extrapolate that savings to the weekly grocery bill or commute costs. The low Services index suggests healthcare and utility expenses are genuinely cheap, yet the high unemployment rate introduces income volatility risk that no price index can offset.

What this means for residents of Jefferson County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55929.0, a median household income of $48,552 in Jefferson County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $27,154,648 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
