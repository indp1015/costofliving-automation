📊 Local Market Analysis

Leslie County presents a cost of living profile that sits 10.1 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a severe structural distortion in local consumer prices. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—represent the highest cost component at 94.5, nearly converging with national levels, while Services plummet to 62.9, the lowest component by a wide margin, and Rental Costs settle at 80.9. This inversion creates a unique budgeting environment: residents pay near-national prices for tangible daily necessities but capture deep discounts on housing and service-intensive categories like healthcare, utilities, and dining. The median monthly rent of $520 consumes only 15.5 percent of the $40,176 median household income, a rent burden significantly lighter than the national benchmark where $928 median rent weighs on a $65,047 income, yet the high Goods index ensures that disposable income remaining after shelter is rapidly absorbed by the supermarket and the gas pump. Purchasing power dynamics reveal the true economic strain beneath the low headline RPP. The Purchasing Power Index of 44,694.6 indicates that the median household’s $40,176 income stretches to the equivalent of roughly $44,695 in national purchasing terms, a gain driven almost entirely by the 37.1 percent discount on Services and the 19.1 percent discount on Rent. However, this advantage is fragile; because Goods carry a 94.5 index, every dollar spent on food, clothing, and transportation yields only 94.5 cents of national value. With a local unemployment rate of 7.4 percent—substantially above national norms—and only 6.6 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree, the labor market offers limited high-wage pathways to offset the regressive nature of goods inflation. The median age of 42.2 suggests a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the educational attainment data implies a concentration in sectors where wage growth historically lags productivity, leaving households vulnerable to supply-side shocks in fuel or food supply chains. Over the past decade, the economic trajectory has favored income growth over cost escalation, though the starting base was exceptionally low. The RPP drifted marginally from 90.433 in 2010 to 89.89 in 2023, a negligible 0.6 percent decline in relative prices, while median household income surged 55.3 percent from $25,872 to $40,176. Median rent increased only 18.7 percent over the same period, rising from $438 to $520, confirming that housing costs have not eroded income gains. This divergence suggests that local wage growth—likely influenced by minimum wage adjustments and tightness in specific service sectors—has outpaced the modest inflationary pressure on shelter, a rare dynamic in the current national environment. However, the stability of the Goods index near 95 implies that the primary volatility risk for household budgets remains external, tied to national commodity cycles rather than local market forces. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. The low rent burden—$520 monthly—frees cash flow for vehicle ownership, a necessity in a rural county where service access requires travel, but the 94.5 Goods index means fuel and vehicle maintenance carry near-urban price tags without urban wage offsets. Healthcare and utility services, indexed at 62.9, provide a meaningful buffer against catastrophic expense, yet the low educational attainment and elevated unemployment signal that income disruption risk is higher than average. A household budget here functions efficiently only if transportation costs are contained and employment is secured; the margin for error is defined not by rent, which is cheap, but by the grocery bill and the gas pump, which are not.

What this means for residents of Leslie County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 44694.6, a median household income of $40,176 in Leslie County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $17,956,502 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
