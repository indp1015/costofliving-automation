📊 Local Market Analysis

Caribou County’s cost of living is notably below the national benchmark, with an overall RPP of 92.2, meaning consumer prices are roughly 7.8 percent cheaper than the national average. The Goods component sits at 95.9, just under parity with the nation, while Services register at 86.9, indicating a 13.1 percent discount relative to the United States. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable segment at 68.1, reflecting a substantial 31.9 percent reduction from the national median rent of $928. The local cost structure is driven primarily by Goods, which remain the highest index at 95.9, suggesting that everyday items such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced close to the national norm. In contrast, Services and Rental Costs are markedly lower, making daily expenses for dining, utilities, and shelter relatively inexpensive. Consequently, residents experience a balanced budget where essential services and housing consume a smaller share of income, while the Goods category maintains a modest premium that offsets the overall affordability. Median household income of $66,653 exceeds the national median of $65,047, providing a slight income advantage. With a median monthly rent of $800, rent consumes 14.4 percent of income, leaving ample discretionary cash flow. The Purchasing Power Index of 72,263.5 underscores the county’s ability to purchase goods and services, as the lower cost‑of‑living environment amplifies real disposable income. After housing, a typical household retains roughly $4,750 per month, which, when adjusted for the 92.2 RPP, translates into a purchasing capability well above the national average for comparable earnings. Local demographics shape this economic picture: a low unemployment rate of 2.9 percent signals a tight labor market, yet only 9.7 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, which may limit high‑skill wage growth. The median age of 38 years reflects a mature population that often prioritizes stable, lower‑cost housing and essential services. These factors combine to keep housing demand modest despite income gains, reinforcing the affordability observed in the rental market. Over the past decade, the RPP has edged down from 93.2 in 2010 to 92.236 in 2023, a marginal 1.0 percent decline, while median income has risen 14.9 percent to $66,653. Median rent, however, has surged 47.6 percent to $800, outpacing wage growth. The overall cost‑of‑living index’s slight decrease suggests that reductions in Services and other components have offset rent increases, allowing household budgets to remain balanced. Prospective residents should budget approximately $800 for rent, anticipate Goods expenses near the national average, and recognize that Services and utilities will be notably cheaper, resulting in a net monthly outlay well within the median household’s income.
