📊 Local Market Analysis

Cleburne County presents a cost of living profile that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet the internal composition of that index reveals a distorted consumer landscape where the price of daily necessities barely discounts against the nation while core services collapse to nearly half the national rate. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—carry an index of 93.1, making them the highest cost component and only a marginal bargain compared to metropolitan centers, whereas services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 56.7, and rental costs settle at 81.9. This bifurcation means a household budget weighted toward transportation and food experiences a far smaller relief than the headline figure suggests, while those consuming heavy local services capture disproportionate savings. The median household income of $55,979 trails the national median of $65,047 by roughly 14 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 64,484.5 indicates that local wages stretch further than nominal dollars imply, effectively granting residents the purchasing capacity of a $64,500 income in an average-cost environment. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 18.3 percent of gross income, with median monthly rent at $853 against the national $928, leaving significant discretionary capacity for the median earner. However, the labor market context complicates this picture: a 6.5 percent unemployment rate exceeds typical national lows, and an educational attainment rate of just 12.5 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher suggests a workforce concentrated in lower-wage sectors, limiting upward mobility even as current costs remain manageable. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative price levels, while median income surged 30.5 percent from $42,905 and median rent climbed 29.2 percent from $660. Wage growth has marginally outpaced shelter inflation, improving the rent-to-income ratio over the period, though the velocity of rent increases signals tightening rental inventory relative to population dynamics. The aging demographic profile, with a median age of 49.5 years, implies a rising dependency ratio that may pressure local service pricing—particularly healthcare—going forward, even as the current services index remains historically depressed. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that track near national norms, resisting the assumption that a low overall index translates to cheap daily staples. The outsized discount on services—especially utilities and healthcare—represents the primary fiscal advantage, effectively subsidizing fixed monthly overhead. Conversely, the elevated unemployment rate and low educational attainment signal a fragile wage floor; any disruption to household income would be felt acutely given the limited local high-skill employment base. Budgeting models should weight goods consumption at near-parity pricing while treating housing and services as the primary sources of arbitrage value.

What this means for residents of Cleburne County, Arkansas
