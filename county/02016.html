📊 Local Market Analysis

The Aleutians West Census Area registers a cost of living that modestly exceeds the national baseline, with an overall RPP of 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices sit at 103.7, barely above the average, while services are priced 3.3 % lower than the national norm at 96.7. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the outlier at 113.3, indicating that a typical apartment costs roughly 13 % more than the U.S. average. Median monthly rent of $1,886 dwarfs the national median of $928, confirming that shelter is the dominant expense driver for residents. The expense profile is therefore weighted heavily toward housing. Even though groceries, apparel and fuel are only slightly above average, the high rent consumes a disproportionate share of household budgets. With a median household income of $107,344—62 % higher than the national median of $65,047—most families can absorb the elevated housing bill, as rent represents 21.1 % of income, well below the conventional 30 % affordability threshold. After deducting the median rent, a typical household retains roughly $103,458 in annual discretionary income, reflected in a Purchasing Power Index of 103,918, which signals that residents enjoy slightly greater buying power than the national average despite the pricey rentals. Demographic factors reinforce the cost picture. The area’s unemployment rate of 4.1 % aligns closely with the national rate, suggesting a stable labor market, while only 14.8 % of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, higher‑wage occupations such as fishing, tourism, and government services. The median age of 39.1 years points to a mature population likely at peak earning potential, further supporting the ability to meet higher housing costs. The combination of modest educational attainment and stable employment explains why wages have risen faster than rents but not enough to erase the housing premium entirely. Over the past decade, the RPP index nudged upward from 103.099 to 103.297, a marginal 0.2 % increase, while median household income surged 27.3 % from $84,306 to $107,344. Rental prices, however, jumped 48.5 % from $1,270 to $1,886, outpacing income growth and widening the gap between earnings and housing costs. The data suggest that while overall purchasing power has improved, the relative burden of rent remains the principal budgetary pressure. Prospective residents should allocate a larger portion of their monthly budget to shelter than the national norm, anticipate modest savings on everyday services, and plan discretionary spending around a higher baseline cost for housing. Careful budgeting for utilities, healthcare and food—each priced near or slightly above national levels—will be essential to maintain a comfortable standard of living in the Aleutians West region.
