📊 Local Market Analysis

Hawaii County's cost of living remains above the national benchmark, with housing costs driving the premium. The overall Cost of Living index of 109.7 indicates consumer prices are 9.7 percent higher than the national average of 100, while the breakdown shows goods at 110.3, services at 128.7 and rental costs at a striking 191.7. The disparity is most pronounced in housing, which exceeds the national baseline by 91.7 percent, dwarfing the modest 10.3 percent premium for goods and the 28.7 percent premium for services. Consequently, daily life in the county is expensive primarily because shelter consumes a disproportionate share of household budgets, leaving less room for discretionary spending. The consumption profile underscores housing as the dominant cost driver. Services rank second at 128.7, reflecting higher expenses for healthcare, utilities and dining out, while goods remain the cheapest category at 110.3. A median household earning $77,215 faces a rent burden of 21.9 percent, with median monthly rent of $1,411. After allocating $1,411 to housing, roughly $4,800 remains for other expenses before taxes, and after accounting for the overall cost‑of‑living premium, disposable income is further compressed. The Purchasing Power Index of 70,385.5 suggests that, relative to the national baseline, the median worker’s real purchasing ability is modestly reduced, despite an income level that exceeds the national median of $65,047. Local demographics shape this cost structure. An unemployment rate of 6.2 percent is above the national norm, limiting labor market flexibility and potentially inflating wages for scarce skilled positions. Only 19.0 percent of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, which may constrain high‑paying sector growth and reinforce reliance on service‑oriented employment. The median age of 44.1 years indicates an older population, often associated with higher healthcare utilization and stable housing demand, further pressuring rental markets. Historical trends reveal divergent movements. The Regional Price Parity (RPP) has been nearly flat, moving from 109.56 in 2010 to 109.703 in 2023, a negligible 0.1 percent increase. In contrast, median household income has risen 48.2 percent, from $52,108 to $77,215, while median rent has climbed 33.6 percent, from $1,056 to $1,411. Wage growth has outpaced housing cost inflation, yet the overall cost of living remains elevated because housing’s weight in the index is disproportionately large. Prospective residents should budget for a rent burden near one‑fifth of income, anticipate service costs that exceed the national average by roughly 29 percent, and recognize that goods are only slightly more expensive than the national baseline. The combination of high housing premiums and elevated service expenses means that discretionary spending will be constrained unless income growth continues to outstrip rent increases.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Hawaii County, Hawaii

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70385.5, a median household income of $77,215 in Hawaii County, Hawaii has the equivalent buying power of $54,348,164 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 191.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
