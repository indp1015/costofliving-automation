📊 Local Market Analysis

Pike County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national average, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 89.9 for 2023. This overall discount masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — run at 94.5, merely 5.5 points below the national baseline, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 62.9, a staggering 37.1-point discount that represents the county's primary affordability engine. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 80.9, roughly 19 points cheaper than the national mean. The goods category effectively drives the local cost structure upward; a household spending heavily on transportation fuel, food at home, and clothing experiences prices nearly comparable to metropolitan areas, whereas those consuming more services — medical visits, restaurant meals, utility-heavy lifestyles — capture outsized savings. The median household income of $42,179 translates to a purchasing power index of $46,922.9, meaning the typical earner commands roughly $4,744 more in real goods and services than the nominal wage suggests after adjusting for local prices. Rent consumes 23.8 percent of gross income at a median $837 monthly, well below the national median rent of $928 and the 30-percent burden threshold, leaving discretionary capacity for other outlays. However, the labor market context complicates this picture: a 6.2 percent unemployment rate exceeds national norms, while only 8.8 percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher, constraining upward wage mobility. The median age of 42.9 years signals an older workforce with potentially fixed incomes or limited retraining options, amplifying sensitivity to the goods-side inflation that disproportionately affects daily necessities. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted marginally from 90.433 to 89.89, a negligible 0.6-percent decline indicating stable relative price levels. Nominal median income rose 27.1 percent from $33,183 to $42,179, yet median rent accelerated 35.9 percent from $616 to $837, outpacing wage growth by nearly nine percentage points. This divergence eroded the rent-to-income ratio over the decade, even as the current 23.8-percent burden remains manageable. The faster rent appreciation suggests housing supply constraints or demand pressures not fully captured by the broader RPP stability, a dynamic that warrants monitoring for budget planning. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that rival national averages, with the 94.5 goods index implying a weekly food-and-transport run costing perhaps $180–$220 for a family of four versus $190–$230 nationally. Healthcare and utility savings are substantial — the 62.9 services index suggests a typical monthly utility bundle of $250–$300 against a $400–$480 national norm, and primary-care visits potentially 30–40 percent cheaper. Rent at $837 median leaves room in a $3,515 monthly gross budget, but the 6.2 percent unemployment rate and low educational attainment mean income volatility is a real consideration; maintaining a larger emergency fund than the standard three-month recommendation would be prudent given the local labor market's fragility.

What this means for residents of Pike County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 46922.9, a median household income of $42,179 in Pike County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $19,791,610 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
