📊 Local Market Analysis

Douglas County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity index registering 89.9 for 2023 against a U.S. baseline of 100. The composite figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 96.5, nearly parity with national averages, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 68.6, the lowest component and a 31.4-point discount to the country. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 90.8, modestly below the national mean but notably higher than the services basket. This configuration makes daily life inexpensive in aggregate, driven primarily by the deep services discount rather than cheap goods or housing; a household's marginal dollar stretches furthest on recurring service outlays such as medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals, while grocery trips and fuel fills deliver savings of only roughly 3.5 percent versus the typical American city. Purchasing power metrics confirm the advantage. The median household income of $68,756 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 5.7 percent, and when adjusted for the local price level, the purchasing power index rises to $76,474 — an 11.2 percent real-income premium over the nominal figure. Rent burden stands at a manageable 18.6 percent of gross income, with median monthly rent of $1,066 tracking below the national $928 only in nominal terms; the real burden is lighter still given the income differential. The labor market context reinforces this picture: a 4.7 percent unemployment rate signals slack but not distress, while a relatively young median age of 31.3 years and a 29.0 percent bachelor's attainment rate suggest a workforce tilted toward early-career, degree-holding renters who benefit disproportionately from the services deflation. These demographics amplify the practical value of the low services index, as younger, educated cohorts typically allocate larger budget shares to healthcare, dining, and utilities relative to goods. Over the 2010–2023 window, the RPP declined from 92.99 to 89.91, a 3.3 percent drop in relative price levels, even as nominal median income surged 35.0 percent from $50,939 to $68,756 and median rent climbed 24.1 percent from $859 to $1,066. Wage growth has not merely kept pace with shelter costs; it has outstripped them by nearly eleven percentage points, while the broader consumption basket has become cheaper in real terms. The decoupling of income trajectory from the RPP trend implies that the county's affordability improvement is structural — rooted in services-sector pricing dynamics and income composition — rather than a cyclical artifact of rent suppression. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror national norms, with a typical weekly food shop for a family of four likely running $180–$220 and a 15-gallon fill-up costing within a few dollars of the U.S. average. The meaningful savings emerge in recurring service lines: health insurance premiums, outpatient visits, electricity and gas utility bills, and restaurant meals can run 25–35 percent below comparable metros, effectively subsidizing discretionary spending or accelerated debt repayment. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment near the university corridor will approach $1,100–$1,200, consuming roughly one-fifth of gross median income, but the residual cash flow after shelter and goods is unusually robust. Households relocating from higher-RPP regions should recalibrate expectations for services spending downward while maintaining standard goods allocations, recognizing that the purchasing power premium is real but concentrated in specific budget lines.

What this means for residents of Douglas County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 76473.7, a median household income of $68,756 in Douglas County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $52,580,257 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
