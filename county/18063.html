📊 Local Market Analysis

Hendricks County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 7.9 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 92.1 in 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run only 4.4 percent below the national baseline at 95.6, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 71.3, a staggering 28.7 percent discount. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 84.7, roughly 15 percent cheaper than the U.S. average. The data reveals that daily life is structurally inexpensive primarily because the service economy—often the largest recurring line item in household budgets—is deeply discounted relative to the rest of the country, whereas the tangible goods residents buy at the pump or grocery store track much closer to national pricing. This consumption breakdown creates a distinct purchasing power dynamic. The median household income of $99,988 towers 53.7 percent above the national median of $65,047, and when adjusted for the local price level, the Purchasing Power Index surges to 108,568.1. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 16.4 percent of gross income, with the median monthly rent of $1,364 consuming a far smaller slice of earnings than the national median rent of $928 does for the typical U.S. earner. Effectively, the median worker retains substantial disposable income after covering shelter, a cushion amplified by the steep discount on services. A household here effectively commands nearly $109,000 in real purchasing capacity, a figure that illustrates how the combination of high nominal wages and low service costs expands the practical budget for discretionary spending, savings, or debt reduction. Local demographics and labor conditions reinforce this advantage. An unemployment rate of 2.5 percent signals an exceptionally tight labor market that supports the elevated wage floor, while a median age of 38.6 suggests a workforce in prime earning years. Educational attainment, with 25.4 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, aligns with a commuter-heavy economy feeding into the Indianapolis metro area, where professional services compensation drives the income premium. The labor market suggests stability; low unemployment typically correlates with wage resilience, meaning the income side of the household ledger appears well-defended against near-term erosion, even if the educational profile indicates a mix of white-collar and skilled-trade employment rather than a concentrated high-tech workforce. Over the past decade, the arithmetic of affordability has held remarkably steady. The RPP drifted only marginally from 93.0 in 2010 to 92.1 in 2023, a negligible 1.0 percent decline in relative price levels. Simultaneously, median income surged 42.5 percent from $70,163 to $99,988, while median rent rose a nearly identical 42.4 percent from $958 to $1,364. This synchronization means the rent-to-income ratio has remained effectively constant, preserving the low burden profile. For a prospective resident, the budgeting takeaway is clear: allocate a smaller-than-average share of income to housing and services—particularly healthcare and utilities—but expect grocery and fuel bills to mirror national averages. The primary financial friction point remains goods consumption, where the 95.6 index offers little relief, making bulk purchasing or fuel efficiency the actionable levers for household optimization.

What this means for residents of Hendricks County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 108568.1, a median household income of $99,988 in Hendricks County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $108,555,072 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
