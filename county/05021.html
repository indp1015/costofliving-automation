📊 Local Market Analysis

Clay County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 86.8 against a U.S. average of 100. The composite figure masks sharp divergence across categories: goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run at 93.1, only marginally cheaper than the country at large, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining collapse to 56.7, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs settle at 81.9, meaning a household paying the median monthly rent of $696 spends roughly 17.2 percent of the $48,500 median income on shelter, well under the 30 percent threshold that typically signals burden. That rent-to-income ratio translates into a purchasing power index of 55,869, effectively granting the median earner the equivalent of $7,369 in additional real income compared with the nominal figure. The local labor market, however, frames this advantage in sobering context: a 6.3 percent unemployment rate exceeds the national norm, and only 9.4 percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower-wage sectors. The median age of 42.6 years further indicates a population skewing older than the national average, which may suppress wage growth while increasing demand for the very services — healthcare chief among them — that are unusually inexpensive here. Over the past decade the RPP has drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative price levels. During the same span nominal median income surged 49.0 percent, from $32,552 to $48,500, while median rent climbed 36.7 percent, from $509 to $696. Wage growth has therefore outpaced both inflation and housing cost appreciation, expanding real purchasing power for incumbent households. The gap between income gains and rent increases also implies that new entrants to the rental market face less pressure than in metros where shelter costs have decoupled from earnings. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that track close to national prices, offset by deeply discounted healthcare, utility, and restaurant expenses. The 17.2 percent rent burden leaves roughly 83 cents of every dollar for non-housing consumption, a cushion that narrows quickly if commuting distances lengthen or if household size pushes grocery spending above the goods index baseline. With services priced at barely half the national level, out-of-pocket medical costs and utility deposits represent the most volatile line items; a single uncovered procedure or seasonal rate spike can erase months of savings accumulated from low rent.

What this means for residents of Clay County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55869.1, a median household income of $48,500 in Clay County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $27,096,514 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
