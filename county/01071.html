📊 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County, Alabama, exhibits a cost of living that remains below the national benchmark, with an overall RPP of 89.1 (National = 100). Goods (groceries, apparel, fuel) sit at 94.6, slightly above the national average, while Services (healthcare, utilities, dining) are markedly cheaper at 61.6. Housing, measured by rental costs, tracks closely with the national level at 89.9. The disparity between the relatively high Goods index and the low Services index indicates that everyday consumables drive the local cost structure, making routine purchases modestly more expensive than the national norm while utilities and personal services are substantially affordable. The median household income of $49,454 yields a purchasing power index of 55,505.8, reflecting the amount of goods and services a typical resident can acquire after accounting for local price levels. Rent consumes 18.5% of that income, well within the conventional affordability threshold, leaving discretionary income for other expenditures. The labor market presents an unemployment rate of 5.4% and an educational profile where only 9.5% of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, a demographic composition that tends to align with lower-cost consumption patterns and limited high‑wage opportunities. Over the past decade, the county’s RPP has edged down from 89.783 (2010) to 89.097 (2023), a modest -0.8% shift, while median income has risen 31.0% from $37,745 to $49,454 and median rent has increased 35.1% from $564 to $762. Wage growth outpaces the slight decline in overall cost pressure, suggesting that residents’ earnings are gradually catching up to, and modestly surpassing, the incremental rise in rental expenses. The Goods component, however, has remained relatively stable above the national baseline, indicating that inflation in consumable categories has not been fully offset by income gains. Prospective residents should budget for a higher proportion of spending on groceries and everyday items, as the Goods index of 94.6 implies that a $100 national basket costs roughly $95 locally. Utilities and dining remain inexpensive, offering savings that can offset the modest rent burden of $762 per month. The median age of 43.4 and limited higher‑education attainment suggest a community where long‑term residents may prioritize essential consumption over discretionary spending. Accordingly, a realistic household budget should allocate roughly 30% of the median income to housing and essentials, leaving approximately 70% for other needs and savings, while monitoring the cost trajectory of Goods to avoid unexpected strain on weekly grocery and fuel expenditures.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Jackson County, Alabama

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55505.8, a median household income of $49,454 in Jackson County, Alabama has the equivalent buying power of $27,449,838 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 89.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
