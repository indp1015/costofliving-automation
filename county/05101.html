📊 Local Market Analysis

Newton County, Arkansas registers an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8, placing the cost of living roughly 13 percent below the national baseline and establishing the area as a distinctly low-cost environment for consumer expenditures. The aggregate figure masks significant divergence across categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel carry an index of 93.1, only seven percent below the U.S. average, while services — encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining — plummet to 56.7, nearly half the national price level. Rental costs sit at 81.9, offering meaningful shelter savings relative to the broader market. Consequently, daily life is inexpensive primarily because service-intensive consumption — medical visits, electricity, restaurant meals — comes at a steep discount, whereas the unavoidable weekly outlay for goods tracks much closer to national norms. The median household income of $47,395 trails the national median of $65,047 by 27 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 54,596.2 indicates that local wages stretch considerably further than nominal dollars suggest. Rent burden stands at a remarkably low 15.9 percent of income, with the median monthly rent of $629 comparing favorably against the $928 national figure. Demographic factors reinforce this dynamic: a median age of 48.9 years signals a population weighted toward retirement and fixed incomes, while a bachelor’s degree attainment rate of just 9.3 percent reflects a labor market anchored in lower-wage sectors. The 3.9 percent unemployment rate suggests a tight labor market, but the educational profile implies limited upward pressure on wages for the existing workforce. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, a modest 2.0 percent decline in relative price levels that contrasts sharply with local nominal growth trajectories. Median household income rose 37.2 percent from $34,536, yet median rent accelerated 70.0 percent from $370, outpacing income growth by a factor of nearly two. This divergence indicates that while the region has become marginally cheaper relative to the nation, the primary shelter cost for renters has surged in absolute terms, eroding a portion of the income gains accumulated since 2010. For a prospective resident, budgeting requires weighting the near-parity pricing of goods against the deep discount on services. A household relocating from a metropolitan area should anticipate grocery and fuel bills that feel familiar, while healthcare premiums, utility bills, and dining expenses will likely drop significantly. The critical vulnerability lies in rental trajectory: although the current 15.9 percent rent-to-income ratio provides ample breathing room, the historical velocity of rent increases — double the pace of income growth — suggests that shelter affordability could deteriorate rapidly if local supply constraints persist. Fixed-income retirees drawn by the low service costs must specifically model goods inflation risk, as their consumption basket leans heavily on the category where local pricing offers the least advantage.

What this means for residents of Newton County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 54596.2, a median household income of $47,395 in Newton County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $25,875,869 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
