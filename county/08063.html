📊 Local Market Analysis

Kit Carson County residents navigate a cost structure that sits marginally above the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) registering 101.9 for 2023 against a national index of 100. This aggregate figure masks a stark bifurcation in household budgets: while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run 0.8 percent below the national average at 99.2, and rental housing costs sit a significant 13.2 percent cheaper at 86.8, the services category—encompassing healthcare, utilities, and dining—surges to 130.5, or 30.5 percent above the national norm. This services premium acts as the primary inflationary driver for local consumers, effectively neutralizing the savings captured on rent and retail goods and pushing the overall cost of living into slightly positive territory. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life affordability hinges on the specific composition of a household’s spending basket. A median monthly rent of $916 consumes only 18.4 percent of the $59,810 median household income, a rent burden substantially lighter than the national median rent of $928 against a higher national median income of $65,047. However, the Purchasing Power Index of 58,720.2 indicates that after adjusting for the local price level, the real value of local earnings falls roughly $1,090 below the nominal median income. Residents allocating a larger share of expenditure to healthcare, childcare, or utility services face a steeper effective cost curve than those whose budgets weight heavily toward housing and tradable goods, creating a divergence in lived experience that the headline RPP obscures. Local labor market dynamics and demographics provide critical context for these price pressures. An unemployment rate of 2.8 percent signals a tight labor market that typically exerts upward pressure on service-sector wages, potentially feeding the elevated services RPP. Meanwhile, an educational attainment rate of just 13.1 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher—well below state and national averages—suggests a workforce concentrated in sectors where wage growth may struggle to outpace service inflation. The median age of 39.0 years implies a population in prime earning years, yet the decade-long trend shows the RPP creeping up 1.3 percent from 100.59 in 2010 to 101.856 in 2023. Over that same period, nominal median income surged 47.3 percent from $40,603, outpacing the 36.3 percent rise in median rent from $672, suggesting wages have largely kept pace with shelter costs but the data is silent on whether they have matched the trajectory of the services index. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus requires weighting the certainty of low housing costs against the volatility of service expenses. The 13.2 percent discount on rent relative to the nation provides a stable, predictable anchor for the household ledger. Conversely, the 30.5 percent premium on services introduces exposure to categories—healthcare, utilities, local professional services—where price transparency is low and substitution is difficult. A household moving from a high-cost metro area will likely experience immediate relief on lease obligations but may encounter sticker shock on insurance premiums, medical out-of-pocket costs, and utility bills. The net disposable income outcome depends entirely on the household’s specific service intensity; a young, healthy household with low medical utilization captures the full housing arbitrage, while a family with high healthcare needs may find the real cost advantage evaporates.

What this means for residents of Kit Carson County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 58720.2, a median household income of $59,810 in Kit Carson County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $35,120,552 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
