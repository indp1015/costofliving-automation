📊 Local Market Analysis

Murray County’s cost of living sits modestly below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) indicating an overall index of 96.5 versus 100 nationwide. Goods prices—covering groceries, apparel, and fuel—register at 97.7, only 2.3 points under the national benchmark, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 88.3, a full 11.7 points below the countrywide level. Rental costs follow the same pattern, with a housing index of 92.3, translating to a median monthly rent of $828, which is $100 less than the national median of $928. These figures collectively suggest that everyday expenses in Murray County are generally more affordable, especially for service‑oriented consumption, while goods remain only slightly less expensive than the broader U.S. market. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 88.3 pulls the composite RPP down despite goods prices hovering near the national norm. Daily life therefore leans toward affordability in areas that typically consume a large share of household budgets—healthcare, utilities, and restaurant meals. Rent, while still below the national median, is the second‑largest expense category, representing 14.6% of median household income ($67,880). At $828 per month, rent consumes roughly $9,936 annually, leaving $57,944 of pre‑tax earnings for other expenditures. After accounting for the lower service costs, the median resident retains a purchasing power index of 70,346.3, indicating that a household in Murray County can purchase roughly 30% more goods and services than the average American household with comparable income. This surplus reflects the combined effect of cheaper services and modestly lower housing costs. Demographic and labor‑market characteristics reinforce the affordability narrative. The unemployment rate of 5.0% aligns closely with the national average, suggesting a stable labor supply, while only 8.8% of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. The median age of 39.3 years points to a mature population likely entrenched in established employment patterns. Median household income in Murray County ($67,880) exceeds the national median ($65,047) by 4.4%, yet the historical trend reveals a 91.0% rise in income since 2010, outpacing the 30.2% increase in median rent over the same period. The RPP index itself has moved from 94.574 in 2010 to 96.494 in 2023, a modest 2.0% rise, indicating that price pressures have been relatively tame compared with the near‑doubling of earnings. Consequently, wages have kept pace with, and substantially outstripped, the growth in consumer costs. Prospective residents should base budgeting on the $828 monthly rent figure and allocate a larger share of income to goods, given that grocery and fuel prices are only slightly below national levels. With services costing roughly 12% less than average, utilities and healthcare bills will likely be lower, freeing additional cash for discretionary spending. A prudent monthly budget might earmark $300–$350 for groceries, $150 for transportation fuel, $200 for utilities and health expenses, and $828 for rent, leaving roughly $1,200 for savings, entertainment, and other variable costs. The combination of higher median income, low rent burden, and substantially cheaper services creates a fiscal environment where the median Murray County household can maintain a comfortable standard of living while preserving a meaningful discretionary margin.

What this means for residents of Murray County, Georgia

With a Purchasing Power Index of 70346.3, a median household income of $67,880 in Murray County, Georgia has the equivalent buying power of $47,751,068 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 92.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
