📊 Local Market Analysis

Piatt County’s cost of living sits just below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 98.8 versus the benchmark of 100. Goods prices are modestly higher than the countrywide average, reflected in a 101.6 index, while services are noticeably cheaper at 92.4. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most affordable component, registering an 80.4 index—roughly 20 % below the national level. The median household income of $87,516 outstrips the national median of $65,047 by 34.5 %, positioning residents with a substantial earnings cushion relative to the broader United States. The composition of expenses reveals that goods drive the local price structure. At 101.6, the goods index exceeds both services and housing, indicating that groceries, apparel, and fuel cost slightly more than elsewhere. Nevertheless, the low housing index offsets this pressure; median monthly rent of $950 is only 2 % above the national median rent of $928, yet it represents just 13.0 % of median household income, well under the 30 % rent‑burden threshold commonly cited as affordable. Consequently, day‑to‑day living feels inexpensive in the services sector—healthcare, utilities, and dining are 7.6 % cheaper than the national average—while the modest premium on goods is mitigated by the overall affordability of shelter and the high income base. Purchasing power in Piatt County translates into a robust disposable income margin. The Purchasing Power Index of 88,622 points to a net real‑income advantage of roughly 12 % over the national average. After allocating the median rent of $950, a household retains $86,566 annually for other expenses, equating to $7,213 per month. Subtracting an estimated 30 % of income for utilities, transportation, and food—consistent with the higher goods index—leaves roughly $5,000 of discretionary cash each month, underscoring a strong capacity for savings or non‑essential spending. Local demographics reinforce the economic picture. Unemployment sits at 3.3 %, well below the national rate, indicating a tight labor market that supports wage growth. Only 20.6 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not demand advanced credentials but still command solid pay, as reflected in the high median income. The median age of 42.5 years points to a mature population, likely with established earning power and lower dependency ratios, which further eases the budgetary strain on households. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline, while median household income rose from $66,261 to $87,516, a 32 % increase. Rent climbed from $745 to $950, a 27.5 % jump, yet the rent‑burden metric remained low at 13 % of income. The divergence between modestly falling consumer prices and sharply rising earnings indicates that wages have outpaced expenses, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. Prospective residents should budget with the goods premium in mind, allocating a slightly larger share of income to groceries, fuel, and apparel than the national average. Housing costs remain modest, but the $950 median rent still exceeds the national median, so renters must confirm that the 13 % rent‑to‑income ratio aligns with personal affordability thresholds. Utilities, healthcare, and dining out will be cheaper than elsewhere, providing room in the budget for discretionary spending or debt repayment. A realistic monthly plan would earmark roughly $1,200 for rent, $600 for utilities and transportation, $600 for food and apparel, and reserve the remaining $2,600 for savings, entertainment, and unforeseen expenses, ensuring a comfortable standard of living in Piatt County.
