📊 Local Market Analysis

Pottawattamie County residents navigate a cost environment that sits 11.2 percentage points below the national benchmark, with the 2023 Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity registering 88.8 against a U.S. baseline of 100. The consumption breakdown reveals a distinctive distortion: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run at 96.6, nearly kissing parity, while services collapse to 66.0, the lowest component by a wide margin. Rental costs settle at 87.3, modestly below average. This architecture means daily life feels expensive at the checkout counter but cheap at the meter, the clinic, and the restaurant; a household buying a typical basket of tangible items saves only pennies on the dollar versus the national average, yet captures a 34-percent discount on healthcare, utilities, and dining services. That pricing dynamic translates into tangible purchasing power. The median household income of $71,446 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 9.8 percent, and when adjusted by the local RPP, the purchasing power index rises to $80,485—roughly $15,400 above the nominal median. Rent consumes just 16.3 percent of gross income at a median $970 monthly outlay, compared with the national median rent of $928, leaving substantial discretionary capacity. The labor market reinforces this cushion: unemployment stands at 3.8 percent, the median age of 40.1 suggests a workforce in prime earning years, and the 16.0 percent bachelor's attainment rate, while below coastal hubs, aligns with a regional economy anchored in logistics, healthcare, and manufacturing rather than knowledge-intensive sectors. Over the past decade, the RPP has drifted downward from 91.1 to 88.8, a 2.6 percent decline in relative price levels, while nominal median income surged 37.5 percent from $51,976 to $71,446. Median rent climbed 28.5 percent from $755 to $970, lagging income growth by nearly nine percentage points. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have widened the gap, expanding real purchasing power even as goods prices clung to national levels. The services deflation—likely driven by lower healthcare reimbursement rates and utility cost structures—has acted as a silent subsidy for household budgets. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror Des Moines or Omaha receipts, but expect utility statements, insurance premiums, and restaurant tabs to arrive at a meaningful discount. The rent-to-income ratio of 16.3 percent offers rare breathing room for savings or debt reduction, yet the goods-heavy exposure means inflation spikes in food or energy transmit quickly to the kitchen table. With services so deeply discounted, any policy shift that raises healthcare reimbursement or utility rates would disproportionately erode the county's cost advantage.

What this means for residents of Pottawattamie County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 80485.3, a median household income of $71,446 in Pottawattamie County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $57,503,527 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
