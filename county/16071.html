📊 Local Market Analysis

Oneida County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 92.2, meaning consumer prices are roughly 8 % below the national average of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel, and fuel—registers at 95.9, still modestly under the nation but the highest sub‑index locally. Services, which include healthcare, utilities, and dining, are the cheapest segment at 86.9, while rental housing is dramatically lower at 68.1, indicating that shelter costs are 31.9 % cheaper than the national norm. The composition of the index shows that housing drives the county’s affordability. With rents averaging $1,009 per month, the rent‑burden ratio is only 16.7 % of median household income, well under the conventional 30 % affordability threshold. By contrast, goods prices are only 4.1 % below the national level, suggesting that everyday purchases such as food and clothing are only slightly cheaper. Services are the most discounted, at 13.1 % below the national benchmark, which reduces discretionary spending on health and recreation. Consequently, daily life in Oneida County is inexpensive primarily because housing costs are suppressed; other categories hover near national levels, so residents experience modest savings on most routine expenses but not wholesale cheapness across the board. Median household income in the county stands at $72,563, 11.5 % higher than the national median of $65,047. After deducting the average monthly rent, a median household retains roughly $66,554 annually for all other expenditures, translating to a disposable‑income‑to‑income ratio of about 91.7 %. The Purchasing Power Index of 78,671 reinforces this advantage: residents can purchase roughly 21 % more goods and services than the average American with the same nominal income. The low rent burden amplifies disposable income, allowing households to allocate a larger share of earnings to savings, education, or leisure without compromising basic needs. Demographically, the county’s unemployment rate of 1.5 % signals a tight labor market and abundant job opportunities, which buttress wage growth. However, only 17.8 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill occupations that may limit upward income mobility. The median age of 40.2 years suggests a mature population, likely with established earning power and stable consumption patterns. Together, these factors create a cost‑of‑living environment where low housing costs offset modest educational attainment, and near‑full employment sustains income levels above the national median. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity index slipped from 93.2 to 92.2, a 1 % decline, while median household income surged from $41,338 to $72,563, a 75.5 % increase. Median rent rose from $530 to $1,009, a 90.4 % jump, yet the rent‑burden ratio fell from roughly 15 % to 16.7 % because income grew faster than rent. The modest dip in RPP combined with robust wage growth indicates that wages have outpaced price inflation, enhancing real purchasing power for residents. Prospective residents should budget around $1,009 per month for rent, which consumes only a sixth of median earnings. Allocating roughly 4 % of income to groceries, 2 % to apparel, and 3 % to fuel aligns with the goods index, while utilities, healthcare, and dining together should not exceed 7 % of income, reflecting the low services index. With disposable income exceeding $66,000 annually, households can comfortably cover these categories and retain a sizable margin for savings or discretionary spending. The primary budgeting focus remains housing, but the overall affordability profile is favorable compared with national standards.

What this means for residents of Oneida County, Idaho

With a Purchasing Power Index of 78671.0, a median household income of $72,563 in Oneida County, Idaho has the equivalent buying power of $57,086,038 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 68.1 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
