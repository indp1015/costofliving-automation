📊 Local Market Analysis

Ouachita County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023. This discount is heavily anchored by a services index of 56.7 — the lowest component by a wide margin — which suggests healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses run roughly 43 percent cheaper than the U.S. baseline. Goods prices tell a different story, tracking at 93.1 and narrowing the savings gap for daily necessities like groceries, apparel, and fuel. Rental costs, indexed at 81.9, provide moderate relief but do not offset the near-parity pricing of tangible goods, making the weekly shopping trip the primary friction point in household budgets rather than shelter or service consumption. The median household income of $49,838 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 57,410.4, effectively granting the median earner a 15.2 percent boost in real buying capacity relative to nominal wages. This dynamic is visible in the rent burden metric: at $714 monthly, housing consumes just 17.2 percent of gross income, a stark contrast to the national median rent of $928. However, the low services index — often correlated with limited local healthcare infrastructure and fewer dining options — implies that retained disposable income may face higher time or travel costs to access specialized care. With only 9.3 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree and a median age of 44, the labor market skews toward lower-wage sectors, reflected in a 4.6 percent unemployment rate that sits above the national norm, capping upward wage pressure for the existing workforce. Over the 2010–2023 period, nominal median income surged 59.3 percent against a 30.8 percent rise in median rent, a trajectory that significantly improved the rent-to-income ratio. Simultaneously, the overall RPP drifted downward from 88.6 to 86.8, indicating that local consumer prices grew slower than the national basket. This divergence suggests wages have not merely kept pace but have outstripped the local inflation rate for the basket of goods and services measured, a rare occurrence in many comparable rural economies. The compression of the services index to 56.7 likely played a disproportionate role in this trend, as service-sector inflation nationally has consistently outpaced goods. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that feel nearly metropolitan while enjoying housing costs that remain firmly rural. The 6.9 percent discount on goods versus the national average offers limited runway for savings compared to the 43.3 percent discount on services, meaning daily variable expenses will consume a larger share of the paycheck than the topline RPP suggests. The demographic profile — older, less credentialed, with elevated unemployment — signals a labor market where wage negotiation leverage is thin, making the current purchasing power advantage vulnerable to any uptick in goods inflation or reduction in transfer payments.

What this means for residents of Ouachita County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57410.4, a median household income of $49,838 in Ouachita County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $28,612,195 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
