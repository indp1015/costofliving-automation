📊 Local Market Analysis

Casey County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 10.1 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals distinct pressure points for household budgets. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — represent the highest relative cost component at 94.5, barely discounted from national levels, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining plummet to 62.9, the lowest segment and the primary driver of the county’s overall affordability advantage. Rental costs sit at 80.9, offering meaningful shelter savings compared to the national benchmark, but the near-parity pricing on daily essentials means the discretionary dollar stretches far less at the checkout counter than the aggregate index suggests. The median household income of $41,689 translates into a Purchasing Power Index of $46,377.8, effectively granting residents a 11.2 percent boost in real purchasing capacity relative to their nominal earnings. This adjustment is anchored by a median monthly rent of $645, consuming only 18.6 percent of gross income — a stark contrast to the national median rent of $928 and a rent burden that remains well below the 30 percent stress threshold. However, with the national median income at $65,047, the local wage floor remains 35.9 percent below the U.S. midpoint, meaning the enhanced purchasing power merely narrows, rather than closes, the standard-of-living gap. The unemployment rate of 8.9 percent signals significant labor market slack, while an educational attainment rate of just 6.7 percent for bachelor’s degrees or higher and a median age of 41.5 years suggest an economy reliant on lower-wage sectors with limited upward mobility for the existing workforce. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP drifted marginally from 90.433 to 89.89, a negligible 0.6 percent decline indicating stable regional price levels. Nominal income growth of 28.9 percent, rising from $32,341, outpaced the modest inflation differential, yet median rent accelerated 34.7 percent from $479, eroding a portion of the real wage gains. This divergence highlights a tightening shelter market even in a low-cost environment, where housing cost inflation exceeded income growth by nearly six percentage points. The services deflation — or lack of price growth in healthcare and utilities relative to the nation — absorbed much of the rental pressure, keeping the overall index flat, but households allocating a larger share of spending to market-rate rent felt the squeeze acutely. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror national averages, negating the perceived discount on the headline index, while capturing significant savings on healthcare premiums, utility bills, and restaurant meals. The low rent burden provides a buffer for fixed costs, but the elevated unemployment rate and low educational attainment correlate with income volatility that complicates long-term financial planning. Households moving from higher-wage metros will experience an immediate reduction in nominal cash flow that the Purchasing Power Index only partially offsets; the budget reality hinges on whether consumption patterns lean heavily toward goods — where no discount exists — or services — where the 37 percent discount dominates.

What this means for residents of Casey County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 46377.8, a median household income of $41,689 in Casey County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $19,334,441 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
