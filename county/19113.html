📊 Local Market Analysis

Linn County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 88.8, meaning the average consumer price level is roughly 11 % below the national benchmark of 100. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 96.6, still modestly under the national average but far higher than the county’s service‑sector index of 66.0, which reflects markedly cheaper healthcare, utilities and dining. Rental costs, measured by the BEA RPP housing sub‑index, register at 87.3, indicating that monthly rents are about 13 % cheaper than the countrywide norm. The median rent of $915 compares favorably with the national median of $928, reinforcing the view that housing expenses are relatively affordable in the county. The consumption profile is driven primarily by the goods sector, which accounts for the highest share of price pressure. Although groceries, apparel and fuel are priced only 3.4 % below national levels, they remain the dominant cost driver because services are priced at a deep discount of 34 % relative to the nation. Daily life therefore feels inexpensive in categories that dominate household budgets—energy bills, medical visits and restaurant meals are substantially cheaper, while the modest premium on goods is offset by low housing costs. For a household earning the local median income of $76,421, the rent burden sits at 14.4 % of earnings, well under the 30 % threshold that typically signals affordability stress. After deducting the median monthly rent of $915, a median earner retains roughly $71,500 of annual disposable income, a figure that translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 86,089.7. This index, expressed in dollars, shows that a Linn County resident can purchase about $10,000 more in goods and services than a median American with the same nominal income, owing to the county’s lower overall price level. Demographic and labor‑market conditions reinforce the purchasing‑power advantage. Unemployment stands at 3.7 %, comfortably below the national rate, suggesting a stable job environment that supports income growth. Yet only 23.5 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, indicating a workforce skewed toward occupations that may not command premium wages, which helps keep wage inflation modest. The median age of 38.5 years points to a mature, working‑age population likely to be in the peak earning phase, further cushioning households against cost pressures. Over the past decade, the Real Personal Consumption Expenditure index fell from 91.139 to 88.769, a 2.6 % decline, while median household income rose 28.8 % from $59,322 to $76,421. Rental prices increased 31.7 % from $695 to $915, roughly matching income growth and preserving the low rent‑burden ratio. The modest dip in the RPP index combined with robust income gains indicates that wages have outpaced consumer price growth, enhancing real purchasing power. Prospective residents should anchor their budgets around the relatively low service and housing costs while allowing for a slightly higher allocation to goods. A realistic monthly budget might allocate 30 % of income to housing, 20 % to transportation and groceries, and the remaining 50 % to utilities, healthcare, and discretionary spending, reflecting the county’s service‑price advantage. Because rent consumes only a small slice of earnings, households can afford a broader range of consumption choices without sacrificing savings potential. Monitoring the goods price index remains prudent, as any upward shift would directly affect grocery and fuel expenditures, the two largest non‑housing line items for most families.

What this means for residents of Linn County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 86089.7, a median household income of $76,421 in Linn County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $65,790,610 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
