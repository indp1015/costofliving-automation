📊 Local Market Analysis

Greene County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 88.8 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the closest to the national level at 96.6, while services are markedly cheaper at 66.0, and rental costs trail modestly at 87.3. The disparity signals that everyday purchases such as groceries, apparel, and fuel cost roughly 3.4 % less than the countrywide average, whereas healthcare, utilities, and dining out are nearly one‑third cheaper, providing a clear cushion for household budgets. The dominant driver of the local price structure is the services sector, whose index of 66.0 pulls the overall cost profile down. Even though goods are only slightly cheaper than the national benchmark, the low cost of services offsets higher relative prices in other categories, making daily life in Greene County relatively inexpensive. For a family that spends a typical 30 % of its budget on food, transportation, and clothing, the 3‑4 % savings on those items is modest; however, the 34 % discount on services translates into substantial reductions in health‑care premiums, utility bills, and restaurant meals. Rental costs, at 87.3, are also below average but not as dramatically low as services, meaning housing remains affordable yet not the primary source of savings. Disposable income for the median household can be gauged by the Purchasing Power Index of 66,643.8. With a median annual income of $59,159 and median monthly rent of $774 (15.7 % of income), a typical family retains roughly $48,500 after rent, equating to an effective after‑housing income that is about 75 % of the national median after‑housing figure. The low rent burden—well under the 30 % threshold commonly used to flag housing stress—allows a larger share of earnings to be allocated to discretionary spending, especially given the cheap services. Consequently, the median resident enjoys a purchasing power that, while below the national median income, is amplified by the county’s low cost environment, yielding a real‑terms standard of living that rivals or exceeds many higher‑priced regions. Demographically, Greene County’s labor market is characterized by a 3.2 % unemployment rate, well under the national average, and a modest educational attainment level, with only 14.7 % holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 44.8 years suggests a mature workforce, likely anchored in stable, lower‑skill occupations that align with the county’s lower price structure. The combination of low unemployment and an older, less‑educated population tends to suppress wage growth, yet the inexpensive services and housing mitigate the impact on household budgets, allowing residents to maintain a comfortable consumption pattern despite modest earnings. Over the past decade, the RPP fell from 91.139 in 2010 to 88.769 in 2023, a 2.6 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. In the same period, median household income rose from $46,898 to $59,159, a 26.1 % increase, while median rent climbed from $588 to $774, a 31.6 % rise. Income growth outpaced the modest decline in overall price levels, but rent inflation exceeded income gains, slightly eroding the housing affordability advantage. Nevertheless, because rent still consumes less than one‑sixth of earnings, the net effect remains favorable for disposable income. Prospective residents should anchor their budgets around three core figures: a $774 monthly rent, a 15.7 % rent‑to‑income ratio, and a services cost base that is roughly two‑thirds of the national level. Allocating about 30 % of after‑tax income to housing leaves roughly 70 % for groceries, transportation, and discretionary spending, with the latter benefitting from the 34 % service discount. A realistic monthly budget might therefore allocate $1,200 to rent and utilities, $600 to groceries and fuel, $400 to healthcare and personal services, and $300 to entertainment and dining, leaving a modest surplus for savings. Understanding these cost dynamics equips newcomers with a clear framework for managing household expenses in Greene County’s affordable yet nuanced economic landscape.

What this means for residents of Greene County, Iowa

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
