📊 Local Market Analysis

Pendleton County’s cost of living sits well below the national average, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 89.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices are the most elevated component at 94.5, still under the national benchmark, while services are markedly cheaper at 62.9. Housing, measured by rental costs, registers at 80.9, indicating that rent is roughly 19 % lower than the national level. The median monthly rent of $800 compares favorably to the national median of $928, reinforcing the affordability of shelter in the county. The consumption profile is driven primarily by goods, which account for the highest index reading. However, the low services index offsets the modestly higher goods cost, producing a mixed picture of daily expenses. Grocery bills, apparel, and fuel are only about 5 % above the national norm, whereas healthcare, utilities, and dining out are roughly 37 % cheaper. For a typical household, the net effect is a relatively inexpensive everyday life, especially in categories that dominate routine budgets such as utilities and medical care. Rental costs, while not the cheapest component, remain well under national levels, further cushioning overall expenditures. Disposable income analysis shows that the median household earns $66,601, slightly above the national median of $65,047. After accounting for the average rent of $800, rent consumes 14.4 % of household income, far lower than the often‑cited 30 % affordability threshold. The Purchasing Power Index of 74,091.7 translates to a real‑term purchasing power that exceeds the national average, reflecting that residents can stretch their earnings further on goods and services. In practical terms, a median earner retains roughly $58,800 after rent, which, when adjusted for the lower services index, affords more discretionary spending than a comparable household in a higher‑cost region. Demographically, the county’s labor market features a 4.9 % unemployment rate, modestly above the national average but still indicative of a relatively stable job environment. Only 11.1 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in sectors that may not command premium wages. The median age of 41.7 years points to a mature population, likely with established consumption patterns and lower propensity for high‑cost lifestyle choices. These factors combine to keep demand for premium services muted, reinforcing the low services index. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 90.433 to 89.89, a 0.6 % decline, indicating a slight easing of price pressures. Median household income rose from $48,353 to $66,601, a 37.7 % increase, while median rent climbed from $583 to $800, a 37.2 % rise. Income growth outpaced the modest decline in overall price levels, suggesting that wages have kept pace with, and even outstripped, the incremental cost of living. The near‑parallel rise in rent and income preserves affordability, as rent remains a small share of earnings. Prospective residents should allocate a larger share of their budget to goods—particularly groceries and fuel—while expecting modest expenditures on healthcare and utilities. With rent representing less than one‑sixth of median income, housing costs are unlikely to dominate the budget. A prudent monthly plan might earmark 30–35 % of take‑home pay for goods, 15–20 % for services, and maintain the 14 % rent share, leaving a healthy margin for savings or discretionary spending. Understanding these ratios equips newcomers to navigate Pendleton County’s inexpensive yet balanced cost structure without overcommitting to any single expense category.

What this means for residents of Pendleton County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 74091.7, a median household income of $66,601 in Pendleton County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $49,345,813 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
