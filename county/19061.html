📊 Local Market Analysis

Dubuque County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 for 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distinct bifurcation in daily expenses. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run only 3.4 percent cheaper than the U.S. baseline at 96.6, meaning a trip to the supermarket or the gas station delivers minimal savings relative to coastal metros. By contrast, the services category, which captures healthcare, utilities, and dining out, plunges to 66.0, a staggering 34 percent discount that acts as the primary engine suppressing the headline index. Rental costs, indexed at 87.3, occupy the middle ground, roughly 12.7 percent below national norms, but the services anomaly suggests that locally delivered labor — from medical visits to haircuts — is where the household budget finds its most significant relief. This consumption breakdown dictates that daily life is structurally inexpensive for service-intensive households but offers little arbitrage for goods-heavy consumption. The median household income of $75,919 exceeds the national median of $65,047 by 16.7 percent, while median rent of $944 barely outpaces the national $928, resulting in a remarkably low rent burden of just 14.9 percent of gross income. When the BEA’s price level adjustments are applied to nominal earnings, the purchasing power index rises to $85,524, effectively granting the median earner an additional $9,600 in real buying capacity compared to their nominal paycheck. That surplus represents discretionary bandwidth for savings, debt reduction, or discretionary spending that is simply unavailable in higher-parity regions, though the modest 3.4 percent discount on goods tempers the magnitude of that advantage at the checkout counter. Demographic and labor indicators reinforce the stability of this cost environment. An unemployment rate of 3.8 percent signals a tight labor market that supports wage growth without triggering the overheating seen in boomtowns, while a median age of 38.8 years and a bachelor’s attainment rate of 21.7 percent suggest a workforce anchored in established, mid-career roles rather than transient student populations or high-turnover service sectors. Over the 2010–2023 window, the RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8, even as nominal median income surged 39 percent against a 35.6 percent rise in median rent. Real income growth has therefore outpaced shelter inflation, widening the gap between earnings and housing costs, while the persistent services discount has likely cushioned healthcare and utility escalation that has plagued other Midwestern counties. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. Housing consumes a negligible share of income, freeing cash flow for transportation, childcare, or retirement contributions, but the near-parity pricing on groceries and fuel means those line items will not shrink to match the lower rent check. Healthcare and utilities, indexed at roughly two-thirds the national level, represent a structural windfall — a family allocating $1,200 monthly to these categories nationally might expect to spend closer to $800 locally. However, the goods index warns that stocking a pantry or commuting long distances erodes the purchasing power premium quickly. The data portrays a locale where the cost of *living* — shelter and services — is genuinely affordable, but the cost of *consuming* — goods and mobility — remains stubbornly national.

What this means for residents of Dubuque County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 85524.2, a median household income of $75,919 in Dubuque County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $64,929,117 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
