📊 Local Market Analysis

Northwest Arctic Borough’s cost of living sits modestly above the national baseline, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 103.3 versus the United States average of 100. Goods prices—covering groceries, apparel, and fuel—track slightly higher at 103.7, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are priced 3.3 % below the national level at 96.7. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the most pronounced outlier: the housing index reaches 113.3, indicating that renters pay roughly 13 % more than the average American household. Median monthly rent of $1,336 exceeds the national median of $928 by $408, reinforcing the housing premium. The rental premium dominates the local cost structure. Although everyday goods are only marginally more expensive and services are actually cheaper, the 113.3 housing index drives the overall RPP upward. For a household earning the local median income of $81,298, rent consumes 19.7 % of earnings, well under the conventional 30 % affordability threshold, yet the absolute dollar outlay remains high. After accounting for rent, the median resident retains $71,962 annually, or roughly $5,997 per month, to cover utilities, food, transportation, and discretionary spending. The Purchasing Power Index of 78,703.2 quantifies this residual buying capacity: residents can purchase about 21 % fewer goods and services than a household with national median income and median rent, reflecting the drag of elevated housing costs despite higher earnings. Demographic and labor‑market characteristics amplify the lived experience of these numbers. The borough’s unemployment rate stands at 16.3 %, more than double the national average, limiting the pool of earners who can benefit from the $81,298 median income. Only 8.4 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, often seasonal occupations that may not translate into the median figure. A median age of 28.3 years points to a youthful population, likely with limited savings and higher reliance on rental housing. The combination of high unemployment and low educational attainment constrains upward mobility, meaning many households may fall below the median income and face a rent burden that exceeds the 19.7 % figure reported for the median earner. Over the past decade, RPP rose from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023, a modest 0.2 % increase, indicating relative price stability. In contrast, median household income surged 27.7 % from $63,648 to $81,298, while median rent climbed 17.1 % from $1,141 to $1,336. Income growth outpaced both overall price changes and rent inflation, improving disposable income for those who remain employed. However, the high unemployment rate tempers this benefit, as a sizable share of the population does not share in the income gains. Prospective residents should budget rent as the primary expense, allocating roughly $1,336 per month and anticipating that this will represent close to one‑fifth of total earnings for a median‑income household. Utilities and other services are comparatively affordable, but food and fuel costs remain slightly above national levels, requiring careful grocery planning. With a disposable income of about $6,000 per month after rent, households must prioritize essential categories—food, transportation, and healthcare—while recognizing that any deviation from the median income will quickly erode the margin for discretionary spending.

What this means for residents of Northwest Arctic Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 78703.2, a median household income of $81,298 in Northwest Arctic Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $63,984,128 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
