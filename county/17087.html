📊 Local Market Analysis

The Johnson County market remains modestly below the national cost of living benchmark, with an overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) of 98.8 against the 100 baseline, indicating that consumer prices are slightly cheaper than the average American household. Goods, however, sit above the national average at 101.6, while services and housing are notably cheaper at 92.4 and 80.4 respectively, creating a cost structure where everyday items are a bit pricier but shelter is a clear discount. This divergence suggests that while groceries, apparel, and fuel may strain budgets marginally, rent remains a relatively affordable component of the monthly budget. The consumption breakdown is driven primarily by the Goods category, which sits 1.6 points above the national index and represents the highest cost component locally. Services, at 92.4, are the most economical, and Rental Costs at 80.4 are the lowest, meaning that a typical household can allocate a smaller share of income to housing than in most other regions. The relatively low rent burden of 11.7%—derived from a median monthly rent of $634 against a median household income of $65,203—underscores how housing affordability cushions overall expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 66,027.0 further reflects the capacity of median earners to command goods and services, though the index does not directly translate to disposable income without accounting for taxes and other necessities. Local demographics shape this cost environment in nuanced ways. An unemployment rate of just 2.4% signals a tight labor market, which can support wage stability but also limits labor supply in lower‑skill sectors. The educational profile—only 12.2% of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—suggests a workforce that may be more concentrated in trades and service roles, potentially influencing the modest wage growth observed over the past decade. The median age of 42.7 years indicates an older population that may prioritize healthcare and utility services, yet the services index remains below the national average, reflecting either efficient delivery or lower demand intensity. Trend analysis reveals that while consumer costs have edged down by 3.0% since 2010 (RPP 101.778 to 98.752), median household income has surged 54.2% from $42,287 to $65,203, dramatically outpacing rent growth of just 1.6% over the same period. This divergence demonstrates that wages are increasingly outpacing housing expenses, though the overall cost of living has remained relatively flat. The modest decline in the RPP suggests that inflation has been contained, allowing real purchasing power to expand even as goods prices hover slightly above the national norm. For a prospective resident, budgeting should emphasize the modest rent outlay—approximately $7,596 annually, or 11.7% of median income—while allocating a slightly higher proportion for groceries and discretionary items. Services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are comparatively inexpensive, offering flexibility for discretionary spending. The low unemployment rate and stable rent environment provide a predictable baseline, but the higher Goods index means that routine shopping will cost a bit more than the national average. Overall, Johnson County offers a cost structure where housing is a clear advantage, goods are marginally pricier, and services remain affordable, creating a balanced but slightly goods‑heavy budget profile for households.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Johnson County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 66027.0, a median household income of $65,203 in Johnson County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $43,051,585 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
