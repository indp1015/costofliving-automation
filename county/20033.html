📊 Local Market Analysis

Comanche County registers an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9, positioning local consumer prices roughly ten percent below the national baseline, yet the aggregate figure masks severe internal divergence across spending categories. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — carry an index of 96.5, nearly matching the U.S. average and representing the highest cost tier in the county, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining sit at a strikingly low 68.6, the lowest component and a full 31.4 points beneath the national norm. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 90.8, suggesting that while shelter is modestly discounted, the essential daily outlays for food and transportation offer residents little relief compared to metropolitan counterparts. This bifurcated cost structure dictates the texture of daily household budgeting: a median monthly rent of $672 consumes only 16.3 percent of the $49,417 median household income, a rent-burden ratio that ranks among the most manageable in the region, yet the near-parity pricing on goods forces families to allocate a disproportionate share of disposable income to the grocery aisle and the gas pump. The Purchasing Power Index of 54,964 quantifies this dynamic, indicating that the median earner commands roughly $15,500 more in real purchasing capacity than the nominal income suggests, a premium driven almost entirely by the deep discount on services rather than broad-based affordability. However, that advantage erodes quickly for households with above-average medical needs or high transportation mileage, where the 96.5 goods index exerts full weight. Demographic and labor market fundamentals reinforce the current pricing regime. An unemployment rate of 2.4 percent signals a tight labor market that typically exerts upward pressure on wages, yet the median income lags the national median of $65,047 by 24 percent, reflecting an economy likely concentrated in lower-wage sectors consistent with the 15.0 percent bachelor’s degree attainment rate. The median age of 50.8 years — significantly above the national figure — implies a population skewing toward fixed-income retirees and established workers, a cohort that benefits acutely from the 68.6 services index on healthcare and utilities but possesses less elasticity to absorb goods inflation. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined 3.3 points from 92.99 to 89.91, even as median rent surged 31.5 percent from $511 to $672, outpacing the 9.1 percent rise in median income from $45,288 to $49,417. The widening gap between shelter cost growth and income growth has been offset by the persistent deflation in the services index, which has kept the overall RPP trending downward. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is precise: expect near-national prices at the checkout counter and the pump, anticipate significant savings on insurance premiums, utility bills, and restaurant meals, and recognize that housing costs, while low in absolute terms, have accelerated at more than three times the rate of earnings over the last decade.

What this means for residents of Comanche County, Kansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 54964.0, a median household income of $49,417 in Comanche County, Kansas has the equivalent buying power of $27,161,560 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 90.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
