📊 Local Market Analysis

Franklin County’s cost of living sits modestly above the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parity index at 103.6 versus a baseline of 100. Goods such as groceries, apparel and fuel are priced slightly below the country average at 98.2, while services—including healthcare, utilities and dining—are markedly higher at 123.2. Rental costs are the only category below the national level, recorded at 93.7, which translates to a median monthly rent of $1,044—still 12.5 % above the national median rent of $928 because the index reflects a broader basket of housing‑related expenses. The service sector drives the county’s price structure. A 23.2 % premium on services outweighs the 1.8 % discount on goods and the 6.3 % discount on housing, indicating that everyday expenditures for medical care, electricity, water and restaurant meals are the primary source of higher living costs. For a typical household, the cheapness of goods is largely offset by the steep service surcharge, making daily life feel expensive despite affordable rent. The rent‑burden figure of 20 % of income confirms that housing consumes a relatively modest slice of earnings, leaving a larger proportion of the budget exposed to elevated service prices. Disposable income can be gauged through the Purchasing Power Index of 60,533, which measures the amount of goods and services a median household can afford relative to the national benchmark of 100,000. With a median household income of $62,734—4.2 % below the national median of $65,047—the county’s residents retain roughly 60 % of the purchasing power of an average American household. After paying $1,044 in rent, a median earner has $51,690 left for all other expenses, a figure that must stretch to cover the 23 % service premium. Consequently, while housing costs are manageable, the net disposable income is constrained by the high cost of essential services. Demographically, the county’s labor market is shaped by an older median age of 49.2 years and a relatively low educational attainment—only 14 % hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The unemployment rate of 6.9 % exceeds the national average, suggesting a labor pool with limited high‑skill, high‑wage opportunities. Older populations typically have higher healthcare utilization, which dovetails with the elevated service index, while lower educational levels can suppress wage growth, reinforcing the modest purchasing power observed. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 100.392 in 2010 to 103.636 in 2023, a 3.2 % increase, whereas median household income surged from $40,401 to $62,734, a 55.3 % gain. Rent climbed from $711 to $1,044, a 46.8 % rise. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and housing costs, yet the service component’s steep ascent has eroded a portion of that gain, leaving residents with a net purchasing power still below the national average. Prospective residents should budget aggressively for service‑related outlays. Assuming the median rent of $1,044, a realistic monthly allocation might be $300–$350 for utilities, $200–$250 for healthcare, and $250–$300 for dining and personal services, leaving roughly $1,200–$1,300 for groceries, transportation and discretionary spending. The 20 % rent‑burden leaves about 80 % of income for all other categories, but the high service index means that even modest spending in those areas can quickly erode discretionary cash. Careful tracking of utility bills, medical expenses and dining habits is essential to avoid overspending in a market where services dominate the cost structure.

What this means for residents of Franklin County, Florida

With a Purchasing Power Index of 60533.0, a median household income of $62,734 in Franklin County, Florida has the equivalent buying power of $37,974,772 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 93.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
