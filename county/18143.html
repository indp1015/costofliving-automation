📊 Local Market Analysis

Scott County’s cost of living sits comfortably below the national benchmark, with the BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) index at 92.1 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—groceries, apparel, and fuel—are 4.4 % lower than the national average (95.6), while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 71.3, a 28.7 % discount. Housing costs, measured by median monthly rent of $790, are 14.9 % below the national median rent of $928 and translate to an RPP housing component of 84.7. The composite picture is one of a region where everyday expenses are modest, driven chiefly by low service costs and relatively affordable rentals. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 71.3 pulls the overall RPP down far more than the modestly lower goods index of 95.6. Daily life in Scott County is therefore inexpensive in terms of discretionary spending on dining out, medical visits, and utility bills, while essential goods remain only slightly cheaper than elsewhere. Rent burden—defined as the share of household income devoted to rent—is 17.4 % of the median household income of $54,583, well under the 30 % threshold commonly cited as a affordability stress point. This suggests that a typical household retains roughly $45,000 of pre‑tax earnings for all other expenditures, reinforcing the notion that routine household budgets stretch further here than in many parts of the country. Purchasing power, quantified by the index value of 59,267, indicates that the median resident can buy roughly 59 % of the goods and services that a national median earner can afford with the same nominal income. After accounting for the $790 monthly rent, the median worker still has about $4,400 per month left for food, transportation, health, and leisure—a figure that aligns with the low service price index and reinforces the region’s relative affordability. The modest 7.5 % rise in median rent since 2010 contrasts sharply with the 22.8 % increase in median household income over the same period, expanding disposable income and enhancing real purchasing power. Demographically, the county’s labor market is characterized by a 3.4 % unemployment rate, well below the national average, and a relatively young median age of 41.2 years. However, only 7.4 % of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a workforce concentrated in lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations. This educational profile helps explain why service costs are suppressed—local providers operate with lower overhead and wage structures. The low unemployment rate indicates stable employment, yet limited higher‑education attainment may constrain upward wage mobility, keeping overall income growth modest relative to more educated metros. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 93.0 to 92.1, a 1.0 % decline, while median household income climbed from $44,442 to $54,583, a 22.8 % gain. Rental rates rose 7.5 % from $735 to $790. The divergence between modestly rising housing costs and substantially higher income growth means that real wages have outpaced inflationary pressures, bolstering household budgets. The slight dip in the RPP underscores that price growth has been slower than national trends, further enhancing the county’s purchasing advantage. Prospective residents should allocate roughly $790 per month for rent and anticipate that utilities, healthcare, and dining will cost about 30 % less than the national average. With a median income of $54,583, a realistic monthly budget might reserve $1,200 for groceries and fuel, $600 for transportation, $500 for healthcare and insurance, and $400 for discretionary spending, leaving a modest cushion for savings or emergencies. The low rent burden and suppressed service prices combine to make Scott County a financially forgiving environment for households seeking to stretch each dollar without sacrificing basic comfort.

What this means for residents of Scott County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 59266.9, a median household income of $54,583 in Scott County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $32,349,652 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
