📊 Local Market Analysis

Desha County residents navigate a cost structure that sits 13.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023. This aggregate figure masks a sharp bifurcation in daily expenses: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel cost 93.1 percent of the national benchmark, while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—plummet to just 56.7 percent. Rental costs occupy a middle ground at 81.9 percent. The goods category acts as the primary anchor preventing deeper deflation in the local cost profile; households feel national price pressures at the pump and checkout counter almost as acutely as metropolitan consumers, even as service-intensive spending delivers substantial discounts. Purchasing power metrics reveal the practical impact of this disparity. The median household income of $36,064 translates to a purchasing power index of $41,543.60, effectively granting residents a 15.2 percent boost in real consumption capacity relative to their nominal earnings. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 22.9 percent of gross income, with the median monthly outlay of $689 sitting well below the national median of $928. This dynamic suggests that for the median earner, the low service-cost environment—particularly utilities and healthcare—frees discretionary income that would otherwise be absorbed by fixed overhead in higher-cost regions, though the elevated goods index tempers the total gain at the grocery store. Demographic and labor market fundamentals frame the sustainability of this advantage. An unemployment rate of 4.3 percent signals a tight labor market, yet educational attainment remains low at 7.6 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, and the median age of 41.2 years indicates an established, potentially aging workforce. This composition suggests the local economy relies on sectors that do not command high wage premiums, capping income growth even as the cost of living remains suppressed. The labor market appears sufficient to maintain current occupancy and service demand but lacks the high-skill density that typically drives structural wage acceleration. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP declined 2.0 percent from 88.56 to 86.81, indicating that local consumer prices fell relative to the national basket. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 32.6 percent against a 28.3 percent rise in median rent, widening the gap between earnings and housing costs. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have outstripped the primary shelter expense and the broader cost environment. A prospective resident should budget for near-national prices on tangible goods—food and fuel will not feel cheap—while anticipating significant savings on rent, utilities, and healthcare services relative to almost any urban benchmark.

What this means for residents of Desha County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 41543.6, a median household income of $36,064 in Desha County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $14,982,284 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
