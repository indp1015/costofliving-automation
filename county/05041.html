📊 Local Market Analysis

Desha County presents a cost of living profile that sits 13.2 percent below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 86.8 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a stark bifurcation in consumer expenditure categories. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel—command an index of 93.1, merely 6.9 percent below the U.S. average, while Services, covering healthcare, utilities, and dining, plummet to 56.7, a staggering 43.3 percent discount to the national norm. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 81.9. This structure indicates that daily life is fundamentally inexpensive for service-intensive consumption—medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals—but residents realize minimal savings on the tangible goods required for daily survival, creating a consumption environment where discretionary service spending stretches further than the weekly grocery run or fuel fill-up. The median household income of $36,064 trails the national median of $65,047 by 44.6 percent, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 41,543.6 reveals that local wages stretch 15.2 percent further than their nominal value suggests once the depressed price level is applied. Rent burden remains remarkably low at 22.9 percent of gross income, with the median monthly rent of $689 sitting 25.8 percent below the national $928 benchmark. This dynamic suggests that while nominal earnings are low, the cost floor—particularly for shelter and services—allows the median worker to retain a disproportionately large share of disposable income relative to higher-wage, high-cost metros, provided their consumption basket leans heavily on locally priced services rather than nationally priced goods. Demographic and labor market characteristics reinforce this low-cost, low-wage equilibrium. An unemployment rate of 4.3 percent sits near structural norms, but educational attainment is notably depressed, with only 7.6 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, limiting upward pressure on wages. The median age of 41.2 years indicates a mature workforce with limited influx of younger, higher-earning professionals, which helps suppress service-sector pricing—particularly for labor-intensive categories like personal care and dining—but also constrains income growth. The labor market appears segmented: sufficient employment exists to maintain low joblessness, yet the skill composition anchors the wage distribution well below the national median, keeping a lid on service inflation while offering little relief for goods priced in national markets. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined modestly from 88.56 to 86.81, a 2.0 percent drop signaling that local prices fell slightly relative to the national basket. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 32.6 percent from $27,197 to $36,064, outpacing the 28.3 percent rise in median rent from $537 to $689. This divergence confirms that real purchasing power improved meaningfully over the decade; wage growth exceeded both shelter cost inflation and the broader cost-of-living trajectory. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: housing and services offer deep value, but the goods component—groceries, fuel, clothing—will consume a larger share of the budget than the headline RPP suggests, requiring careful allocation toward nationally priced necessities.

What this means for residents of Desha County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 41543.6, a median household income of $36,064 in Desha County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $14,982,284 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
