📊 Local Market Analysis

Campbell County residents operate within a cost environment that sits 10.1 percent below the national benchmark, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 in 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distorted consumer landscape where daily essentials remain stubbornly expensive while service costs collapse. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel — carry an index of 94.5, only a modest 5.5 percent discount to the U.S. average, making routine supermarket trips and vehicle operation the primary friction points for household budgets. Conversely, the services category, covering healthcare, utilities, and dining, plunges to 62.9, a staggering 37.1 percent below national levels, while rental costs sit at 80.9. This bifurcation means the median household income of $77,271 — already 18.8 percent above the national median of $65,047 — stretches significantly further for fixed obligations like rent, which consumes just 16.8 percent of gross income at a median $1,079 monthly outlay compared to the national $928, but offers less relief at the checkout counter. The purchasing power index of 85,961.7 quantifies this advantage, indicating that after adjusting for local price levels, the typical household commands real resources roughly 11.3 percent above their nominal earnings, a buffer largely engineered by the deep discount on services and housing rather than wage dominance alone. Local labor dynamics reinforce this stability; a 3.7 percent unemployment rate signals a tight market that supports income growth, while a median age of 39.0 and a 24.3 percent bachelor’s attainment rate suggest a workforce skewed toward prime earning years with moderate educational specialization. These demographics sustain the income trajectory without the extreme cost pressures seen in higher-density tech hubs, keeping the rent-to-income ratio exceptionally low by national standards and insulating families from the shelter-cost spirals affecting larger metros. Over the 2010 to 2023 window, the overall RPP barely budged, slipping a marginal 0.6 percent from 90.4 to 89.9, masking significant underlying current. Nominal median income surged 41.5 percent from $54,621 to $77,271, yet median rent accelerated slightly faster at 44.3 percent, climbing from $748 to $1,079. While the rent burden remains manageable at 16.8 percent, the velocity of rent appreciation relative to the nearly flat overall price index suggests shelter inflation has been the primary driver of local cost growth, even as goods and services indices likely diverged in opposite directions to net out the minimal aggregate change. Wages have broadly kept pace, but the narrowing gap between income growth and rent growth warrants monitoring for future budget pressure. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus centers on the asymmetry between fixed and variable costs. The housing discount is real and immediate — a median rent nearly 20 percent below the national RPP-adjusted expectation frees cash flow for savings or discretionary spending. However, the goods index at 94.5 implies grocery bills, gasoline, and clothing purchases will feel nearly identical to higher-cost regions, eroding the theoretical advantage of the low overall RPP. Healthcare and utility savings, embedded in the 62.9 services index, provide a structural tailwind for long-term financial health, particularly for older households, but younger demographics with lower medical utilization capture less immediate benefit. The household ledger here favors those who can lock in housing costs and absorb near-national prices for daily consumables.

What this means for residents of Campbell County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 85961.7, a median household income of $77,271 in Campbell County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $66,423,465 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
