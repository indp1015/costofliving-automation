📊 Local Market Analysis

Winnebago County’s cost of living sits just below the national benchmark, with the overall Relative Price Parity (RPP) index at 98.8 against a national value of 100. Goods prices are the only category above the national average, registering at 101.6, while services are 7.6 points cheaper (92.4) and housing—measured by median rental costs—is 19.6 points lower (80.4). The county’s median monthly rent of $986 exceeds the national median of $928, yet the rent‑burden metric shows residents allocate only 18.4 % of household income to housing, well under the 30 % threshold that defines affordability stress. The consumption profile is driven primarily by the goods sector. At 101.6, groceries, apparel, and fuel cost slightly more than elsewhere, offsetting the savings realized in services and housing. Daily life therefore feels mixed: essential consumables such as food and gasoline impose a modest premium, while utilities, healthcare, and dining out are noticeably cheaper. The low housing index means that, despite a higher absolute rent figure, the relative cost of shelter is subdued because household incomes are also higher than the national median. Consequently, residents experience a net‑positive affordability environment, especially for discretionary spending that leans on cheaper services. Disposable income calculations reinforce this view. The median household income of $64,363 translates to roughly $5,363 per month before taxes. Subtracting the median rent of $986 leaves $4,377 for all other expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 65,176.4—derived from the ratio of local income to the national cost‑of‑living index—indicates that a Winnebago household can purchase about 65 % of the goods and services that a typical American household can buy with the same nominal income. In practical terms, after covering rent, a median earner retains enough earnings to comfortably cover the cheaper services and benefit from the modestly higher goods prices, preserving a solid discretionary margin. Demographic factors shape this affordability picture. With 15.6 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, the county lags the national education attainment rate, limiting the pool of high‑skill, high‑wage jobs. The unemployment rate of 7.2 %—well above the national average—suggests labor market slack, which can suppress wage growth and increase reliance on lower‑paid occupations. The median age of 39.7 years aligns closely with national figures, indicating a balanced mix of working‑age residents and retirees, the latter of whom typically have lower consumption needs, further dampening demand for higher‑priced goods. Over the past decade, the RPP slipped from 101.78 in 2010 to 98.75 in 2023, a 3 % decline, reflecting a gradual easing of price pressures. Median household income rose from $48,225 to $64,363, a 33.5 % increase, while median rent climbed from $748 to $986, a 31.8 % rise. Income growth outpaced rent inflation by roughly 1.7 % annually, and the broader decline in the cost‑of‑living index means wages have kept modestly ahead of expenses, preserving purchasing power for most households. Prospective residents should budget around $1,000 per month for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 for utilities and healthcare (reflecting the sub‑national services index), and expect grocery and fuel costs to be 2–3 % higher than the national average. With an 18.4 % rent burden, a median earner retains over 80 % of income for all other categories, allowing for a comfortable margin for transportation, modest entertainment, and savings. Understanding these component costs and the local labor dynamics equips newcomers to plan a realistic household budget that leverages the county’s relatively low housing and service expenses while accounting for the slight premium on everyday goods.

What this means for residents of Winnebago County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 65176.4, a median household income of $64,363 in Winnebago County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $41,949,486 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
