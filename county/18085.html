📊 Local Market Analysis

Kosciusko County’s cost of living remains notably below the national benchmark, with an overall index of 92.1 in 2023, indicating that consumer prices are roughly 8 percent cheaper than the average American market. Goods—encompassing groceries, apparel and fuel—register at 95.6, just slightly under the national 100, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly inexpensive at 71.3, more than 28 percent below the national average. Rental costs sit at 84.7, also below the national level, confirming that housing expenses contribute less to the local price structure than everyday goods. The consumption breakdown shows that goods drive the local cost profile, accounting for the highest index among the three categories. Services, by contrast, are the cheapest component, making routine household expenses such as utilities and medical care relatively affordable. Daily life in Kosciusko County is therefore inexpensive overall, despite median rent of $995 exceeding the national median of $928. The rent burden of 16.2 percent of median household income—$73,922—remains well within a comfortable range, suggesting that housing does not disproportionately strain household budgets. Purchasing power in the county is strong, reflected by a Purchasing Power Index of 80,265.4, which exceeds the national baseline when adjusted for the lower cost environment. With median income $8,875 above the national median, residents retain substantial disposable income after covering local expenses. The low unemployment rate of 2.9 percent reinforces income stability, while the relatively low share of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher (16.0 percent) indicates a labor market that may favor lower‑skill employment but still supports a median income well above the national average. Trend analysis reveals modest changes over the past decade: the Regional Price Parity (RPP) fell from 93.022 in 2010 to 92.097 in 2023, a slight 1 percent decline, while median household income surged 39.9 percent to $73,922 and median rent rose 39.4 percent to $995. The parallel growth in wages and rental costs suggests that income gains have largely kept pace with housing expenses, preserving affordability. The median age of 39 years points to a mature workforce that may command higher earnings, while the low unemployment figure underscores a tight labor market that supports wage growth. For prospective residents, budgeting should prioritize the $995 monthly rent, which consumes less than one‑sixth of median income, and account for goods priced at 95.6 percent of the national level. Services such as utilities and dining are expected to be markedly cheaper, providing additional discretionary funds. Overall, the county’s cost structure—driven by modestly priced goods and inexpensive services—offers a household budget that retains a healthy margin of purchasing power, making it an attractive option for families seeking affordability without sacrificing income stability.

Analysis prepared by the Data in DP Research Team. Based on BEA RPP and Census ACS data.

What this means for residents of Kosciusko County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 80265.4, a median household income of $73,922 in Kosciusko County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $59,333,789 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
