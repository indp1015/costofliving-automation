📊 Local Market Analysis

The Chugach Census Area’s cost of living sits modestly above the national baseline, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) indexed at 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Goods prices—covering groceries, apparel, and fuel—register a 3.7% premium (103.7), while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are priced 3.3% below the national average (96.7). Housing, measured by median monthly rent, is the outlier: a 50.5% higher rent index (113.3) translates to $1,397 per month, compared with the national median of $928. The combined effect is a cost structure where the housing component dominates the expense profile, offset partially by cheaper services. Disposable income for the median household reflects this imbalance. Median annual earnings in Chugach are $88,565, 36% above the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index (PPI) of 85,738.2 indicates that residents retain roughly 14% less real purchasing power than the national average (set at 100). After accounting for rent, the median household spends about 18.9% of its income on housing, leaving roughly 81% for other expenditures. Because services are cheaper, everyday items such as medical visits, utilities, and restaurant meals consume a smaller share of the budget, but the high rent erodes the advantage of higher wages. The net effect is a modest squeeze on discretionary spending despite a relatively affluent income base. Demographic factors reinforce the cost picture. With a median age of 38.4 years, the workforce is in its prime earning years, yet only 20.3% hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a labor pool concentrated in lower‑skill, possibly seasonal or resource‑based occupations. The unemployment rate of 8.7% exceeds the national average, limiting wage growth and heightening the importance of stable employment for covering the elevated rent. The limited educational attainment may also constrain access to higher‑paying jobs that could offset housing costs, reinforcing the reliance on the modest service‑price advantage. Over the past decade, the RPP has barely moved, rising from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023—a 0.2% increase, indicating price stability across most categories. In contrast, median rent surged 21.8% from $1,147 to $1,397, while median household income slipped 2.4% from $90,776 to $88,565. The divergence shows that wages have not kept pace with housing inflation, compressing real disposable income. Prospective residents should budget rent as the primary expense, allocate roughly 19% of gross income to it, and rely on the lower cost of services to balance daily needs. Planning for a modest surplus after rent—approximately 60% of income for food, transportation, and utilities—will be essential to maintain a comfortable standard of living in the Chugach Census Area.
