📊 Local Market Analysis

Chugach Census Area presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) for 2023 registering 103.3 against a national index of 100. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel carry a 103.7 index, while services including healthcare, utilities, and dining come in below the national average at 96.7. The dominant distortion in the local budget is rental housing, which commands an index of 113.3, making shelter the primary driver of elevated expenses for area households. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life is expensive specifically because of shelter costs, not broader consumption. Median monthly rent stands at $1,397, a figure 50.5 percent higher than the national median of $928, yet the rent burden consumes only 18.9 percent of the local median household income of $88,565. This apparent affordability stems from income levels that exceed the national median of $65,047 by 36.2 percent. The resulting Purchasing Power Index of 85,738.2 indicates that after adjusting for the 3.3 percent overall price premium, the median household retains real purchasing power substantially higher than the nominal national median income, though the gap narrows considerably once the 13.3 percent rental premium is fully absorbed. Local demographics and labor dynamics complicate the affordability picture. An unemployment rate of 8.7 percent sits well above typical national levels, suggesting a labor market with significant slack or seasonal volatility that may suppress wage growth for a portion of the workforce. Educational attainment, with only 20.3 percent of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, correlates with an economy likely reliant on sectors where wage growth has lagged. The median age of 38.4 years indicates a workforce in its prime earning years, yet the high unemployment rate implies that the median income figure may not reflect the experience of a sizable segment of the population facing job insecurity or underemployment. Over the past decade, the cost trajectory has eroded real income gains. The overall RPP remained virtually flat, edging up only 0.2 percent from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023, yet nominal median income declined 2.4 percent from $90,776 to $88,565. Simultaneously, median rent surged 21.8 percent from $1,147 to $1,397. This divergence means housing costs have captured a growing share of household budgets while services have become relatively cheaper. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that run roughly 3.7 percent above national norms, utility and healthcare costs that offer slight discounts, and a rental market where lease obligations demand a significantly larger nominal outlay than most U.S. communities, requiring gross income sufficient to absorb that 13.3 percent shelter premium without crowding out other consumption.

What this means for residents of Chugach Census Area, Alaska
