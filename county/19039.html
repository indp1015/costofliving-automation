📊 Local Market Analysis

Clarke County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 11.2 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 88.8 for 2023, yet the composition of that discount reveals a distorted consumer landscape. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — command an index of 96.6, nearly converging with national pricing, while Services plummet to 66.0, the lowest component by a wide margin and a full 34 points below the benchmark. Rental costs occupy the middle ground at 87.3. This configuration means daily essentials such as food and transportation offer scant relief compared to metropolitan areas, whereas the steep discount on services — healthcare, utilities, and dining — drives the county’s overall affordability narrative, effectively subsidizing the household budget through lower overhead on non-tradeable local labor. Purchasing power metrics confirm that nominal income gains have translated into tangible real wage growth. The median household income of $66,821 exceeds the national median of $65,047, and when adjusted by the local RPP, the Purchasing Power Index rises to $75,275, granting the median earner roughly $10,200 in additional real buying capacity relative to a national average jurisdiction. Housing absorbs a mere 15.0 percent of gross income at a median rent of $836 — well below the national $928 — leaving a disproportionately large share of disposable income for savings or discretionary spending. However, the 96.6 Goods index tempers this advantage; a household allocating a standard 12 to 15 percent of expenditures to groceries and fuel realizes almost no geographic arbitrage on those line items, forcing budget discipline in categories where prices are nationally competitive. Demographic and labor market indicators suggest a workforce anchored in lower-wage, service-oriented employment that nonetheless benefits from the county’s depressed service-cost structure. With only 10.7 percent of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher and a median age of 38.4, the labor pool skews toward occupations that typically command modest premiums in high-cost metros but face minimal wage pressure locally. The 4.1 percent unemployment rate signals a balanced labor market, neither tight enough to spark rapid wage acceleration nor slack enough to depress earnings, creating a stable if unspectacular income environment that aligns with the low service-cost regime. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined 2.6 percent from 91.1 to 88.8, indicating that local consumer prices fell relative to the national basket even as median income surged 54.2 percent from $43,327 to $66,821. Rent increased only 23.3 percent over the same span, less than half the income growth rate, widening the gap between shelter costs and earnings. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that mirror national averages, but can anticipate significantly lower outlays for healthcare visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals. The critical budgeting variable remains the Goods category: without the cushion of cheaper food and energy, the county’s affordability advantage rests almost entirely on the service discount, a dynamic that favors households with low medical utilization and high home-cooking adherence.

What this means for residents of Clarke County, Iowa

With a Purchasing Power Index of 75275.2, a median household income of $66,821 in Clarke County, Iowa has the equivalent buying power of $50,299,641 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 87.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
