📊 Local Market Analysis

Lincoln County sits 10.1 percentage points below the national price level, with a 2023 Regional Price Parity of 89.9 that masks sharp divergence across consumption categories. Goods — groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — run only 5.5 percent below the U.S. average at 94.5, making everyday consumables the most expensive component of the local basket. Services, by contrast, register a striking 62.9, roughly 37 percent cheaper than the national benchmark, while rental costs settle at 80.9. This structure means a household's monthly outlays are heavily influenced by whether spending skews toward tangible goods or locally delivered services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining. The median household income of $50,862 translates into a purchasing power index of 56,582.5, effectively granting residents a 11.3 percent boost in real terms relative to their nominal earnings. Rent consumes just 17.2 percent of gross income at a median of $730, well below the national median of $928 and the 30 percent burden threshold that typically signals stress. However, the national median income of $65,047 exceeds the local figure by 27.9 percent, and the goods-heavy cost profile erodes some of that purchasing power advantage each time a resident fills a grocery cart or a gas tank. Disposable income stretches further on housing and services but tightens noticeably at the checkout line. Demographic and labor market conditions reinforce this dynamic. An unemployment rate of 7.6 percent sits well above the national norm, while only 9.3 percent of adults hold a bachelor's degree or higher, suggesting a labor market anchored in lower-wage sectors that limits upward pressure on wages. The median age of 41.9 years indicates a mature workforce with relatively fixed consumption patterns, likely weighting spending toward healthcare and household maintenance — categories where the local 62.9 services index provides meaningful relief. Yet the elevated jobless rate implies that a non-trivial share of households relies on a single earner or part-time work, making the low rent burden a critical stabilizer for family budgets. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP slipped a marginal 0.6 percent from 90.433 to 89.89, signaling broad price stability. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 37 percent from $37,139 to $50,862, outpacing a 28.3 percent rise in median rent from $569 to $730. Wages have not merely kept pace; they have expanded the cushion between earnings and shelter costs. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is clear: allocate a larger share of the monthly plan to groceries and fuel, where prices track close to national levels, and expect significant savings on rent, utilities, and healthcare services. The arithmetic favors households that can lock in housing at or below the $730 median and manage goods consumption carefully.

What this means for residents of Lincoln County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 56582.5, a median household income of $50,862 in Lincoln County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $28,778,991 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
