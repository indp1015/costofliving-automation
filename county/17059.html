📊 Local Market Analysis

Gallatin County presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally below the national baseline, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 98.8 in 2023, yet this aggregate figure masks a significant divergence in household expenditure categories. The primary inflationary pressure stems from the goods basket—groceries, apparel, and fuel—which runs 1.6 percent above the national average at 101.6, making daily consumables the most expensive component of the local budget. Conversely, services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining come in at a substantial 7.6 percent discount (92.4), while rental housing costs represent the deepest value proposition at 80.4, nearly twenty percent below the national norm. This structure creates a distinctive consumer environment where the cost of keeping a pantry stocked and a vehicle fueled outweighs the savings found in shelter and service contracts. The consumption breakdown reveals that daily life affordability hinges heavily on housing tenure. With median monthly rent at $500 against a national median of $928, renters devote only 11.0 percent of the median household income of $54,626 to shelter, a remarkably low burden that frees significant cash flow for the above-average goods expenses. The Purchasing Power Index of 55,316.3 quantifies this dynamic, indicating that the median dollar stretches further here than the nominal income suggests, effectively boosting real earnings by roughly $700 relative to the national median income of $65,047. However, this advantage is contingent on securing rental inventory; the low rental cost index reflects a market with limited demand pressure rather than a surplus of high-quality stock. Local demographics and labor conditions frame the practical limits of this purchasing power. An unemployment rate of 7.3 percent sits well above national levels, signaling a slack labor market that likely suppresses wage growth and limits upward mobility for the workforce. Educational attainment remains low, with only 9.6 percent of residents holding a bachelor’s degree or higher, correlating with the county’s modest income ceiling. The median age of 47.3 years suggests an older, potentially fixed-income population that benefits disproportionately from the low service and housing costs but remains vulnerable to goods inflation, particularly in healthcare-adjacent consumables and fuel necessary for navigating a rural geography. Over the past decade, the economic trajectory has favored the household balance sheet. The RPP declined from 101.778 in 2010 to 98.752 in 2023, a 3.0 percent drop in relative price levels, while median income surged 37.8 percent from $39,636 to $54,626. Median rent increased only 30.5 percent over the same period, lagging income growth and improving the rent-to-income ratio materially. For a prospective resident, the budgeting calculus is clear: allocate a larger share of the weekly budget to the grocery aisle and the gas pump, where prices exceed national norms, while expecting substantial relief on lease payments and utility bills. The primary financial risk lies not in shelter costs but in the volatility of the goods index and the fragility of a labor market where job loss could quickly erode the thin margin between income and the elevated cost of daily necessities.

What this means for residents of Gallatin County, Illinois

With a Purchasing Power Index of 55316.3, a median household income of $54,626 in Gallatin County, Illinois has the equivalent buying power of $30,217,082 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.4 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
