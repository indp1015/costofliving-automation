📊 Local Market Analysis

Cross County’s cost of living sits well below the national norm, with the BEA Regional Price Parities index at 86.8 versus 100 for the United States. Grocery, apparel and fuel prices are only modestly lower than average, registering a goods index of 93.1, while services such as healthcare, utilities and dining are markedly inexpensive at 56.7. Rental costs follow the same pattern, with an index of 81.9 and a median monthly rent of $797, roughly 14 % less than the national median of $928. The composite picture is one of a region where everyday necessities cost roughly 13 % less than the countrywide average, driven chiefly by the steep discount on services. The dominant cost driver in Cross County is the rental market, which accounts for 81.9 % of the national benchmark and consumes 20.3 % of median household income. Goods, while closer to national levels, still contribute a smaller premium, and the ultra‑low services index pulls overall expenses down. After paying rent, a median household earning $47,012 retains $39,215 for other expenditures, equating to a disposable income share of about 83 % of earnings. The Purchasing Power Index of 54,155.1 reflects this advantage: residents can purchase roughly 54 % more goods and services than the average American with the same nominal income, underscoring the real‑term benefit of lower local prices despite a median income that is 28 % below the national $65,047 figure. Demographic factors reinforce the affordability narrative. With only 10.1 % of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher and an unemployment rate of 8.5 %, the labor market is anchored by lower‑skill, lower‑wage occupations that typically demand less discretionary spending. A median age of 40.6 suggests a mature workforce, likely balancing family‑related expenses against modest earnings. The combination of limited high‑skill employment and a relatively older population dampens demand for premium services, helping keep the services index low and preserving purchasing power for essential goods. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parities index slipped from 88.6 to 86.8, a 2 % decline, while median household income rose from $38,103 to $47,012, a 23.4 % increase. Rental rates climbed in lockstep with income, moving from $645 to $797 (+23.6 %). The modest deflation in overall price levels outpaced income growth, indicating that wages have more than kept pace with rising housing costs and have outstripped the slight easing of other price pressures. Prospective residents should budget $800 for rent, allocate roughly $300–$350 for groceries and apparel, and expect utilities, healthcare and dining to cost less than half the national average. With a rent burden of just over one‑fifth of income, the remaining 80 % of earnings can comfortably cover transportation, modest entertainment and savings, making Cross County a financially forgiving environment for households prioritizing essential consumption over luxury spending.

What this means for residents of Cross County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 54155.1, a median household income of $47,012 in Cross County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $25,459,396 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
