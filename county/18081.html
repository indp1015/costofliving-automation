📊 Local Market Analysis

Johnson County, Indiana presents a cost of living profile that sits 7.9 percent below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 92.1 for 2023. This aggregate figure masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods—encompassing groceries, apparel, and fuel—run only 4.4 percent below the national baseline at 95.6, while rental costs sit 15.3 percent lower at 84.7. The most dramatic deviation appears in services, where healthcare, utilities, and dining expenses plummet to 71.3, nearly 29 percent below the U.S. average. This structural anomaly makes daily life notably inexpensive for service-intensive households, though the relative price of tangible goods remains closer to parity, limiting total savings at the checkout counter. The consumption breakdown reveals that services act as the primary deflationary driver in the local cost structure, suppressing the overall index well below the goods and housing components. A median household income of $87,227—34.1 percent above the national median of $65,047—combines with a median monthly rent of $1,208 to produce a rent burden of just 16.6 percent, a figure that signals substantial budgetary breathing room compared to national norms where burdens frequently exceed 30 percent. The Purchasing Power Index of 94,712.1 quantifies this advantage, indicating that the median earner commands roughly $29,665 more in real purchasing capacity than the nominal national median suggests after adjusting for local price levels. Local demographics reinforce this economic posture. An unemployment rate of 3.0 percent signals a tight labor market supporting wage growth, while a median age of 38.1 years suggests a workforce in peak earning years. However, the relatively low educational attainment—only 20.9 percent hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—implies the income premium is likely driven by industrial or logistical employment clusters rather than knowledge-sector density, a factor that sustains wage stability without the cost-push inflation typically associated with high-skill migration hubs. Over the past decade, the RPP has remained remarkably static, slipping only 1.0 percent from 93.022 in 2010 to 92.097 in 2023, indicating minimal inflationary pressure on the local consumption basket. Nominal median income surged 40.4 percent over the same period, rising from $62,147 to $87,227, while median rent increased 42.3 percent from $849 to $1,208. The near-parallel trajectory of income and rent growth suggests housing costs have absorbed a consistent share of wage gains, yet the persistently low service costs have prevented a broader erosion of real disposable income. For a prospective resident, the budgeting implication is clear: allocate a standard share for groceries and fuel where prices track national levels, but expect significant discounts on healthcare premiums, utility bills, and restaurant meals, effectively freeing discretionary capital for savings or debt service that would otherwise vanish in higher-service-cost metros.

What this means for residents of Johnson County, Indiana

With a Purchasing Power Index of 94712.1, a median household income of $87,227 in Johnson County, Indiana has the equivalent buying power of $82,614,523 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 84.7 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
