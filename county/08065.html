📊 Local Market Analysis

Lake County’s overall cost‑of‑living index sits at 101.9, barely above the national benchmark of 100, but the composition of that figure reveals a pronounced skew toward services. Goods such as groceries, apparel and fuel register at 99.2, essentially on par with the countrywide average, while the services index—covering healthcare, utilities and dining—peaks at 130.5, a 30.5% premium over the national level. Housing, measured by rental costs, is the sole category below average at 86.8, indicating that rents are roughly 13% cheaper than the U.S. norm. The service premium dominates the local cost structure. Residents pay substantially more for health care, utility bills and restaurant meals, which pushes daily living expenses upward despite the modest rent burden of 20.5% of household income. With a median monthly rent of $1,599 versus the national median of $928, the absolute rent figure is higher, yet the relative affordability remains favorable because median household income in Lake County reaches $93,655—42% above the national median of $65,047. Consequently, the disposable income left after covering rent and the elevated service costs translates into a Purchasing Power Index of 91,948.4, roughly 8% below the national average (which would be 100,000). In practical terms, a median earner retains about $78,000 after rent, but the high service prices erode roughly $12,000 of that cushion, leaving a net purchasing power that is modestly constrained relative to peers in lower‑cost regions. Demographic factors reinforce this profile. The unemployment rate of 2.3% signals a tight labor market, while 27.0% of residents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, suggesting a skilled workforce that commands higher wages. The median age of 36.9 years places the population squarely in the prime earning phase, which supports the elevated median income. However, the same educated, younger cohort tends to consume more health and wellness services, explaining the inflated services index. The low unemployment also limits wage competition for entry‑level positions, potentially widening the gap between high earners who can absorb service premiums and lower‑income households that feel the pinch. Over the past decade, the Regional Price Parity (RPP) rose from 100.59 in 2010 to 101.856 in 2023, a modest 1.3% increase, while median household income more than doubled, climbing 106.4% from $45,367 to $93,655. Median rent surged 88.1% in the same period, from $850 to $1,599. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rent escalation, indicating that wages have largely kept pace with, and even exceeded, rising costs. The disparity between the modest RPP rise and the sharp income gain suggests that residents have experienced real gains in purchasing power, albeit tempered by the steep service cost component. Prospective residents should budget with the service premium in mind. A realistic monthly allocation might include $1,600 for rent, $500–$600 for utilities and health‑care expenses, and $400–$500 for dining and personal services, leaving roughly $2,000–$2,500 for discretionary spending after accounting for taxes on a median income. The rent burden remains comfortably below the 30% threshold commonly used to flag housing stress, but the high service costs mean that budgeting for healthcare, utilities and dining will dominate the household ledger. Understanding that the cost advantage of cheaper rents is largely offset by expensive services is essential for accurate financial planning in Lake County.

What this means for residents of Lake County, Colorado

With a Purchasing Power Index of 91948.4, a median household income of $93,655 in Lake County, Colorado has the equivalent buying power of $86,114,274 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 86.8 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
