📊 Local Market Analysis

Ashley County’s cost‑of‑living index sits at 86.8, meaning that a typical consumer basket costs roughly 13 % less than the national average of 100. The goods component—groceries, apparel, and fuel—registers at 93.1, only 7 % below the national norm, while services such as healthcare, utilities, and dining are markedly cheaper at 56.7, a 43 % discount. Rental costs, measured by the BEA RPP housing sub‑index, are 81.9, indicating rents are about 18 % lower than the countrywide figure. These three sub‑indices together shape a picture of a market where everyday necessities are modestly priced, but discretionary services are substantially cheaper, pulling the overall index down. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services sector, whose index of 56.7 dwarfs both goods (93.1) and rental costs (81.9). Consequently, daily life in Ashley County feels inexpensive for activities that rely on services—medical visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals cost less than half the national average. Goods remain relatively close to national levels, so food and clothing expenses are only slightly reduced. Rent, at a median of $640 per month, is 31 % below the national median of $928, and the rent‑burden metric of 17.3 % of household income confirms that housing consumes a modest share of earnings. The combination of low service costs and affordable rent makes the overall environment cheap for most households, though the near‑national price of goods tempers the savings. Median household income in Ashley County stands at $44,481, 32 % below the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median monthly rent of $640 (7 % of annual income), a typical household retains roughly $38,000 of disposable earnings. The Purchasing Power Index of 51,239.5 quantifies this gap: residents can purchase about half the goods and services that an average American can with the same nominal income. The low service index amplifies purchasing power for health and utility expenses, but the modest goods index and lower wages limit overall disposable capacity. Demographically, the county’s labor force is characterized by a 6.1 % unemployment rate and a modest 8.7 % share of adults holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median age of 42.7 suggests a mature population, likely with stable employment in sectors that do not command premium wages. Limited higher‑education attainment constrains access to high‑paying occupations, reinforcing the lower median income. The unemployment figure, while above the national rate, does not translate into severe rent stress because housing costs are low, but it does signal a labor market that offers fewer high‑skill, high‑pay roles, which in turn sustains the modest purchasing power. Over the past decade, the regional price parity (RPP) index slipped from 88.56 in 2010 to 86.81 in 2023, a 2 % decline, indicating that consumer prices have softened relative to the nation. In the same period, median household income rose from $34,263 to $44,481, a 29.8 % increase, while median rent grew from $588 to $640, an 8.8 % rise. Income growth outpaced both overall price inflation and rent escalation, suggesting that wages have been keeping pace with, and even exceeding, the rise in living costs. The widening gap between income and expenses enhances the affordability of everyday consumption. Prospective residents should allocate a larger portion of their budget to goods—particularly food and clothing—because those prices remain close to national levels. With rent consuming only 17.3 % of income, utilities, healthcare, and dining out will be comparatively inexpensive, allowing discretionary spending on non‑essential services. A prudent monthly budget might earmark roughly $300 for groceries, $150 for transportation fuel, $100 for utilities, and $120 for healthcare, leaving a comfortable cushion for savings or leisure. Understanding that the primary cost advantage lies in services, newcomers can plan household expenditures around the modestly priced essentials while leveraging the low rent environment to maintain a balanced budget.

What this means for residents of Ashley County, Arkansas

With a Purchasing Power Index of 51239.5, a median household income of $44,481 in Ashley County, Arkansas has the equivalent buying power of $22,791,842 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 81.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
