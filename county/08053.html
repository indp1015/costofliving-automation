📊 Local Market Analysis

The cost of living in Hinsdale County runs marginally above the national baseline, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 101.9 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel are priced at 99.2, essentially on par with the countrywide average, while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are markedly higher at 130.5. Rental costs are the only sector below the national norm, with a housing index of 86.8, reflecting rents that are 20.5 % of median household income and $1,099 per month on average, compared with the national median rent of $928. The dominant driver of the local cost structure is the services component, which exceeds the national level by more than 30 %. This premium on services pushes daily living expenses upward despite the relative affordability of goods and the low housing index. For a typical household, the combination of higher-priced medical care, utility bills, and restaurant meals translates into a cost environment that feels expensive in day‑to‑day consumption, even though grocery bills and fuel purchases remain comparable to those elsewhere. The low rental index mitigates the overall burden, but the net effect is a cost profile that leans toward the higher end of the spectrum because service expenditures dominate household budgets. Disposable income for the median worker in Hinsdale County can be approximated by subtracting the rent burden from median household earnings. With a median income of $64,327 and a monthly rent of $1,099, annual housing costs total $13,188, representing 20.5 % of income. After accounting for this rent, roughly $51,139 remains for other expenses, taxes, and savings. The Purchasing Power Index of 63,154.8 underscores that, relative to the national benchmark of 100, residents retain about 63 % of the purchasing power of an average American household when local price levels are considered. In practical terms, the median household can afford fewer discretionary goods and services than a counterpart in a lower‑cost region, despite earning a figure only 1 % below the national median income of $65,047. Demographic characteristics shape the cost experience in distinct ways. A bachelor’s‑degree attainment rate of 29.6 % and a median age of 54.8 years suggest a mature, relatively well‑educated population, often associated with higher consumption of healthcare and leisure services—precisely the categories where the county’s price indices are steepest. The unemployment rate of 1.5 % indicates a tight labor market, which can sustain wage levels but also limits the influx of lower‑cost labor that might otherwise dampen service prices. Consequently, residents face a cost structure that reflects both the demand for premium services and the limited supply of lower‑priced alternatives. Over the past decade, the RPP rose from 100.59 in 2010 to 101.856 in 2023, a modest 1.3 % increase. In the same period, median household income climbed from $57,083 to $64,327, a 12.7 % gain, while median rent surged from $692 to $1,099, a 58.8 % jump. Income growth outpaced the overall price index but lagged dramatically behind rental inflation, indicating that while wages have kept pace with general consumer costs, housing affordability has deteriorated sharply. The disproportionate rise in rent underscores the importance of budgeting for housing even in a market where other costs remain relatively stable. Prospective residents should allocate a larger share of their budget to services than to goods or housing. With rent consuming roughly one‑fifth of income, the remaining 80 % must cover utilities, healthcare, and dining, all of which carry a 30 % premium over national averages. A prudent household budget would therefore earmark at least 35–40 % of net income for service‑related expenses, 25–30 % for groceries and transportation, and the remaining 30–35 % for savings, debt service, and discretionary spending. Understanding this allocation is essential for maintaining financial stability in a county where service costs dominate the consumer price landscape.
