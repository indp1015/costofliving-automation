📊 Local Market Analysis

Butler County residents navigate a cost environment roughly 10 percent below the national benchmark, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity for 2023 registering 89.9 against a U.S. baseline of 100. This aggregate discount masks significant divergence across consumption categories: goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel sit at 94.5, only marginally cheaper than the national average, while rental costs come in at 80.9. The most striking deviation appears in services—healthcare, utilities, and dining—which clock in at a mere 62.9, dragging the overall index down and signaling that daily service consumption is substantially less expensive than in most of the country. The services component acts as the primary anchor suppressing the local cost structure, effectively offsetting the near-parity pricing of goods. A median household income of $52,003 translates into a purchasing power index of 57,852, meaning the typical earner commands roughly $5,800 more in real goods and services than the nominal wage suggests. Housing affordability reinforces this dynamic; median monthly rent of $688 consumes only 15.9 percent of gross income, a fraction of the 30-percent burden threshold and well below the national median rent of $928. Consequently, disposable income retention after shelter costs is unusually high, allowing households to absorb the relatively stickier goods prices without severe budget compression. Demographic and labor market characteristics frame the sustainability of this advantage. An unemployment rate of 4.2 percent sits near structural norms, yet educational attainment remains low—only 5.1 percent of adults hold a bachelor’s degree or higher—while the median age of 41.1 years skews older than the national profile. This composition suggests a workforce concentrated in lower-wage, service-oriented or manual sectors, which aligns with the modest nominal income of $52,003 versus the national $65,047. The labor market appears stable but lacks the high-wage knowledge-economy drivers that typically push service costs upward, creating a feedback loop where low service prices persist alongside suppressed wage growth. Over the 2010–2023 period, the RPP drifted downward marginally from 90.4 to 89.9, a negligible 0.6 percent decline, while median income surged 55.1 percent from $33,536 to $52,003. Rent increased only 28.8 percent over the same span, from $534 to $688, meaning real wage growth significantly outpaced shelter inflation. For a prospective resident, the budgeting picture is defined by cheap services and housing that together liberate cash flow, but goods purchases—fuel, food, clothing—deliver minimal savings relative to urban centers. Household budgets should therefore allocate standard national amounts for consumables while anticipating materially lower outlays for medical visits, utility bills, and restaurant meals, a structural feature unlikely to reverse absent a fundamental shift in the local economic base.

What this means for residents of Butler County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 57851.8, a median household income of $52,003 in Butler County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $30,084,672 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
