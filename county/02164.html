📊 Local Market Analysis

Lake and Peninsula Borough presents a cost of living profile that sits marginally above the national baseline, with the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parity (RPP) for 2023 registering 103.3 against a national average of 100. This aggregate figure masks significant internal divergence: rental housing costs drive the premium at 113.3, while goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel run moderately elevated at 103.7. Uniquely, services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—actually undercut the national average at 96.7, creating a bifurcated expense structure where shelter dominates the household budget while service consumption offers relative relief. The rental housing component functions as the primary inflationary engine for local consumers. At 113.3, housing costs exceed the national norm by over thirteen percentage points, a differential that far outstrips the 3.7 percent premium on goods. Median monthly rent stands at $933, nearly identical to the national median of $928, yet the RPP suggests the quality-adjusted value of that rental dollar is significantly lower locally. This dynamic compresses disposable income despite a seemingly manageable rent burden of 17.5 percent of the $64,000 median household income. The Purchasing Power Index of 61,957.3 confirms that after adjusting for the 103.3 price level, the real value of local earnings falls roughly $2,000 short of the nominal median, effectively erasing the nominal income parity with the national median of $65,047. Local labor market characteristics amplify the pressure on household budgets. An unemployment rate of 9.5 percent signals substantial slack in the economy, likely contributing to the modest educational attainment figure of 12.3 percent holding a bachelor’s degree or higher. The young median age of 33.4 years suggests a workforce potentially concentrated in entry-level or seasonal sectors—common in remote Alaskan boroughs reliant on fishing, tourism, or public administration—where wage growth struggles to match shelter inflation. This demographic profile implies a population with limited bargaining power in a rental market where supply constraints keep the housing RPP stubbornly high, forcing a larger share of nominal income toward fixed shelter costs. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the overall RPP barely moved, climbing a negligible 0.2 percent from 103.099 to 103.297, indicating broad price stability for the consumption basket. However, nominal median income grew 26 percent, rising from $50,781 to $64,000, while median rent accelerated 32 percent, jumping from $707 to $933. This divergence means housing costs have captured a disproportionate share of wage gains over the decade. For a prospective resident, the budgeting reality is clear: the sticker price of rent appears nationally competitive, but the quality-adjusted cost is high, services are a relative bargain, and goods carry a standard remote-area premium. Household financial planning must prioritize the shelter allocation, as the 17.5 percent burden ratio understates the true consumption sacrifice imposed by the 113.3 housing RPP.

What this means for residents of Lake and Peninsula Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 61957.3, a median household income of $64,000 in Lake and Peninsula Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $39,652,672 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
