📊 Local Market Analysis

Ballard County residents operate within a cost structure that sits 10.1 percentage points below the national average, registering an overall Regional Price Parity of 89.9 against the 100 baseline. This discount is not uniform across the consumption basket. Goods — encompassing groceries, apparel, and motor fuel — represent the highest local cost pressure at 94.5, nearly converging with national pricing. Rental housing follows at 80.9, offering a meaningful 19.1 percent discount relative to the broader market. The dominant deflationary force is the services category, which clocks in at a strikingly low 62.9, driven by below-average healthcare, utility, and dining expenses. This composition creates a daily life where discretionary service consumption is exceptionally cheap, while essential goods purchases erode a larger share of the household budget than the headline index suggests. The median household income of $64,740 tracks nearly identically with the national median of $65,047, yet the Purchasing Power Index of 72,021.4 reveals a significant real income advantage. After adjusting for local price levels, the typical Ballard household commands roughly $7,300 more in real purchasing capacity than the nominal wage gap implies. Housing affordability underscores this dynamic: median rent of $826 consumes only 15.3 percent of gross income, well below the 30 percent burden threshold and substantially lighter than the national median rent of $928. For a renter, this translates to approximately $1,200 in annual shelter savings versus the national benchmark, freeing cash flow for goods consumption or savings. Demographic and labor market characteristics reinforce the low-cost service environment. A median age of 44.2 years and a bachelor's degree attainment rate of just 12.9 percent suggest a workforce concentrated in trades, manufacturing, and service occupations rather than high-wage knowledge sectors. The 4.1 percent unemployment rate indicates a tight but not overheated labor market, consistent with modest wage pressure that helps keep service prices — particularly labor-intensive healthcare and personal care — depressed. This structural profile means the cost advantage is likely durable rather than cyclical, rooted in the region's economic composition rather than temporary slack. Over the 2010 to 2023 period, the RPP declined marginally from 90.4 to 89.9, a 0.6 percent drop signaling remarkable price stability. Meanwhile, nominal median income surged 53.3 percent from $42,244, outpacing the 35.2 percent rise in median rent from $611. Real wage growth has been robust, expanding the gap between earnings and shelter costs. A prospective resident should budget for grocery and fuel bills that run only slightly below city averages, while anticipating significantly lower outlays for medical visits, electricity, and restaurant meals. The rent burden metric confirms that housing quality adjustments — newer units or larger square footage — are attainable without breaching standard affordability ratios, a rare flexibility in the current national climate.

What this means for residents of Ballard County, Kentucky

With a Purchasing Power Index of 72021.4, a median household income of $64,740 in Ballard County, Kentucky has the equivalent buying power of $46,626,654 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 80.9 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
