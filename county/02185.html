📊 Local Market Analysis

North Slope Borough’s cost of living sits modestly above the national baseline, with the overall Regional Price Parity (RPP) index at 103.3 versus 100 for the United States. Goods such as groceries, apparel, and fuel register a 103.7 index, while services—including healthcare, utilities, and dining—are priced 3.3 % below the national average at 96.7. Housing, measured by median monthly rent, is the outlier: a 113.3 index places rental costs 13.3 % higher than the countrywide figure of $928, translating to $1,167 per month locally. The price structure is dominated by housing. Although everyday commodities and services are roughly on par with—or slightly cheaper than—national levels, the elevated rent pushes the overall cost profile upward. A resident spending the national average on groceries and utilities would still face a higher total monthly outlay because rent alone consumes 16.2 % of median household income, compared with the national rent‑to‑income ratio of roughly 13.6 % (median rent $928 on a median income of $65,047). Consequently, daily life in the borough feels expensive chiefly due to shelter costs, while discretionary spending on goods and services remains relatively affordable. Disposable income calculations underscore this dynamic. Median household income in North Slope Borough is $86,313, 32.6 % above the national median of $65,047. After subtracting the median rent of $1,167, a typical household retains $85,146 in annual earnings, or roughly $7,095 per month. The Purchasing Power Index of 83,558.1—expressed as a dollar amount rather than a relative index—indicates that, after accounting for the higher cost of housing, residents possess about $22,000 less purchasing power than a median American household would enjoy in a cost‑neutral environment. In other words, higher earnings are partially offset by the premium on rental housing, leaving a modest net advantage. Demographic factors shape this picture. Only 8.9 % of the adult population holds a bachelor’s degree or higher, well below the national average, suggesting a labor force concentrated in industries that do not require extensive post‑secondary education, such as oil, gas, and government services. The median age of 35.2 years points to a relatively young workforce, while the 6.6 % unemployment rate exceeds the national rate of roughly 3.5 % in 2023. The combination of a younger, less‑educated labor pool and higher unemployment exerts upward pressure on wages needed to sustain household budgets, especially given the housing premium. Over the past decade, the RPP index rose from 103.099 in 2010 to 103.297 in 2023, a marginal 0.2 % increase, indicating price stability for most consumer categories. Median household income, however, surged 18.9 % from $72,576 to $86,313, outpacing the 7.4 % rise in median rent (from $1,087 to $1,167). This divergence suggests that wages have kept pace with, and indeed exceeded, the modest inflation in consumer prices, enhancing overall affordability despite the high housing cost component. Prospective residents should anchor their budgets around the $1,167 monthly rent, which alone represents a sizable slice of income. With utilities, healthcare, and food priced near or below national levels, allocating roughly 30 % of net earnings to housing leaves about 70 % for other necessities and discretionary spending. Given the 6.6 % unemployment backdrop, maintaining an emergency fund equivalent to three months of rent—approximately $3,500—offers a prudent buffer. Finally, the limited prevalence of higher education implies that career opportunities may be concentrated in specific sectors; aligning employment prospects with those industries will be essential for sustaining the modest purchasing power advantage that North Slope Borough affords.

What this means for residents of North Slope Borough, Alaska

With a Purchasing Power Index of 83558.1, a median household income of $86,313 in North Slope Borough, Alaska has the equivalent buying power of $72,121,503 in an average U.S. county.

Because the Housing Rent index is 113.3 (vs. the national average of 100), housing is the primary driver of the local cost of living. Prospective residents should budget carefully for rent or mortgage payments, even if everyday goods and services feel relatively affordable.
