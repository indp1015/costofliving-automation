Cost of Living in Delaware

Below are the counties in Delaware, ranked by Purchasing Power Index (higher is better). A score above 100 means residents have more disposable income relative to local costs.

📊 State-Level Economic Review

Delaware’s overall consumer cost index of 98.8 in 2023 indicates that the state’s average daily cost of living is modestly below the national benchmark of 100, suggesting a slight advantage for households on a day‑to‑day basis. Within this aggregate picture, goods costs sit at 97.3, services at 98.9, and rental costs at 104.4, revealing a nuanced cost structure where housing expenses are the only category consistently above the national average while everyday goods and services remain marginally cheaper. This pattern underscores that while Delaware residents may benefit from lower prices for non‑housing items, they face higher rental rates that can offset those savings. The disparity in consumer purchasing power across Delaware’s three principal counties is pronounced. New Castle County leads with a purchasing power of 91,030.7, whereas Kent County trails at 73,787.7. The gap of roughly 17,243.0 points between the highest and lowest counties illustrates a significant internal variation in residents’ ability to stretch income against local price levels. Such a spread suggests that households in New Castle County can allocate a larger share of their earnings to discretionary or savings‑oriented activities compared with those in Kent County, where the same income translates into a tighter budget. Among the three consumer categories, rental costs emerge as the primary cost driver above the national average. At 104.4, rents exceed the benchmark by 4.4 points, while goods (97.3) and services (98.9) remain below it. This elevation in housing expenses likely exerts upward pressure on overall household budgets, particularly for renters, and may explain why the state’s overall index does not reflect a more pronounced discount despite lower costs for other goods and services. The dominance of rental costs as the outlier category warrants attention for policymakers and analysts assessing affordability. For residents seeking maximum value within their household budget, New Castle County offers the most favorable environment. Its purchasing power of 91,030.7 is the highest among the three counties, indicating that income translates into greater consumption capacity relative to local prices. Coupled with the state’s overall cost index below the national level, households in New Castle County can generally stretch their earnings farther than those in Sussex or Kent County, where purchasing power is lower.
