Cost of Living in South Carolina

Below are the counties in South Carolina, ranked by Purchasing Power Index (higher is better). A score above 100 means residents have more disposable income relative to local costs.

📊 State-Level Economic Review

South Carolina’s cost profile remains modestly below the national benchmark, with an overall consumer‑cost index of 93.5 against a national baseline of 100, indicating that the average daily cost of living is roughly 6.5 percent lower than the United States average. Within this composite picture, goods costs sit at 95.9, services at 80.5 and rental costs at 85.8, each measured on the same index scale. The goods component is the closest to the national level, while services and rents are markedly cheaper, suggesting that households in the Palmetto State allocate a smaller share of income to housing and personal services than the typical American family. The disparity in purchasing power across South Carolina’s counties is striking. The highest purchasing‑power county, Beaufort, records an index of 90 737.8, whereas the lowest, Allendale, registers just 33 808.3. This spread translates to a difference of approximately 56 929.5 index points, or roughly 168 percent higher purchasing power in Beaufort relative to Allendale. The variation underscores how geographic location can dramatically affect a household’s ability to stretch each dollar, even within a state that is already below the national cost baseline. Among the three consumer categories, goods are the most above average. At 95.9, goods are the only component that approaches the national parity point, while services (80.5) and rents (85.8) lag further behind. The relatively higher goods index suggests that everyday items such as food, clothing and household supplies cost more in South Carolina than services or housing, a pattern that may influence budgeting priorities for families seeking to maximize value. Residents seeking the greatest value for their household budgets should focus on the counties with the strongest purchasing‑power metrics. Beaufort, Charleston and York counties each exceed 88 000 on the purchasing‑power scale, indicating that households there can command a larger basket of goods and services for the same income level. While low‑cost counties such as Allendale or Marion exhibit lower living expenses, their purchasing‑power indices are substantially depressed, limiting the practical benefit of cheaper prices. Consequently, the combination of relatively low overall costs and high purchasing power makes the coastal and upstate regions of South Carolina the most advantageous locales for residents aiming to stretch their dollars without sacrificing access to goods and services.
