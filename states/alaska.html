Cost of Living in Alaska

Below are the counties in Alaska, ranked by Purchasing Power Index (higher is better). A score above 100 means residents have more disposable income relative to local costs.

📊 State-Level Economic Review

Alaska’s overall consumer cost index of 103.3 positions the state modestly above the national baseline of 100, reflecting a 3.3 % premium for everyday expenses. Goods costs follow a similar pattern at 103.7, while services remain slightly below average at 96.7. Rental costs, however, stand out at 113.3, indicating a pronounced 13.3 % upward deviation from the national norm. The median household income of $79,407 and the state‑wide purchasing power of $76,872.8 suggest that, despite higher outlays for housing, Alaskans retain a substantial share of income after consumption. The interplay of these figures underscores a cost structure where shelter dominates the expense profile, yet disposable income remains relatively robust. Purchasing power varies dramatically across Alaska’s counties, illustrating stark regional disparities. The most affluent area, Aleutians West Census Area, records a purchasing power of $103,917.8, whereas the lowest, Kusilvak Census Area, registers $46,693.5. This spread amounts to a difference of $57,224.3, or roughly 124 % of the lowest figure, highlighting how economic conditions can shift by more than half a million dollars in annual purchasing capacity within the same state. Sitka City and Borough and Bristol Bay Borough fall near the upper tier, while Prince of Wales‑Hyder, Yukon‑Koyukuk, and Kusilvak occupy the lower echelon, emphasizing geographic clustering of affluence versus hardship. Among the three consumer categories, rental costs are the most above average. At 113.3, rents exceed the state average by 13.3 % points, surpassing the modest 3.7 % premium for goods and the 3.3 % premium for overall consumer costs. Services, in contrast, remain 3.3 % below the national benchmark at 96.7. This divergence suggests that housing affordability is the primary driver of Alaska’s elevated cost of living, outweighing price pressures in other sectors. The disproportionate rent premium likely reflects the unique logistical challenges and limited housing supply in many Alaskan communities. For residents seeking maximum value within their household budgets, the counties with the highest purchasing power present the most favorable environment. Aleutians West Census Area leads with a purchasing power of $103,917.8, followed by Sitka City and Borough at $97,976.7 and Bristol Bay Borough at $97,413.3.
